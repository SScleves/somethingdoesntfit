# Comp — Philip K. Dick, *A Scanner Darkly* / *Una mirada a la oscuridad* (1977; tr. César Terrón, Ediciones Acervo 1980)

> **Coverage:** read in full 11 Oct 2026, every page from the first louse to the last name on the list: seventeen chapters, the three interpolated title-cards, the Nota del autor. ~88k words in a Spain-Spanish translation from a digital edition with a few scan errors ("SÍ HUBIERA SABIDO" for "Si"; "Ardor" for "Arctor" once). All quotes below are as printed in this translation with an English gloss; the originals differ and must be checked before any quote goes to print. Terrón renders Substance D as "Sustancia M" (M for Muerte), the scramble suit as "monotraje mezclador", the scanners as "holocámaras", the straights as "los honrados".
> **Brief:** the handler who is also the handled, for Kalder's [K] track and the reveal that hurts; surveillance read as data; pace; the ending and the afterword; the trap; the plug-in.
> **Standing-rule note:** fiction, set in a 1994 imagined from 1977. Nothing to protect. The Author's Note names real people by first name; it is a dedication, not a claim. See §2g for why that matters to our no-note rule.

> **How to use this file:** inspiration for what is already Santi's tone and ideas, not a template and not a 1:1 copy. The book is in English; language and register notes have been removed. Where a scene is proposed, it is a proposal.

## 1. What it is

Orange County, June 1994. Bob Arctor shares a decaying house with two burnouts, Luckman and Barris, and deals a little to keep a cover. Under the name Fred he is an undercover narcotics agent who reports to a superior called Hank; both wear scramble suits that project a million and a half faces a second, so neither knows who the other is. An anonymous tip names Arctor as a dealer worth watching. Hank assigns Fred to Arctor. Six holographic scanners are installed in the house, and every other day Fred drives to a safe apartment and watches tapes of himself. Substance D, the drug he takes to stay in character, splits the two hemispheres of his brain until the two halves file reports on each other as strangers. When the department finally tells him he is Arctor, he no longer is. His girl Donna, who was the agent all along, delivers him to New-Path, a rehabilitation chain that is secretly the drug's manufacturer, so that what is left of him can be carried inside as a camera nobody can interrogate. In a Napa field he bends to a small blue flower and puts one in his shoe for his friends.

This is the book the brief's two hardest ideas were already written in: the handler who files reports on the handled and is him, and the watcher who turns out to be the instrument. It is also, structurally, a long siege with no battle, which is what Movements III and IV need and Wool only half supplies.

## 2. Mechanisms worth stealing

### 2a. The report scene: a neutral room where feeling is suspended, and the bill comes after

Dick stages the first Fred–Hank session as two blurs facing each other: "la masa difusa que respondía al nombre de Fred" [the vague blur that answered to the name Fred]. Hank reads names off holonotes; Fred answers in a monotone; the one war story Hank tells (a nineteen-year-old who looks fifty) Fred cuts off with "No hace falta que prosiga" [you needn't go on]. Then the paragraph that is the whole technique: in the sessions nothing has emotional weight, and afterwards the feelings "se desbocaban" [bolted]. Hank does not force the neutrality; he "le permitía ser así" [allowed him to be that way], and Fred is grateful. Firemen, doctors and undertakers, the narration says, take the uniform off to feel.

Then Hank asks, "¿Qué hay de Arctor?" [What about Arctor?], and Fred says what he always says, which is nothing much. The tip arrives. Hank asks for Arctor's mother's maiden name and Fred supplies it: Postlethwaite, Welsh, Harlech, 1468. "Mierda, pensó, esto es terrible" [Shit, he thought, this is terrible]. And the scene ends on inventory numbers for the surveillance gear. **That is the Kalder report scene entire:** a room that permits no feeling, a superior who will not say who he is, a question about the man you love, an answer you give because the room gives it, a clock that moves (equipment ordered, a date, a name) and the feeling only on the street afterwards, "entre la pizzería y la gasolinera Arco". Every [K] chapter can be built on this template: competent below, afraid above, and the bill paid outside.

Hank is also the model for the brief's rule that Keepers never argue with the soul claim; they make it funny. He never argues anything. When Fred says one day they'll both be shot by a crazed colleague, Hank says "Será un alivio" [It'll be a relief]. When he tells Fred his brain has two working cells left, he makes a Phillies joke. When Fred asks why he does this work, Hank asks back: "Sinceramente, ¿por qué lo hace?" and Fred: "Me gustaría saberlo" [I'd like to know].

### 2b. The scramble suit: a face made of everyone, and what it licenses

The suit is a Bell Labs accident: a quartz lens and a membrane that projects partial faces of a million and a half people, randomised, so the wearer is "todas las personas y en todas las combinaciones posibles" [everyone, in every possible combination]. The inventor fed in his own face so it recurs once every fifty years: "Representaba su mayor aproximación a la inmortalidad" [his nearest approach to immortality]. The suit is the brief's "part of the world, but nobody" made into a garment. Two uses:

- **Nobody knows who is inside, including the people who pay him.** At the Lions Club, Fred wonders whether the audience could tell if another Fred, or no one, or a voice piped in from the sheriff's office, were in the suit. Later Hank identifies him only "por un proceso de eliminación" [by a process of elimination]: "Sé quién no es usted" [I know who you are not], and the group is small. That is James's funeral arithmetic run backwards by the machine.
- **The suit is a licence.** In the cafeteria Fred realises he could assault a secretary and never be identified: "Esta vestimenta es ideal para cometer delitos" [This outfit is ideal for committing crimes]. For the Housed, the seat is the suit: the thing that makes you nobody is the thing that lets you do anything.

### 2c. The halves stop recognising each other in eight steps, not one

The brief asked for "the moment". There is no moment; Dick ratchets it, and the ratchet is the model for how [K] chapters should move the clock on Kalder himself.

1. The street after the speech: "¿Qué es identidad? ¿Cuándo termina la comedia?" [What is identity? When does the act end?]. Rhetorical, still one man.
2. The first report (2a): he supplies his own mother's name. Pain of being asked, not yet of not knowing.
3. A joke opens the question. Luckman wonders how many Robert Arctors a computer would count, and Bob thinks: at least two, one called Fred, "¿Pero quién soy yo? ¿Fred o Bob?" [But who am I? Fred or Bob?].
4. The plan of the cameras: Fred tells Hank "Me eliminaré del montaje" [I'll edit myself out of the footage]; Hank says do it "creadora" [creatively], or elimination will reveal him. Then, unprompted, Fred pitches Arctor's house to the department as a future asset. Hank asks if he is recommending they seize it after the arrest. Fred cannot answer.
5. The safe-apartment washroom, after the mushrooms: "¡Ese es Fred! ¡El que está en la casa es Fred sin el monotraje mezclador!" [That's Fred! The one in the house is Fred without the suit!]. He now sees Arctor as Fred's costume. Reversed, still one.
6. The locksmith's cheque, in his own handwriting: "lo hizo el mismo hijo de puta de Arctor" [that son of a bitch Arctor did it himself]. Arctor is now a third party. The narration starts calling him "Arctor-Fred". He decides the house "Deberían confiscarle" [should be confiscated from him].
7. The slip on tape ("hacerse pasar por un agente", impersonating a narc) is filed as "una prueba de que yo tenía razón" [proof that I was right]. The watcher needs the watched to be guilty because the cameras must be justified. This is the sentence for Kalder's crisis: the report that condemns James is also the only thing that proves Kalder's work was worth the seats.
8. Hank: "Usted es Arctor." Fred: "¿Yo soy Bob Arctor?" and the line that closes it: "No encajaba con nada que hubiera hecho o pensado" [It didn't fit anything he had done or thought]. A page earlier, in the same chapter, the narration itself had slipped and labelled the speaker in the safe apartment "Arctor" when he says "Tengo dos hijos". Deliberate. Steal that: let the [K] narration once call Kalder by the wrong name.

**What it costs**, itemised, because Dick itemises it: the next pay packet; a misdemeanour charge for voluntary addiction (three thousand dollars and/or six months); every friend, because the agent assigned next will not know them: "Sin saberlo, he dicho adiós" [Without knowing it, I've said goodbye]; two daughters who may not exist ("Se supone que no tiene hijos"; "Quizá no los tenga"); and Hank's last instruction, "Llévese la pistola" [Take the gun], softened by "Opino que usted es muy buena persona" [I think you're a very good person]. The machine kills politely, and it thanks you.

### 2d. The scanner as instrument, and the man who reads the instrument

This is the richest vein for James, and it is not the hologram set-pieces; it is the plumbing around them.

- **The tap is a log, not a feed.** "Todas las cabinas del mundo estaban intervenidas" [Every phone booth in the world was tapped], but no one listens live; every two days an officer pulls a tape with the silences already stripped out, and his skill is spotting the one call that matters: "Era su habilidad. Para eso le pagaban" [That was his skill. That's what they paid him for]. And they do not voiceprint routine deals, on "consideraciones gubernamentales sobre economía" [government cost considerations]. The machine measures only what is worth measuring. That is the ownerless service: a counter, not a camera.
- **Static as data.** Arctor's car radio turns police ignition noise into "un torrente de información" [a torrent of information]: which force, how near, a one-minute tick for timing a raid; a DJ's dedication that is really a dispatch. "Y si alguien sospechaba algo, lo más probable es que pensara ser un loco" [And if anyone suspected, he'd most likely think himself crazy]. A man reading the instrument instead of the picture, and the gorilla mask built in. Movement II, the signal James infers the architecture from.
- **The reviewer's technique.** A colleague tells Fred to run the tape "Seis horas adelante, o incluso ocho, y luego grandes saltos hacia atrás" [six hours forward, even eight, then big jumps back] so he never sinks into the ambience; another says the mind filters like a sleeping mother who wakes only "al oír llorar a su bebé" [on hearing her baby cry]. Alerting, sampling, baseline: James's job in two sentences by two cops.
- **The bicycle.** Room 203 replays a living-room scene: a ten-speed with seven visible gears, and four stoned adults who cannot see that "No es una suma, sino una multiplicación" [It's not a sum, it's a multiplication]. The examiner's gloss: they counted parts and "percibían un todo homogéneo" [perceived a homogeneous whole], missing the relation. The brief's math scene (one in five hundred, the postman, someone at the funeral) is this: everyone counts, James multiplies.
- **Two gauges on one tank.** The diagnosis is given as telemetry: "Es como si su coche tuviera dos indicadores de combustible" [It's as if your car had two fuel gauges], both measuring the same tank, disagreeing; the driver only ever knew the tank through the gauge; "Información cruzada significa recepción nula" [Cross-talk means zero reception]. For James after the crossing, this is the honest description of two readings of one world, and Fred's rejoinder is James's: "Quizá mi visión sea la correcta" [Maybe my view is the correct one].
- **Instrument-first reasoning, then the thing the instrument can't explain.** When the sleeping girl's face slides from Connie to Donna on tape, Fred runs the technical hypotheses in order: a doctored tape, print-through, "diafonía" [cross-talk], a misfired transducer. He walks into the hologram to look. Then he rewinds and sees Arctor wake and see the same face. "Esto elimina la posibilidad de una «interferencia técnica»" [That rules out technical interference]. That is the shape of the crossing's receipt: exhaust the instrument, then accept what two independent readings agree on.
- **The false positive that teaches the method.** The warm cigarette butt in the ashtray: three men build, in a page, a complete model (the cops planted drugs, sell the house, phone the narcs ourselves), and then Donna walks out of a bedroom: "La colilla era de Donna" [The butt was Donna's]. Arctor, who knows the cameras were installed, followed them all the way down. A single datum is not proof; and the man who knows the system is not immune to the story. Movement I's dashboard anomaly can be staged exactly like this, with James as the one who says "based on what".
- **The question itself.** Alone in the house, acting for cameras he cannot see: "¿Qué ve en realidad una holocámara?... ¿Claridad u oscuridad?" [What does a scanner really see? Clarity or darkness?]; "Oscuridad dentro, oscuridad fuera" [Darkness inside, darkness outside]. The scanner sees what the watcher brings. James's version is technical first (a scanner sees what it is configured to see) and, after he crosses, the same sentence read the other way.

### 2e. The sabotage doctrine, and the hunted man's advantage

Driving home after the accelerator rod drops, Arctor lays out the method the brief gives the Keepers: damage that cannot be attributed. A bomb proves an enemy; "si ocurre un accidente, o una serie de accidentes" [if an accident happens, or a series], the victim "tiende a suponer que es una paranoica" [tends to assume he is paranoid], his friends agree, and the campaign takes longer, which is "la ventaja que tiene el perseguido" [the hunted man's advantage]: time to identify the hand. Then the chewing-gum in the photocopier, the mothball in the tank, the servant's dropped vase. Top up the canteen, in 1977. And it gives James his method's justification in the machine's own logic: a slow campaign leaves a pattern, and the pattern over time is the proof.

### 2f. The house and the office: the two rooms and what each is allowed to be

The book alternates a slow, funny, degraded house with a short, procedural office, and the ratio is the pace. House scenes run long and digressive: the silencer that amplifies, the dog-shit day, the hash-man who walks through customs saying "No, nada", the psychological pregnancy, the microfilm building three centimetres high, "¿Cuánto vale un Henway?" "Unas tres libras", with Fred, watching, answering a beat before Arctor does. Office scenes are one notch each: cameras ordered; cameras installed; first tape; first test; second test; blood; removal. The third room is the test lab, where the machine inspects its own handler for drift, which is a [K] chapter nobody has written yet: Kalder examined by the Review's clinicians, told his readings disagree.

The comedy carries the horror because the horror arrives inside the joke: Luckman choking on the kitchen floor while Barris, smiling, finishes winding string on a hash pipe, then performs panic for the phone, and when Luckman revives says he entered "un estado alfa" [an alpha state]. Or the breath-mint girl who corners Fred minutes after the lab tells him he is split, so the chapter ends on Lavoris versus Micrin. Or the title-cards: "Si hubiera sabido que era inofensivo, lo habría matado yo misma" [If I'd known it was harmless I'd have killed it myself], the straight woman and the dragonfly, which is also the Review's whole attitude to an awakening. The brief's rule for James (commit, get smacked, joke, retry) is already Dick's rhythm.

And the siege: "Todos estos tipos caminaban en un mismo tablero... a diferentes distancias de la meta" [They were all walking the same board, at different distances from the goal]. No one-day battle. The book proves a siege holds 88k words if every cycle house→office→house moves one notch and the notch is always a date, a name or an access.

### 2g. The ending, the cost, and the list

New-Path strips him of wallet, name and money: "Por la misma razón que no tienes cartera o apellido" [For the same reason you have no wallet or surname]. The Game, a circle of residents screaming at him, gets one answer out of him: "Soy un ojo" [I am an eye]. A retarded child named Thelma tells him to write his name on the wall so it won't wash off. An old woman juggling badly is called Donna; he knows she isn't; he feels better and does not know why. On the farm the director covers his eyes with a hand and says "Epistemología". Then the blue flowers between the corn rows, the drug grown in the open, "la flor del futuro. Pero no para ti" [the flower of the future. But not for you], and the flower in the shoe: "Un regalo para mis amigos" [A present for my friends].

Two things to take, one warning.

- **The watcher was the instrument, made literal.** Mike Westaway, the staff member who is also federal, says of Bruce: "No pueden interrogar a alguien, a lo que sea, que carece de mente" [They can't interrogate someone, something, with no mind], and, driving him to the farm, "los muertos son nuestras cámaras" [the dead are our cameras]. Donna's line is the reveal that hurts: "sacrificar algo o alguien, un ser viviente, sin que ese ser lo sepa" [to sacrifice something or someone, a living being, without its knowing]. And her clue in plain sight: "Un día le dije que no creyera en nada que yo dijera" [One day I told him not to believe anything I said]; he took it for a joke. That is the brief's indicator idea with the sign reversed: in Dick the emptied man is carried in by the good side; in ours the machine's count was a man. Either way the receipt is the same object: a flower he cannot read, kept by reflex, "Una reacción, no una acción" [A reaction, not an action].
- **The afterword works because it is placed after FIN, claims the cost and not the plot, and is a list.** "No hay ninguna moraleja en esta novela" [There is no moral in this novel]; "No soy un personaje de esta novela, soy la novela en sí" [I am not a character in this novel, I am the novel itself]; then fifteen first names with outcomes and "un largo etcétera". It does not break the book because nothing in it says the events happened; it says the price did. **Our rule stands: no note.** But the shape is already inside the brief: every [K-past] chapter "ends on a cost paid and a seat granted". That is Dick's list, one entry per chapter, dramatised instead of appended. The coda's dog burial is the dedication the book is not allowed to write.
- **The warning:** the New-Path chapters sag. Four scenes repeat the same beat (he is empty; someone is kind; the warmth leaves). Dick needed them for the author's note to land. We don't have a note, so we don't get the slack.

### 2h. Smaller usable pieces

- "Hay algo que no encaja, pensó" [Something doesn't fit, he thought]: the novel's Spanish title, verbatim, on the page where Fred fails to understand why the sheriff is watching Arctor. Worth knowing, not worth quoting in the book.
- The kitchen cabinet: Arctor bangs his head getting a popcorn popper out from under the sink and in one second of pain knows he hates his wife, daughters, lawn and heating system, and leaves. A single physical shock that clarifies a life: the model for the [J-past] chapter where James is first told to leave something and does.
- The insurance-investigator marriage: he wrote reports at night while his wife burned her hand, got a mote in her eye, dusted the typewriter. Sabotage doctrine at home. Ana is not this, by design; it is what Ana's tinfoil hat refuses to become.
- Barris's radio with four options (the authorities / a paramilitary / the syndicate / aliens) and Bob's radio that really does transmit: the fake and the real wiretap in the same car. Noise beside Thread.
- The cassette under the sofa with the door left unlocked and a note to Donna: the amateur surveillance that the professionals will find in ten minutes and reset. Frank's water-memory joke lives here.
- "Todos y cada uno de los yonkis son grabaciones" [Each and every junkie is a recording]: the Keepers' word for the Told.

## 3. The trap

- **The drug register.** "Muerte lenta", "ruedas", the hash pipe, the supercharge, the withdrawal on the hillside. Addiction is Dick's engine and it is not ours. Nothing James does should read as a hit, and the psychedelic window in Movement I must stay a window, not a habit. If the crossing ever smells of the hash pipe, it has become Lantano.
- **Paranoia as texture.** Dick confirms his own paranoia: the patrol cop is a narc, Donna is a narc, Mike is a narc, the rehab makes the drug. Everyone is in. That is precisely the "hundred-Keepers cabal" and the "everyone's an agent" feeling the brief refuses; our math says one in five hundred, and the loneliness comes from arithmetic, not from a reveal every forty pages. Take Barris's radio as Noise; do not take the ending's chorus of agents.
- **The 1970s.** Lions Club, John Birch, Leary's slogan, phone booths, eight-tracks, Konrad Lorenz on wolves, "educassettes", holograms as the future. Also the sexual politics: Barris on Donna, Connie bought for ten hits, the secretary's legs. None of it survives.
- **The block-quote science.** Dick drops whole paragraphs of Bogen, Wigan and Sperry into the test scene, and Goethe in German into the taxi. It is bold and it stops the book cold. Our reality rule allows real papers only in dialogue; the Weir explaining-mode is talk, not inserts. Entry #26 is fictional for exactly this reason.
- **The sting that explains too late.** Hank's "Debíamos tender una trampa a Jim Barris" [We had to set a trap for Jim Barris] and the McDonald's debrief are told, not shown; the reader never saw Donna handle him. If Kalder's reveal arrives as a two-hander explaining what the cameras were really for, it is this scene, and it is the book's weakest.

## 5. The source's own failures

- **The plot is a pretext and knows it.** A sheriff's department burns its own agent's brain to flush an informant it could have arrested for a bad cheque; Hank's explanation lands as an afterthought and the feds' New-Path operation is retrofitted in a McDonald's. The lesson is the Penultimate Truth lesson again: the intimate half is unforgettable, the machinery half is stapled on. Our Competence Ledger is the antidote; every move the machine makes against James must be cheaper than the alternative.
- **The paranoia is proven true**, which is the one thing a book about paranoia should not do. By the end the only people who are not agents are the ones with brain damage. It cheapens Arctor's earlier, better fear, the one where he cannot tell bad luck from a hand.
- **The women are instruments or furniture.** Donna is the exception and she gets her interiority only once the book needs her to be a cop. Ana's whole design in the brief (permission to not believe and stay) is the correction.
- **Second-half repetition.** Fred complains the tapes repeat; the reader agrees; then New-Path repeats the emptiness four times. Dick's repetition is thematically earned and still costs pages. The Revision Pass should treat any scene that reaches no new notch as a Napa field.
- **The inserts.** Sperry and Goethe interrupt rather than deepen, and in this translation the German sits untranslated in the taxi. Bravura, not craft.
- **The translation itself** drops the Pauline title (the one place it surfaces is "A través de un espejo en enigma"), moralises "straights" into "honrados", and chooses a slang set that dates it twice over. See §6.

**One line to carry forward:** the report room permits no feeling and the bill is paid on the street; the handler ratchets, in eight notches, from supplying his own mother's name to hearing "Usted es Arctor" and not recognising it; and the sentence for Kalder's crisis is already written: the slip he files as proof that he was right. The scanner sees what the watcher brings, so the handler who can report the crossing could only see it because he has it, and the count was a man carrying a flower in his shoe.
