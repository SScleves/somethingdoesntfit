# Comp — John le Carré, *Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy* (*El Topo*)
### Read 11 Oct 2026 — full read, Spain-Spanish translation, all three parts, Thursgood frame to the Immingham platform

> **Coverage, honestly.** The whole text, start to finish, in the one Spanish (Spain) edition on hand: ~139k words, 38 chapters in three parts. I did not check the English. The translation is serviceable but careless (names drift: "Priddo" for Prideaux, "Hayman" for Haydon in one place, "Hajek" misprinted twice; a few sentences are plainly mangled), so where I quote, I quote as printed, under fifteen words, with a gloss. Where the translation is the problem rather than le Carré, I say so. Nothing here is from memory of the film or of other le Carré.

> **Your brief:** investigation by files; two people in a room; the mole's psychology and the cost being a friend; the siege-then-one-morning pace; the compartmented institution; the ending; the trap; the plug-in by movement and track.

---

> **How to use this file:** inspiration for what is already Santi's tone and ideas, not a template and not a 1:1 copy. The book is in English; language and register notes have been removed. Where a scene is proposed, it is a proposal.

## 1. What it is

A retired intelligence officer, George Smiley, is pulled back by a civil servant (Lacon) to find a Soviet mole, "Gerald," at the top of the British service (the Circus). He cannot use the service's own tools, because the tools belong to the suspects. So he works from a hotel room under a false name, reading files a civil servant smuggles to him each evening and takes back each morning, and from a string of conversations with people the new regime has discarded. The mole is Bill Haydon: brilliant, charming, the man everyone in the book has modelled a life on, and the lover of Smiley's wife. The proof is a source called Merlin whose product ("Witchcraft") is too good, and a botched operation in Czechoslovakia (Testify) in which Jim Prideaux, Haydon's oldest friend, was shot in the back. Haydon is caught in a safe house, confesses in fragments, and is killed before he can be exchanged. Nobody says who did it. The book knows.

Its first sentence is a schoolboy watching a crippled man arrive in the rain. Its last is the same schoolboy deciding the revolver he saw was a dream.

## 2. The mechanisms worth stealing

### 2a. Reading as action: the Hotel Islay nights, and how le Carré makes a file move

The investigation proper is a man alone in a bad hotel room (the "Islay," under the name Barraclough) reading at night. Le Carré makes this the most tense stretch of the book, and the method is exact enough to copy.

**The files arrive on a shuttle.** Lacon drops the day's folders on his way to the train and collects them on his way to the office. Smiley can only read between dusk and breakfast, because by day the papers must be back on a Whitehall desk or their absence becomes a scandal. That constraint gives every night a clock, and the clock is institutional, not melodramatic.

**The room becomes the past.** The hotel room transposes, in his head, into Control's old office; the sounds through the wall are the sounds from the fifth floor. He reads "para comparar los recuerdos oficiales con los suyos" [to compare the official memories with his own]. That is the trick: the file is thin, the memory is thick, and the chapter alternates between them paragraph by paragraph. The document says what happened on paper; the memory supplies the dinner, the argument, the thing nobody wrote down. **Your style guide should work the same way: the hero reads a revision and remembers the conversation it was written to erase.**

**Le Carré never quotes a document whole.** He summarises, then lifts one phrase and lets Smiley react to it. Alleline writes that the reading room will be guarded by "mi conserje" and the narration notes only: "Smiley se fijó en el posesivo «mi»" [Smiley noticed the possessive "my"]. Four words of document, one sentence of reading. That ratio is the whole technique.

**A date becomes a clue three times, each differently:**

1. *The missing pages.* Guillam breaks into the duty officer's room to check the log for the night of Tarr's telegram from Hong Kong. The pages for the tenth to eleventh of April have been cut out with a razor; the porters' sheets are gone too; a note in Esterhase's hand, dated the previous Friday, says all enquiries to London Station. The Friday is the day Tarr landed. **Absence is the evidence, and the date on the cover-up is the confession.**
2. *The routing slip.* Control's mothers say he flew to Belfast. The travel allowances show no one flew to Belfast that month, but a first-class ticket to Vienna was authorised by "G. Smiley," who authorised nothing. The accounting line contradicts the story, and the forged signature is a man he trusts.
3. *The discrepancy a junior notices.* A new Whitehall reader, Ribble, writes politely that Witchcraft report 104 is dated the 21st of April, sourced to a General Markov, but on the 21st Markov was in Paris and Merlin was at a rocket site outside Leningrad, and there are four more like it. He is told to mind his own business. Smiley reads the rebuke, not the report. **This is your credible negative result with ridicule out of proportion to the claim.**

**The two-column sort.** The night after Jim's story, Smiley takes every Witchcraft report and splits them into two piles: things that were news the week they arrived, and things that could have been tinned months earlier and opened on a slow day. He lists only the first pile with dates, then lays those dates against Polyakov's recorded trips to Moscow and Esterhase's expense-claimed weekends abroad. Nine out of nine fresh reports coincide with Polyakov in London or Toby on a plane. He calls it, later, "meterlo todo en un tubo de ensayo, para ver si explotaba" [put it all in a test tube, to see if it exploded]. **That is James's competence precisely: not a hunch, a reconciliation of two time series.** Note also that the book refuses the eureka: "No, no fue una revelación explosiva" [no, it was not an explosive revelation]. It is a corroboration of what three people already suspected from three directions. Keep that modesty.

**The number that is wrong.** Buried in the Treasury correspondence: a London house, sixty thousand pounds, charged to an operation that is supposed to run abroad. Alleline refuses to give the address. "¿Qué conocimiento?" [What knowledge?], Smiley asks the page. The house is the mechanism. Everything else is message. Your ownerless service that sends traffic every night to an address that resolves nowhere is this house.

**The list with numbers.** The indoctrination list for Witchcraft is numbered by date of entry, "al igual que los miembros del Partido comunista" [like members of the Communist Party]. It was retyped after Control's death, and Smiley is no longer on it; the same four names head it. **That is your style guide's "Rev." reissue: a document whose edition history is the plot.** The fourth revision is the one where someone was dropped.

### 2b. Two people in a room: the interview catalogue

Le Carré's interviews obey a rule you have already written down: no memo read in silence can do what a person wanting something can. Each interviewee wants, and the want is the lever.

| Who / where | What Smiley gets | What they want | The handle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tarr**, at Lacon's house, all night | Irina's diary: the existence of Gerald, the name Polyakov, the fact that London stalled | To be believed; a flat in Scotland for a woman who is already dead | Guillam's contempt makes Tarr perform. Smiley interrupts only on dates: "Miércoles, día once" [Wednesday, the eleventh] |
| **Connie Sachs**, Oxford, sherry, the dog | Polyakov's medals; the Karla school; the three colonels; that she was fired for being right | To be told the old days were real. | **Esterhase** (first), Acton | A lie: no special work. The files later show eight trips charged to Witchcraft | Promotion; to be older than the young men above him | He gives Smiley perfume for Ann. The bribe is the tell |

| **Haydon** (pre-Witchcraft), Smiley's own house | Deliberate misreadings; "¿Quién le maneja?" answered about Percy, not Merlin | The top job, openly: "es tiempo que sea algo de una sola pieza" [time to be something of one piece] | He handles the pictures on Smiley's wall. "Si tú reservado, yo también reservado" | Smiley speaks plain English and lets Max choose not to speak German |
| **Jerry Westerby**, a curry house, "Jau" | The Czech conscript's story: the Russians were in the woods a day early | To be back in the tribe. A Christmas card instead of whisky is what he noticed | Smiley burned Jerry's letter and says so first. Absolution before the question |
| **Jim Prideaux**, a motel then a hill | Testify from inside; the colour-coded career sheet; the interrogator with two pencils; "¿Y qué dijo de mí?" | To obey the order to forget. To protect Bill | Smiley tells Jim the story and lets him correct it. Walking loosens him; sitting locks him |
| **Esterhase** (second), a safe house, Fawn in the hall | The whole mechanism, and the question that breaks it: "¿quién es el agente de Polyakov?" [who is Polyakov's agent?] | To be on the right side at the right time: "si está en lo cierto, tú también quieres estar en lo cierto" | Smiley narrates a thesis and asks only for corrections. Toby corrects |

Three things to lift from the table.

**The interviewer supplies the story.** With Jim and with Toby, Smiley does not ask; he tells, at length, what he thinks happened, and watches where the other man flinches. Toby's first words after the thesis are to deny being deaf, which is not a denial. It also obeys Steed-Asprey's maxim the book quotes: "probad las historias como quien se prueba un traje" [try stories on the way you try on a suit]. Facts first, story second.

**Every interview is paid for in a currency the interviewee names.** Connie wants a kiss and her boys back. Max wants nothing and gets a bus fare. Jerry wants whisky. Toby wants Lacon's name on something. Nobody is threatened. The only violence in any interview is Tarr strangling Smiley, and it comes when Smiley guesses right about Tarr's daughter.

**The one where Smiley is handled while he thinks he's handling.** Delhi, in memory, told to Guillam over bad coq au vin. Smiley goes to a prison cell to turn a Russian radio man called Gerstmann, who is Karla. Karla says nothing for two days. Smiley, feverish and wrecked by his wife, starts talking about the prisoner's wife, then about Siberia, and gives him Camels and the lighter Ann engraved. "Comencé un interrogatorio en el que yo era el interrogado" [I began an interrogation in which I was the one interrogated]. Karla leaves with the lighter and shows it to Jim twenty years later. **This is your "his own words turn up in their documents" beat, done as a conversation: the hero's confession is harvested in a room where he thinks he is the one asking.** The companion beat is Haydon in Smiley's house, handling the Callot and the Mieris and asking what Ann gave in reparation. The handled man is told the price of his own belongings.

### 2c. Reporting upward, and what the mole is

**Reporting upward is a telegram that stalls.** Tarr's cable from Hong Kong is marked top urgency; London's reply is marked merely immediate and unsigned ("Ahora ya no se firman"), and it asks for more detail. Irina is on a stretcher before the detail arrives. Later Alleline's reply to Tarr's ransom note is the same gesture in the open: "exijo aclaraciones y muestras de la mercancía" [I demand clarification and samples of the goods]. Delay is the machine's only verb. Kalder's chapters should end on a reply that asks a question back.

**The mole talks, and the talking is the horror.** Haydon is "the one who talks." He confesses over two sessions, writes a political preamble Smiley remembers only in fragments, asks for his shirts to be sent on and a girl paid off with a four-figure cheque, and wants to be remembered as the silent man in a Ionesco play. He explains himself as "una conclusión primordialmente estética" [a primarily aesthetic conclusion], and the narration will not accept it: Smiley decides "la traición es, en gran parte, cuestión de costumbre" [treachery is, for the most part, a matter of habit], and then rejects that too, and settles on the Russian dolls: only Karla ever saw the last one. **Le Carré refuses to let the mole's reason be good.** That is the discipline to copy. Bram's cover story should be beautiful; Kalder's should not be.

**Mapping Haydon onto Kalder.** Your brief: very close to the machinery; very capable; knows more than most and never all; loyal possibly only because it suits him; becomes close to James; a reveal that hurts.

*What transfers:*
- Close to the machinery: Haydon runs London Station; the mole is not outside the house, he is the house. "Poder ser uno más entre una multitud" [to be one more among a crowd] is how the narration describes the art of being Gerald. The bigger the committee, the safer he is. Your Handlers hide in your Housed.
- Knows more than most, never all: Haydon does not know when he was recruited, or he won't say; he does not know what his two Soviet medals are for. Karla keeps him on the road. "Bill había emprendido el camino, y Karla tuvo buen cuidado de mantenerle en él" [Bill had taken the road, and Karla took care to keep him on it]. That is loyalty that suits, maintained from above.
- Becomes close to James: Haydon is close to everyone. He is Guillam's model, Jim's other half (the 1937 letter: "Es mi otra mitad"), Ann's lover, Connie's wonder boy. Guillam's reaction when the door opens is not rage but "huérfano." The reveal hurts because the friendship was real on the victim's side.
- The one act of care that is also the crime: "¡Rescaté a Jim! ¡Lo rescaté! ¿No?" [I got Jim out! I got him out! Didn't I?]. He did. He also needed Jim back so Jim could be told to shut up. Kalder's favour to James should be exactly this shape: a real kindness with a use.

*What does not transfer, and why it matters:*
- Haydon was not sent. The friendship predates the treachery by thirty years, and the hurt is retrospective. Kalder arrives because James was noticed; the friendship is assigned. Le Carré gives you the mechanism for that too, but it's not Haydon's motive, it's his instruction: Karla told him to sleep with Ann "si se me presentaba la oportunidad" [if the chance presented itself], because Ann was "la última ilusión de un hombre sin ilusiones" [the last illusion of a man without illusions]. **That is the reveal that hurts for your book: not "my friend was a Keeper," but "the thing my friend loved in me was in his brief."** Kalder was told near enough to know what James loves, and the reader has watched him learn it.
- Le Carré never gives Haydon a point of view. Not one paragraph. The reader infers him from what others say and from one letter written in 1937. Your [K] track gives Kalder his own chapters under dramatic irony. Different bargain: le Carré buys shock, you buy dread. Do not try to have both; the book shows you can't.
- Haydon's politics are a wardrobe. Hawk one year, pacifist the next; Control says he sat on the fence so long "debe tener el trasero como un colador" [must have a backside like a colander]. Kalder has no politics; he has standing. Keep that cleaner than Haydon.

### 2d. The siege, the one morning, and the late telling

**Weeks of attrition.** The present action is roughly a fortnight: Ascot, Oxford, Islay nights, Guillam's two break-ins, nine interviews, one trap. Nothing happens fast. Time passes as sausages and tomato, a gas meter that eats shillings, a Welsh drunk on the stairs. The reader is made to feel the hotel.

**A single night of violence, told last.** Testify is the book's one morning of blood, and it is withheld until three-quarters through, then given three times before it is given whole: Sam's version (the switchboard), Max's version (the driver's seat), Jerry's conscript (the Russians in the woods a day early). Only then Jim, on the hill, walking. By the time the reader gets the floodlights and the two bullets, they already know it was staged, and the dread is in watching Jim walk into a trap the reader has already measured. **Tell the crossing the way Testify is told: partial, from the outside, from three people who each saw one wall of it, before the man who was inside it speaks.**

**Prideaux as present/past engine.** Jim is in the present (the school, the boy, the revolver under the pew) and in the past (the Czech mission) and the two tracks converge in a single chapter when Smiley gets out of the blue Ford outside the chapel. That is your Convergence from the Wool notes: two tracks heading for one room from opposite directions, and the man in the trailer has been expecting the man in the coat for a week because he ran a test with two envelopes.

**The Circus as compartmented institution; need-to-know as a weapon.** Lacon's statement of the problem is the cleanest line in the book: "¿quién puede espiar a los espías?" [who can spy on the spies?]. Every instrument of investigation belongs to the suspects. From there the book builds compartmentation as the mole's shield, layer by layer:
- Alleline's "lateralism": one London Station, no regional baronies, "la mano derecha ignore lo que hace la izquierda" ends, which means one man sees everything.
- The reading room with a porter and a photo list; no passes, because passes can be lent.
- The file whose cover orders the finder to return it "SIN LEERLO" [WITHOUT READING IT].
- The Testify file whose first pages list what has been removed to London Station.
- Guillam made to sign that he will report anything he learns to "nosotros, los mayores" and to no one else: "hay muchas más ruedecitas, dentro de otras ruedas" [many more little wheels inside other wheels].
- Toby in Sarratt with a thousand pounds and the message that the matter is being handled "en las más altas esferas."

Your tiers, mapped: the **Review** is Karla, never seen in the present, an instruction that arrives by lighter and Camel; the **Stewards** are Alleline and Haydon, one the face and one the hand; the **Handlers** are Esterhase, who carries film, pays off Max with a hundred pounds, silences Jerry with a Christmas card where whisky used to be, and tells Jim to forget; the **Housed** are the sixty-eight on the numbered list, who signed; the **Told** are Millie McCraig with her milk bottles, the porters who stamp the time in pink ink, Norman the boots. Each tier is told a different story about what Witchcraft is. Nobody is told what it is for. Toby's defence, word for word, is your Housed man's: "He de ganarme el pan de todos los días, George" [I have to earn my daily bread, George].

### 2e. The instrument: turning the machine's own protocol into the trap

The capture at Lock Gardens is engineered from inside the mole's own procedures, which is the move your Movement IV needs. Smiley learns the house, the milk-bottle signal, the light switch that starts the tape, the three-note bell that means one of ours. Guillam reverses one switch so the recorders run when the mole thinks they are off. Smiley drafts, for Tarr to send, the telegram that will force Gerald to run to Polyakov; then stands in the dark in his socks and hears Haydon read aloud the words Smiley wrote forty-eight hours earlier. **The count is the trap.** The machine's own measuring device records the man who built it. That is what "the watcher was the instrument" should feel like in your book: not a gadget, a switch turned upside down behind a plastic plate.

Note how little force there is. Lacon: "No quiero sangre" [I don't want blood]. Guillam shakes Haydon by the collar and lets go because "sólo se trataba de Bill." Then they wait for Percy, and the scene turns into a committee negotiating what the mole is worth in Czech agents. The book's harshest line about institutions is that one.

## 3. The trap: what not to take

- **The Cold War furniture.** Soviet trade delegations, Czech generals, Karla's school outside Moscow, networks called Aggravate and Plato. All of it is period, and all of it would date your book in a page. The only thing from that furniture worth keeping is the shape of Merlin: a source whose product is good *because* the enemy wrote it, and whose price (fifty thousand Swiss francs a month) is what persuades the buyers it is real. "Cuanto más pagas por una falsificación" [the more you pay for a forgery] is a line for Frank.
- **The clubland register.** Roddy Martindale at dinner, Oxford quads, the Travellers', "los Óptimos," a schoolmaster's cricket jersey. The book runs on a caste its narrator half-loves and the translation makes it sound like a costume drama. James's world is standups, dashboards and a golf course; the moment anyone in your book sounds like Martindale, cut.
- **The cast.** Around forty named people, four of them with the same social voice (Lacon, Alleline, the minister, Martindale), a Guillam subplot about a flautist girlfriend that goes nowhere and is meant to. You have a rule about names that sound alike; le Carré breaks it (Bland/Bryant/Brixton, Alleline/Allitson/Alwyn). The book survives because Smiley carries every scene. Your James must carry his the same way, which means fewer rooms, not more.
- **The passive detective.** Smiley never once initiates physical action; his biggest risk is a walk in Kensington with a shadow behind him. That works because the Circus is a world of paper. James watches systems, but your book has a crossing in it and a kitchen with twenty objects on the table. Don't borrow Smiley's immobility; borrow his patience.
- **The mole's non-motive.** "Conclusión estética" is the right answer for Haydon and would be the wrong answer for anyone in your machine. Keepers never argue with the soul claim; they make it funny. Haydon is never funny about it. He weeps from the nose.
- **The melancholy as default register.** Every chapter is autumn, rain, a widower's coat. Your voice rule is commit, get smacked, joke, retry. Le Carré's rhythm is commit, brood, read, brood. Take the reading and leave the brooding.

## 5. The ending and its cost

Nobody wins. Haydon is to be exchanged for whatever is left of the networks; before the exchange someone breaks his neck in the dark at Sarratt, where Smiley had asked three times for the perimeter to be guarded and was told he exaggerated. The minister blames the Russians. Smiley says the Russians take pride in getting their men home, and then says nothing, and the reader assembles the rest from an owl with a broken neck in the first chapter and a revolver taped under a pew in a Somerset church. Jim did it. The book keeps Jim's hands clean on the page so that the reader's are not.

The costs are itemised without a list: Irina, Ivlov and Boris hanged in the Lubyanka in July; the Aggravate and Plato networks shot; Connie's job; Jerry's whisky; Max's five fingers and three; Guillam's "huérfano"; Ann. Smiley gets the Circus back as night watchman and feels "No tenía la menor sensación de conquista" [not the slightest sense of conquest]. The last image of the main action is him on a station platform where the train no longer stops, watching his wife walk toward the ticket office without seeing him.

The transferable thing is not the sadness. It is the refusal: the book will not let the institution be worth the loyalty it extracts ("¿A santo de qué tenía uno que ser leal a semejantes hombres?") and will not let the mole be right either. Both refusals at once. Your ending has a dog and a container for grief; it can afford to be kinder than this. But the cost should be a friend, and the friend's death or departure should be done by someone the reader likes, off the page, and never confirmed.

## 6. The source's own failures

- **The frame is too cute.** Bill Roach, the fat lonely observer, is a sentimental mirror for Smiley ("la versión definitiva de aquel prototipo del que Bill Roach era sólo el proyecto"). The school chapters are charming and they stall the book twice. Your cold opens should be images, not a child who sees too much.
- **Guillam's private life is filler.** Camilla, the flute, the music teacher who is her husband: pages of a man's paranoia dressed as subplot. Le Carré wants a second nervous system for the reader; he gets a second chapter about doubt. Ana does that work in your book by being funny and leaving.
- **The mole's reveal is not a reveal.** By the Islay nights the reader knows it is Haydon, and the book half-admits it: "Lo sabía, desde luego. Siempre había sabido que era Bill." The pleasure is in how, not who. If your Kalder is known to the reader from his first chapter, you are in the same position, and you must make the how carry everything.
- **The reading-room exposition is lectures.** Smiley's thesis to Toby runs for pages with Toby saying "Sí, sí, claro." It works because the thesis is a trap, but it is still a man explaining. Your rule, two people talking, is the right rule; make sure the second person wants something enough to interrupt.
- **The translation is working against it.** See below.

## 7. One craft note

Where the draft has a conversation, keep it in direct speech, formatted as dialogue, even when the content is a file. Reported speech flattens interviews.

**One line to carry forward:** the mole is caught by a man in his socks who reversed one light switch and then listened to the enemy read aloud, in a borrowed house, a telegram he had written himself two days earlier; the proof was never a document, it was two columns of dates that matched a diplomat's travel, and the price of the proof was the one friend who had gone to warn the traitor before the trap was sprung. **Give James the two columns, give Kalder the brief that told him what to love, and let the person who pays be someone the reader would rather not have watched.**
