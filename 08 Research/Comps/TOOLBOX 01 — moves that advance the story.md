# TOOLBOX 01 — moves that advance the story
### 16 Nov 2026. Sixteen moves, each written as something you can do to a chapter. Fourteen come from the thirteen novels in `08 Research/Engines/`. The last two come from your own earlier conversation about the villain and the stakes, and are not in the plan yet. The longer argument is in `STRUCTURE 01 — the mechanism and the chain.md`; the evidence is in each book's map.

Use it this way. When a chapter feels like James is only being handed things, pick the move that fits, ask its test question of the chapter, and write the answer into the chapter. Every move here obeys your rules: nobody is hacked, pressured or broken into, and a door opens only when a person chooses.

## 1. Two copies

**The move.** Give James two versions of one thing that should match and don't, and let him put them side by side.

**Where it comes from.** *The Club Dumas*: the whole plot is a man comparing copies of one book, and a second copy opens doors that money does not. Borges's "Tlön" gets dread from one copy that has extra pages.

**How to use it.** Name the two copies before you write the chapter. One is in his hands already. The other has to be fetched from a person, who decides whether to show it. The difference between them is small and dull, and he finds it himself.

**In this book.** His copy against the first impression. The job's name against his screenshot. The neighbor's kind account against Els's folder. The annex against the HR list. Henk's words against what he felt at the funeral.

**Test question.** What are the two copies in this chapter, and who is holding the second one?

Chapters: The sentence; The control case; The payload; The math; The money; The drill; The other ten; The reading room, continued; What was on the phone

## 2. Their answer is the next clue

**The move.** The organization never blocks him. It agrees with him and reissues the thing in a duller version, and the reissue is a new second copy.

**Where it comes from.** Orwell's *1984* (Part One, chapter 4): every edit is filed as a correction. *The Name of the Rose* (Second Day, Terce): the catalog lies about a book's status, never about its existence.

**How to use it.** When James is stuck, don't send him a new stranger with a document. Ask what they reissued after his last step: a renamed job, a kind account of a death, a form that explains him, a post restored with nonsense added. Then ask where the old version still sits.

**In this book.** The job gets an owner and a ticket. The forum query gets the polite correction. He gets a wellbeing referral. His deleted post comes back with bloodlines. His slip is marked "in conservation".

**Test question.** What did they reissue after the last thing he did, and where is the old version?

Chapters: The week it vanished; The corporate day; The gorilla mask; Leiden; The reading room, continued; The night before

## 3. One verb, one person, one object

**The move.** A step is James doing one thing to a person or a system and leaving with an object he did not have.

**Where it comes from.** Gibson's *Pattern Recognition*: every clue is an object that a person hands over at a personal price, and the heroine follows her nose, not a method.

**How to use it.** Pick the verb: compare, capture, build, ask, test, sign, wait, tell, refuse. Pick who it is done to. Pick what he carries out of the room. If he only reads paper someone handed him, the chapter is a delivery, and the fix is that he makes the comparison himself or asks for the thing by name.

**In this book.** He captures the payload. He signs the referral. He initials the annex. He builds the watcher. He writes down Henk's words. He puts a request slip on the table.

**Test question.** What is the verb, who is it done to, and what is in his hands when he leaves?

Chapters: The payload; The corporate day; The money; The gorilla mask; The other ten; The reading room, continued; The sheets

## 4. Someone else pays

**The move.** Every check he makes is counted, and the bill lands on whoever opened the door for him.

**Where it comes from.** King's *11/22/63*: the past pushes back, and it pushes on the people near the hero, through things he set going himself.

**How to use it.** After each step, name who helped and what it cost them, and have James say it in one sentence. If he doesn't say it, the reader thinks he hasn't noticed and stops liking him.

**In this book.** Noor pays for his visit with an afternoon and a clipboard. Buddy pays for the barbecue. The dealer is seen not telephoning. The eighth address pays for his rule. Everyone who gets a sheet is counted.

**Test question.** Who opened the door in this chapter, and what does it cost them by the end of the next one?

Chapters: Groningen; Leiden; Leiden, Friday; The gorilla mask; The vet; Frank, loose; The sheets

## 5. The gate

**The move.** After a failure, the hero sets himself a rule about when he may act, and the rule costs him.

**Where it comes from.** King's *11/22/63*: the hero may not act until he has ruled out the bigger explanation, and the waiting is where the story happens.

**How to use it.** Let James try the winning move too early and fail in front of a person. He then writes the rule in his own words. While he keeps it, something goes wrong that breaking it might have prevented. It opens only when a person gives him what the rule asks for.

**In this book.** He tells Saskia too soon and she files him as a tired man. His rule: tell nobody until he knows what they were told and can put their own paper in front of them. An address is emptied while he keeps it. Kalder's list opens it.

**Test question.** What is he not allowed to do yet, who made that rule, and what is it costing?

Chapters: Enjoy it while it lasts; Twenty minutes; The other ten; Frank, loose; What was on the phone

## 6. The invented item

**The move.** He makes something up, puts it where one person can pick it up, and watches who knows it.

**Where it comes from.** Eco's *Foucault's Pendulum* (chapters 75 and 76), turned around: something invented is believed, and the believers show themselves.

**How to use it.** One item, one hook, a readout through another person, and a price he cannot take back. He never invents an edit on a real book. The readout must happen in front of a witness or it proves nothing.

**In this book.** The book that does not exist, named in the pub, which Julian knows. The same title dropped on Frank at the barbecue as the control. The kennel booked for the printed date. The half sentence posted to strangers.

**Test question.** What did he invent, who alone could react to it, and who else saw the reaction?

Chapters: On land; The gorilla mask; The schedule; The sheets

## 7. Each layer is a true small conspiracy

**The move.** Each thing he uncovers is real, is what one tier of people was told, and covers the next thing.

**Where it comes from.** Shea and Wilson's *Illuminatus!*: every solved layer is the cover story for the next, and a layer only drives the plot when someone acts on it as if it were the bottom, and pays.

**How to use it.** Write down what each tier was told, in their word for it. Make James act on one layer as if it were the last. Break that layer on a document, not on a speech.

**In this book.** A publisher's fraud. An employer watching staff, told as "honeypot". Executives buying a lifeboat, told as "disaster recovery". Records and people moved, told as "conservation" and "heritage". Under all of it, recall. He signs the seat annex believing the seats are the point.

**Test question.** Which layer does he think is the bottom here, and what does he do because he believes it?

Chapters: Frank's kitchen; The money; The drill; The schedule; What was on the phone

## 8. One helper, one truth

**The move.** Every helper was told one small reasonable thing, and each can be reached only with their own paper.

**Where it comes from.** DeLillo's *Libra*: a plot run in compartments, smaller than it looks from outside. Its limit matters too: the right key gets a helper talking, it does not decide for them.

**How to use it.** For each helper write what they want, what they were told, and the one page of their own that contradicts it. James brings that page and nothing else. Some helpers read it and still refuse, and that has to be allowed.

**In this book.** Saskia and the one line in her log she did not write. Pieter and his children "per allocation" beside a desk sold four times. Anneke and her own thirty signatures. The brewery man, who reads his sheet and says he is in.

**Test question.** What was this person told, and which page of their own does James put in front of them?

Chapters: Groningen; The money; The sheets; The leak; The night before

## 9. Quiet is counted

**The move.** Stopping does not get him out, because going quiet is itself recorded and read as worse.

**Where it comes from.** Pynchon's *The Crying of Lot 49* (chapter 5): the heroine tries to quit and quitting produces more evidence. She can never tell whether she is being played.

**How to use it.** Give James a chapter where he does the sensible thing and stops asking. Then show the reader, from the other side, how that was written down. This is what answers "why doesn't he just leave it".

**In this book.** He signs the referral and stops raising things at work. In "A car park" the handler reports that he has stopped asking, and the voice says: stopped out loud, which is worse. The review date moves.

**Test question.** If he did nothing after this chapter, what would they write down about that?

Chapters: The corporate day; A car park; Enjoy it while it lasts

## 10. The leak, kept narrow

**The move.** A fragment of the life before reaches a person, and it only moves the plot when it has a tell, a second witness and one detail that can be checked.

**Where it comes from.** Dick's *The Man in the High Castle*: another reality arrives as objects in people's hands. Crouch's *Recursion*: memories of a life that did not happen, which a character must act on.

**How to use it.** One kind of leak only. Here it is the last minutes of the previous life: a place, a hand, sometimes words. James gets the hand and no words, twice. Other people give him the words, each before he tells them his. No leaked objects, no vision on demand.

**In this book.** The flash at the funeral. Noor's hand under her arm. Henk's words, from Marijke. Els's father's saying. Strangers who finish the half sentence. Anneke at her door. Ana, half asleep, in the Epilogue.

**Why it belongs to this book.** Your MindBook convergence work found that forgetting is the idea the old traditions share most (Plato's river, the Gnostic cup, the Sufi remembrance), and that the pass at the gate is always knowing who you are. Recall is the forgetting failing for a minute. The sentence is the pass.

**Where it solves a problem.** Use it where a chapter needs a person to open and no document can do it. Els opens because James repeats a line that turns out to be her father's. The floor night gets a cause, because Henk's words have the same shape as what James felt at the funeral. The sheets get a proof that did not come from the organization's own paper, because strangers finish the half sentence. Anneke opens her door before he knocks, because one row of the count was about her. If you want it wider, the next step is a person who knows a room they have never been in. That would solve a locked-door problem once, and it would cost you the rule that James is not a seer, so it should belong to someone else, such as Noor or Aylin, and be used a single time.

**Test question.** Who else has this, how can James tell, and what can he check it against?

Chapters: The funeral; The math; Leiden; The other ten; The night he stops being afraid; Twenty minutes; The sheets; The night before; Epilogue

## 11. Two clocks

**The move.** He has a date and they have a date, and theirs moves only when he does something.

**Where it comes from.** King's *11/22/63* runs on a fixed date the hero is walking toward. *Libra* runs a second line toward the same day from the other side.

**How to use it.** Write one calendar and keep it in front of you. His clock is fixed and inherited. Theirs moves rarely, each time because of a specific act of his, and the reader sees it move in a chapter he is not in.

**In this book.** His: the second of December, then the Monday. Theirs: the review, which moves in "A car park" because he signed and went quiet, and in "The leak" because of the brewery man's report.

**Test question.** What day is it, how many days are left on each clock, and did anything he did here move theirs?

Chapters: The control case; A car park; The schedule; The leak; The night before

## 12. The reader one step ahead

**The move.** Show the reader what the organization is before James meets it, so that every friendly scene is read twice.

**Where it comes from.** DeLillo's *Libra* alternates the hero's chapters with the plotters' chapters from the start. The dread comes from knowing who the pleasant man is while the hero is still pouring him a drink.

**How to use it.** A short chapter without James, placed just before the scene it poisons. It gives one word or one face and no explanation. Never use it to explain the plan.

**In this book.** "Utrecht, 1993" gives the word "recall" thirty years early. "A car park" and "Leiden, nine years ago" tell the reader who Julian is before the pub. "Leiden, 1931" sits right before the ledger is read.

**Test question.** What does the reader know going into this scene that James doesn't, and which line in the scene hurts because of it?

Chapters: Utrecht, 1993; A car park; Leiden, nine years ago; On land; Leiden, 1931

## 13. The clown who is right once

**The move.** Keep a ridiculous person near the question, and let one of his ridiculous ideas turn out to be the true one, secondhand.

**Where it comes from.** *Foucault's Pendulum* keeps clowns beside the question. *Illuminatus!* keeps the comedy going under the escalation, and that is why its escalation can be borne.

**How to use it.** The clown wants something small and human. He holds one true thing without knowing it. James dismisses it, and a serious person later confirms it. The joke and the clue are the same line.

**In this book.** Beard Man wants to be listened to and to do as little as possible. His bucket theory is the professor's, improved. He sells whole lots because that way he never has to telephone anyone, which is the organization's one mistake.

**Test question.** Who is funny in this chapter, and what true thing are they carrying without knowing?

Chapters: Reading with a pen; The week it vanished; The math; The sheets; A market, Sunday

## 14. Never contradict the record

**The move.** The invented history fits inside the gaps of the real one and never changes a documented fact.

**Where it comes from.** Powers's *Declare*: a secret history that never contradicts a known date, place or document.

**How to use it.** In a real-history chapter, state what is on the record plainly, with the date. Put the invented part only where the record is silent. Let the narrator decline, once, to connect a real person to the plot.

**In this book.** Darwin changed his own book between printings. The 1953 panel met for four days. A real man disappeared in Mexico City and the narrator says he will not connect it. The edits are always on invented books.

**Test question.** Which sentence here is on the record, which is invented, and could a reader with a library tell them apart?

Chapters: Down House, 1860; Croton, 530 BC; Mexico City, 1994; Washington, 1953; Urfa, 1994

## 15. The counter-investigation

**The move.** Every thing he checks is answered by one small wrong thing at home, so the search has a second search inside it.

**Where it comes from.** Your own earlier conversation about the villain: weird things start when the crates come home, and a man who notices for a living files each one under nothing until the filing fails. *Pattern Recognition* does the same with a flat that has been entered and a browser history that is wrong.

**How to use it.** One wrongness per chapter, small enough to explain away: a meter inspection nobody booked, a dealer asking who bought the lot, a post of his that is alive again. He notices it, names the dull explanation, and moves on. Three chapters later the reader can add them up before he does. Each one has to be something a person did, so that it can later have a face.

**In this book.** The deleted post that comes back with bloodlines. The man on the tee who asks about the dog first. The vet who already had Buddy booked for Thursday. Part One has the fewest of these and could carry two more.

**Test question.** What small wrong thing happens near him in this chapter, and what dull explanation does he give it?

Chapters: A round with a stranger; Leave it; The control case; The gorilla mask; The schedule; The vet

## 16. The switch on paper

**The move.** Before he goes further he arranges for what he knows to be sent out if he stops checking in, and he does it on paper, through someone dull and legal.

**Where it comes from.** Your own earlier conversation: sealed envelopes held by a notary, mailed to a hundred addresses if he does not call by a date, with the forum as the noisy copy nobody reads. It answers the question every reader of a conspiracy asks, which is why they don't simply remove him.

**How to use it.** He sets it early, because of the small wrong things, and tells one person it exists. The organization learns of it and that changes what they can do to him, so they buy and discredit. In the last part the switch must cost him something, or it is only insurance.

**In this book.** It is not in the plan yet. The nearest things are his promise with Ana, the sheets and the twelve envelopes. If you want it, it goes in around "The promise", and the twelve envelopes become the switch firing by his own hand.

**Test question.** If he vanished tonight, what would go out, to whom, and who knows that?

Chapters: The promise; The sheets; The leak; The night before
