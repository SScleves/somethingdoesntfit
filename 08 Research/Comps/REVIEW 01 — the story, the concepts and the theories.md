# Review 01 — the story, the concepts and the theories
### 15 Nov 2026. What was read: THE BOOK v8.2 (every line), the v8 brief, The List, the MindBook brief, and drafts v8/51–56, v7/45, v7/49, v8/52–53 to check claims.

The story makes sense as a chain of events. A man finds an edited sentence, finds a job that counts people who ask, is walked into the machine's own paperwork, loses his fear on a kitchen floor, and wins by handing ordinary people their own documents. That spine holds and nobody else has it. What does not yet hold is the thing the spine is about. The plan gives three different answers to "what did the edits take out," never says what the organisation gains by hiding it, and leaves the one deleted sentence as a placeholder, so the ending currently pays off a feeling and not a fact.

The three things that most need fixing. First, the central truth and the motive for hiding it (1.1, 1.2, 1.3). Second, Anneke: she sits in the opening hearing everything, then is turned weeks later by a sheet that tells her less than she heard (2.1). Third, the two biggest reveals of Part Three break your own rules: the phone is opened by drugging a man, and the truth arrives as a nine-item speech with nothing on the table (5.1, 5.2).

The three strongest. The win itself: compartments attacked with each helper's own paper, and "a body would confirm every envelope" (1.10). Ana ending the game herself with the pills after the promise to run. And the flask, the bag by the door and the invented book: small objects planted early that pay off cleanly (3.6).

## 1. The concepts

**1.1 The forty-one edits mean three different things.** In "The sentence" the edits turn *given* into *arose*: a claim about history, that someone handed knowledge to people. In Part 0 the deleted sentences "aren't history. They're instructions" for dying without fear. In "The night he stops being afraid" all forty-one "take out the thing that passes between people." The draft of "The final conversation" tries to join them ("We took the passing"), but Tresidder's page 118 is still a sentence about early peoples, not an instruction. Pick one meaning and make the other two follow from it. The cheapest join: the people who *gave* the knowledge were the ones who remember across lives, and what they gave was the method. Then history, instruction and "passing" are one thing, and it must be said once, plainly, before Part Three. *Severity: breaks the story.*

**1.2 The hidden thing is on sale in every bookshop.** The plan's own research quotes the Tibetan and Egyptian books of the dead: published instructions for dying without fear and keeping yourself across the gap. So the organisation has not hidden what happens when we die. The plan has the answer but uses it only once, for the Dalai Lama search: "the ceremony that makes the real thing unthinkable." That is the real concept, and it is better than deletion: they do not remove the instruction, they make it exotic, ceremonial or funny. Say so early, through Noor, or the reader who owns a paperback Thödol stops believing on the spot. It also changes what the forty-one edits are for: they remove the *ordinary* versions, the ones in dry books a sensible person would trust. *Severity: breaks the story.*

**1.3 The motive for hiding it is not there.** Part 0 says the organisation keeps people afraid "so that nobody persists." It never says what the organisation gains when nobody persists. The old man remembers across lives; other people scattering costs him nothing and gives him nothing in the plan. Anneke's reason (collapse, "killers and savages") is a reason for *her* to keep the secret after the vote, not a reason anyone started. "Every cycle costs them more" is the nearest thing to a motive and it is unexplained: costs what? You need one sentence of self-interest, for example: they can only come back into a world that still has their building, their names and their chair, so the world must not change faster than they can return to it. Kalder also needs a line here: he crossed, has no fear and no love, so what happens to him? *Severity: breaks the story.*

**1.4 Nobody says what the review does or what the time is bought from.** Part 0 promises James "buys the world time" and that the organisation is "preparing a small number of people to survive whatever comes next." But the review in v8.2 is records moved and eleven addresses visited. Then the bunkers turn out to be "fuel" and "the date passes and nothing loud happens." So there is no event to survive and nothing the world was about to lose. This reads like a reset that was cut from an earlier version while its consequences were left in. Either put a consequence back (what happens to a population when a review completes) or cut "buys the world time" and "whatever comes next" from the idea paragraph and let the stake be eleven addresses, which is honest and enough. *Severity: weakens it.*

**1.5 Killing is not a rare failure in this plan.** The List's rule is "correct → ridicule → buy → vanish. Killing is the failure mode," and the Deventer visitor says "a body's a fingerprint." Yet the plan shows a canal road in 1995, a canal in Zwolle, the professor in the blue chair, and "three were resolved this quarter" out of eleven addresses in one region. Three deaths a quarter per region is a routine. "Monday" even says they killed before "and it was cheap," which contradicts the fingerprint line. Decide which organisation this is. If killing is rare, make the three "resolved" addresses a care home, a bankruptcy and one death, and let the Zwolle canal be the exception people inside are uneasy about. That also makes Kalder the anomaly, which he should be. *Severity: weakens it.*

**1.6 The numbers do not add up, top or bottom.** "A few hundred professionals" must fill the right column, which only a handler who has crossed and stands close can judge. The payload shows 112 flagged people at one bank, and Kalder spends eight months of golf on one of them. A few hundred people cannot do that for the world. At the other end, "millions of helpers" are defeated by about two hundred sheets and one bank's board vote, and the old man retires. Is he the head of everything or of one Dutch foundation? The draft's "a generation, perhaps, in this part of the world" is the honest scale. Say in Part 0 that the book shows one regional node, and let "The math" do the handler arithmetic as well as the helper arithmetic. *Severity: weakens it.*

**1.7 Erasing the job is the opposite of the method.** "Leiden, 1931" states the method: leave the misprints, "a clean plate is a confession." In "The week it vanished" the organisation wipes a job from every backup going back years, "which should be impossible," and closes a ticket against one that never existed. That is the cleanest plate in the book and it tells James he is right. The same people leave the seat list findable. Also, the job is erased in Part One but "the job is gone in a month" at the end, and page 212 exists to hide a job that still runs. Make the Part One move a dirty one: the job is renamed, given an owner and a boring ticket, and only James's screenshot shows the difference. *Severity: weakens it.*

**1.8 The machine hands James everything he beats it with, and never says why.** Pieter puts him on the continuity programme ("they asked for the person who reads things"). From that role he reads the invoice, the seat annex and the field schedule with its Visit column. Bram even says "The wife will find the schedule. Let her." The seats are a decoy by design, but the invoice and the schedule are real evidence, and a machine built on nobody knowing anything keeps its hit list in a bank's programme files. The only reading that works is recruitment: "buy" comes before "vanish," and they are bringing him in. State that in a handler chapter, and give the schedule a reason to be where a new joiner can see it. *Severity: weakens it.*

**1.9 The sheets promise "you are not on the list" to people who are on it.** Part 0, truth three: James shows each helper "that they are not on the list." But Pieter, Anneke and the brewery man are on the seat list, and Pieter's sheet shows his children "per allocation." At the time of "The sheets" James still believes the seats are real; "the bunkers are fuel" comes four chapters later. So he cannot tell anyone the promise is empty, and the sheets work only on conscience (the three addresses). That is fine and probably better, but then truth three is wrong as written. Either move the fuel reveal to "What was on the phone," as a document, so the sheets can carry it, or rewrite truth three. *Severity: weakens it.*

**1.10 The win works; the seats as a decoy and the thermostat work.** Attacking the compartments with each person's own paperwork is the right ending for a monitoring engineer, and "nobody rises up. People just stop. And some don't" is the right scale. "Two hundred people hold their own names, and a body confirms every envelope" is a sound reason he lives. The seats "left findable" as the satisfying wrong answer is a good mechanism and matches "the bunkers are fuel." The heating half of the thermostat (grants in cold years) is well sourced and gives Frank's kitchen question an answer. Do not touch these. *Severity: works.*

## 2. Story logic and progression

**2.1 Anneke hears everything in December and is "turned" weeks later.** She sits in the opening and in "The reading room, continued" while James lays out the invoice, the eleven addresses and three outcomes, and the old man offers five seats. In "The sheets" she reads her invoice and says "I'll need the rest of it," as if new to it. She is also "due to sign the quarter's schedule," which makes her a professional, not a helper. The v7 draft patches this with "You were in that room," but she still waited weeks after hearing about three deaths. Decide what she knew on the second of December and why she was in that chair. Simplest: the old man brought her to watch a recruitment, she had never seen the schedule, and that is the page that moves her. *Severity: breaks the story.*

**2.2 The dealer's phone call and "counted the night you asked" cannot both be true.** In "Groningen" the dealer telephoned about James "three weeks ago, before the ticket." But the ticket is opened in "Leave it," and Groningen comes after three more weeks, the payload, the control case and the week it vanished. Three weeks before Groningen is after the ticket. Separately, Part 0 says writing to a dealer puts you in the left column, so James was counted when he wrote to the dealer, not "the night you asked" in stand-up, and the payload should already list him. Fix the order (dealer call first, and it is why Kalder turns up on the golf course in "A round with a stranger") and let the payload show two entries for him. *Severity: weakens it.*

**2.3 The review date is moved four times and the last weeks do not fit.** The date comes forward in "A car park," "Leiden, nine years ago," "The long table, again" and "The leak." It is the same beat each time. Part Two ends with "the review is two weeks away" on the second of December. Part Three then has the dinner, the phone, and "over three weeks James prepares" the sheets, before the review is "brought forward to Monday." Three weeks do not fit inside two, and a date already passed cannot be brought forward. Move it twice at most, and write one calendar from the second of December to the Monday. *Severity: weakens it.*

**2.4 James leaves Buddy alone at an address he knows is listed for the dog.** In "The schedule" he reads "companion" beside his own address, says "it's the dog," names the man from the pub, sends Ana and Mora away, and keeps the old dog in the house, "knowing a house with one old dog in it is a target." Eleven days later the dog is poisoned from a water bowl by the back gate. The man the whole book is built on would not do this, and readers forgive a hero almost anything except that. Give him a reason he cannot move Buddy (the stairs, the vet, a kennel that fails) or have him take precautions that Kalder beats. *Severity: breaks the story.*

**2.5 James knows who Kalder is long before "the nail," and does nothing with it.** By "The money" he has read a seat contract that "runs through Kalder's consultancy." Then: Julian K. on the invoice, the invented book, "Deventer," the schedule. Each time the plan says "filed." Noor's identification in "The vet" is the seventh tell. The same habit recurs elsewhere: the four key-holders ("He says nothing"), and the depot reference format, which Noor explains in "Leiden" and which he then sees on a spine in "The drill" and "doesn't connect." A man who "checks everything" cannot file this much. Cut the tells to three, let him test one at work, and make Noor's call give him something new: the flask and Zwolle. *Severity: weakens it.*

**2.6 The thin book is stolen for no reason, and the brief says it is not stolen.** "Deventer, March" has the visitor photograph every page. So the organisation has the notebook, and Kalder has no need to poison a dog to take it. He also has no need to poison a dog to enter a house that stands empty every working day, and his own report says "the old dog is not a problem." The plan says the thin book is "gone" and photographs sit on the tablecloth; the v8 brief says it "is not stolen" and is on the tablecloth. Choose. The cleaner version: nothing is taken, the poisoning is the message, and it is Kalder's private act against the method. That is what "he fucked up with the dog" already says. *Severity: weakens it.*

**2.7 Part Two is thirty-one chapters and James mostly reads paper in it.** Thirteen of the thirty-one are not James in the present (interludes, villain, past). Of the rest, eight are "James finds a document": the folder, the generals' report, the invoice, page 212, the schedule, the other addresses, the accounts, the money shown to Frank. His first act that changes anything is the sheets, in Part Three. "The other ten," "The band" and "Frank, loose" sit together after the book's emotional peak and all add evidence the reader no longer needs. Fold "The band" into "Frank, loose," move "The other ten" before the floor night, and give James one thing to *do* in the middle that costs him. *Severity: weakens it.*

**2.8 Part One ends on a summary and the climax happens without James.** Part One's last chapter is "Groningen," a quiet conversation; the turn is in "The week it vanished," three chapters earlier. End the part there, or on the payload line with his number on it. Part Two ends well: he says no. Part Three's climax, the board vote, is "what happens elsewhere, imagined," while James putts. That is in character and the draft makes it funny, but it means the last thing he does on the page is deliver an envelope. Let Anneke's six a.m. doorstep carry more: one question from her that only he can answer. *Severity: weakens it.*

**2.9 Ana and Kalder act from their own motives; Noor, Aylin and Frank less so.** Ana refuses, leaves the address "not him," comes back, opens the door and improvises the pills: every step is hers. Kalder wants the seat, likes James, and acts for himself. Noor's rule is "never publish, never ask, always hide," yet she opens contact with a stranger on a forum the plan says is farmed; give her a reason that outweighs the rule. Aylin goes from proud keeper to propping the door at midnight on one question; she needs her own discovery in the visitor book. Frank is given a sheet although he was never a helper, and "honestly doesn't remember" a stand-up from the same week with no cause given. *Severity: weakens it.*

**2.10 The ending in Part 0 is not the ending in the chapters, and James has a man removed.** Part 0: the organisation "has to stop, re-think, and start again somewhere else." The chapters: the same foundation under Anneke, continuity stays, the truth stays hidden, James agrees to silence. Those are different books; the second is better and sadder, so rewrite the idea paragraph to match. Also, "Part of the deal was you gone" means James asked the organisation to hunt Kalder, and "the colleagues close in." A man who cannot pressure anyone has ordered something that looks like a killing, by the people whose killing he stopped. Decide what closing in means and whether James knows. **2.11 Dates, counts and objects that disagree.** The payload is forty kilobytes in the plan, forty-one in the brief. "Deventer" is said at the barbecue in the plan, at the pub in the brief. The last chapter is "Epilogue" in Part 1 and "Coda" plus an empty "Epilogue" in Part 2. The invoice is "billed per flagged person" yet steps only six times in thirty-one years while the list grows nightly. Corporate day calls Kalder "the stranger from the spring," but the books reach the market around June. The opening tray holds a "handwritten ledger" open "at page 118," which is the printed book's page. Kalder carries seven wedges and hits a driver: count to fourteen. The List still has about forty entries, coordinates on the last page, a date three weeks after the death, and entry 26 as a withdrawal, not "shared → coincident." *Severity: small.*

## 3. The reveals

**3.1 The opening spends Part One's two discoveries.** On page three the old man says the seats are findable and "you were counted the night you asked." Part One then builds to "The payload" (asking gets you counted) and ends on "the proof that his own bank counted him the night he asked." The reader has known since page three. The seats line costs nothing, because James misreads the seats until the end. The count line costs you the best turn in Part One. Keep the tray, the ledger, the silent woman, the seats and the generator in the opening; move "you were counted" to "The reading room, continued." This is your call. PLACEHOLDER. *Severity: weakens it.*

**3.2 The appointment that drives Part One has no payoff.** The thin book ends on a reference number, the second of December and the word *Ask*. Noor says "nobody has ever simply asked for it." Then James arrives, the tray is already out, and the scene becomes the old man's offer. He never asks, never reads the item, and decision 1 ("the one deleted sentence James reads on the second of December") has no beat in either reading-room chapter. Aylin's "Not today, but we were expecting you" also makes it unclear whose appointment it was, and why a man who died in March was booked for December. Put the asking and the reading on the page; it is the object the whole book has been walking toward. *Severity: breaks the story.*

**3.3 "Some of us remember" is not news when the old man says it.** In "The night he stops being afraid" James sees lives, "short and ordinary and cut off by somebody's administration," and a hand he has "seen all his life." He has remembered. The reader has known since "Leiden, nine years ago" that crossing is real. So item six of the final conversation, delivered "lightly," confirms what both already have. What is new is only that the top does it on purpose, with a method, to stay. Lead with that. The callback ("it's not for staying") is the real reveal in the scene and it is James's; the plan should say so and cut the build-up to the old man's line. *Severity: weakens it.*

**3.4 Part 0 promises a religion layer that has no chapter.** "Where every reveal goes" lists, for Part Two, "through Noor, the shelf and Beard Man": the knowledge-bringers punished, the old gods rewritten as demons, "the warden who calls himself God," "within" turned into "through us," the names as passwords, the window the size of a meal. No Part Two chapter summary contains any of it. Two items appear as PLACEHOLDER topics under "Leiden." Beard Man does not appear after "The week it vanished." The warden is never paid off: the top turns out to be people. "Frank, loose" adds a third cosmology (a simulation that renders what is observed) beside the pool and the warden. Choose the pool, give Beard Man a second scene, and cut the warden or say who he is. *Severity: weakens it.*

**3.5 Things set up and not paid off.** "The original has a hand in it": who gave is never answered (see 6.3). "That night James starts building something … and doesn't describe it": never named. The visitor book is shown, copied at midnight and delivered, but the plan never says what it proves. "The hearings on the bar TV" are laid on the table in December with no earlier scene. The edited forum persona, the gorilla fitted to James himself, is seen by eleven people and costs him nothing with Ana, Frank or work. The shelf of novels is "paid off later" only by a line about VALIS; Blindsight argues against the book's own thesis and nobody notices. The Dalai Lama search is "confirmed" without ever having been raised. *Severity: weakens it.*

**3.6 The reader's knowledge of Kalder is well ordered; the small objects pay off.** "Leiden, nine years ago" names Kalder and his crossing before "On land," so the pub and barbecue play as dread, not puzzle. That is the right choice, and it settles decision 4: the car park chapter can stay anonymous or go, because two chapters later the reader is told anyway. The flask (Zwolle, Noor, the widow's photograph), the invented book (pub to dinner), the bag by the door (dinner to market) and the promise to run (kitchen floor to dinner) are each planted once and used once. Leave them alone. *Severity: works.*

## 4. The theories and real material

**4.1 Down House, 1860: the dates and the "pressure" need checking.** Darwin did add "by the Creator" to the last sentence in the second edition, and the regret letter is real (to Hooker, 29 March 1863: he had "truckled to public opinion"). Check this: the second edition was corrected in late 1859, partly at Ilkley, and published 7 January 1860, weeks after the first, so "1860, at his desk" and "under public pressure" may both be off. The 1863 letter is about using the word "creation," not expressly those three words. Check where the letter is held (Cambridge University Library, I believe). Note also that Darwin *added* a hand and the organisation removes them; let James say that, or the chapter argues the wrong way. *Severity: small.*

**4.2 Croton, 530 BC: the source is a joke told against Pythagoras.** The dog story survives as a few lines by Xenophanes, a contemporary, quoted centuries later by Diogenes Laertius, and it is usually read as mockery. It gives no place and no year; Croton and 530 BC are when Pythagoras is thought to have moved there. The brief's rule for interludes is "real people doing documented things only." So tell it as what it is: a rival's verse, the first surviving mention of the idea, already wearing a gorilla mask in the sixth century BC. That fits the book better than a straight scene and keeps you inside the rule. Check this against a translation before quoting. *Severity: small.*

**4.3 Mexico City, 1994: accurate, but its placement accuses.** Jacobo Grinberg did vanish in December 1994 and his "transferred potential" experiments used pairs in separate shielded rooms. Check this: the result has not been solidly replicated, and the brief wants that said. The problem is position. "Utrecht, 1993" invents a Dutch replication, the foundation calls it "recall," the author dies on a canal road, and entry 26 notes "same method." Then comes the real disappearance, "facts only." The reader will conclude your organisation took a real man whose family is alive. That is an invented act attached to a real person, which the rule forbids in spirit. Add one sentence of the narrator refusing the inference, or move the interlude away from "Leiden." *Severity: weakens it.*

**4.4 Washington, 1953: not a weekend, and not proof the policy ran.** Check this: the Robertson Panel met 14 to 17 January 1953, Wednesday to Saturday, and one of the five arrived late. "Five scientists, one weekend" looks wrong. The recommendation to debunk through mass media, with Disney named, is in the Durant report; quote from the report itself, not from a secondary book, and confirm the "burden of proof" wording is there. "Frank's kitchen" then has James read it as "a standard of proof that was taught until nobody remembered it was taught." The record supports a recommendation; whether the education program was carried out is disputed. Let Frank say that, since it is exactly his objection, and let "the memo's the table" stand as the draw. *Severity: small.*

**4.5 Urfa, 1994: sound, with one loose number.** Klaus Schmidt did visit Göbekli Tepe in 1994, the name does mean Potbelly Hill, and it lies about fifteen kilometres from Urfa, so Tresidder walking out of the town in 1929 fits. Check this: the site was noted in a 1963 survey and dismissed, which is a gift you are not using, and the direction is northeast. "The date of civilisation moves by six thousand years" is the popular comparison with Stonehenge, not a finding; say what moved (monumental building before farming). "The book never says he found it" is the right restraint. *Severity: works.*

**4.6 The 1999 French report is not quite what "Leiden" says it is.** COMETA was a private association's report, published in a magazine, by a committee chaired by an air force general with other senior officers and engineers; it was not an official government document and its authors were not all generals. Check this: the passage about sacred texts, films and "suitable sects" is in the political chapter, not the last chapter, and it is a thought experiment about how visitors might handle us. "Its authors feared only being seen up close" is the MindBook's reading, tagged probable there. Noor is a conservator and would be exact: have her say "a private report by retired officers" and read the passage as a thought experiment. It still works on James. *Severity: small.*

**4.7 "Every book" breaks the rule against invented edits on real books.** The rule: "no invented edit attached to a real book." But the old man says they took one thing "out of every book in the world," Part 0 says "every book that said knowledge was *given* now says it *arose*," and the religion layer has "within" turned into "through us," which any reader will hear as the Gospels. A universal claim lands on real books by definition, and the narration half-confirms it through James's floor night. Keep the forty-one as the only edits the book asserts. Make "every book" visibly the old man's boast, and present "within / through us" as a known translation and doctrine dispute that Noor points at, not as an edit. *Severity: weakens it.*

**4.8 The final conversation names real films, a real studio founder and a living institution.** The brief allows real works "only as the old man's *claims*," and the draft keeps to that. Three still carry risk. "I Origins was made to discredit a good theory" says living filmmakers were instruments. "Walt was our best customer" names a man whose company exists. The Dalai Lama search "confirmed" as cover is a claim about a living religious tradition and a living person. Check this: Dick's 1974 experience, his son's diagnosis and the eight years of writing are roughly as stated, but he was trying to explain it, not disprove it. Keep the list short, keep James laughing at it, and consider cutting I Origins to "a small film about eyes." *Severity: small.*

**4.9 The List breaks the rule in one entry and is out of date.** Entry 31 puts an invented log transcript and an invented memo ("handled with humor") on the real P-59 program and three pilots. The MindBook brief says the gorilla-mask anecdote has no primary document and "the narration should not assert it." Frank telling it as a story is fine; the List entry is an invented edit on a real record. Entry 7 dates a water-erosion paper to 1979; the real hypothesis in geology is from the early 1990s, so an invented 1979 paper predates the idea it leans on. Check this. Entries 14, 17, 22, 34 and 38 are safely invented and well made. Bring the List into line with v8.2 before anyone drafts from it. *Severity: small.*

## 5. The rules

**5.1 The biggest document in the book is taken by force.** The rules: James "cannot access sealed archives by stealth, pressure institutions, or 'hack' anything," and a door opens only when "someone *chooses* to open it." In "The dinner" Kalder is drugged and "they unlock his phone with his thumb." Everything in "What was on the phone" (handlers, schedule, allocations, the tier list that makes the sheets possible) comes from that. Ana's pills are a great beat and hers to do. The thumb is the breach. Alternatives that keep the rule: Kalder leaves the phone behind on purpose because he likes James and is loyal only to himself; or the photographs of him asleep are the whole prize and the tier list comes from Pieter or Aylin, who choose. *Severity: breaks the story.*

**5.2 The truth arrives as a speech, twice.** The rule: "Reveals arrive as objects in James's hands, never as speeches." "The final conversation" is planned as nine items from the old man with "nothing on the table." The draft breaks it up well and has James doubt it, but the central truth of the novel is still something a villain says. "Compartments" is 1,500 words of "the understanding, said plainly," with no scene, and it repeats "The math" and the phone list. Give the final conversation one object, the page James asked for in December, and let the old man talk around it. Fold "Compartments" into "What was on the phone" as a paragraph. *Severity: weakens it.*

**5.3 The dog rule is broken by the organisation's tables, and that could be the point.** "Every chapter with a table has a dog under it." No dog is planned in "A room with a long table," "The long table, again," "Deventer, March," "Washington, 1953," either reading-room chapter, "Frank's kitchen" or "Leiden." The v8 draft of "The final conversation" turns the lack into a line: "There was no dog under the table. First table in a year." That is the better rule: every table James sits at has a dog, and the organisation's tables never do, which is "companion animals are not accommodated" made visible. Write it that way in Part 0, and put Buddy in Frank's kitchen and Noor's. *Severity: small.*

**5.4 Part Three has no jokes in the plan.** "The book stays funny to the end" is a rule, and Parts One and Two have their comedy built into the beats: the toaster story, the hat sizes, the gorilla mask, Beard Man. From "What was on the phone" to "The leak" the beats are understanding, delivery and threat, with no comic object named. The drafts find some (Walt, "I'm not your secretary"), which shows it can be done, but a plan that does not name the joke loses it in revision. Name one per chapter in the beats. Beard Man is the obvious missing instrument: he has not been seen since Part One and he owes the book a second theory. *Severity: small.*

**5.5 The doors that open do open by choice.** Els rings back because of one line. The Groningen dealer stops telephoning after one question. Noor refuses to be a witness and gives a shelfmark instead. Aylin shows the visitor book and props the door. Pieter asks what to do and is told "nothing." James reads only what his new role lets him read, and the payload capture is inside his own job. Outside the dinner, the rule holds through the whole plan, and it is what makes James different from every other conspiracy hero. Keep "Twenty minutes" exactly as planned: objects on a tablecloth, six "I don't know"s, "I believe you." *Severity: works.*

## 6. Questions only Santi can answer

**6.1 What is the sentence?** Decision 1, "the one deleted sentence James reads on the second of December," is the object the book exists to deliver. Everything in 1.1, 1.2 and 3.2 waits on it. It has to be an instruction a reader could use, short enough to remember, plain enough to have sat unnoticed in a dry book, and connected to "given." It should not be attached to any real text. Until you write it, nobody can draft either reading-room chapter or the epilogue honestly. PLACEHOLDER. *Severity: breaks the story.*
