# Comp — Philip K. Dick, *VALIS* (1981)

> **Coverage:** read in full 11 Oct 2026, Spanish (Spain) translation by Rubén Masera, Ultramar 1988 digital text (~85k words): fourteen chapters, the appendix *Tractates Cryptica Scriptura* (53 numbered entries), and the permissions page. Quotes below are as printed in this edition, including its OCR slips (“Doung” for Doug, “Dionisto”). The translator renders Horselover Fat as **Amacaballo Fat**; I keep that spelling in quotes and use “Fat” elsewhere.
> **Brief: FORM, not content.** Santi’s essay already draws on VALIS (Black Iron Prison, “the Empire never ended,” 2-3-74, the hernia, the Exegesis) and the novel’s in-world system was once called V.A.L.I.S. in the ledger. So nothing here is about what Dick believed. It is about how he put one man on the page as two, how he tested himself, how he placed a document inside a novel, and how he ended without ending.
> **Standing-rule note:** the book names real living people as characters (Dick himself, Tim Leary, Jim Pike, Linda Ronstadt, Nixon as “Ferris F. Fremount”). Our reality rule forbids that. Take the mechanisms, not the roll-call.

## 1. What it is

A science-fiction writer named Phil narrates eight years in the life of his friend Horselover Fat, who is Phil. Fat’s breakdown starts with a suicidal friend’s phone call (1971), runs through a failed suicide, a psychiatric lock-up, a dying woman he moves in with, and a beam of pink light in March 1974 that fires information into his head, including a correct diagnosis of his son’s undiagnosed hernia. Fat writes a 300,000-word “exegesis” trying to work out what hit him. A sceptical friend drags the group to a low-budget film called *VALIS* whose details match Fat’s experience; they trace the filmmakers to Sonoma, meet a two-year-old girl who may be the Saviour, and the girl dissolves Fat back into Phil with a sentence. Then a laser accident kills her. Fat comes back, goes abroad to keep searching, and Phil sits at home in front of the TV, waiting for the next sign. The whole book is a man checking himself and refusing to stop.

What it is for us: the only first-rate model I know of a narrator who splits himself in two *and admits it on page three*, keeps the split working as drama for 250 pages, closes it at the moment of contact, and reopens it when contact fails.

## 2. Mechanisms worth stealing

### 2a. The split narrator: how Dick does Fat/Phil on the page

The brief marks [J-past] as “[Assumption] third person, so the reader sees him before he knew,” and names Pirsig’s Phaedrus as the model to check. Dick is the other model, and he does something Pirsig doesn’t: he keeps the past self *in the car*.

**He admits it at once.** The first sentence is pure third person: Fat’s breakdown began the day Gloria called. Six paragraphs in: «Yo soy Amacaballo Fat y estoy escribiendo esto en tercera persona» [I am Horselover Fat and I’m writing this in the third person], «con el fin de ganar la tan necesitada objetividad» [to gain much-needed objectivity]. No reveal is held back. The reader owns the secret from the start and the book spends its energy elsewhere.

**The pronouns slip, and the slip is the tell.** Same chapter: «Bob y yo —quiero decir Bob y Amacaballo Fat—» [Bob and I — I mean Bob and Horselover Fat]. In VALIS ch. 3: «nunca encontré —quiero decir, nunca encontró Amacaballo Fat—». In ch. 13, after the cure: «(en otro tiempo habría dicho «Al examinar Amacaballo Fat sus libros de consulta»)». Three self-corrections across the book, each marking the seam. The device needs maybe one per movement; more and it becomes a tic.

**Then he stops apologising and lets the two of them act.** From ch. 2 Phil is a character with a job («Soy, de profesión, escritor de ciencia-ficción»), friends, his own cat, and Fat is «mi amigo». They disagree («Fat y yo no podíamos ponernos de acuerdo» [Fat and I couldn’t agree]), they sit in a bar as two bodies with two drinks, a waitress remembers Fat’s dead girlfriend, and Fat calls him by name: «Phil, si no lo encuentro voy a morir». Fat even states the rule of the device: «no puedes existir sin mí, ni yo puedo existir sin ti» [you can’t exist without me, nor I without you]. Dick gets a sceptic and a believer in one head, and he gets dialogue out of it, which the Voice Rules prefer to monologue.

**The authorship is inverted.** «Cuando Fat me convenció de que empezara a escribir esto» [When Fat persuaded me to start writing this]. The past self commissions the book; the present self writes it. For James that is exactly the relation: the boy at sixteen is why the man is keeping a record.

**The outside world reads the split and names it.** In ch. 10 the filmmaker decodes the name over the phone: «Fat» es la traducción alemana de «Dick» [“Fat” is the German translation of “Dick”]. A second admission, from a stranger, late. Lesson: let someone other than the narrator notice the two-ness once, in dialogue, and let the narrator go «rígido como un trozo de madera».

**It closes at the moment of contact.** The child says: «Phil, Kevin y David. Sois tres. No más.» [Phil, Kevin and David. You are three. No more.] Fat is gone mid-scene. «Nunca dejó de ser yo mismo» [He never stopped being me]. And then the comic-literal consequence: Phil realises he will now have to pay Fat’s child support. That one line is what makes the merge believable; metaphysics backed by a bank transfer.

**It reopens when contact fails.** After the girl dies: «El que lo hacía era Amacaballo Fat» [The one doing it was Horselover Fat]. The relapse is written as the split returning, and the two halves end in two places: Fat on a plane to Tokyo, Phil in a chair. The device *is* the diagnosis of the ending.

**What it costs.** “I” can always disown. Every wild theory is Fat’s; every doubt is Phil’s. The reader forgives this because Dick is visibly in on the joke, but the price is that Phil is never on the hook until ch. 12. **Where it fails:** ch. 7–8, when the supposedly sane half starts theorising as wildly as the sick one (Zebra is Fat’s own future self; the lake dream means «soy mi propio padre»). The sceptic is contaminated and the device loses its reason to exist for thirty pages. Also, in the bar scenes, two bodies drinking two beers strains the contract. Dick knows: «Estamos en esto juntos».

**For [J-past].** Dick suggests: write the boy as “he,” admit it in one sentence the first time it happens, let one «quiero decir» per movement show the seam, and let the boy *do* something the man can’t (the photograph gag in ch. 1: a funeral picture with a VW parked behind the wreaths, «Se me ve entrando furtivamente en él en mitad del servicio» [You can see me sneaking into it mid-service]; a past self seen in third person by way of an object). Merge the tracks at the crossing; if the crossing is later doubted, let “he” come back for a page. One caution: in Dick the split is a symptom, dissociation after Gloria, and the cure is reintegration. In the novel the split is craft. Unless Santi wants James to carry a wound that splits him, the admission should be dry and the merge should be quiet.

### 2b. A man checking himself: the self-tests, and which come back negative

The novel’s rule is nothing concluded on a first attempt and one negative result per act. Dick runs more tests than any novel I’ve read, and the honest ones fail. Mapped:

| Test (VALIS ch.) | What was checked | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Fraunhofer lines** (2) | Fat claims the pink is beyond “A” in the solar spectrum; Phil looks it up | **Negative.** «no existe A». «Lo que Fat vio o creyó ver no era luz alguna» [What Fat saw or thought he saw was no light at all] |
| **The laser on the street** (2) | A pink dot on Phil’s arm; two teenagers with a kit | Deflationary. «¿Ese es el color rosa?» Fat won’t answer. A test the subject refuses |
| **LSD 1964** (3) | Could the 1964 acid trip explain the later Greek? | **Negative** as explanation: Fat never took psychedelics in quantity, and Latin on acid doesn’t produce koiné ten years later |
| **Koiné Greek** (3) | Beth recognises the phonetic scrawl as Greek Fat doesn’t know | Inconclusive. Phil offers Jung’s phylogenetic memory and dismisses it himself: «meramente especulativo» |
| **The hernia** (2) | The one claim that could be checked by a surgeon | **Positive, with caveat.** «para ser del todo justo, tengo que admitir» [to be entirely fair, I have to admit]. Then the caveat: maybe he knew unconsciously |
| **Sensory deprivation / pain** (4) | Kevin’s theory: infected tooth + fear + no sleep = Tibetan Book of the Dead | Offered, never run |
| **The psych ward tests** (4) | Formal evaluation | Passed, but «Fat había falseado las respuestas». A false negative, and the narrator says so |
| **Dr Stone** (4–5) | The psychiatrist’s interview | The confirming authority: «Usted es la autoridad». Fat’s own gloss: «Los que están de acuerdo con uno están locos» [Those who agree with you are mad] |
| **Rorschach** (7) | Schizophrenia? | **Negative.** «El test sólo reveló una ligera neurosis» |
| **Blood pressure, pets** (7) | Hospital finds no physical cause; both animals die of tumours | Unexplained. «Os cuento estas cosas por lo que valen. Son ciertas; han sucedido.» |
| **GABA / phosphenes** (7) | Dick quotes his own earlier novel: a drop in GABA produces exactly these images; pentothal + the fish necklace as the trigger | A full mechanistic explanation, «Así de simple había sido todo», and then **not accepted**, because it can’t explain the hernia or the letters |
| **The Soviet letters** (7) | Predicted Monday, arrive Wednesday; Beth opens them | Positive but unverifiable. Dick refuses to say what was done with them |
| **Maurice** (6) | Was Beth’s departure engineered? | A paranoid belief *confirmed*: «no era un paranoico después de todo» |
| **The film’s date** (9) | Did *VALIS* predate 1974? | Kevin says 1977. «Pero no lo sabes en realidad.» **The one test Dick never runs.** |
| **The Lamptons** (11–12) | Are the corroborators sane? | **Negative.** The child herself: «Están enfermos» |
| **The Saviour** (13) | Can the thing protect what it made? | **Negative.** Dead at two, by accident |
| **Coincidence** (14) | REY / FELIX in two consecutive commercials | Named and left: «Debe de ser sólo sincronismo» |

Three things to take. First, the shape: one verified effect (the hernia), a dozen tests, most of them failing, and the narrator reporting the failures with more care than the successes. Second, the standard of proof is stated in the narrator’s own voice and is better than anything in the theology: «Realidad es lo que no desaparece aun cuando hayas dejado de creer en su existencia» [Reality is what doesn’t go away when you stop believing in it]. That is James’s criterion exactly; it sits in his mouth without translation. Third, the test Dick skips is the one James must run: the date. An observability engineer checks timestamps first. Make the novel’s equivalent of “did the film predate the experience” a scene, with a result.

Also the structural lesson: Dick lets the *sceptic* find the evidence. Kevin, the man with the dead cat, is the one who sees the film and says «Aguantad conmigo». When Frank is the one who brings James the thing that can’t be dismantled, it will land the way the film lands.

### 2c. The Exegesis as a document inside a novel

Phil calls it a diary; Fat calls it an exegesis; Fat wins («El término «diario» me pertenece; no corresponde a Fat»). From 300,000 words he extracts the *Tractate*, and Dick mocks the title in the same breath: «A Fat el título en latín le parecía mucho más impresionante» [Fat thought the Latin title sounded much more impressive]. The mockery is what buys the right to quote it.

**How it is placed.** Entries are quoted by number, short, and **out of order**: 37, 36, 38, 35, 32, 30, 48, 28, 1, 2, 18, 24, 47. The reader meets the document the way the narrator does, by need, not by sequence. The full set sits in an appendix for rereading, which means the body of the novel never has to be complete.

**How much is quoted.** Most entries are one or two sentences. Two are pages (#47, the cosmogony, and #48). Those two are where the book sags: ch. 6 ends on the entire cosmogony quoted whole, and nothing in the plot needs it.

**What load-bears.** The entries that work are the ones that are short, strange, and later turn out to be *keys*. #7, «El Buda se encuentra en el parque» [The Buddha is in the park], is quoted back to Phil over the phone by a stranger in ch. 10. #9, «Vivió mucho tiempo atrás, pero aún sigue vivo» [He lived long ago, but is still alive], turns up in a dossier on screen in the film. #18–19, the two-word cipher REY FELIX, becomes the password that gets the group through the door and, at the end, the sign in the TV commercials. Doctrine sags; passwords load-bear.

**The narrator writes on the document.** «El subrayado me pertenece» [The underlining is mine]. And after #32: if «no se advierte que Fat está escribiendo acerca de sí mismo» you have understood nothing. The narrator reads the holy text as symptom, in brackets, while quoting it. That is the register for James reading the professor’s List: the entry, then the man.

**Documents as objects in scene.** The book is full of paper changing hands: Dr Stone’s «preciosa copia dactilográfica» of Nag Hammadi; the certified letter from the USSR; the sign in the ward (NO LOBOTOMIES WITHOUT WRITTEN CONSENT, which the patient edits in pen); Maurice’s list of ten wants; the list of recognised film details, made in Fat’s living room with Kevin’s fountain pen; Lampton’s letter with one word and an arrow; four glossy photographs of a Greek krater in a briefcase. Every one is a scene, and each is handled, not read in silence. For the Record, the List and the style guide: give each a first appearance as a physical thing, a second as a thing quoted short, and a third as a thing that unlocks something.

### 2d. The clue in the garbage

The satellite in the film appears five times, once as a crushed beer can a taxi runs over. The last sign of the book is two words straddling a supermarket ad and a cartoon cat. Dick states the rule: the divine shows up «en los estratos de la basura» [in the strata of rubbish]. This is Wool’s capitalised “Supply” done the other way round: the clue is not hidden in a document, it is hidden in noise, and only the man who is already looking sees it. For the ownerless service on a dashboard at the same minute every night, the register is exactly this. Nobody else at standup can see a beer can as a satellite.

### 2e. The handler’s phone call

Ch. 10, 2 a.m., a stranger with a British accent: «¿Cuántos sois vosotros?» [How many are you?]. Then: «hemos hecho averiguaciones acerca de usted» [we have made enquiries about you]: drugs, Leary, arrests, the agent, the publisher. Then warmth: «Todos sus sufrimientos han terminado». The vetting disguised as welcome, the file read aloud as friendship. That is Kalder’s first scene with James, and the trap Dick hands us for free: Phil «había dejado de temblar». The relief is the hook.

### 2f. The ending

Sofía, two years old, sits among goats. She answers in the register of a system that returns only valid queries: «Soy la que soy», and to anything else «A eso, Sofía no replicó nada» [To that, Sofía said nothing]. Kevin names it: a terminal. She dissolves Fat, gives the group one speech (its only new line: the true god is man himself; no more gods), tells them to leave, and promises the tape will record only the book she was reading. Then Mini kills her, by accident, with the laser he was using to feed her information. Phil: «en lugar de dolor experimentaba un amargo furor» [instead of grief I felt a bitter rage]. Two days of news. «El mundo continuaba como siempre.»

**What it refuses to resolve:** whether VALIS exists; whether the child was the thing or a bright girl with a speaker in her skull; whether Phil is one or two; whether the search ends. **How it refuses:** by letting the narrator speak the strongest case against, in full, to Fat’s face: «No existe el Salvador»; religion’s secret name is death; «Vete a follar». And then, after that, the commercials. Dick names coincidence («Debe de ser sólo sincronismo») and does not argue with it. Linda is pregnant again; Fat phones from Tokyo about two thousand islands; Phil sits down. Last clause: «Permanecía sentado; esperaba; miraba; me quedaba despierto» [I stayed seated; I waited; I watched; I stayed awake].

**Does it work?** Yes, for three reasons the novel can use. The final image is domestic and checkable (a man, a chair, a television), not cosmic. The refusal is a verb (waiting) rather than a shrug. And the doubt is given to the most credible voice, Kevin, who gets the last honest line about Fat: «Depende de cómo definas «muerto»». The novel’s «Check.» is the same move: a small word in a kitchen after a large claim. Where the ending slightly fails: the “mission” the child gives the group is a thread Dick never ties, and the comic-literal relapse (Fat robbed of his camera by a girl in Metz) runs two pages longer than the grief can carry.

One more gift from the ending: the group’s motto, a dream in which Fat is a fish who cannot hold a rifle. «Los peces no pueden portar armas» [Fish cannot carry guns]. That is the Lantano refusal as a joke. When James is offered the instrument, this is the register of his no.

## 3. The trap

- **The theology dumps.** VALIS ch. 5 (Logos, 1 John, Heraclitus), ch. 6 (the cosmogony entire), ch. 8 (Parsifal, *salvator salvandus*, where Dick admits «No sé lo que esto signifique»), ch. 11 (Jung, Dionysus, Jonestown, written, he says, «con mano pesada; estoy tan cansado»). The narrator confesses fatigue mid-paragraph. Rule: when the narrator says he is tired, cut. Our essay carries the theology; the novel carries a man.
- **The Lantano problem is the Lamptons.** The moment the revelation is externalised into other people (three-eyed builders from Albemuth, a laser rig in Sonoma) the book stops being a man checking himself and becomes an SF plot. Dick saves it only by having the child call them sick and by killing the plot with the laser. Noor and Bram are our Lampton risk. Noor gets one line, not a house in wine country. Bram brings files, not a cosmology.
- **Self-pity.** Ch. 5–6, the Sherri chapters: a man who moves in with a dying woman to be needed, a therapist shouting «Usted no vale nada», and the verdict on the women, «Arrastrado a la tumba por dos mujeres malignas» [Dragged to the grave by two malign women]. This is the least likeable stretch in Dick, and it is where grief turns the dead into the narrator’s wound. James’s father and Ana must never be written as what happened to James.
- **The decade.** Nixon, Jonestown, Ronstadt, Leary, the counterculture phrasebook Dick himself mocks («Se llega lejos»). Period texture dates faster than any idea in the book.
- **Real people.** Dick, Pike, Leary, Disch, Galen by name. Our rule forbids it. The vetting call (2e) works with invented names.
- **Kevin’s cruelty.** Take his function (the sceptic who finds the evidence, the dead cat as the reader’s standing objection), not his register. Frank tells the Smithsonian story as a joke; he does not tell James to go get laid.

## 5. The source’s own failures

- **The sceptic is contaminated** (ch. 7–8). Once Phil theorises as freely as Fat, the device has no reason to exist and the reader loses the handrail. Keep Frank unconverted; keep James’s doubt louder than his wish.
- **Doctrine quoted whole.** #47 and #48 are the Exegesis at its worst and the novel at its slowest. No entry in the List longer than a paragraph in the body text.
- **The externalised revelation** (ch. 11–12). The Lamptons, Albemuth, three eyes. Dick’s own child calls them sick, which is the author admitting the graft didn’t take.
- **The women as wounds.** Gloria, Sherri, Sofía: «tres en total». The book is a man who cannot save women and whose god is the third one. That is Dick’s pattern, not ours.
- **The untied mission.** Sofía gives orders nobody follows. If the professor’s crate gives James an instruction, it has to cost him something by the end.
- **Honesty as structure, not just tone.** The book’s real success is that every failure above is confessed inside it. Dick writes «esta es otra trampa china» [this is another Chinese finger-trap] a dozen times about his own reasoning. The confession is what keeps the reader. James’s equivalent is the Voice Rules’ commit → smacked → joke → retry, done to his own theories.

---

**One line to carry forward:** admit the two of you in one sentence on the first page, let one slip per movement show the seam, merge at the touch and split again when the touch fails; test everything, print the negatives with more care than the hit, and end on a man in a chair doing a verb.
