## a. The mechanisms, one by one

**A. The gate.** *Supported by:* King (strongly); *The Name of the Rose* (William sets his own gate, Day 4, Vespers, while Bernard Gui, who has none, burns the wrong people); *Declare* (passed by refusing, ch. 8); *Libra* (from outside: Oswald brings every paper to Mexico City and still gets no); Pynchon (the two columns, said by a clown and refused, ch. 6); Eco's *Pendulum* (inside the canary); Borges; Orwell (the dial). Weak in Gibson and *Illuminatus!*. *Best example:* King, *11/22/63*, ch. 3 and 7: "ninety-five percent isn't a hundred," and knowledge loses its value once used. The calendar, not the doubt, makes the wait bearable. *New:* *Rose*'s rival reader as the clock. Waiting costs because a worse reader's answer becomes the official one; in SDF the plausible endings are that answer, and every week James waits another address closes as "heart" or "sold." *Fits SDF?* Yes, if James sets the gate himself and a person (Frank) chooses to open it. *Verdict:* use it. Still the named gap, now with four more books behind it.

**B. The canary.** *Supported by:* Eco's *Pendulum* (central), Gibson, Orwell, Borges, *Rose*, *Declare* (both ways, ch. 7 and 9); *Libra* and *Illuminatus!* run it from the conspiracy's side. *Best example:* Eco, ch. 75–76: one fake sect in a real chronology; Agliè asks who mentioned it, asks again, checks his watch. *Revised:* the cheapest canary in ten books is *Rose*, Day 2, Terce. William says loudly that nobody may touch Venantius's desk, and by evening three monks have tried to steer him off it. One true public sentence about a real object. James already does this in v7 The reading room, continued ("to see which one made the woman by the window move"). *Fits SDF?* Yes, with invented titles only, and a true sentence wherever one will do. *Verdict:* keep the one invention the plan has (On land, cashed in The dinner) and give it Eco's four parts: a unique item, a hook someone can act on, a readout through a third person, a price. Add no second invention. That removes Tier 1's fake sheet in The reading room, continued.

**C. The front.** *Supported by:* *Illuminatus!* (deciding); *Rose* (each solved layer covers the next, and on Day 5 a true smaller secret closes the case officially); *Libra* (built by the antagonist, and by Oswald, who hides the rifle "to give them something to uncover, a layer to strip away"); *Declare* (Hale wears fronts and must live up to them, ch. 16); Borges, Orwell, Gibson. In Pynchon fronts run four deep but are only learned. Weak in Eco's *Pendulum* and King. *Tier 1 said* C is only ever learned, so not a Part Two engine. That holds for Tier 1 and Pynchon. *Tier 2 says* it works under four conditions (*Illuminatus!*): (1) someone acts on a layer as if it were the bottom, and pays; (2) the layer breaks from outside him, on an object or a second person, never on his deduction; (3) each layer is true at the rank of whoever hears it; (4) there is a floor, or you get Gimmel's appendices and the reader stops caring. SDF's floor is The night he stops being afraid. The map also corrects the brief: the four helper words on Kalder's phone are compartments side by side, not rungs. SDF's ladder is James's own climb, from asker (The payload) to program staff (the badge "one note up," v7 The drill), annex role, schedule, and the offer of a seat. Each promotion hands him the next layer, so C is already the shape of Part Two; condition (1) is what's missing. *Best example:* *Illuminatus!*, Trip 8: Danny takes Mama Sutra's kind counter-history, ends up defending the Illuminati, and a clearance line ("Mama isn't cleared for this") breaks it. *Fits SDF?* Yes, with no impersonation, no invented task, no staged ordeal. *Verdict:* it works when James acts on a layer and pays. Two changes below do that (The money, The math). Build no more.

**D. Lock and key.** *Supported by:* King, Gibson, Orwell, Pynchon, *Rose* (doors open by choice); *Libra*, with a limit (a key opens talk, not choice); *Declare*; *Illuminatus!*, inverted (Joe gets every key and declines, as James declines the lower door). *Best example:* Gibson, ch. 29–31: the address bought with a Curta on Bigend's money, and the tie comes due. *Fits SDF?* Already on the page (Groningen, Els's line, Noor's shelfmark, the forum password). *Verdict:* nothing to build. Keep *Libra*'s limit for The sheets: a key gets a helper talking; it doesn't decide for them.

**Further mechanisms, merged, with verdicts:**

- **The cost after the gate.** King ch. 9, 26–29; *Libra* (Ferrie's exit comes with a killer at the theater). Being right doesn't make the act safe. *Fits;* travels with A.
- **Duty: the hero does the machine's homework.** Orwell I.iv (Ogilvy); Pynchon (duty as the engine; the hero as carrier, ch. 5); *Declare* ch. 9. *Best:* Orwell I.iv. *Fits;* it is C's condition (1) in SDF's clothes. The money.
- **The findable trail: who handed me this?** Orwell II.ix (true bait); Pynchon ch. 6 ("Every access route… traced also back to the Inverarity estate"); *Libra* (the Curator chooses what to send); *Illuminatus!* (the planted resting place, Trip 10). *Best:* Pynchon ch. 6. *Fits;* the opening already says "we leave them findable." Missing is James sorting pulled from handed. Twenty minutes, Frank, loose.
- **The side channel carries the bill.** King ch. 23–25; Gibson ch. 29–31. *Fits:* the friendship is the channel, the dog the bill.
- **Make it useless, don't take it.** Orwell III.iv (the dust put back); *Rose* Day 2 (the lenses stolen, the page left; "ruined by mice"); Borges (the withdrawn copy). *Fits,* if the thin book stays in the kitchen (d).
- **The file that discredits him.** *Illuminatus!* Trip 4 (Saul told he is "Muldoon"); Eco ch. 108–109 (the frame); *Declare* ch. 3 (a file improved to discredit); *Rose* ("no one will believe you"). *Best:* *Illuminatus!* Trip 4. *Fits* exactly: Els's folder prints the rule ("reclassified") and nothing on the page uses it. Take one: *Illuminatus!* 5.2, *Declare* 5.1 and Pynchon 5.4 all want the same slot.
- **The invention that can't be taken back.** Eco ch. 76, 118. The price of any invented canary, and the reason to keep to one.
- **Smaller, used as runners-up or in Part Three:** the truth filed among the nonsense (*Rose* Day 6); evidence put up for sale (Pynchon's lot 49); the kind counter-history (*Illuminatus!* Trip 8); co-optation (*Rose*'s Benno) and the slip at the middle level (*Libra*'s Mackey); holding back as an action and the old post as tripwire (Gibson); the broken man in public (Orwell III.vi).

**On lore, all ten agree:** it holds when someone in the room can lose by it. *Rose* proves it with the same material: the Dolcino history stalls on Day 3 and runs at Remigio's trial on Day 5, where a life hangs on each answer. Your long lore scenes (Frank's kitchen, Leiden, The reading room, continued) each have someone who can lose: Frank, Noor, Anneke. Keep it so.

**Does not work:** Gibson's deflated conspiracy; Borges's withdrawal; Pynchon's open ending; Branch sealed in his room (*Libra*); the bottomless stack and Hagbard's kidnaps and drugs (*Illuminatus!*); the solicitor as leverage (*Declare*); William's stealth; Oedipa's lies for reactions.
