## M. Scene seeds

Two people in a room. Who, where, what each wants, what the reader learns that James doesn't. Movement and track.

## N. Santi's taste, read from the library

What the seventeen books say about what you find convincing.

- **You trust the checker over the believer, and you keep both on the shelf.** Peebles (a declared skeptic) and COMETA (declared believers) sit next to each other, and the Q&A notes record each author's posture before each answer `watch-the-skies/91-qa.md`. The book you built the pipeline around is a *verification* pipeline. The novel's contract has to match it: a finding is a thing that survived an attempt to kill it.
- **You gravitate to one method: pattern over instance, with the instance checked.** The essay says it outright ("Patterns are harder to ignore") and the library is a set of pattern books — Vallée's control system, Talbot's holographic lineage, Campbell's rule-set, Puech's five-world table. But every one of them is here with its weakest claim flagged (Vallée's one graph; Talbot's double standard; Campbell's "take a vote" satire). You like the big shape, and you like being told where it is thin.
- **You like insiders who regret.** Vallée's two renunciations ("If I still believed it today I would not be publishing this book"); Pasulka's Tyler weeping on the concrete over Challenger; Moore's confession speech; Bernard, Adams, Morat in your comps. The character you keep buying is the one who went along and knows it. That is Bram and Kalder, and it is the register the Keepers need: self-exculpation as the tell.
- **You distrust rescue.** Muses's saucer offering "a lift" and Vallée declining; COMETA's Herodians; Rushkoff's "you can monopolize, but you can't escape." Your coda is an old man and a dog. The library's answer to the Keepers is never a bigger ark.
- **How far the novel can push its claims before a reader like you stops trusting it.** Use the essay's own weighting as the ceiling. Grounded tier (Göbekli, the rewriting pattern, messenger capture, the physics): characters may state these as real. Anomaly tier (Moon, Dogon, Cuba, water memory, Grinberg): characters may *tell* them, the narration may not endorse them, and James should disprove at least one on the page. Interpretive tier (the Gnostic frame, Vallée's hypothesis, the thermostat): shown as structure, never stated as fact by the narration, and only ever said by Noor or Bram with the attribution on it. The one claim that can go above the ceiling is the one the library cannot supply and you own: that someone, somewhere, counts.
