# Borges, "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius" — questions an SDF agent will ask

> Pointers: `00-map.md` (the map), `01-the-story.md` (the section note), and the story's parts (I, II, Postscript). v8.2 chapters are named by title from `02 Structure/THE BOOK v8 — every chapter explained.md`.

**1. What is the story, in two lines?**
One copy of an encyclopedia volume has four extra pages on a country called Uqbar, and every other check comes back blank (I). Later a whole volume about an invented planet turns up, a secret society is revealed by letter, Tlön objects enter the world, and the world adopts Tlön (II, Postscript). See `00-map.md` §1.

**2. Why read it for SDF at all?**
It is the shortest model there is of a textual variant turning into dread, and its variant is as trivial as "transmitted to / arisen among." It also shows a conspiracy that edits its own text to be believed (Postscript), which is the organization's method in *The sentence* seen from the editor's side. See `00-map.md` §4.

**3. How does Borges make a trivial variant frightening?**
Through checks, not through a find. Index, spellings, two atlases, the National Library, Bioy's own index and a bookshop copy all come back blank, and each removes a dull explanation (I). The dread is the narrowing. See `01-the-story.md` "Why it grips" and `00-map.md` §2, rows 1–4.

**4. Doesn't *The sentence* already do this?**
Yes, copy against copy and the apparatus check (typos, blemish, copyright line) are on the page, and the disappointment at a trivial variant is already better than Borges's. What Borges adds is a control on the other side (is the odd copy the only one?), checks outside the book, and nothing more. See `00-map.md` §5 opening.

**5. And *The control case*?**
The plan has a third 1931 copy (whole run) and nine other books (nothing). Both test the second impression. Nobody tests whether another *first* impression survives, which is Borges's whole Part I. Proposal 1 in `00-map.md` §5.

**6. What exactly is "the apparatus that wasn't updated"?**
Bioy's volume has 921 pages but the same spine key, Tor-Upps, which doesn't cover Uqbar, and his own index lacks the name (I). Whoever inserted the pages left the furniture alone, and the furniture gives it away. In SDF the copyright line already does this; proposal 5 adds the 1931 index.

**7. Is the index idea consistent with the plan?**
Yes. *Leiden, 1931* has the printer reset three lines and leave the misprints, because a clean plate is a confession; resetting the index would be a fourth visible change. The index entry is a fossil in every 1931 copy. It is a proposal, not canon (`00-map.md` §5, proposal 5).

**8. What does the narrator lose?**
Almost nothing: a private doubt about Bioy (I), regret over a question he didn't ask Ashe (II), a circle cut in his palm by the cone, and the history of his childhood (Postscript). The map says so plainly in §2. Don't cite this story as a model for cost.

**9. Then why doesn't the passivity kill it?**
Because the evidence gets heavier as his part shrinks: four pages, a 1,001-page book, a letter, a compass, a cone. Fifteen pages can run on that. A novel can't, which is why every SDF proposal attaches a cost (`00-map.md` §5).

**10. Where can the narrator stop, and does he?**
After Mastronardi's blank, end of Part I: he stops for two years and something comes to him. And at the end, by choice: "Here I end the personal portion." See `00-map.md` §2, rows 4 and 8–9.

**11. How does a 3,500-word lecture not stall?**
The inventor is still unknown, each doctrine is a deadpan joke, and the last item, the hrönir, is set up for the Postscript (II). It still stalls for a reader not there for philosophy. SDF's brief breaks speeches at about 150 words, so don't take it (`00-map.md` §3, §6).

**12. What are hrönir and why do they matter?**
Objects produced by expectation: prisoners and schoolchildren dig up what they were shown or hoped for (II). They state the canary's rule (the tested must not know it is a test), and they model the Tlön project itself. See `01-the-story.md` "Mechanism notes".

**13. Which of A–D does the story support most?**
C (front) most strongly: Uqbar is a front for Tlön, the First Encyclopaedia a draft for *Orbis Tertius*, a country becomes a planet (Postscript). B and an inverted A also hold; D appears as a missed key. See `00-map.md` §4.

**14. What is the fifth mechanism?**
Four candidates: checks as the engine; the apparatus that wasn't updated; the heavier object; and the organization pruning its own lie to be believed (the Memphis copy cuts the hrönir). The last matches "given / arose" most closely. See `00-map.md` §4.

**15. How does the conspiracy move the plot if it never meets the narrator?**
By sending objects. Bioy brings a volume, Ashe leaves a book in a bar, a compass arrives in someone's silver, a cone falls from a dead man's belt. Orbis Tertius never notices him. See `01-the-story.md` "How the conspiracy moves the story here".

**16. Is there a canary in the story?**
Yes, twice: the hrönir rule (II) and Andrea's imaginary Rosy Cross, "which other men later founded, in imitation" (I). For SDF, *On land* already runs the invented-book trap once; proposal 2 adds controls (Frank, Beard Man) so the result means something.

**17. What does proposal 2 cost James?**
A lie to Frank, which Frank raises in *Frank, loose*, and a muddier result: Beard Man claims to know the invented book too, so Kalder could be a Beard Man. James can't act on it, which matches the plan's "passing without knowing if he's passing." See `00-map.md` §5.

**18. How does Borges fix *The money*?**
Through the spine key: in proposal 3 the contract's list of annexes skips C. James must ask Saskia, in writing, for a role to open a document the contract says doesn't exist, and her name is now on the grant. Proposal 3; checked against v7 *24 — The money*.

**19. Is the style-guide comparison (proposal 4) safe under the rules?**
Yes: both copies are given to James by people who chose to (Els, Noor), and he compares them in Noor's kitchen. The cost is a breach of Els's trust and Noor's exposure, which points at *Leiden, Friday*. Which line differs is PLACEHOLDER.

**20. Does SDF already have Borges's heavy object?**
Yes. The flask in v7 *41 — The other ten*, which the widow presses on James ("take it, I don't want it in the house now"), is the cone: a small object a witness hands over that carries the weight of the case. Don't redo it.

**21. Does SDF already have the guided discovery?**
Yes. The Memphis find may have been "consented to and guided" (Postscript); the old man in *The reading room* says "We leave them findable" about the seats. Same move, already placed.

**22. What about the real people in the society?**
Dalgarno and Berkeley are named as members (Postscript). SDF forbids real people as complicit; real names may appear only as the old man's unconfirmed claims in *The final conversation*. See `00-map.md` standing-rule note and `01-the-story.md` "Flags".

**23. Is the Anglo-American Cyclopaedia real?**
The story calls it a reprint of the 1902 *Britannica*, which is real; the section note could not confirm the *Cyclopaedia* from the text [from memory, not from the text]. Either way the lesson is the rule: never an invented edit on a real book. *Waters Before Memory* is invented, so proposals 1 and 5 are safe.

**24. Should SDF end like Borges?**
No. The narrator withdraws to translate Browne, "though I never intend to publish" (Postscript). James is allowed to publish in *Leave it*. *The other ten* already ends on James choosing not to tell the widow; one Adrogué ending is plenty (`00-map.md` §6).

**25. What makes the Postscript work as a device?**
It reframes parts I and II as a reprinted 1940 article with "editorial cuts," so what you just read becomes evidence, and the narrator is quietly doing what the society does: trimming his text for a new reader. Then it hands the rest to "every reader's memory." See `00-map.md` §3.

**26. Is the story funny, and does that matter for SDF?**
Yes: Englishmen "afflicted with unreality," a friendship that soon eliminates conversation, Reyes tired of "subordinate drudgeries of a detective nature," an ending about a translation. The deadpan carries the doctrine, which is why SDF's rule "funny to the end" is compatible with dread. See `01-the-story.md` "Why it grips".

**27. Best single line to quote to Santi?**
"instead of 917 pages, Bioy's volume had 921." (I). It is the variant stated as a count, which is how James would log it. Others in `00-map.md` §7.

**28. Which proposal should be built first?**
Proposal 1 (the copy that isn't there), because it gives Part Two its missing gate: from *The control case* on, every check has a price and James has to choose. Then 3 (*The money*) and 2 (*On land*). See `00-map.md` §5.
