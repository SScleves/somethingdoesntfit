## Why it grips
- **One more copy, and the differences turn into a scheme.** Two copies gave five differences; a third gives three more, and no plate varies the same way twice. The reader gets "a pattern with a purpose" in one paragraph ("Los muelles del Sena").
- **A task and a chase alternate.** In the library Corso studies, bluffs and hides what he sees; outside, a BMW waits. Each scene sets what he knows against what he must conceal.
- **The scene that does the most work is the plate-by-plate reading with the baroness.** She gives a confident symbolic reading of each plate; at each one Corso silently checks a physical difference on the copy in front of him. The narration lets the reader see both readings at once.
- **Humiliation carries the pace.** A failed night with Irene, a nosebleed, the punch to La Ponte, and La Ponte's defence that all he did was go to bed with her.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The hidden side now acts openly, but through a theatre of literature. Rochefort attacks twice, each time with a punch behind the right ear. Liana plays Milady, and a note in Richelieu's hand is left under a cigar butt. Each blow takes something: the first nearly took the bag, the second takes it. The staging keeps Corso reading people instead of copies. The cost to him is steady: bruises, the lost bag, and the end of the idea that La Ponte is an ally. The plate pattern, the thing that matters, stays in his head.

## How the lore gets in
- **Replinger (about 2,500 words, conversation).** He authenticates by a trade method: a filed sample of the same hand and paper. The one useful fact is the paper's colour. It slows the story on purpose.
- **Ungern's talk (about 7,000 words, interview with objects).** Demonology, Torchia's years in Prague, the plate captions and a hidden-verse card. It stays alive because Corso hides what he sees, and it ends in a trade (the photograph).
- **Corso's table (about 1,200 words, his own notes).** The reveal (8 differences, 19 plates altered, 8 by "L. F.") comes as his own conclusion over a drink in a bar, then he doubts it.

## Mechanism notes
- **How he compares.** In the baroness's library he lays copy Three on the table and reads it plate by plate while she talks: three towers on the city instead of four (I), an arrow in the archer's quiver where the others are empty (III), the hanged man hung by the right foot instead of the left (VI). Left alone he fills in "los cuadros comparativos iniciados con el número Uno", and adds a second table for the engraver marks A.T. and L.F. Crossing the two tables he finds that every plate that differs also differs in its inventor credit: of 27 plates, 19 are credited to the printer as designer and 8 to L.F. (two in One, three in Two, three in Three). He concludes the printer did not forge; he scattered one book across three. He had also matched 16 fragments of burnt copy Two to pages of One, and found that every recovered fragment is text, none a plate or the title page: the part that differed was taken before the fire.
- **D (lock and key).** The baroness lets him in on the strength of a phone call, then treats him like a promising pupil. She will not leave him alone with the book. Her key is her past: Corso's counter-key is a 1943 German magazine page showing her as an astrologer's assistant beside Himmler. He gives it to her, and she tears it up. This is blackmail, the one place in the book where a key is a threat. Replinger's lock is only mistrust; Corso offers to pay costs and gets his answer.
- **C (front).** Replinger shows the Dumas pages are genuine, so the manuscript is no puzzle. The pursuers still use the Dumas script (Liana as Milady, the Richelieu safe-conduct, Montecristo cigars). Corso reads it as a staged plot, too elaborate to be a joke. A second front sits inside the book: Ungern says the plates are a formula for summoning the devil, and also that the book is false.
- **Who follows and why.** Rochefort waits outside the foundation, jumps Corso on the Quai steps, takes the bag at the Crillon. Irene follows, kicks Rochefort, and sleeps with him. Neither says why. La Ponte says Liana knew Rochefort and let him in.
- **What each discovery costs.** The plate pattern costs him the bag and notes; the loss of Two's plates shows the other side wants the differences gone, not the books.
- **Real and invented kept apart.** Ungern backs the invented book with real figures (Bacon, Dee, Bruno, popes, an astrology circle round Himmler). [From memory, not from the text: the Delomelanicon, Torchia and Ungern are invented; Cazotte's Le Diable amoureux is real.]

## Quotes
- "Aristide Torchia había confesado la verdad a sus verdugos; pero no completa." ("Los muelles del Sena"): the printer's confession read as the sum of three copies. (Aristide Torchia had told his executioners the truth, but not all of it.)
- "Se llevaron los grabados. Los nueve" ("El número Tres"): the theft shows what the other side wants gone. (They took the engravings. All nine.)
- "Trabajo mejor a solas." ("Los muelles del Sena"): the request the owner will not grant without a lever. (I work better alone.)
- "Es por orden mía y para bien del Estado por lo que el portador de la presente hizo lo que hizo." ("Se complica la trama"): the staged literary front. (It is by my order and for the good of the State that the bearer did what he did.)

## Flags
- Corso gets the baroness's consent by showing her a photograph that exposes her past. SDF's hero cannot threaten or pressure.
- Real persons support an invented book: Himmler's circle, popes, Bruno, Bacon, Cazotte, Nerval linked to the book's provenance. SDF must not do this.
