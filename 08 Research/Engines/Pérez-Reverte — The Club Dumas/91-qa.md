## The comparison

**1. What exactly does Corso do when he compares two copies?**
He brings his copy to the other one and is lent a side room. He rules a sheet into strips, one per copy, and goes page by page writing down anything that differs. He checks the plates last, line by line, with a lens, and ends with a collation table and a list of five plain words, one per difference (`02-part-two.md`, "El número Uno y el número Dos"). The reveal is a sentence in his own notes, not something an expert tells him.

**2. What does he record before he looks for differences?**
What is the same: the number of gatherings, the position of every plate, one erratum and one broken letter that both copies share (`02-part-two.md`). That proves the copies came off one press together. Without it a difference could be wear, a repair or a later state. *The sentence* already does this (same typos, same blemish on 118, same line breaks) before it shows the changed middle.

**3. What does he do with one copy before he has a second?**
He checks it against its descriptions. He types the title page, the collation and one line per plate into a file, sets the book beside a photocopied bibliography entry, and writes the first mismatch in the margin: the bibliography says this copy lacks plate nine and it has it (`01-part-one.md`). So the first clue is a disagreement between a book and a record of it.

**4. Why does the pattern need a third copy?**
Two copies give five differences, which can be read as "one of these is a fake," the client's own claim. The third adds three more, and no plate varies the same way twice, so nothing is being corrected. Crossing the differences with the engraver's credits shows every differing plate carries the second credit, and the total comes to eight across three copies where the book has nine plates (`03-part-three.md`). Two gives a list; three gives a rule and a count.

**5. Where was the crossing detail planted?**
At the end of the binders' lecture, as a trade remark: seven plates credit the printer as designer and two credit someone else (`02-part-two.md`, "Sobre apócrifos e infiltrados"). It reads as texture until Corso gives it its own table in Paris. A detail noted before anyone knows it matters is the cheapest way to make a later crossing feel fair.

**6. How is the last twist prepared?**
In part one a bibliography claims one copy lacks plate nine (`01-part-one.md`). In part four plate nine is the only plate identical in all three copies, and Corso concludes it is a modern forgery by the binders who taught him the method (`04-part-four.md`). He reaches it by thinking back over his table, with no new inspection. See question 31 for a wrinkle in the notes.

**7. Does Corso ever get a comparison wrong?**
Not on the plates. His errors are about people: he fits the scarred man to a Dumas character and later finds the image came from a film (`01-part-one.md`), and he joins the two plots (`04-part-four.md`). The book keeps the bench work reliable and puts all the error in the reading of motives. SDF does the same: James is right about page 118 and wrong about what the seats are for.

**8. How long are the comparison passages, and do they stall?**
About 1,000 words for the first table and 1,200 for the second (`02-part-two.md`, `03-part-three.md`). They hold because pressure follows at once: a tail on the road and a death after the first, a beating on the quay after the second. In SDF terms: a table scene is safe if the next page costs something.

## The owners

**9. What does each owner want before Corso may look?**
Borja wants an agent with no cause of his own, paid in advance (`01-part-one.md`). Fargas wants a fellow believer and a witness to the two copies lying together; he will not sell and nothing leaves his house (`02-part-two.md`). The Paris dealer wants his costs paid; the baroness wants a pupil and will not leave him alone with the book (`03-part-three.md`). Balkan and, at the end, Borja want an audience (`04-part-four.md`).

**10. What actually opens a door in this book?**
Carrying the other copy. Fargas is moved by seeing the pair, and Borja lets Corso in at the end because he brings the book (`02-part-two.md`, `04-part-four.md`). Money does the opposite: when Corso mentions the client's money Fargas turns suspicious and the offer is dropped. Map proposal 5.1 uses that for Els in The math.

**11. Which of Corso's ways in can James not use?**
The hired theft of copy Two (`02-part-two.md`), the 1943 photograph he shows the baroness to get her out of the room (`03-part-three.md`), and force (`04-part-four.md`). The invented Swiss client he uses to close a sale (`01-part-one.md`) is a lie for gain and is out too. Each map proposal says what James offers or risks instead: money that is refused, a question asked where it is counted, his half of the pair shown first, public catalogs, his only first impression carried into their building.

**12. Does a key ever get Corso the copy itself?**
No. Every key gets him a look. Fargas lets him study and keeps the book; the baroness lets him read under her eye. This agrees with the limit Addendum 05 takes from *Libra*: a key opens talk, it does not decide for the owner.

**13. Who in SDF is an owner in this sense?**
Els (the folder), Noor (the two versions of the Utrecht paper, v6 Leiden), the Groningen dealer (his ledger and the receipt), the widow in Zwolle (the flask), Aylin (the visitor book), and the old man (the 1931 ledger on the tray). All but one already choose to show James something. The gap is that James rarely collates what he is shown with his own hands; see map 5.3 and 5.5.

## What a look costs

**14. What does each comparison cost?**
The owner and the copy. Fargas drowns and copy Two burns the night after the Sintra table (`02-part-two.md`); the baroness burns with copy Three (`04-part-four.md`). Corso himself pays in bruises, a ban from Portugal, the bag with copy One and his notes (`03-part-three.md`), and finally the fee (`04-part-four.md`).

**15. What is removed, the books or the differences?**
The differences. Every fragment Corso recovers from the grate is text; the plates and the title page were taken before the fire (`03-part-three.md`). The other side has no interest in the copies as copies. Addendum 05 already has "make it useless, don't take it"; this is the same idea aimed at the one part that matters.

**16. What happens to Corso's table?**
After Sintra it is the only record of copy Two. In Paris the bag with his notes is stolen, and the pattern survives only in his head (`03-part-three.md`). SDF has the same shape in The vet (the thin book gone) and Twenty minutes ("photographs where the thin book should be"). Map 5.3 and 5.5 add looks that leave James with nothing he can show.

**17. Can Corso tell whether he caused a loss himself?**
No. He had told a Portuguese contact to steal copy Two, so when it burns he cannot be sure the fire did not come from his side (`02-part-two.md`, "Postuma necat"). SDF cannot use the theft. Map 5.4 gets the same doubt lawfully: a catalog status changes after James starts watching it, and he cannot rule himself out.

**18. How does SDF's version of the cost stay inside "kills rarely"?**
By using the organization's own paperwork. v8.2's schedule already has a column for records "moved for conservation" beside the visits, and Leiden, Friday already shows an owner made quiet with a clipboard. A withdrawn copy or a quiet owner removes evidence as completely as a fire. REVIEW 02 5.1 notes the plan already has two acts that cost someone (The schedule, The vet).

## The front

**19. How does the Dumas plot work as a front?**
It carries every event that looks like plot: a hanged man, a widow, anonymous calls, a pursuer, cigar ends left in two flats, a note in a cardinal's hand (`01-part-one.md` to `03-part-three.md`). The real task happens at tables. Corso reads each event through a Dumas character, which keeps him looking at people while the answer is in the copies.

**20. Who built the front?**
Nobody built it for him. The club is a real society with a real feud over a sold chapter, and Balkan insists no one ever said the two stories were connected; Corso filled the gap (`04-part-four.md`, "Corso y Richelieu"). That is the map's one addition to mechanism C: a front that is an unrelated true story.

**21. Does it meet Addendum 05's four conditions for C?**
Yes. Corso acts on the layer as if it were the bottom (he goes to Meung). It breaks from outside him, on a person explaining and on his bag under a bed. The club is true at its own rank. There is a floor: the client, undramatic, opening his door on the fifth knock (`04-part-four.md`).

**22. Does SDF already have this?**
Yes, and the map proposes no new front. The seats are plot-shaped, left findable, announced on page three and deflated in The final conversation ("the bunkers are fuel"). In v7 The reading room, continued James lays six findings about the bank in front of the old man and none about the books, on the day the professor's book list sent him there. Map 5.5 has him put his two printings beside the ledger in that scene.

**23. Is there a second front inside the main plot?**
A small one. The baroness gives a confident symbolic reading of each plate while Corso silently checks a physical difference on the same plate (`03-part-three.md`). The meaning of the pictures sits in front of the fact that they differ. For SDF: the old man talking about method while a struck line lies on the tray.

## Lore

**24. Which long lore scene works best and why?**
The baroness, about 7,000 words (`03-part-three.md`, "El número Tres"). Corso is hiding what he sees on the page while she lectures, he needs her out of the room, and a man waits in the street. Compare the Dumas bibliography in part one, about 900 words of titles with nobody at risk (`01-part-one.md`), which stalls.

**25. How does the book teach a method without stalling?**
It gives the lecture to tradesmen with tools on the bench and ends it on a plot fact, then has the hero use the vocabulary two chapters later (`02-part-two.md`). Els's folder in The math is SDF's version: the other side's method on three photocopied pages. Map 5.2 has James use it once, on purpose, in the same chapter.

**26. How is the final explanation delivered?**
Twice, by two different people. Balkan's is a walk and a terrace talk of about 5,500 words; Borja's is a ritual with props, about 4,500 words, during which Corso keeps asking for his money (`04-part-four.md`). The first is an explanation that removes a theory, the second a scene. SDF's The final conversation is closer to Balkan's; the v8 brief's rule that a speech over about 150 words gets broken applies.

## Real and invented

**27. How is the invented book kept apart from real history?**
It has invented reference works that describe it (a 1929 bibliography, a dossier), and those can be wrong. It sits on tables and shelves beside real books, which stay unaltered (`01-part-one.md`, `02-part-two.md`). The method Corso borrows is credited to a real bibliographer *[from memory, not from the text: the study he cites is real]*.

**28. Where does it break SDF's rule?**
Real thinkers are made to quote or own the invented book, and real papyri are cited for it (`01-part-one.md`, `03-part-three.md`). Balkan claims to hold the real manuscript of a real novel, with an invented provenance (`04-part-four.md`). Real people are listed as club members (`04-part-four.md`). SDF's rule: invented edits live in invented printings; real works appear only as the old man's claims.

**29. Is there a safe version of "a detail that does not fit history"?**
Yes: put the impossible detail inside the invented object. The imprint on the invented book names an authority that could not have licensed it (`01-part-one.md`). SDF already does this with the second impression that still says "first edition" and kept its typos (The sentence).

## The notes

**30. Do the notes disagree about the plate numbers?**
Yes. `02-part-two.md` gives the labyrinth as plate III; `03-part-three.md` gives plate III as an archer and says copy Three differs on I, III and VI, while also saying no plate varies the same way twice. Both cannot be right. *[From memory, not from the text: the labyrinth is the fourth plate, which removes the clash.]* The map avoids plate numbers for that reason.

**31. Do the notes disagree about the count and the forged plate?**
Partly. `03-part-three.md` says in one place "19 plates altered, 8 by L.F." and in another that 19 of 27 plates credit the printer and 8 credit L.F.; the second is the consistent one (two, three and three across the copies). `04-part-four.md` says the forgery fills the plate one copy lacked, but its verified quote says the last plate was false in all three copies. Check the text before building on which copies carried the forgery.

## Transfer

**32. What does SDF already do with comparing copies?**
The sentence is the full routine on two impressions. The control case adds a third copy of the same impression (proving the whole run) and nine controls that show nothing. The thin book is forty-one comparisons done by other hands. In v7 The other ten and The band James compares documents about people and money. Between The control case and The reading room, continued he never sets one printing beside another.

**33. Does the third copy in The control case do what Corso's third copy does?**
No. It is a second copy of the second impression, so it confirms and adds no new kind of evidence. The thing in SDF that plays copy Three is the 1931 ledger with the struck line: two printings are a variant, the ledger is the order. It is on the tray in the opening and in The reading room, continued, and no draft has James read it against his copies. Map 5.5.

**34. Which proposals sit in the stretch REVIEW 02 calls passive?**
5.1 and 5.2, both in The math. 5.1 makes Els's refusal the result of James offering money. 5.2 has him post the true page-118 question after reading When a Reader Notices and lay the courteous reply beside the photocopy. Take one if The math is already too long; 5.2 is the one with a price.

**35. Does anything here answer REVIEW 02 finding 5.2 (James never uses his trade)?**
5.4. The four-in-the-morning build in The gorilla mask becomes a nightly check of public catalogs for three first printings, and The schedule's "records moved for conservation" column is read against his own log. REVIEW 02 already proposes the monitor for the eleven addresses; this is the same build with a different feed, so choose one.

**36. Does any proposal add another signature to the organization's paper?**
No. REVIEW 02 2.1 says keep two (the annex, the request slip). 5.5 costs James a pencil transcription in his own hand and nothing in their register. 5.2 adds a counted question, which the plan's machine already does to anyone who asks.

**37. Which proposals collide with others already on the table?**
5.1 with *Illuminatus!* 5.1 (both give Els's refusal a cause). 5.3 with Gibson 5.3 (both put a condition on Noor's kitchen). 5.4 with REVIEW 02 5.2 and Borges 1 (a monitor; a withdrawn copy). 5.2 needs the order in The math settled against "he withdraws from everyone." 5.5 has to be decided with Santi's decision 1 and the request slip.

**38. Does this book change Addendum 05's open question on Borges's checks?**
It supports Borges 5 (a fossil in the apparatus) in principle, because Corso proves twinship from shared flaws before he trusts a difference. It offers an alternative to Borges 1: a copy that vanishes after the look is more plot than a copy already missing. It gives no support to adding visitor-book entries.

**39. What should an agent not import by accident?**
A death per comparison, a hero who lies to close a deal, and real names vouching for the invention (map section 6). Also the supernatural floor: the girl who says she is a fallen angel (`04-part-four.md`) closes the book's emotion and is not part of the mechanism.
