## Why it grips
- **Each layer works as the full answer for a while**, then the reader is moved up a level (see Mechanism notes).
- **Dangling hooks.** "George Dorn is screaming" cuts into Saul's scenes twice before the jail scene explains it. Flash-forwards are dropped and left: a general raising a pistol to Mocenigo's head; Mocenigo's next date being his last with a woman; a festival seven days ahead. The reader moves forward to reach things already promised.
- **Signal and noise side by side.** The missing dogs (no food, no bowls, no tins) is honest detection. The CLARK KENT sticker Saul puts down to a magazine staffer is later shown, to the reader only, as a stray left by an obscene caller.
- **Orientation.** The narrator admits the confusion on page one. Each jump carries a date or clock time, things happening at the same moment go in parentheses, refrains ("Rock Around the Clock") work as hinges, and sometimes the narrator just warns that a jump is coming. It fails three times: the point of view moves from Saul into Muldoon's "I" mid-scene; a nameless "I" after Cartwright's call turns out to be Atlanta Hope; George moves from third to first person mid-paragraph. Each time the reader loses about a page.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
It puts every person in the room. Hagbard intercepts two spy broadcasts and wires New York to activate Malik and prepare Dorn (Trip 2). So Malik's investigation, which Saul is reconstructing, was started by a rival conspiracy, and George's assignment was bait. Then Cartwright tells Atlanta Hope that the rescue was allowed. The investigators are steered by the conspiracy they chase and by the one fighting it. The real escalation is the reader learning that each discovery was set up.

## How the lore gets in
- **Eight memos (Trip 1), about 2,500 words, documents in a found box.** Two or three at a time, each batch broken by argument, a phone call or a break-in. The sources get worse on purpose, from encyclopedia to an underground paper saying Weishaupt replaced Washington. No stall: someone reacts within a page.
- **Rebecca on the phone, about 300 words.** It is all a student hoax; one fact rides inside a flirtation.
- **Saul's analysis (Trip 1), about 1,200 words of reasoning.** It stalls, and one line from the narrator ends it.
- **Atlanta Hope's confession (Trip 2), about 900 words.** The cell structure arrives through her fear, and ends in an order to put protesters in hospital.

## Mechanism notes
- **C (front), the core of this section.** Six layers, each delivered and broken differently:
  1. *Right-wing assassins in Mad Dog* (George's brief). Held for a Trip. Broken by the Sheriff dismissing him and by the Illuminati chapel inside the jail.
  2. *A student hoax* (Rebecca). Held for one line. Broken by Saul's hunch.
  3. *The Illuminati are anarchists* (Saul's deduction). Held for a paragraph. Broken by the narrator, with no evidence given.
  4. *The jail is communist* (Mavis: the sheriff discredits America on purpose). Held for a few pages. Broken by an object, the eye-in-triangle tattoo on her chest.
  5. *Hagbard's people beat the Illuminati to George.* Held for a chapter. Broken by a phone call the reader overhears and George does not.
  6. *The Order is the top layer* (Atlanta). Broken from inside: she doubts the Five see the whole picture, and the banker Drake, who funds her, seems to know more than she does.
  The pattern to copy: a break is either seen by the hero (chapel, tattoo) or told only to the reader (the call, the narrator). The second kind puts the reader one layer ahead, which creates dread without the hero doing anything.
- **The clown is the method.** Rebecca's hoax story is SDF's funding-nonsense rule exactly: students sending out press releases so that believers have something to point at. The memos put the Washington swap beside real Masonic history. Saul throws out the silly half first, and that is what makes him trust the rest.
- **A tiny edit with consequences (Trip 2).** A Houston store sign gains "NO SPITTING"; rich customers take offence, and God's Lightning gains rich members. The Management did not put it up. The closest thing here to SDF's "given" to "arose": one change, no traceable author, and it moves people rather than informs them.
- **The Law of Fives as compartments.** Atlanta's cells match SDF's one-task helpers; her fear shows the structure from inside.
- **A and D (gate, key).** Simon gets nothing more until he makes the right move; the key is his dead father's sentence in a stranger's mouth. George must say "Hail Eris" himself.
- **Comedy that escalates with the danger.** Over Fernando Poo, each power is sure the other has troops there, and Moscow plans to sneak troops in so it can be seen withdrawing them. The same paragraph about the leader runs three times, word for word, for Washington, Moscow and Peking. Each layer gets sillier as it gets more frightening (a nuclear ultimatum), so the book stays funny without lowering the stakes.
- **Proposal (PLACEHOLDER):** borrow layer 5 for SDF. A door that a person chooses to open for James is later shown to have been allowed by the organisation, because his finding it suits them. The cost is that what he did with it served them. This keeps the rule that doors open only by choice and adds the missing cost of being wrong.

## Flags
- Real people named as complicit: the Kennedy shooting is given to Harry Coin, Oswald, Bernard Barker and a fourth man, with Howard Hunt at the Grassy Knoll; Nixon, Mayor Daley and William Buckley appear through the memos. SDF must not do this.
- The memos hang invented claims on real books and magazines (Ellul, the *Britannica*, *Playboy*); [from memory, not from the text] some citations are real. Too close to SDF's ban on invented edits to real books: use invented sources only.
