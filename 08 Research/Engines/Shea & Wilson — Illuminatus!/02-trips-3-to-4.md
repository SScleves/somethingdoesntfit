## Why it grips
- **Every answer arrives already cracked.** The reader gets a theory and the next scene breaks it. Saul's long deduction (Trip 4) wins Barney's praise, then Barney produces a list of Catholic assassins and goes one better, and Saul tops that with Fernando Poo. Each detective is Holmes for a page, so the case speeds up though nobody leaves the flat.
- **The reader is shown the trick before the victim falls for it.** Saul explains how the conspiracy recruits: it pretends to help, and it tells the target a story that fits his ideals (Trip 4). Twenty pages later Hagbard tells George that the right-wing version was "a test" and that a left-winger would have got the other one. So the friendly submarine feels dangerous.
- **The frame is a con, and that is the last thing we learn.** Trip 4 opens with Hassan i Sabbah's severed head screaming from hell and closes with the head climbing out of a trapdoor, "undamaged". The founding miracle was staged, and the reader learns it only after George's hanged double has vanished through a ceiling with no trapdoor. One trick is explained, one left open.
- **The heaviest scene attacks the detective's method.** The Playboy Club ordeal (Trip 4) wears down Saul's scepticism. Each fake slide has one flaw he catches (a missing mole), and the next slide fixes it. His jokes are his defence, and the captors aim straight at them.
- **Orientation in a book that jumps.** Every memo is dated. A hymn line ("WE SHALL NOT BE MOVED") stands between strands like a chapter break. Smiling Jim's speech is a running clock; parentheses mark time jumps. Repeated objects (the Sacred Chao, the mermaid, 23) link strands that never say they connect.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The conspiracy works by handing out explanations, not by blocking anyone. Saul names its method himself: kill rarely, because the dead man may have hidden copies, and neutralize, ideally by recruiting. The section acts it out. George is recruited with a version of the story tailored to him. Joe is recruited through checks he set himself. Saul is offered a diagnosis that turns him into a mental patient. Each layer arrives through a person who wants something from the hero, and the investigator turns out to be inside it. Joe, whose papers drive the detectives' case, is the man who will bomb his own office.

## How the lore gets in
- **Pat's memos (Trips 3–4), about 5,000 words in total, documents.** They come in 150 to 700 words at a time, and each is followed by the detectives arguing or by a cut to another strand. They never stall, because each memo contradicts the last, so each is a plot event; Pat herself notices the contradictions look deliberate.
- **Simon's 23 rant and JAM history (Trip 3), about 1,500 words, talk.** It is framed as Joe's experiment in believing for one evening, so the reader may doubt it, and Dillinger interrupts. The Daleville robbery is played as a scene in the present rather than told.
- **Father Muldoon on the phone (Trip 4), about 1,300 words, a lecture.** Saul steers it with questions toward his own hypothesis (light-bringer, atomic light). It ends in a decision to go underground, then the capture.
- **Hagbard's 200-year history (Trip 4), about 1,500 words, talk.** George asks for a sign, and the proof is a phone call George controls: he dials a number Hagbard can't see. He learns the office has been bombed and Joe is missing.

## Mechanism notes
- **C (front), at full strength.** The layers come at three scales. In the memos, each source's Illuminati is a mask for the next (Webster says the anarcho-communist utopia was only a mask). In the deduction, communist gives way to fascist, then Satanist, then Eschaton, and Saul then calls Fernando Poo itself a false front. In the cast, the JAMs and the LDD are fronts with absurd names. Each layer is held as the full answer for about one scene. What breaks it is always a *second person* with one more fact: Barney's slip of paper, the W.H. letter, Hagbard's admission. The hero never breaks a layer by thinking alone. Robison's lodge degrees (memo 12) state the front structure as lore, and Dillinger enacts it by telling Joe he is not cleared yet.
- **The clown is part of the plan.** The W.H. letter is the organisation's best cover: a calm, helpful reply saying the whole thing is a campus joke. Hagbard says it outright: the yellow submarine and the silly name work because everyone already thinks Illuminati-hunters are crackpots. This is SDF's clown-beside-every-question with the clown run by the conspiracy itself.
- **A (gate), weakly.** Joe won't commit until his own tests come back (Trip 3, the Tlaloc note). They are checks on objects (a tooth, a date), which James could legitimately run.
- **B (canary), reversed.** Each recruit is told a different version and watched (Hagbard's test): the canary is aimed at the hero.
- **Fifth: the diagnosis.** The organisation's reply to an investigator is a file saying he is ill and was never who he thinks he is. It discredits without killing, and it reads his cleverness as a symptom. For SDF: James's nightly-counted questions could come back to him as a document that reads him as the anomaly.

## Quotes
- "they keep creating masks so all sorts of scapegoat groups will get the blame for being the 'real' Illuminati" (Trip 3): the front stated outright.
- "If you suspect either of these groups of being involved in something sinister, you have probably just fallen for one of their put-ons." (Trip 4): the clown as a document.
- "We came on like right-wing paranoids, at first, to see how you'd react. It was a test." (Trip 4): the earlier layer revealed as bait.
- "Behind the mask is a man and behind the man is another mask." (Trip 3): the engine in one line.

## Flags
- Real people named as complicit: Bernard Barker (CIA) on the grassy knoll; Stanton behind Lincoln's death; Wilson poisoned; Orwell killed; John McCone on Malik's list; Billy Graham's face on the summoned being. SDF must not do this.
- Invented hidden meanings in real books (Moby-Dick as a Sabbah text, the Silmarillion as counter-propaganda). Not edits, but close to SDF's rule.
- The racial and sexual shock material in Saul's ordeal is this book's tone, not SDF's.
