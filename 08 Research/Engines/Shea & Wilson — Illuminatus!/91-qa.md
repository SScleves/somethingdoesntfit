# Shea & Wilson, *The Illuminatus! Trilogy* — questions an SDF agent will ask

> Answers point to the section notes in this folder (`01-trips-1-to-2.md` … `09-appendices.md`) and, where relevant, to a v8.2 chapter by title. The map is `00-map.md`. Everything about SDF here is a PROPOSAL; Santi's decisions are PLACEHOLDER.

---

## The verdict on C

**1. Does C, the front, work as an engine or only as lore?**
Both, and the book shows the difference. When Saul sits in Malik's flat reading memos (Trips 1–4), each layer is broken by the next and the pages turn, but nothing he does is changed by it (`01-trips-1-to-2.md`, `02-trips-3-to-4.md`). When someone *acts* on a layer as if it were the bottom, C becomes an engine: George stays on the submarine (`01`), Drake provokes the hidden layer (`04-trip-6.md`), Danny ends up defending the conspiracy (`06-trip-8.md`), Joe bombs his own office (`07-trip-9.md`). See map section 4, verdict (a).

**2. So was ADDENDUM 05 wrong?**
Right about Tier 1, incomplete as a rule. In Tier 1 every front was something the hero learned. Illuminatus shows the missing ingredient: the hero acts on the wrong layer first and pays. *Libra* (the decoy layer) and *Declare* (the hero wears fronts) had already said C can be done; this book says what makes it move.

**3. What breaks a layer?**
An object (the jail chapel, the tattoo, a film, a gun), a second person with one more fact, the teller's own admission, a clearance detail ("Mama isn't cleared for this"), or a promotion (`01`, `03-trip-5.md`, `05-trip-7.md`, `06-trip-8.md`). The hero's theories are always wrong ("Saul was convinced. He was also wrong," Trip 1). He can catch a flaw in an object, as Saul spots the missing mole on a faked slide (`02`), but he doesn't deduce the next layer. That matches SDF's brief: insights happen to James, and reveals are objects.

**4. The notes say "never by deduction." Is that strictly true?**
No. In Trip 9 Joe tells Hagbard "I read all the clues… I know you're the fifth Illuminatus Primus" (`07-trip-9.md`), and the Trip 5 note counts "the hero's doubt" as one way a layer breaks (`03-trip-5.md`). The pattern holds for the middle of the book. The one real deduction comes at the end, and the hero then refuses to act on it. For James, keep deduction for the reading-room offer, which he refuses, and let every earlier layer break on paper.

**5. What does "true for the rank" mean, and does it match SDF's helper tiers?**
Each layer is what a person at that level is told, and it is true enough at that level: Flanagan is "only a Fourth-degree Illuminatus," George will "become a deacon," Hagbard lies "because the human mind can only accept a little of the truth at a time" (`03-trip-5.md`). That ladder is vertical. SDF's four helper words (honeypot, disaster recovery, conservation, heritage; v7 What was on the phone) sit side by side. They are compartments, not rungs. SDF's real ladder is James's own climb: asker, then program staff (The drill), then the annex role (The money), then the schedule (The schedule), then the offer of a seat (The reading room, continued).

**6. What is the "planted resting place"?**
A middle layer built so the investigator has somewhere to stop. Hagbard confesses it: "I sent Jim Cartwright to you with that yarn about three conspiracies" (`08-trip-10.md`). Gruad hands out five rival histories for agents to plant (`07`). SDF already has it twice: "we leave them findable" (the opening) and "You found the seats and thought they were the point. They all do" (v8 The final conversation). Map 5.3 makes James's use of it cost something.

**7. Why does a front need a floor?**
Because if every layer is a front, the reader stops caring which is true. The appendices show it happening: Gimmel stacks theory on theory down to a drugged misreading of a misprint, then a joke (`09-appendices.md`). Trip 10 avoids it by landing on a method said plainly (the power to say No; fear of death as the lever) while the documents stay incomplete (`08`). SDF's floor is what happens when we die, reached on the floor in The night he stops being afraid. The final conversation must keep the old man's film claims as his claims, set against that floor.

**8. Can James wear a front himself?**
Yes, if it is made of true things and costs him. *Declare* 5.3 (the sheets in the program's format) and *Libra* 5.4 (a decoy ticket) are already proposed. This book adds the cheapest kind: a front the organization has already written for him, the wellbeing referral in map 5.2. It also points out one he already wears, the seats-as-lifeboat line on every sheet (map 5.3). Chips's rule is why a true front is enough: "They won't believe it, but they'll think you believe it. That's good enough" (`08`).

**9. What fronts must James never wear?**
Anything that needs impersonation, an invented task, a staged ordeal or drugs. Hagbard's whole method (kidnap, a faked-porn ordeal, layered lies "for your own good"; `02`, `05`) makes the hero a handler. Ana's sleeping pills at The dinner are hers, improvised, and stay that way.

## The new devices

**10. What exactly is the "diagnosis"?**
The answer to an investigator is a file saying he is ill. Saul is drugged and told he is a Trenton patrolman called Muldoon, and his skepticism is treated as a symptom (`02`). Marsh is ridiculed until he disappears and his file is closed as "suicide during drug psychosis" (`03`). Leakers are filed as nuts who "get it all scrambled" (`04-trip-6.md`). It discredits without killing, which is SDF's rule for the organization.

**11. Who runs the diagnosis in the book?**
Not only the enemy. Saul's "doctor" turns out to be Joe Malik (`02`), and the next Trip calls it deprogramming (`03`). So the diagnosis can also recruit: it breaks the old self so a new allegiance fits. Marsh's file is the hostile version. For SDF, use the hostile version only, and keep it kind on the surface.

**12. Is the diagnosis already in SDF?**
As a rule, yes. Els's folder says a correct reader is "agreed with and then reclassified" (v8.2 The math; v6 The math word for word: "agreement, followed by reclassification"). Nothing on the page reclassifies anyone. Map 5.1 uses it on the dead professor (the neighbor's "he wasn't well") and 5.2 on James (the referral).

**13. What is the "kind counter-history"?**
Mama Sutra (Trip 8, `06`). She's kind and credible and already knows what Danny is carrying. She gives him a full history in which the Illuminati are the persecuted party of reason and every leak against them is propaganda. The tell: she asks whether his memos "portrayed them in an unfavorable light" before he has described them. His findings are left alone and given a frame that points the other way. Map 5.1 gives SDF one, the professor's neighbor, whom v7 What was on the phone already lists under *Conservation*.

**14. How does the counter-history break?**
On a clearance detail, cheaply: "be sure to ask for Miss Mao, not Mama Sutra. Mama isn't cleared for this" (`06`). The grand account becomes one ring of five. In map 5.1 the break is a line on paper James already holds, *When a reader notices*.

**15. What's the "clown run by the conspiracy itself," and does SDF need it?**
The W.H. letter, a calm reply saying the whole thing is a campus joke (`02`); Gruad's five planted histories (`07`); Naismith selling "Illuminati" cons he doesn't believe are true (`05`). SDF has it already in the thermostat (The band) and the polite forum correction (Leiden). Nothing to add.

**16. What about the reasonable skeptic as cover?**
Hauptmann dismisses conspiracy theories as unscientific with twenty-three dead Illuminati in the room (`08`). A sensible man next to the real question does a clown's job. In SDF it is Pieter's "leave it" and the old man's "what they are in a room is nothing" (v7 The reading room, continued). Already there.

**17. "The door that was allowed": should James learn a door he chose to walk through was opened for him?**
The book does it (the rescue "according to plan," `01`; the film shown to Joe with the door left unlocked, `05`). For SDF it's already proposed: *Libra* 5.2 (pulled versus handed), Pynchon 5.1 (the errand that signs him in). Don't add a third.

## Comedy and orientation

**18. How does the book stay funny while the stakes go up to nuclear war?**
Five habits. Every horror gets a deflation in the same paragraph ("the President shot / 'Let's toast some marshmallows'," `07`). The jokes target institutions, never victims (`04`). The atrocity passages carry no jokes, and the comedy stands on either side of them (`03`). Revelations come in a comic voice without the stakes dropping (`06`). Each layer gets sillier as it gets more frightening: the same paragraph about Fernando Poo runs for Washington, Moscow and Peking (`01`).

**19. What is the one-line tone rule?**
Mavis: "It's not true unless it makes you laugh, but you don't understand until it makes you weep" (`03`). It suits SDF's "funny to the end" with Buddy poisoned and the other ten. James's voice already does the first half, and the corridor and the vet do the second.

**20. Which SDF chapters most need the pairing rule?**
The ones where the organization is at its worst and James isn't in the room: A street, night; Leiden, Friday; The leak; and Monday's "why they don't kill him." Illuminatus's version is evil as a dull office (FDR demoted to "Monotony Monitor," `07`). SDF already speaks this language ("it's a budgeting thing," Utrecht, 1993; "a question of air," v7 The reading room, continued). Keep the jokes on the institution, never on Henk, Noor or the dog.

**21. How does the reader stay oriented across so many jumps?**
Always *when*, not always *who*. Every cut carries a date, a clock, or "while" tied to an event the reader already knows ("On April 25, while Carmel looted Maldonado's safe," `07`). Parallel clocks ("10:30 P.M., Ingolstadt time," `03`). Parentheses hold the present frame during a flashback (`06`). Refrains and hymn lines work as section breaks (`02`). Each voice has a fixed idiom, so you know who is speaking before the name (`06`). A ticking clock runs under long talk: the seismograph climbing grade by grade (`08`).

**22. Where does orientation fail?**
When the point of view slips mid-scene: Saul into Muldoon's "I," a nameless "I" who turns out to be Atlanta Hope, George moving from third to first person mid-paragraph. Each slip costs the reader about a page (`01`). SDF's single first person, with villain-alone chapters set apart, avoids most of this. Don't let a villain chapter open mid-thought without a time.

**23. Can the fixed-idiom trick settle PLACEHOLDER #4 (the car park)?**
It gives a middle option. Keep the handler unnamed but give him one phrase the reader has heard from Julian (v6 A car park already has the flask: "he'd said he was a flask man"). The reader knows who he is and James doesn't, which is dread without anyone acting, as with Hagbard's call that George never hears (`01`). It isn't an action by James, so it isn't a map proposal.

**24. How long can a lore dump run without stalling?**
The memos never stall at 150 to 700 words a piece, because each one contradicts the last (`02`). Mama Sutra holds for 5,000 words because the bourbon is a gauge and the account ends on Hagbard, back in the plot (`06`). Malaclypse stalls at 3,000 and is saved only by a physical proof and an order (`04`). The rule matches the v8 brief: any speech over about 150 words gets broken by the other person, a dog or a phone.

## The proposals

**25. Which map proposal would you take first?**
5.2, reclassified. It cashes a rule SDF already prints (The math) and gives A car park's "stopped asking out loud" a cause James chose. It also answers verdict (b) with the cheapest legal front. It costs one scene with Pieter and a few lines with the counselor.

**26. Does 5.1 (the neighbor) overload The math?**
It might. The math is about 2,800 words in v8.2 and already holds Els, the folder, the arithmetic and the breakfast. The alternative is the end of The week it vanished, after Beard Man's account of the death (PLACEHOLDER). Either way the scene should be short: a garden, one cup, one question she asks too early.

**27. Does 5.1 decide Santi's line to Els (PLACEHOLDER #5)?**
No. It gives the line a job: James has walked in with the neighbor's word "illness" and has to undo it on his way out. What he says stays Santi's.

**28. Are 5.2 and *Declare* 5.1 compatible?**
Yes, in sequence. Under the referral James goes quiet, and the handler punishes it ("out loud, which is worse," A car park). In Leiden, Noor says a loud reader is a story they can manage (v6 Leiden), and *Declare* 5.1 has him go loud on a timer. He tries both fronts and each is read. Pynchon 5.4 (photographed among cranks at a funded event) is a third discredit, and that's one too many. Pick one.

**29. Does 5.3 break the deal in The final conversation?**
Only slightly, and on purpose. The deal is James's silence about the organization. Telling Pieter "nobody's kept, the ones on it included" repeats the old man's own claim to the one helper who was on the list. PLACEHOLDER: whether Anneke hears of it, and whether it costs James anything more than knowing he said it.

**30. Should James ever run a ladder of lies the way Hagbard does?**
No. That's the first thing in Do not take. Twenty minutes is the counter-model: every object on the cloth, "I don't know" said six times. 5.3 lets James notice he used the organization's version once, and give the bottom layer plainly to one person.

**31. Is there a canary to borrow?**
Only one, aimed at the reader: "you have lost track of Joe Malik's mysterious dogs by now" (Lamed, `09`). A thread is planted in Trip 1 and checked at the very end. Section 09 suggests a List entry whose margin note points back at something the reader was allowed to forget. In SDF the dogs are never lost track of, so pick a different object (PLACEHOLDER).

**32. Is there a misprint idea SDF can use?**
Gimmel's doctrine grows from one misprint in a fictional book (`09`): a corrupted edition *creating* belief, SDF's premise turned inside out. It's clean to borrow because the book is invented. Don't attach it to a real one.

## Rules and risks

**33. What exactly can't come over?**
Real people as complicit (Kennedy's killers named, Hoover, FDR, Hitler, Rothschild, Hegel; `01`–`09`, Flags). Real organizations as fronts (the AMA, the Federal Reserve, the Pentagon, the John Birch Society). Invented secret meanings for real books, films or the Tarot (*Moby-Dick*, *Gunga Din*, *The Manchurian Candidate* as "a mind leak," Crowley chapter 23 as a spell). And the book's slurs and sexual shock material.

**34. Is Dutch Schultz's last words (a real document read as code) allowed in SDF terms?**
At the edge (`04`). It's a real text re-read, not edited, which SDF's rule permits only as a character's claim, the way the final conversation treats the films. Don't build a plot step on a real document's "hidden" meaning.

**35. Does anything here change the four mechanisms' standing in ADDENDUM 05?**
C moves from "does not work as a Part Two engine" to "works when James acts on a layer and pays, breaks on paper, and has a floor." A stays the gap (Illuminatus barely uses it). B is confirmed as something the organization runs on James (tailored versions, `02`). D: nothing to build. Note the book's D inverted: Joe gets every key and declines (`07`), as James declines the lower door.

**36. Any contradictions in the notes I should know about?**
Four. (1) "Never by deduction" (`02`, `07`) against Joe's "I read all the clues" (`07`) and "the hero's doubt" as a break (`03`); see Q4. (2) `08` says the fifth Primus is "still unnamed" at the start of Trip 10, but in `07` Joe has already named Hagbard. Read it as Joe knowing and George not yet. (3) Cartwright appears on the Illuminati side in Trip 2 ("We let Celine's crowd take Dorn," `01`) and as Hagbard's messenger in Trip 10 (`08`). That's deliberate in the book, but cite with care. (4) The diagnosis is called the organization's reply (`02`) though the doctor is Joe; see Q11.
