## Why it grips
- **Every layer comes with its own doubt.** Hagbard gives five reasons the Illuminati have not taken over the world, and George points out "Those reasons contradict each other". Iggy's lecture takes itself apart in the middle. Joe's special-effects suspicion is the strongest argument in the section, and nobody answers it. The reader always holds two versions.
- **Order of telling against order of events.** Iggy's debunking (1969) comes *after* the Atlantis battle (1973–74), so the reader has already "seen" Atlantis when told the history was invented in 1776. Layers are undercut by placement, never refuted.
- **Keeping the reader oriented.** Date tags in parentheses ("This was in '72"). Simon's dollar-bill lecture is picked up in three years, each named ("Simon asks in nineteen sixty-nine... Simon shouts in nineteen seventy"). One clock: "10:30 P.M., Ingolstadt time". A counting chant ("big rhinoceroses") runs across cuts until we learn it is Sherri's. Joe's "flash forwards" turn the structure into a symptom of the initiation.
- **The scene that does most work: Drake's study.** A naive messenger, a parchment, an expert with a jeweler's glass who will "know if they are not" real, and a contract with "considerations of equal value yet to come" added. His only asset is the truth, which is that he has nothing more.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The conspiracy sets the jobs. Project Tethys (raising the temple) causes the sea battle, the battle produces the statues, the statues buy Drake. Flanagan's scene shows the enemy as an organisation with ranks and shortages. He is "only a Fourth-degree Illuminatus and not fully instructed in the history". Both sides keep their own people on partial layers; George will "become a deacon" after this mission. Hagbard gives the conspiracy's theory of itself: five leaders make 120 possible coalitions, and a random element is added on purpose. They recruited both ecology leaders and polluters "to create ambiguity", so anyone describing them sounds paranoid. That is the SDF clown principle, stated by a character.

- **Marsh's book** (about 600 words). A document in a detective's hands, page 123, delivered "in seventeen minutes". A discredited book confirms the deepest layer, and nobody explains it to him.

## Mechanism notes
- **C (front), the core of this Trip.** Six layers. (1) 1776 and the dollar bill (Simon, a lecture with a prop). (2) Atlantis, 30,000 years (Hagbard). An object too big for the old story breaks layer 1, and the teller admits he staged it for George's level. (3) Joe's special-effects doubt. It comes from the hero, is rewarded, and is left open. (4) Iggy: everything before 1776 is invented, "Or maybe we're just a front for the Illuminati". The teller breaks his own layer on a timer. (5) Mavis: the lloigor, a being kept "inside the Pentagon". Saul supplies it himself ("I've got it"), so it does not feel handed over. (6) Marsh's book, an outside discredited document, confirms layer 5. Layers hold from minutes (Iggy) to whole Trips (1776). They break in one of four ways: an artifact, the teller's admission, the hero's doubt, or a promotion. **New for SDF:** the front is a training ladder, and each version is true *for that rank*. Proposal: someone who gave James a document later says calmly that it is the version handed out at his level. The cost: James has already acted on it in front of someone. - **Discredit, not prevent.** Marsh is ridiculed until he disappears, and his file is closed as "suicide during drug psychosis". Emperor Norton's money circulated *because* he was mad, while Spooner's sane bank was suppressed.
- **Cases closed by category.** Oedipuski's death goes uninvestigated because it is "connected with God's Lightning". The narrator says what nobody traced (the spiked KCUF punch). Close to SDF's nightly job: the system files it away and nobody decides anything.
- **Fifth: the book reviews itself.** Wildeblood pans "this mishmash" without reading it and lists every fault a reader might find. It disarms the objection in advance.

**Comedy under escalation.** The Mothers March gags (Polio, Psoriasis, Hernia, Dandruff, then a whole dollar for Muzak) run under the numerology. Drake farts on the stairs after the deal. "Pajamas? Aisle seven" answers Simon's revelation. Waterhouse says "Just to fuck you up, man." The atrocity passages carry no jokes; the comedy stands either side of them, and the Fire Drill turns a joke into evidence. Mavis states the rule (Quotes).
