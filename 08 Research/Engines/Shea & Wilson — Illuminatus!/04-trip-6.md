## Why it grips
- **One document, read again at every depth.** Dutch Schultz's last words open the trip as phone threats (each quote a line "that would really sink us all"). Then they become thesis material, then the novelist's occult reading ("the Chinaman and Hitler's commander"), then the Illuminati formula to summon the lloigor. Last, in Drake's dream, Jung decodes them as acronyms and mocks the whole game ("Child's play! Glasspielen!"). The reader decodes alongside Drake, so Jung's mockery lands on the reader too.
- **The interrupted card.** The janitor hands Drake the card ("The Grand Master of the Eastern United States is ready to talk to you"), and the scene breaks off for twenty-three years: Malaclypse in Chicago, Talbot's suicide, Drake's last dinner. Only then does it resume, with "And he turned the card over". The reveal is an object held in his hand for about 3,000 words.
- **Two deaths cut together.** Drake's end is cut line by line against the Dutchman's hospital transcript. The document that started the hunt narrates the man who solved it being consumed.
- **How the reader stays oriented.** Each cut opens with a date or an elapsed time ("April 25 began", "in 1936", "twenty-three years later", "In present time again"). Cuts happen on a repeated word ("Holy Cleopatra!" becomes "Kleopatra?"). Simultaneous action goes in parentheses. Refrains link the strands: "Camptown Races", "fun is fun", "unify the forces", Jung on power.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The conspiracy is the plot's engine in Drake's strand and the reason anyone moves in the present strand. Drake cannot do business with the visible layer (the Mafia) until the invisible one answers, so he provokes it: Lovecraft, the bank, "Will the All-Seeing Eye please look in my direction?" The conspiracy answers by recruiting him, which is the cost: he becomes its satrap. His defection to the Discordians kills him and puts George on the road. In the present, Drake's death and the April 25 killings send George back to Hagbard; Saul's disappearance pulls Rebecca to the hotel.

## How the lore gets in
- **Phone calls (Drake to the Mafia), about 2,000 words.** Lore as threats: each quoted line gets a reaction (cold sweat, a mass said, soldiers sent). No stall.
- **The novelist's lecture (Thule, Golden Dawn, Vril), about 1,500 words.** His evasion is part of the scene, and cut-aways (Hitler screaming, Luciano's lawyer tracing Drake) keep it moving.
- **Lovecraft, about 1,200 words.** A debate, not a lecture: two warnings exchanged. Four men vanish off-page at the end.
- **Malaclypse to Joe (Jesus, Melos, neophiles, transcendental illumination), about 3,000 words.** This one stalls. It is rescued by a proof Joe performs with his own arm (through Malaclypse's side), by the joke that he might be "doing a two-thousand-year-old man routine", and by an order (test AUM).
- **Maldonado and Dillinger's counter-histories, about 1,000 words each, as dialogue.** Each opens by declaring the last layer "bullshit".
- **The real Francis King book, one paragraph, a document.** George opens it at random. One sentence of real history makes a new problem ("Several Illuminati?").

## Mechanism notes
- **C (front), shown in its pure form.** Each layer is delivered by a speaker who calls the one before it a front. Maldonado: "Adam just acted as her front"; the Haute Vente was so secret "Mazzini was a member all his life and never knew". Dillinger: "This Bavarian stuff is all bullshit… They're mostly Englishmen." Malaclypse: the Illuminati used "Plato's Academy as a front." Each layer lasts one conversation. What breaks a layer is always the next speaker's confidence, never evidence. The register also flips at each step: gangsters, then the occult, then a dull bureaucrat ("No mystical trappings"), then a monster in the Pentagon. For SDF (proposal): break each layer on a *document* rather than a speaker, and change register each time. The house style rule would read first as a publisher's quirk, then as a budget line, then as a continuity procedure.
- **Discredit, not prevent, stated four times.** The pentagon shape: "They will be dismissed as superstitious cranks." Lovecraft is safe because his lore is "published as fiction". Maldonado files the leaks under madness: nuts "get it all scrambled". The FBI dismisses the widow's correct identification of Albert Stern as "a no-account junkie" who confessed falsely. Each dismissal is shown from the inside, which is why it works as plot.
- **The clown beside the real question.** Canvera: a crank's phone line, mutating weekly, ends with an invitation priced at 5⁵ dollars and a shooting. The narrator then reveals the death "had nothing to do with the Illuminati": it was a sex grudge and a Mafia favour. The reader has been made the paranoid. Wildeblood reviews this very novel as "a fairy tale for paranoids", unread.
- **B (canary).** Hagbard's gorillas: he claims they were men in suits to test whether Joe trusts his senses or an authority. Passing it means disbelieving the guide.
- **Comedy under escalation.** Death camps, Hiroshima and the plague sit beside farce (the VSR marching song, a Vice President met through a personal ad) with no change of tone. The jokes are about the institutions, not the victims.

## Flags
- Real people and institutions named as complicit: Wilson "hoodwinked" into the Federal Reserve, the National Security Act of 1947 and the Pentagon as Illuminati designs, Cecil Rhodes, Luciano/Capone/Lepke, Hitler as a "medium". Real figures in invented scenes (Lovecraft, Jung, an unnamed Nobel novelist [Thomas Mann, from memory, not from the text]). SDF must not do this.
- Lovecraft is said to have quoted Levi's formula "not in full" on purpose. That is an invented intention behind a real text, which comes close to SDF's ban on invented edits to real books.
- Dutch Schultz's last words are a real document [from memory, not from the text] re-read as code. Allowed (read differently, not edited), but the exact edge.
