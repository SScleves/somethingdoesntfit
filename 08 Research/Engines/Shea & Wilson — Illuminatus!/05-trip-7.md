## Why it grips
- **Each answer is broken by the next scene.** The Vegas strand is a run of solutions, each corrected within pages: the FBI theory (Mocenigo had two women), the blackboard chart of the two descriptions, the pimp's first-person panic, Filiarisus's computer account. The reader is a step ahead of the agents, having watched the Midget and Naismith invent their women, and laughs at an investigation built on lies told for small private reasons.
- **The right answer is the trap.** Filiarisus gives the section's most honest speech: punishment makes witnesses lie, so a government cannot learn the truth. He uses it to argue for suspending the Bill of Rights, and only then does the narrator give his order name.
- **Orientation by hinge lines.** The book jumps narrator, time and grammatical person (George becomes "I" partway through the Mavis scene; Joe's strand opens in first person and goes back to third). It stays readable because each jump is pinned to a time marker and an echo: "And, twenty-five hours earlier"; "And twenty-three hours later", with the Midget's exact words playing on Knight's earphones. The reader always knows *when*, even when not *who*.
- **The comedy is bodily and never stops.** The apocalypse moves forward next to a coffee urn the Midget escapes in, an FBI bug under a CIA desk, a President on Demerol ("Tree that coon"), and Joe's bladder after the end of Atlantis ("Piss on Atlantis"). Every escalation has a pratfall next to it, and the pratfall never cancels the stakes.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
It puts people in rooms by using them. Each Vegas witness is drawn in because a planted plague passed through his body, and each self-protective lie pushes the investigation toward the Illuminati's goal. The conspiracy prevents nothing; punishment makes people lie, and lies make the government panic. For Joe it works the other way: the hidden board *summons* him and *gives* him lore, with a condition (he may only hint at it in *Confrontation*). For Otto it demands a killing; the Morituri "bomb nest" turns out to be an empty flat, a front that covers his shooting of Flanagan. FUCKUP's closing briefing then joins the strands into one plan: Fernando Poo was a feint to make heads of state jumpy, Vegas is the "ace in the hole", Ingolstadt the strike. The band poster Joe pinned up earlier shows four Illuminati Primi.

## How the lore gets in
- **Hagbard and Mavis on the Robot (George), about 6,600 words, a stoned conversation.** It moves because two physical acts break it up, each with a cost (the gun; Coin through the panel). Stretches stall (the property dialectic), and the book admits it: Mavis is "stoned, too; otherwise she'd be explaining it better".
- **The fnords and the saucer (Joe), about 1,400 words, first-person discovery.** The lore arrives as an object he can test: the *New York Times* at breakfast, then a check of the advertisements (no fnords there). Holding a document and testing a hunch against it is the James move.
- **"When Atlantis Ruled the Earth", about 5,600 words, a film told in present-tense synopsis.** The biggest dump in the section, interrupted once: Joe "sat up with a jolt" when Gruad, the first Illuminatus, turns out to love novelty, which contradicts what he was taught. It ends in the bathroom gag, and holds because the reader knows a costumed board staged it and chose to show it.
- **FUCKUP's forecast, about 1,000 words, a computer dialogue.** Lore as a probability list ("fourteenth in order of probability"), cut off at "The end."

## Mechanism notes
- **C (front) is the backbone.** Six layers, each held as the full answer for a different time:
  1. *Mocenigo's sex life* (FBI): about a page, broken by the chart.
  2. *Carmel the immune carrier* (Filiarisus): three paragraphs, broken by the narrator naming him Gruad. A true answer used as cover for the move it justifies.
  3. *Illuminati founded in Bavaria, 1776*: held since early in the book, broken by the film ("Ancient" means "a lot older than 1776").
  4. *Saucers as a government hoax*: one exchange. Hagbard's real saucer hides *inside* the debunk.
  5. *Fernando Poo as the crisis*: broken by FUCKUP; it was a feint.
  6. *George's fear of homosexuality*: seen as "a false front" over the fear of the Robot.
  The rule underneath: a layer breaks when someone *shows* the protagonist something (chart, film, saucer ride, gun), never by argument alone.
- **The clown beside the real question.** Naismith sells a "vast Illiminati conspiracy" and the John Dillinger Died For You Society as cons, never suspecting they are true; Mavis then tells George, deadpan, that Dillinger is alive and shot Kennedy. The truth hides among frauds whose sellers disbelieve it. SDF's organisation funds exactly this.
- **The authoritative edit.** The Midget changes one line on a store sign and nobody questions it, because it is signed by management. It is SDF's "given"/"arose" principle as a joke: the edit survives because of the authority of its frame, not because it is plausible.
- **Fifth: disclosure as recruitment.** The board *chooses* to show Joe the film, then leaves him alone with the door unlocked. A door opened by choice, with a price attached. For James: someone hands him the next layer with a condition and walks out.

## Flags
- Real people named as complicit: Hitler, Himmler and Bormann carry out an Illuminati-arranged mass sacrifice, and Bormann is called an Illuminati Primus (FUCKUP). Real novels (*The Monk*, *Melmoth*, *Frankenstein*, *Otranto*, de Sade) are put down to the psychic after-effect of Weishaupt's rites: a hidden cause behind real books, not an edit to them. SDF must copy neither.
- No invented edits on real books; the Midget's edits are to invented signs and memos.
