# Orwell, *Nineteen Eighty-Four* — questions an SDF agent will ask

> Answers point at the section notes in this folder (`01-part-one-a.md`, `02-section-two.md`, `03-section-three.md`, `04-section-four.md`) and at the map (`00-map.md`). Chapter cites are Part.chapter. Everything about SDF is a proposal; Santi's decisions are PLACEHOLDER. 11 Nov 2026.

## The book's engine

**1. Why does Winston keep going when he believes from page one that he'll be caught?**
Because stopping changes nothing: the thought is already the crime, and tearing out the diary page would not undo it (I.i–ii). Orwell sets the punishment as certain, so the tension moves from *whether* to *when and through whom*. See `01`, Movements row 1 and "Why it grips."

**2. Isn't Winston as passive as James, since everything is handed to him?**
Nearly everything is delivered (the note, the route, the address, the book), but each delivery needs a risk he takes himself: sitting at Julia's table, taking the train, renting the room, ringing O'Brien's bell (II.i–viii). His own initiatives (the pub, the canteen) fail. That is the difference from James in *The drill* and *The money*, who receives and risks nothing. See `02`, "Why it grips."

**3. Where does the "why can't he stop" pressure change character?**
At the arrest (II.x). Before it, motive drives him: he alone remembers, he wants an organization Julia won't believe in. After it, there is no choice left, and each answer under questioning becomes the next question. See `00-map.md` section 2 and `03`, Movements.

**4. What does the Brotherhood actually do in the plot?**
In Part One nothing; it exists only as the Party's broadcast (`01`, "How the conspiracy moves"). In Part Two it is the one thing Julia can't give Winston, so wanting it pushes him out of the safe room toward O'Brien (`02`). In Part Three it may not exist: "That, Winston, you will never know" (III.ii, `03`).

**5. When does the reader learn the trap was the state's?**
In stages: Charrington's changed face and the telescreen behind the picture (II.x), O'Brien's "They got me a long time ago" (III.i), and "I wrote it" (III.iii). Each reveal re-reads every earlier door as opened from the other side. See `03`, "Why it grips."

**6. Were there clues before the arrest?**
Yes, planted as kindness or good craft: Charrington offers the room unasked and mentions "two entries to the house"; O'Brien writes his address in view of a telescreen and knows the rhyme's last line (II.iv–viii, `02`). Right before the arrest, the stove is empty though Julia filled it, and the clock and the light disagree (II.x, `03`).

**7. How does Orwell keep it funny?**
Ogilvy as an office job with a rival (I.iv), Julia falling asleep over Goldstein (II.ix), Ampleforth jailed over a rhyme for "rod" and Parsons proud of the daughter who denounced him (III.i), the bootlace quota and the comma sub-committee (III.vi). See `01`, `03`, `04`. SDF's "funny to the end" rule has a precedent at the bleakest point.

## Rewriting records vs. SDF's editing machine

**8. How does Winston's job compare with SDF's editing machine?**
Both use the language of error as cover. Winston's orders call every forgery a correction of "slips, errors, misprints" (I.iv); SDF's machine leaves typos in so no one checks (*The sentence*, *Leiden, 1931*). Orwell's editing is total and works because memory is controlled too; SDF's can't control memory, so it must count and visit. See `01` and `03`, Mechanism notes.

**9. Should SDF add errata slips or "misprint" labels to its edits?**
No. It would contradict the plan's design (no "second impression, corrected," typos left in, *The sentence*). And the error-language idea is already everywhere in SDF: "diagnostic artefact" (*Leave it*), the duplicate ticket (*The week it vanished*), *When a Reader Notices* (*The math*), "No impact observed" (v8 *Leave it*). See `00-map.md` section 6.

**10. What is the Ogilvy scene and why does it matter?**
Winston invents a dead war hero to replace an unperson, worries about cross-referencing, and is sure his version will beat Tillotson's (I.iv). It is about 3,500 words of lore that does not stall, because he has a task with a result and is proud of it. It is the model for proposal 1 (*The drill*, *The money*). See `01`, "Why it grips."

**11. Can the editor prove his own edit?**
No: "it would never be possible for me to prove it, even when I did the falsification myself" (II.v). The one proof he ever held, a photo, he destroyed himself (I.vii), and it was proof with no venue anyway. That is SDF's "discredit rather than prevent" in pure form. See `01` and `02`, Quotes.

**12. Is there a rival-versions idea SDF could use?**
Yes, lightly: a dozen people may write competing versions of one forgery and an unseen senior picks one (I.iv). Two printings that differ in different ways could be competing drafts. Not proposed in the map; possible texture for *Leiden* or *The math*. See `01`, Mechanism notes.

**13. Who is Ampleforth and why is he flagged?**
A poet-editor jailed because a rhyme wouldn't let him make a change (III.i): the editor caught by his material. He works on a "definitive edition" of Kipling, an in-world edit of a real poet. SDF must not copy that. See `03`, Flags.

## The forbidden book as the state's trap

**14. What exactly is the trap?**
The Party writes the opposition's book, keeps its enemy on screen daily, and leaves the way in open (a café that's "ill-omened," not forbidden; a room with no telescreen). Whoever reads the book and acts on it has identified himself. See `00-map.md` section 4; `01` and `03`, Mechanism notes.

**15. Is Goldstein's book false?**
No, and that is the point. O'Brien calls it true as description and "nonsense" as program (III.iii). Winston trusts it because it tells him what he already knows (II.ix). True bait proves nothing about why it was left; the question James should reach is "why could I find it," not "is it real." See `03`.

**16. How does that map onto "we leave them findable"?**
The seat list in *The money* is accurate, like the book. Proposal 2 makes James ask who granted the role that opened it, then read it anyway and see *read (annex)* in the next payload. Already partly on the page in the opening line of *The reading room*. See `00-map.md` section 5.

**17. Who hands Winston the book?**
A stranger, mid-riot, during the Hate Week switch of enemy: "Excuse me, I think you've dropped your brief-case" (II.ix). O'Brien arranged it (II.viii, the brief-case swap). Note 01's memory aside, that O'Brien hands it over, is contradicted by note 03's reading of the text. See `03`, Quotes.

**18. How is the handover set up?**
A deliberate misprint in Winston's work queue is the summons (II.viii): "one of the messages among your morning's work will contain a misprinted word." The error the system tolerates becomes the invitation. See `02`, Mechanism notes. In SDF the forum phrasing already does this (*Leiden*, *The sheets*).

**19. What happens to Winston after the trap closes?**
He is released with a sinecure and a reserved café table, and nobody sits near him (III.vi). The state doesn't need to stop him; a broken man in public proves the trap works. See `04`, "How the conspiracy moves." Proposal 5 puts one in *The other ten*.

**20. Why does the state keep Goldstein alive?**
So the heresy can be "defeated, discredited, ridiculed" every day and never ended (III.iii). A discredited opposition is worth more than none. SDF already has this as the thermostat (*The band*) and the farmed forum (*The gorilla mask*), so don't add it again. See `03`.

## The four candidate fixes

**21. Does *1984* support A, the gate?**
Negatively first: Winston has no gate and trusts O'Brien on one look (I.i, "there was no way of doing so"). The catechism (II.viii) looks like a gate but only records. The dial (III.ii) is a true gate with a cost of being wrong; take the shape without the pain. See `01`, `02`, `03` Mechanism notes; `00-map.md` section 4.

**22. What does the catechism actually collect?**
The one refusal. Winston agrees to murder and acid but won't separate from Julia, and O'Brien notes it: "It is necessary for us to know everything" (II.viii). Room 101 later uses exactly that. Proposal 3 makes James's refusal (no weekend without Buddy) the source of *companion* in *The schedule*. See `02`.

**23. Does it support B, the canary?**
Yes, run on the hero: the book is invented by the state to see who believes it, and the coded mention of Syme watches who reads it as a signal (II.vi). *On land* already uses B once and drops it (PIPELINE.md). See `02` and `03`.

**24. What is the "restored tell"?**
Winston leaves a grain of dust on his diary (I.ii); in III.iv he learns they "had carefully replaced" it. The defense against a canary is to put it back. Proposal 4 gives James a dog hair on the thin book in *The gorilla mask*/*The vet*. See `01` and `04`.

**25. Does it support C, the front?**
Yes: the Brotherhood fronts for the Party, the book fronts for the Party's motive, and the bottom layer stays unanswered for good (III.ii). SDF's final conversation leaves the film claims unchecked in the same way. See `03`.

**26. Does it support D, lock and key?**
Partly. The nursery rhyme is a countersign; O'Brien holds the last line (II.iv, II.viii). Room 101 is a key cut from Winston's own dream (III.v), which SDF can't use as threat, but the craft rule carries: the key comes from material the reader has already seen. See `02` and `04`.

**27. What fifth mechanisms does the book show?**
True bait; the error-language cover; the restored tell; the broken man left in public as proof; and evidence shown then taken away (the photo burned, III.ii). The error-language cover and evidence-taken-away are already in SDF (*The week it vanished*). See `00-map.md` section 4; `03` and `04`, Mechanism notes.

## Lore delivery

**28. How long are the Goldstein extracts, and do they stall?**
About 5,300 and 5,500 words (II.ix). The first doesn't stall: it explains the switch of enemy we just watched. The second, read aloud to Julia, slows; Orwell pays with her sleep, a cut sentence, and the arrest two pages later. See `03`, "How the lore gets in."

**29. Where does the book genuinely stall?**
I.vii, the proles essay and copied textbook, about 1,800 words. It is paid for by the photograph story that was interrupted four chapters earlier (I.iii). See `01`.

**30. How does Orwell interrupt lore usefully?**
In I.iii Winston begins the one time he held proof and the telescreen instructor shouts his number; the story is told in I.vii. Surveillance cuts the lore at its best moment and it becomes a debt to the reader. SDF does the same at book scale with *The reading room* and *The reading room, continued*. See `01`.

**31. How do O'Brien's long lectures in the cells stay alive?**
Each rule is tested on Winston as it is said, and each wrong answer costs on the dial; he argues back and loses, so it is a fight (III.ii–iii). In III.v each paragraph comes with the cage moving closer. For SDF the transferable rule is the brief's: break any speech over about 150 words, and attach a stake to the answer. See `03`, `04`.

**32. Is the Appendix a model for SDF's ending?**
Possibly: a calm past-tense voice from outside the regime explains the machine after "THE END" (`04`). SDF's *The final conversation* already gives the explanation to the defeated old man in a room. A later document in a later voice is an option for the undecided Epilogue (PLACEHOLDER).

## Transfer and rules

**33. Which proposals are rated highest?**
1 (James reconciles and initials Annex C in *The drill*/*The money*), 2 ("why could I find it," *The money*), 3 (the recorded refusal, *On land*/*The schedule*). They give James an action, a cost, and the missing cost of being wrong. See `00-map.md` section 5.

**34. Does proposal 1 break the "no hacking, no break-ins" rules?**
No. It is his assigned job on the continuity program, done well. The cost is complicity, not risk of discovery: his initials sit on the machine's paperwork, which makes him a helper like Pieter.

**35. Does proposal 4 fit canon?**
It depends on a canon conflict Santi has to settle: the v8.2 plan's *The vet* says "the thin book gone," the v8 brief says it "is not stolen," and v7 *The reading room, continued* puts it on the old man's tray. The proposal follows the brief (book stays, photographed, tell restored). PLACEHOLDER.

**36. What must never be taken from *1984*?**
Invented edits on real books (the Newspeak classics, Ampleforth's Kipling), torture (Room 101, the dial), threats or blackmail (the recorded oath), and the racial description of Martin. See the Flags in all four notes.

**37. Does the book name real people as conspirators?**
No. Cromwell appears only as a statue (`02`); O'Brien names the Nazis and Russian Communists as precedent, not hidden conspirators (`03`). Pynchon's introduction does name real people, and it is not Orwell's and is not used (`01`).

**38. What in the notes is from memory rather than the text?**
Note 01's aside that O'Brien hands over the book (wrong; a stranger does, `03`). Note 02's aside about Charrington and the telescreen (confirmed by `03`). Note 04's claim that Room 101 was set up in Part Two, which no note verifies on the page. Treat the last as unconfirmed.
