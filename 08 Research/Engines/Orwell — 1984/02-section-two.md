## Why it grips
- **Every advance arrives as a delivery, and accepting it is the crime.** The note is slipped into his hand (P2 ch. 1), the route to the wood is murmured in a crowd (ch. 1), O'Brien's address comes on a torn page (ch. 6), the book is promised by brief-case (ch. 8). His own initiatives fail (the pub, the canteen twice). He is never passive, though, because each delivery needs a risk he takes himself: sitting at her table, taking the train, renting the room, ringing O'Brien's bell.
- **Doom said early, so the tension moves to how.** "We are the dead" (ch. 3); "The end was contained in the beginning" (ch. 6).
- **Objects that come back.** The rhyme goes from Charrington (P1 ch. 8) to Julia (P2 ch. 4) to O'Brien, who knows the last line (ch. 8). The rat shows its nose below the engraving, and Julia says "I bet that picture’s got bugs behind it" (ch. 4).
- **The scene that does the most work** is O'Brien's flat (ch. 8). It is built on a possible misreading: O'Brien goes on dictating Newspeak, looks irritated, and Winston fears he has "simply made a stupid mistake". Then the telescreen goes off, there is a silence, and O'Brien asks "Shall I say it, or will you?" A clock runs the lecture (three wristwatch checks, the telescreen off for half an hour at most), there is a third person in the room (Martin), and the catechism is a run of yeses broken by a single no.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The Party is the weather here. It never acts against Winston himself: Syme just vanishes. It sets the price of every move (thirty-second exchanges, talk "by instalments", hostel curfews) and so limits what he *can* do, but it never decides what he does.

The Brotherhood decides. It is the one thing Julia cannot give him, and wanting it pushes him out of the safe room and towards O'Brien. O'Brien takes Julia into the room too and charges her: the catechism tests whether the couple will split, and both refuse. The Brotherhood's rules then make Winston wait: cells, three or four contacts, orders without reasons, and a promise that he will confess and die. Joining adds no safety, only a vow.

The trap itself stays below the surface. On the page: Charrington offers the room without being asked and mentions "two entries to the house" (ch. 4). O'Brien writes his address in full view of a telescreen (ch. 6) and knows the rhyme's last line. Martin is told to memorise their faces: "You will be seeing them again. I may not." Each reads as kindness or as good craft. *[from memory, not from the text: in Part 3 Charrington is Thought Police, a telescreen hides behind the engraving, and O'Brien co-wrote the book.]*

## How the lore gets in
- **The Lottery (P1 ch. 8), about 200 words of narration on the walk.** The prizes are "largely imaginary" and the winners "non-existent persons". Record-faking at street level, between a quarrel and a pub. No stall.
- **Julia on sex and the Party (P2 ch. 3), about 400 words of conversation.** It stays short because she doesn't care about doctrine.
- **Winston on the abolished past (P2 ch. 5), about 300 words, said to a sceptic.** Julia answers "what good was that?" and then falls asleep. The listener deflates the lore, so it becomes a scene about the couple.
- **O'Brien on the Brotherhood (P2 ch. 8), about 900 words of lecture.** It holds because of the timer, Martin, the wine, and because it mostly withholds ("You will never learn much more..."). The full lore, the book, is put off to a later delivery.

## Mechanism notes
- **Winston's job as SDF's editing machine.** He falsifies production reports "to cast discredit on a prominent member of the Inner Party" (P2 ch. 1). That is discrediting by editing the record, which is what SDF's organisation does. SDF's deliberate typos are the opposite design: a flaw left in so that nobody checks. The overlap worth taking is that O'Brien's signal **is** a misprint in Winston's work queue (ch. 8), so the error the system tolerates becomes the summons. PROPOSAL: for SDF, a typo that James's monitoring eye catches could be the invitation, not the cover.
- **"We leave the seats findable."** Charrington's room is the seat: cheap, no telescreen, a back door, a discreet owner who offers it as a courtesy. Winston still chooses it himself; the idea came "through" the paperweight (ch. 4). So the organisation can *furnish* the choice without forcing the door, which keeps to SDF's rule.
- **A (gate), run backwards.** The catechism (ch. 8) is easy to pass. What it actually collects is the one refusal. It records rather than blocks, and the cost comes later.
- **B (canary).** O'Brien's reference to the unperson Syme (ch. 6) is a planted piece of thoughtcrime that watches who reads it as a signal. Winston does.
- **D (lock and key).** The rhyme as countersign (ch. 4): whoever holds the last line holds the power. The brief-case swap (ch. 8) is an object handed over under a protocol.
- **C (front).** The Brotherhood is built so there is no next layer to find ("No such list exists").

## Quotes
- "It looked almost exactly as it had looked before – nothing had been crossed out – but it was one name shorter." (P2 ch. 5): an edit you notice only if you know what was there.
- "it would never be possible for me to prove it, even when I did the falsification myself." (P2 ch. 5): the editor cannot prove his own edit.
- "one of the messages among your morning’s work will contain a misprinted word, and you will have to ask for a repeat." (P2 ch. 8): a deliberate error as a summons.
- "It was like the two halves of a countersign." (P2 ch. 4): a scrap of the past used as a key.

## Flags
- No real people named as complicit (Cromwell appears only as a statue). The real rhyme and real churches are texture with no invented edits.
- Martin is described in racial terms (ch. 8). Do not take that.
- The book's contents are not in this section; they belong to the next note.
