# Comp — George Orwell, *Nineteen Eighty-Four* (1949)

> **Coverage:** Penguin edition, English, read in full via four section notes, 11 Nov 2026: `01-part-one-a.md` (I.i–vii), `02-section-two.md` (I.viii–II.viii), `03-section-three.md` (II.ix–III.iii), `04-section-four.md` (III.iv–vi, Appendix). Cites are Part.chapter.
> **Brief:** Winston's job rewriting records, against SDF's editing machine; the forbidden book as the state's own trap, against "we leave them findable."
> **Standing-rule note:** the classics "translated" and the originals destroyed (I.v, Appendix) and Ampleforth's Kipling (III.i) are invented edits on real books. Room 101 and the dial are torture. Martin is described in racial terms (II.viii). Take none of it; take the structure.

---

## 1. What it is

Winston Smith forges records at the Ministry of Truth and keeps a forbidden diary. He finds Julia and a room above a junk shop with no telescreen. O'Brien, an Inner Party man, invites him into the Brotherhood, and a stranger hands him Goldstein's banned book. The room was bugged all along; the shopkeeper is Thought Police; O'Brien helped write the book. In the cells O'Brien takes him apart, and Room 101 makes him betray Julia. He ends at a café table, loving Big Brother. A dry appendix on Newspeak follows "THE END."

Every way forward Winston finds was put there by the other side, so the reader's hope and the trap are one thing until it shuts. The conspiracy never has to stop him; it lets him want something, hands it to him, and writes down what he does.

In rows 5–7 every advance is a delivery, but each needs a risk he takes himself: her table, the room, O'Brien's bell (`02`). James in Part Two receives and risks nothing; he could stop on any page.

## 3. How the lore gets in

**Goldstein's book (II.ix), two extracts, about 5,300 and 5,500 words.** Carried by its setup: handed over mid-riot, carried unopened for six days, opened in the one safe room. "War is Peace," read silently, does not stall: it explains the switch we just watched him forge for, and the reading itself is the event. "Ignorance is Strength," read aloud to Julia, slows. Orwell pays three ways: she falls asleep (a joke on the book), it cuts off at "This motive really consists…", and about two pages later the iron voice speaks from behind the picture (II.x). (`03`)

**O'Brien's explanations.** In the flat (II.viii, about 900 words) a clock holds it: the telescreen is off for half an hour at most, and the lecture mostly withholds. In the cells (III.ii–iii, about 11,600 words) every rule is tested on Winston as it is said; he argues back and loses, so it plays as a fight. In III.v each paragraph about Room 101 comes with the cage moving closer. (`02`, `03`, `04`)

**The Appendix (about 4,000 words, after "THE END").** The story stops on purpose. It works because the voice is past tense and outside the regime, so it can explain the machine calmly. (`04`)

**Stalls:** I.vii, the proles plus a copied textbook, about 1,800 words of essay. Paid for by the photograph story the telescreen cut off four chapters earlier (I.iii). (`01`)
**Doesn't:** I.iv, the Records Department, about 3,500 words, the longest block in Part One. Winston has a task with a result: four slips, a rival across the hall, a finished forgery he's proud of. The lore is the job. (`01`)

## 4. The mechanism we came for

**Setup (I.i, I.v).** The Party is the only source of news about its enemy. Goldstein is on screen daily; his book is "the book"; his speech uses more Newspeak than any Party member. The café where the purged drank is not forbidden, only "ill-omened." The way in is open and in plain sight.

**Rule.** The bait is true. O'Brien calls the book accurate as description, "nonsense" as program (III.iii). Winston trusts it because it tells him "what you know already" (II.ix). The trap needs no lie, only his relief at being confirmed, and it sorts people: whoever reads and acts has picked himself out.

**First use (II.vi–viii).** O'Brien mentions the unperson Syme, a planted thoughtcrime that watches who takes it as a signal; writes his address in view of a telescreen; runs a catechism that is easy to pass and really collects the one refusal ("It is necessary for us to know everything"). The summons is a deliberate misprint in Winston's work queue.

**Escalation and cost (II.ix–III.iii).** A handover so casual it feels like luck; then the bugged room, and his oath played back as evidence. Every door that opened was opened from the other side.

**Payoff (III.iii, III.vi).** "I wrote it." Then what SDF most needs: the state keeps its heresy alive to discredit it daily, and once Winston is broken it lets him go: a sinecure, a reserved table, nobody sitting near him. The broken man in public is the proof.

**Against A–D:**
- **A (gate): contradicts, then adds.** Winston never tests O'Brien: "there was no way of doing so" (I.i). The catechism records rather than blocks. The dial (III.ii) is the real gate: someone who knows more asks, and a wrong answer costs. Take the shape, not the pain; a wrong answer should close a door, a name, a copy.
- **B (canary): supports, run on the hero.** The book is invented to see who believes it. Winston's own canary, the dust, is noticed and *put back* (III.iv): the defense against a canary is to restore it.
- **C (front): supports.** The Brotherhood fronts for the Party; the book fronts for its motive; the bottom layer stays unanswered (III.ii).
- **D (lock and key): partly.** The rhyme as countersign (II.iv); Room 101 cut from his own dream (forbidden to SDF, but the craft point stands: the key comes from material the reader has already seen).
- **Fifth:** **true bait** (accurate material left findable so the reader identifies himself); **the restored tell**; **the broken man left in public as proof**. The fourth candidate, **the error-language cover** (edits filed as "misprints," I.iv; a misprint as summons, II.viii), is real but already all over SDF (section 6).

## 5. Transfer to SDF

**1. James does the forgery's homework. *The drill*, *The money*.**
- *Change:* the continuity program gives James a task with a result: reconcile Annex C against HR records before the drill and sign it off.
- *James does:* the job, well. He finds Pieter's "dependants per allocation: 3" against two children, corrects it, and initials the reconciliation (Ogilvy, I.iv; "anxious… that the forgery should be perfect," II.ix).
- *Cost:* his initials are on the machine's own paperwork; he is a helper by his own hand. The old man can thank him for the cleanest annex in years (*The reading room, continued*); James's own name belongs among *The sheets*.
- *Already on the page?* No. V7 *The money*: Saskia says "See if anything's expired" and he reads. V7 *The drill*: five idle hours. Nothing he produces carries his name.

**2. "Why could I find it?" *The money*.**
- *Change:* the role that opens the annex was granted before he asked.
- *James does:* before opening Annex C he asks Saskia who approved it. "It came through already ticked," dated the week of his ticket. He opens it anyway.
- *Cost:* next night's payload has a new line against his number: *read (annex)*. He counts himself, knowingly: the book sorting its reader (II.ix, `03`) with SDF's own machine.
- *Already on the page?* Partly. The opening has "We leave them findable" (plan, *The reading room*); v7 *The money* has the role added "with a sigh, because I'd asked," and James never asks why it was easy.

**3. The one refusal is recorded. *On land*, *The schedule*.**
- *Change:* like the catechism (II.viii), the "companion" note comes from one thing James refuses in front of Kalder.
- *James does:* at the pub Kalder offers a weekend at his club, two rounds and a hotel, "no dogs, sorry." James declines because of Buddy, as a joke. In *The schedule* he reads *companion* and knows which sentence put it there.
- *Cost:* the threat to Buddy traces to James's own mouth, so *A street, night* has a cause he can feel guilty for. The "I told them what I loved" beat moves from the end of v7 *The reading room, continued*, where nothing follows from it, to where it costs.
- *Already on the page?* Partly. V7 *The corporate day* ("Bring the old boy"); plan *A car park* ("near enough to know what he loves").

**4. The restored tell. *The gorilla mask*, *The vet*, *Frank, loose*.**
- *Change:* James sets a tell on the thin book; the other side puts it back (III.iv). They don't take the book; they make it useless.
- *James does:* after the barbecue (perhaps the plan's unnamed "building something at four in the morning," PLACEHOLDER) he lays one of Buddy's gray hairs across the thin book. Home from the vet at six, the hair is exactly in place; he tells Ana nothing was touched and doesn't copy the book. In *Frank, loose* he orders a library copy of one of the forty-one titles to show Frank, and it is "withdrawn for conservation." So is the next.
- *Cost:* the book was photographed, and his two-copy method has lost its second copies. Frank gets the invoice, not the edit. Discredit, not prevent: nothing stolen.
- *Already on the page?* No. Canon conflict: plan *The vet* says "the thin book gone"; the v8 brief says it "is not stolen"; v7 *The reading room, continued* puts it on the old man's tray. This follows the brief. PLACEHOLDER.

**5. The broken man at his table. *The other ten*.**
- *Change:* one of the four "quiet" addresses becomes a person: alive, cheerful, politely avoided (III.vi), the organization's best outcome.
- *James does:* goes to the sports club whose newsletter dropped its treasurer and has a coffee with him. The man insists nothing ever happened and returns to his garden every time.
- *Cost:* James meets his likeliest ending, which is not a canal; Ana's "that's a Tuesday" gets harder to answer. "Quiet" in Aylin's visitor book (*The night before*) becomes someone the reader has met.
- *Already on the page?* Partly. V7 *The other ten* has the quiet four as records only; the widow is the only person he meets.

**6. True bait, James's. *The sheets*, *The leak*.** (PLACEHOLDER) James gives the brewery man his sheet last, knowing he'll report it. Cost: the man at Pieter's door is partly James's doing. *Already?* Partly (plan *The sheets*: "we know he'll report it," not chosen). Risk: James as manipulator.

## 6. Do not take

- **The error-language cover as news.** SDF has it: typos left in (*The sentence*), "diagnostic artefact" (*Leave it*), the duplicate ticket (*The week it vanished*), *When a Reader Notices* (*The math*), Julian K.'s eleven-minute fix (*Enjoy it while it lasts*), "No impact observed" (v8 *Leave it*).
- **A broadcast Goldstein.** The organization feeding its own opposition is already the thermostat (*The band*) and the farmed forum (*The gorilla mask*).
- **Total control of memory.** SDF's organization can't edit memory, which is why it must count and visit. Keep it that weak.
