# Stephen King, 11/22/63 — section 6 (chapters 22–25)

> Coverage: chapters 22–25 (about 35,000 words), read in full, 11 Nov 2026. Edition: converted text as in the scratch file; Part 5 ("11/22/63") opens at chapter 23.

## Why it grips
- **The test is stolen as it arrives.** Ch 22 times the stakeout to the minute (3:30, 3:33, 3:39). Marina's knock delays Jake just long enough for the call to catch him. Had he left on time, it would have rung in an empty apartment.
- **The hero admits the ugly thought.** In ch 22 §5 Jake briefly weighs Sadie's life against learning whether Oswald was alone. The gate becomes a moral cost before any action.
- **Two clippings side by side** (ch 23 §1): Walker on page 1, Sadie on page 7. The investigation and its price share a newspaper, with no comment.
- **The waiting is a love story with a clock inside it.** The Jamboree, surgeries and Candlewood weekend fill about two thirds of the section, each with a dated history event in its margin (Lee on the New Orleans dock, the test-ban promise).
- **A verifiable prediction carries the suspense.** Jake stakes his sanity with Sadie on a fight result (ch 24 §10). The fight runs at full length and Case goes down twelve seconds in. Belief, money and Al's notes ride on one outcome.
- **The reversal comes from off-stage.** Once the gate opens, the beating arrives from the bookie subplot that paid for the waiting, while the reader was watching the CIA thread.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The possible conspiracy is the only thing between Jake and the trigger. What he has to know is one specific fact: **was anyone else in the alley with Oswald on 10 April, especially de Mohrenschildt?** Al's notes call this the "window of uncertainty". The text gives three reasons it blocks action. A pointless kill would mean a reset (ch 23 §7). He refuses to kill without having "seen", as he did with Dunning (ch 23 §7). And Sadie makes him promise "never by mistake" (ch 24 §11), which turns the gate into a vow to someone he loves. The gate holds from 10 April to 5 August, about four months of story time and roughly 25,000 of the section's 35,000 words. It opens through a person who chooses to answer, holding a document. Once it opens, Jake files the Ruby clipping as "just another harmonic".

## How the lore gets in
- **Two newspaper clippings** (ch 23 §1, about 400 words). Documents. No stall: the reader gets the news as Jake does.
- **The Easter bug** (ch 23 §6, about 1,000 words). An overheard scene with a gap in the tape. No stall: the gap is the event.
- **The Dealey Plaza bench** (ch 23 §7, about 900 words). The big CIA theory as inner monologue. It risks a stall, but King ties it to a yes/no decision (kill Lee before the 24th?) and treats the theory as a list of costs.
- **The Around Town clipping** (ch 25 §4, about 100 words of quoted newsprint). An object handed to the hero by a suspect. It closes one question and quietly brings in a new name.
- **The hillside explanation to Sadie** (ch 25 §8, about 1,200 words). Why not tip off the police. It doesn't stall, because Sadie pushes back and her questions test the rule out loud.

## Mechanism notes
- **A, the gate.** This is the clearest instance in the book so far. The gate is precise (one fact about one night). It has a deadline (Oswald back on 3 October). It's enforced by cost (a reset that erases Sadie) and by a promise to another person. Test one fails by bad luck, test two by a gap in the tape, and test three works only when he stops observing and **asks**. For SDF: one checkable fact, with a first try that fails for an unrelated human reason.
- **The past pushes back with no face.** Every blow comes from an ordinary agent with a small motive of his own (Clayton's jealousy, Gutierrez's syphilis, Roth's pride), none linked to Kennedy. The rule is stated once (ch 25 §8). The strongest lesson: **the cost lands on the side-channel the hero opened to keep the wait going** (the bets that paid for Sadie and the rent), not on the investigation.
- **C, the front.** The document that closes the gate contains the next layer: Jack Ruby raising a glass. The hero dismisses it. The reader [from memory, not from the text] knows Ruby's later role, so the irony works. Good shape for SDF, with an invented name.
- **D, lock and key.** Sadie will believe only after one verified prediction (the Case–Tiger round). Her belief, and her promise "never by mistake", become a second gate.
- **A fifth shape: the borrowed authority.** Jake gets past de Mohrenschildt by repeating things only a watcher could know (the stuffed rabbit, the "how did you miss" joke). The other man fills in "The Company" on his own. The truth is real, the frame is false, and the suspect builds the frame himself.

## Flags
- Real people named as possibly complicit: George de Mohrenschildt (speculated CIA cutout and possible shooter, ch 23 §7), Jack Ruby (ch 25 §4), Carlos Marcello as the mob boss above the bookies (ch 24 §13, ch 25 §15). SDF can't copy this. Its edits, people and companies stay invented.
- Jake's methods break James's rules: impersonating the CIA and holding a gun on de Mohrenschildt (ch 25 §2–4), and blackmailing Jessica Caltrop (ch 25 §6). Only the shape transfers: a person chooses to produce a clearing document.
- Violence level (Sadie's disfigurement, Clayton's suicide, the kneecapping) is far above SDF's "killing is rare and a failure".
