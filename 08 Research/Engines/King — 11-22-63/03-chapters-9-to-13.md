## Why it grips

Oswald is off the page for most of this section. The suspense comes from three other sources. First, **the rules get tested before the big job.** The Derry redo (ch 10) rehearses Dallas: Jake states a theory, resistance proportional to consequence (ch 9 §5), then pays for it item by item in ordinary bad luck that piles up past coincidence. He has prepared (a mechanic paid $20 the day before), so it is his foresight against the past's inventiveness. The headache lifting in the cemetery ("on the other side") shows resistance has a limit.

Second, **every success costs something.** Chapter 9's best scene: Jake phones Ellen Dunning to confirm his triumph and learns Harry died in Vietnam. Ellen reads his voice (“You sound like you’re no more than thirty-five”) and turns his false identity against him. It is funny until it isn't, and it ends on "Where were you then, you son of a bitch?" Al's suicide follows within a page and closes off the option of quitting.

Third, **the waiting becomes the stakes.** Chapters 12–13 fill the gate with a life: fake diploma, substitute teaching, *Of Mice and Men*, Mimi, Sadie. King checks the Oswald clock at moments of happiness. At the curtain call Jake thinks of the newlyweds in Minsk (ch 13 §4). When Mimi offers a contract he works out the Oswalds' arrival date before he says yes (§5).

## How the conspiracy moves the story here

The conspiracy question decides **when Jake may act**, not whether he will. Al's notes keep "a small but statistically significant possibility" that Oswald was not alone (ch 12 §8). Jake adds the patsy scenario: scare Oswald off and Kennedy may still die by "some crazy Mafia or CIA plot,", costing him five more years (§9). So: watch, do not interfere, and close the window on Al's date, April 10, 1963.

What Jake has to know before he may act is that Oswald is the only shooter. The section never says how he will prove it, only that he must wait and watch.

The gate is held by three locks working together: (1) the conspiracy doubt, (2) the butterfly rule, under which any approach changes Oswald's course and wastes the foreknowledge, and (3) the calendar, since Oswald is out of the country. Lock 3 does the real holding. Locks 1 and 2 explain why he does not shoot Oswald on arrival.

King also adds pressure in the other direction. Al's suicide removes "calling hesitation research" (ch 12 §2). The gate stops Jake from acting early, and the deadline stops him from investigating forever.

The conspiracy also **puts people in the room**. The bookie Gutierrez is linked by gossip to the New Orleans Mob, which Jake connects to Oswald's uncle (ch 12 §3). His fear of Gutierrez drives him out of Florida early.

## How the lore gets in

- **Rules of time travel** (ch 9 §4–5): dialogue in a moving car, about a page. Al asks "Could you give it to me in English?" and Jake answers with a concrete comparison (the Poulin girl against the Dunnings). A nurse interrupts a few lines later. No stall.
- **Derry's cover story** (ch 9 §7): two newspaper archives, read as objects. Jake knows the official version is false because he was there (bayonet, not hunting knife). About a page. Echoed by "In Derry, we keep ourselves to ourselves" (ch 10 §13).
- **Al's Oswald notes** (ch 12 §2, §8–9): an object Jake carries, quoted in fragments ("Red dress, white slippers"; "Open auditorium, no bullet-pr’f podium"). The longest block, two pages of reasoning outside the Depository, sits inside a scene of menace: the sixth-floor window whispers an invitation ("Come on in"). Within two sections a man shoots a girl and then himself on Greenville Avenue.
- **Dallas as a place** (ch 12 §9): a page and a half of billboards, then the shooting. Lore is followed by violence within about two pages, every time.
- **The Oswald family** (ch 12 §19): no exposition at all. Jake watches Marguerite chase Robert down the street. Character delivered as surveillance.

## Mechanism notes

- **A, gate.** King builds the gate from a doubt plus a date. The doubt alone would not hold, because a reader would ask why he doesn't just look. What makes it work is that the doubt cannot be checked yet. Transfer: James's gate needs an event he cannot inspect before a known date (a reprint run, an archive release, an AGM), so that waiting is forced rather than chosen. Then fill the wait with a life he comes to value, as Jodie does for Jake. PROPOSAL.
- **The faceless antagonist.** The past acts only through deniable, ordinary causes: corroded plugs, a gravel truck, kids "who were out drinking and carousing," blamed for a firebombed house (ch 12 §7). Only the pattern is hostile. This fits SDF's rule that the organisation discredits rather than prevents: the harm is always explainable.
- **Cost of being wrong.** Ellen's call (ch 9 §8) is the model SDF lacks: a correct action produces a fresh casualty, and he learns it by phone, from a person.
- **B, canary (reversed).** Jake's bets use knowledge he should not have, and that marks him. Gutierrez's "my Yanqui," and then "Stupid me" after Faith Financial (ch 12 §17–18). Using what you know makes you visible.
- **C, front.** The Derry paper's tidy hero story (ch 9 §7) is a front read by the one man who was there.
- **D, lock and key.** The Cullums (ch 11): no coercion, just an odd offer, and the wife decides. "God sent me an answer, but not the whole answer." The door opens because a person chooses.
- **Fifth: echo.** The past rhymes: a nurse named Doris, a second Al's Diner in Jodie, "JIMLA." Cheap dread that is also funny. An SDF version would be a word from the edited passage turning up in James's ordinary day.

## Flags

- The hero kills a man in cold blood and is not punished for it (ch 10). Under SDF's rules killing is rare and a failure.
