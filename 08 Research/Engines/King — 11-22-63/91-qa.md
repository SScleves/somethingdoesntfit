# Stephen King, *11/22/63* — questions an SDF agent will ask

> Answers point to the section notes in this folder (`01`–`08`) and, where relevant, to a v8.2 chapter by title. The map is `00-map.md`. Everything about SDF here is a PROPOSAL; Santi's decisions are PLACEHOLDER.

---

## The gate

**1. What exactly must Jake know before he may act?**
At the start (ch. 3), that Oswald was the Walker shooter, with Al's bar set at ninety-eight percent rather than ninety-five (`01-front-matter-to-chapter-4.md`). By ch. 17 it narrows to one named question: was de Mohrenschildt involved in the Walker shooting on 10 April 1963? If Oswald acted alone then, Al's notes put a second gunman in November near zero (`04-chapters-14-to-17.md`). By ch. 20–21 it is stated as an act of seeing: Jake must watch Oswald fire alone (`05-chapters-18-to-21.md`).

**2. How long does King hold the gate, and what fills the wait?**
From ch. 3 to ch. 25, about seven tenths of the book by the notes' word counts, and nearly five years of story time. Two things fill it: equipment (bugs, a key, rentals, a microphone) and a life (Jodie, the school, Sadie), and the life is what the gate will cost (`03-chapters-9-to-13.md`, `04-chapters-14-to-17.md`). The stretch from 10 April to 5 August alone is roughly 25,000 of the 35,000 words in `06-chapters-22-to-25.md`.

**3. Why doesn't the reader ask why Jake doesn't just check?**
Because the doubt cannot be checked yet. Oswald is in Russia until June 1962, so the calendar does the real holding, while the conspiracy doubt and the butterfly rule only explain why he doesn't shoot Oswald on arrival (`03-chapters-9-to-13.md`). A gate made only of doubt would read as dithering. Add a date and the wait is forced.

**4. What stops the gate from becoming an excuse to investigate forever?**
A deadline pressing the other way. Al's suicide removes "calling hesitation research" (ch. 12), and the 22 November date is fixed (`03-chapters-9-to-13.md`). The gate stops Jake acting early, and the deadline stops him dawdling. SDF has the equivalent pieces (the review date, the schedule's dates, 2 December) but nothing yet stops James acting early. See map proposal 4.

**5. How does the gate finally open?**
Not by observation. The Walker test is stolen by Clayton's call (ch. 22), and the Easter bug leaves a gap (ch. 23). Jake then simply asks de Mohrenschildt, who hands him a clipping that places him elsewhere that night (ch. 25, `06-chapters-22-to-25.md`). But Jake gets that answer at gunpoint while posing as the CIA, so in SDF terms the door was forced. Take the shape ("ask the one who knows") and not the method.

**6. Is the gate ever really passed?**
No. King says outright that acting alone once does not prove acting alone twice, and a stranger at the bus stop might be a co-conspirator (`05-chapters-18-to-21.md`). Note `07-chapters-26-to-29.md` says Jake acts before he is certain and learns Oswald was alone only afterward. The Afterword gives King's own estimate (`08-chapters-30-to-31.md`).

**7. What does Derry add to the gate?**
Timing. Knowledge loses its value once you use it, and any warning wastes a known time and place (ch. 7, `02-chapters-5-to-8.md`). So the gate controls when Jake may act as well as what he must know. In SDF, James's knowledge of the schedule is worth something only while the organisation doesn't know he has it. Map proposal 1 builds on that.

**8. What does the gate cost once it opens?**
Everything after it. The certainty is bought with Sadie's death and becomes useful only for a second run (`07-chapters-26-to-29.md`). Then the future he made is a ruin: he was right about the man and still wrecked the world (`08-chapters-30-to-31.md`). Lesson for SDF: ruling out the bigger explanation should not make James's act safe, only make it his own.

**9. Is there a second gate?**
Yes, and Jake cannot pass it. To save Sadie he would have to know what a warning postcard would do, and he never can, so he burns it (Final Notes, `08-chapters-30-to-31.md`). In this book a gate that can't be passed ends in a refusal to act, which costs as much as acting.

**10. Does the gate need someone else's rule?**
It helps. Al writes the threshold in his notes ("GOOD ENOUGH TO TAKE THE SON OF A BITCH OUT"), so Jake works to a rule rather than to certainty (`04-chapters-14-to-17.md`). Sadie's "never by mistake" turns it into a promise to someone he loves (`06-chapters-22-to-25.md`). SDF already has the professor's rule in Leiden ("until we're a hundred per cent"). What it lacks is James setting his own smaller version and paying for keeping it.

## The past pushes back

**11. How does a faceless antagonist work on the page?**
Through ordinary failures that can each be explained away: flat tires, a bridge down, flu, a jammed door, amnesia (`01`, `02`, `03`, `07`). Only the pattern is hostile. It pushes in proportion to the size of the change and harder on repeat (a thrown rod the second time, `01-front-matter-to-chapter-4.md`; the rule stated in ch. 9 and ch. 25).

**12. Does it fit SDF's organisation, which discredits rather than prevents?**
Closely. Every harm has a plausible cause, which is exactly what the review's "each ending plausible" already is. The difference: King's force mostly makes Jake hesitate (`05-chapters-18-to-21.md`). SDF's organisation should do the same thing to James: make him doubt the paper, rather than block him from it.

**13. Who delivers the blows?**
Small agents with motives of their own and no link to Kennedy: Clayton's jealousy, Gutierrez, Roth's pride (`06-chapters-22-to-25.md`). In SDF the equivalents are already ordinary people (Pieter, Saskia, the Groningen dealer). A routine process can hurt James with no handler behind it. See map proposal 5.

**14. When does it get a face, and what is it like?**
Only at the end. The Card Men "watch. Sometimes we warn"; the strain wears out their minds, and Zack Lang ends up on his knees begging (`08-chapters-30-to-31.md`). That is close to SDF's old man defeated and talking in The final conversation, and to Bram. Don't give the organisation a face any earlier than v8.2 already does.

**15. What are the "harmonics" and are they worth copying?**
Rhymes: a second Doris, a second Al's Diner, a white-over-red Fury three times, "JIMLA" (`03`, `05`). They work best when they carry information. JIMLA turns out to be a billboard that tells Jake to buy a long-range microphone, and Sadie's name read under his thumbs as DORIS DUN warns him about her husband (`04-chapters-14-to-17.md`). SDF already rhymes (02:14, eleven). Make at least one echo tell James what to do next.

## Cost of being wrong

**16. How could James's Part Two have a cost of being wrong without violence?**
Copy King's three non-violent costs. A secret kept costs a relationship (Sadie leaves, `04`). Routine paperwork exposes him (the immunization record, `04`). A dated test fails because of his own presence (`06`, and map proposal 1). Map proposals 1, 4 and 5 are the non-violent ones. Only proposal 2 involves harm, and it is harm v8.2 already has (Buddy poisoned).

**17. What is the Poulin printout, and is it already in SDF?**
Two printings of one page: in one a girl is in a wheelchair, in the other she isn't, and Jake's root-beer trip caused the change (`01-front-matter-to-chapter-4.md`). SDF already has James's harmless act changing a record (The payload, "asked (job)"; The money, the step the week of his ticket; The gorilla mask, his forum account edited). What SDF lacks is the harm landing on someone else. See map proposal 6 (Noor).

**18. What's the best single scene of a cost arriving?**
Ellen's phone call (ch. 9): Jake rings to confirm his triumph and learns Harry died in Vietnam. She hears his voice is too young and ends with "Where were you then, you son of a bitch?" (`03-chapters-9-to-13.md`). The cost comes by phone, from a person, and is aimed at him. Map proposal 6 puts this shape into The vet / Leiden, Friday.

**19. Where does King put the cost so the reader doesn't see it coming?**
On the side channel. The bets that paid for the wait bring Roth's men, while the reader watches the CIA thread (`06-chapters-22-to-25.md`). In SDF, the channel James keeps open to watch is the friendship with Kalder, and the dog is the bill. See map proposal 3.

**20. Does Jake ever choose to let something bad happen to learn more?**
He considers it in Derry (let it happen once to learn details) and rejects it because it defeats the test (`02-chapters-5-to-8.md`). In Dallas he watches Marina beaten and won't even call the police, and an old woman calls him a coward (`05-chapters-18-to-21.md`). That witness is the model for map proposal 1: James watches the eighth address rather than warning it, and has to live with the choice.

## Lore

**21. How does King deliver a big lecture without the story stopping?**
He sets it at a deathbed (Al coughing blood, `01`), at bayonet point (Turcotte, `02`), or in a negotiation where each side holds something (Hosty, `07`). He ends it on an order or an object. Violence or a decision follows within about two pages (`03`). For The reading room, continued, the old man's method talk already has an offer at the end. Keep his speeches broken by objects on the table.

**22. Where does the bulk of King's lore live?**
In an object: Al's Oswald notebook, studied off the page and quoted a line at a time (`01`, `03`, `05`). The notebook goes wrong partway through and becomes a risk (`05`), and its gap forces the climax (`07`). SDF's thin book and the List already work this way. The transferable bit is letting the document's gap or error force an action.

**23. Where does King stall?**
On the Hargis–Walker broadcast (ch. 20, `05`), on Derry's catalogue of omens (ch. 8, `02`, kept short), and on the Final Notes diary (`08`), deliberately. Treat TV and catalogue lore as the risk. In SDF, the hearings on the bar TV in The reading room, continued should stay a single paragraph.

## Specific SDF chapters

**24. Which King move fits The money?**
Routine paperwork as the antagonist (the school immunization record, `04-chapters-14-to-17.md`). The role Saskia grants James can come back through the bank's access review, and the cost falls on her. See map proposal 5. The "number wrong by a clean multiple" is already on the page there, so don't re-propose it.

**25. Which King move fits The other ten?**
The Walker test (`05`, `06`) and the Derry rule about when (`02`). The eighth address, with a future date, becomes the test James must witness, and he has to choose between warning and watching. v7 The reading room, continued already holds the trace ("I was there. Nobody came. Why."). See map proposal 1.

**26. Which King move fits The schedule and A street, night?**
Harry's essay, which is true in outline and wrong in a detail that kills (`02-chapters-5-to-8.md`). The schedule is right about the visit and wrong about the date, and the date moved because of James's own verb (A car park). See map proposal 2.

**27. Which King move fits On land and The gorilla mask?**
The cost landing on the side channel (`06`) and Turcotte's use of Frati to test Jake (`02`). Kalder testing James "in small clever ways" after the pub is already the Frati move. What's new is James keeping the friendship going on purpose and paying for it. See map proposal 3.

**28. Which King move fits Frank, loose?**
A gate opened by a person who chooses to answer (`06`), and the certain paranoid as a foil (Clayton, `05`). Frank is the reverse: the doubter whose failure to explain is the threshold. Make it a rule James states in Leiden and keeps until Frank, loose. See map proposal 4.

**29. Which King move fits The reading room, continued?**
The suspect who builds the frame himself (de Mohrenschildt fills in "The Company", `06`) and the clearing document that carries the next name (Ruby in the clipping, `06`). The old man explaining away the eighth address ("nobody came because you'd gone") is the stolen test used against the hero. Proposal 1 sets that up.

**30. Which King move fits Twenty minutes?**
Sadie believes only after a prediction comes true (the Case fight, `06`), and Harry and Sadie open up to one private fact (`08`). v8.2 already does it differently and better for its tone: Ana counts six "I don't know"s and believes him. Don't add a prediction there.

**31. Is "the investigator becomes the theory" worth using?**
It's already in The gorilla mask (his forum account edited with bloodlines). King's version is larger: Amberson becomes a conspiracy figure with conventions (`08`). If anything, use it in Leave it: the book James may publish could be filed among the cranks, which v8.2 already implies ("filed as fiction").

## Rules and risks

**32. What in King breaks SDF's rules?**
Bugging a rented house, paying Ivy for a key and threatening her (`04`); a forged reference and a mail-order degree (`03`); posing as the CIA, the gun on de Mohrenschildt, blackmailing Jessica Caltrop (`06`); invented testimony about real people (`07`). Real people named as complicit run through `03`, `05`–`08`. None of it transfers.

**33. Is there a King move that fits "doors open only when a person chooses"?**
Several: the Cullums' wife deciding on an odd offer (`03`), Ellie and Deke lending the yearbook (`04`), the Depository co-worker opening the door once Jake recalls his name (`07`), and Harry and Sadie opening up to one private fact (`08`). The children give the address only after Jake gives them a true warning (`02`).

**34. What's the tone risk?**
King's violence and period slurs are far from SDF's register (`02`, `03`, `05`, `06`). But his comedy never stops, even at the worst moments (the beggar haggling over a crutch, `07`; "Charlie Fucking Chaplin", `08`). That matches SDF's rule to stay funny to the end.

**35. Did the notes find a fifth mechanism beyond A–D?**
Five. The page that changed because of the hero's act (`01`). The witness's record that is wrong in one detail (`02`). The cost landing on the side channel (`06`). Echo as a clue-giver (`03`, `04`). The investigator becoming the theory (`08`). Also, from `05`, the certain paranoid as foil: Clayton's "statistical analysis does not lie" against Jake's window of uncertainty.

**36. Where do the notes disagree with each other?**
`06` says the gate opens with the clipping in ch. 25; `07` says Jake acts before he is certain and learns afterward. Both are true if the post-beating amnesia and the gaps in the notebook reopen it. `01` gives the gate's question as seeing Oswald fire at Walker, `04` as de Mohrenschildt's involvement. The question narrows as the book goes on. `06` calls de Mohrenschildt's answer a person choosing to answer, but its own flags show it came at gunpoint.
