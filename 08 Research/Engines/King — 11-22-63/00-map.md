# Stephen King, *11/22/63* — the map

> **Coverage:** Scribner first hardcover, November 2011 (eBook ISBN 978-1-4516-2730-5) per note 01; the other notes used a converted text with no edition data. English. Read in full through eight section notes (`01`–`08`, about 273,000 words), 11 Nov 2026. Anything from outside the notes is marked *[from memory, not from the text]*.
> **Brief:** mechanism A, the gate. What Jake must know before he may act, how long King holds the gate, what fills the wait, and how "the past pushes back" works as an antagonist with no face.
> **Standing-rule note:** real people are named as complicit or suspect (de Mohrenschildt, Ruby, Marcello, Hosty, Hoover, Angleton; `03`, `05`–`08`). A real local paper is printed in altered versions (`01`). Jake bugs a house, buys a key, forges a degree, poses as the CIA, holds a gun on a witness and blackmails a woman (`03`, `04`, `06`). Take the shapes, none of that.

---

## 1. What it is

Jake Epping, a Maine English teacher, is shown a pantry that opens onto 9 September 1958, and every trip resets. His dying friend Al gives him a mission (stop Oswald) and a rule (not until you know Oswald acted alone). Jake rehearses on a family murder in Derry, then lives five years in Texas under a false name. He falls in love with Sadie and watches the Oswalds while he waits for a test date, the Walker shooting of 10 April 1963. Missing the test costs him, and so does a beating. He saves Kennedy, Sadie dies, the future he made is a ruin, and he resets everything and gives her up.

What makes it work: the reader always knows the date, so the suspense comes from what Jake is not allowed to do before it. And every success is billed to someone he loves, usually through a channel he opened himself.

## 3. How the lore gets in

- **Rabbit-hole rules** (ch. 1–2, `01`): the trip first, then about 1,500 words of rules, each answering a question the reader already has.
- **Al's pitch** (ch. 3, about 4,500 words, `01`): a lecture at a deathbed, broken by coughing fits, ending on an object.
- **Al's notebook** (`03`–`07`): the bulk of the history sits in an object studied off the page; on the page, a line or two, then action. Its gap (no rooming house) forces the plan (ch. 27).
- **Overheard talk** (the Cuba speech, ch. 19, `05`; the Easter bug, ch. 23, `06`): long, but Jake listens for one name, and the gap in the tape is the event.
- **Lore as obstacle** (Turcotte's sister, ch. 8, `02`): told at bayonet point while the clock runs.
- **Where it stalls:** the Hargis–Walker broadcast (ch. 20, `05`), which Jake ends by switching the set off, and the Final Notes diary (`08`), which stalls on purpose. The working rule: violence or a decision comes within about two pages of any lore (`03`).

## 4. The mechanism we came for: the gate

**Setup** (ch. 3, `01`). Al gives a number (ninety-five percent isn't a hundred), a deciding event (Oswald firing at Walker), a date before which the gate cannot open, and a price for opening it wrongly: Kennedy dies anyway and five years of a finite life are gone.

**The rule** (ch. 7, `02`). Derry adds the other half: knowledge loses its value once you use it. A warning throws away a known time and place. So the gate controls *when* he may act, not only *what* he must know.

**First use.** A rehearsal gate Jake sets himself (ch. 4): see a big change play out first. He passes it; the bill arrives by phone (ch. 9).

**Escalation** (`03`–`05`). Three locks hold together: a doubt, the rule that any approach changes Oswald's course, and a calendar. The doubt alone wouldn't hold, because the reader would ask why he doesn't just look. The calendar is what makes waiting forced. Then the question narrows to one named man and one dated stand-in test, and King admits it can never be fully passed (ch. 21). Every attempt to pass it early makes things worse.

**The wait** is filled with equipment (getting ready to listen) and a life (what listening will cost). King checks the Oswald clock at moments of happiness (`03`, `04`). Measured by the notes' word counts, the gate holds from chapter 3 to chapter 25, about seven tenths of the book.

**Cost** rarely comes from the enemy. Sadie leaves because Jake won't explain, and a school clerk exposes him (`04`). An unrelated man's jealousy steals the test, and the beating comes through the betting that paid for the wait (`06`). Waiting until the 22nd puts Sadie on the stairs (`07`).

**Payoff** (`07`–`08`). Certainty arrives after the act. Being right about the target made nothing safe. A second gate follows (warn Sadie?), and he cannot pass it.

**The past pushes back** only through ordinary, deniable causes (tires, a jammed door, flu, amnesia), in proportion to the change, harder on repeat, each delivered by a small agent with his own motive (`01`, `03`, `06`, `07`). It also rhymes, and the rhymes carry information: JIMLA turns out to be a billboard that tells Jake what to buy next (`04`). It gets a face only at the end, a worn-out guard who can only warn and beg (`08`).

**Against A–D.** A: supported, with two additions (the gate governs timing; passing it doesn't make the act safe). B: small (Al's carved tree, `01`; Frati sent to test Jake, `02`). C: weak (the Derry paper, `03`; Ruby inside the clearing clipping, `06`; the FBI's cover story, `07`). D: steady (the children, the Cullums, the Depository co-worker, private facts as keys, `02`–`08`). **Fifth mechanisms:** the page that changed because of the hero's harmless act (`01`); a witness's record right in its claim and wrong in a detail (Harry's essay, `02`); the cost landing on the side channel that kept the wait going (`06`); echo as a clue-giver (`03`, `04`); the investigator becoming the theory (`08`).

## 5. Transfer to SDF

Already on the page, so not proposed: the hero's harmless act changing the record (The payload; The money's step the week of his ticket); the investigator turned into the theory (The gorilla mask); echo (02:14, eleven); the threshold as a number (Leiden, "until we're a hundred per cent"); the cost of keeping the secret (The math; The schedule); "ask the one who knows" (the thin book's *Ask*). The gap is a gate and a cost of being wrong.

**1. The eighth address as the Walker test.** *The other ten → The reading room, continued.*
- *Change:* the eighth address, dated nine days off, becomes the test James must witness before anyone gets a sheet. If someone comes, the schedule is real, and so is his own line on it.
- *James does:* on the date he sits in his car on a public street with Buddy. He chooses between knocking to warn them, which tells the organisation he holds the schedule (King ch. 7), and watching. He watches. Nobody comes.
- *Cost:* the test fails because he was there, and a week later the address goes quiet anyway (PLACEHOLDER: quiet, moved, or dead). The old man turns it on him.
- *On the page?* Partly. v7 The other ten holds the dates back; v7 The reading room, continued has only the margin note "I was there. Nobody came. Why." and the old man's "nobody came because you'd gone." The v8.2 plan has neither.

**2. The schedule is right about the visit and wrong about the date, and James moved the date.** *The schedule, A car park, A street, night, The vet.*
- *Change:* James trusts the printed date the way Jake trusts Harry's essay. The visit comes early because the review moved when he stopped asking out loud.
- *James does:* he books Buddy into Frank's flat for the printed week and writes it in his diary.
- *Cost:* Buddy is poisoned before that week (PLACEHOLDER: how many days). In The vet James sees that the verb that moved the date was his.
- *On the page?* Partly. v8.2 has the eleven days, a date moved by a verb (A car park), and Ana leaving "knowing a house with one old dog in it is a target." James never plans around the date, so its moving costs him nothing he chose.

**3. The friendship is the side channel, and it carries the bill.** *On land, The gorilla mask, A street, night.*
- *Change:* after the invented-book trap James keeps the friendship going deliberately, as his way of watching, the way Jake's bets pay for the wait.
- *James does:* he agrees to the barbecue for a reason he keeps from Ana: to see what Buddy does with Kalder in the house. He gets his answer (four hours in the doorway).
- *Cost:* the barbecue is where Kalder learns the back lane, the fourth gate and the water bowl.
- *On the page?* Partly. In v8.2 *Ana* insists on the barbecue; On land says only that the friendship "continues." A street, night uses the layout but never says where Kalder learned it.

**4. A written gate with a skeptic as its keeper.** *Leiden, Frank, loose, The sheets.*
- *Change:* after Noor gives him the professor's rule, James writes his own smaller one, as Jake does after Poulin: no sheet to anyone until Frank has tried to explain one piece of paper and failed.
- *James does:* he holds the sheets back until Frank, loose, while the ninth date approaches. Frank's "I believe the invoice" opens the gate.
- *Cost:* the ninth date passes while he waits (PLACEHOLDER: what happens there).
- *On the page?* Partly. v7 Frank, loose has James decide Frank "needed to see one thing he couldn't argue with." Nothing states the condition in advance, and nothing is lost while he waits.

**5. The cost lands on the person who opened the door.** *The money, Enjoy it while it lasts.*
- *Change:* the annex role Saskia granted "with a sigh" turns up in the bank's quarterly access review, the same kind of routine paperwork as the immunization record that exposes Jake (ch. 17).
- *James does:* he writes his own justification on the review form for Pieter to sign. He gives the true, dull reason and leaves the annex out.
- *Cost:* Saskia loses the right to grant roles and is moved; James's access narrows.
- *On the page?* No. In v7 The money the role is added and nothing follows. The v8.2 Enjoy it while it lasts narrows his access with no person paying.

**6. The price of a visit, from a person.** *The vet, Leiden, Friday, The other ten.*
- *Change:* James learns what his Leiden visit cost Noor as Jake learns about Harry: by phone.
- *James does:* he rings her workshop. A colleague says she has stopped taking private clients and "said you'd call." From then on he stops visiting helpers in person and works on paper, which is The sheets' method.
- *Cost:* Noor's livelihood, and James knowing his visit caused it.
- *On the page?* Partly. v8.2 The vet ends "Then nothing"; Leiden, Friday shows the cost to the reader, never to James.

## 6. Do not take

- Jake's methods (bugs, bought keys, forged papers, posing as the CIA, the gun, blackmail; `04`, `06`). In King the gate opens at gunpoint; in SDF someone must choose to open it.
- The body count and register (a child killed on the page, a kneecapping, a murder left unpunished; `02`, `03`, `06`).
- Real people as suspects, and the altered real newspaper (`01`, `05`–`08`).
