# DeLillo, *Libra* — questions an SDF agent will ask

> Answers point at the section notes (`01`–`04` in this folder) and at the map (`00-map.md`). v8.2 chapters are named by title. Everything about SDF is a PROPOSAL; Santi's decisions are PLACEHOLDER.

**1. What is Libra in one line, for our purposes?**
A conspiracy that is small, compartmented and run on paper, which gets away from its own designer because nobody inside can see more than his own task. The reader, who knows the date, is the only one who holds the whole thing. See `00-map.md` §1 and `01`, "Why it grips."

**2. Who is the protagonist?**
There are three lines. Everett and his men (chapters named by date), Oswald (chapters named by place), and Branch the archivist (short passages inside both). The engine table follows whichever man is pushing at that moment. See map §2.

**3. What was Everett's original plan?**
A staged attempt that misses on purpose: "We miss him" (17 April), a Secret Service man winged ("Hit, don't kill," 20 May), and a paper trail to Cuban intelligence, with a second layer pointing at the CIA's own plots against Castro. See `01`, Movements and Mechanism notes; `02`, "How the conspiracy moves the story." Notes `03` and `04` mark this as from memory, but `01` and `02` have it from the text.

**4. How exactly does the miss become a kill?**
At the middle level. Everett reaches Mackey only through Parmenter. Mackey never tells the shooters it is a miss (20 May), decides on a kill alone in a car (15 July), and cuts off contact (6 September). See `01`, `02`, `03`. Nobody above him knows.

**5. Is the plot big or small?**
Small, and it gets smaller: five men, a paint scraper, a parked car (`01`). Miami's wall of shooters becomes "an everyday Texas homicide" (`03`). Ferrie admits they didn't arrange Oswald's job or the route (`04`). Branch's verdict is that it worked "due mainly to chance."

**6. Why does the small plot frighten more than a big one would?**
Because nobody big is needed for it to work, and nobody can stop it, its designer included. See `01`, "Why it grips," and `03`, "How the conspiracy moves the story." For SDF, v7 Compartments already reaches "a few dozen men with codes... that's a club." Libra says a club is enough to kill.

**7. What is Branch's role, in a sentence?**
A CIA-commissioned archivist who reads the whole record decades later, is sent material by a "Curator," never acts, and ages in the room. See `01`, Mechanism notes; `02`, How the lore gets in; `03` and `04`, Branch passages.

**8. Exactly how does DeLillo keep Branch from stalling the book?**
In six ways: short slices at chapter edges; the clock running in other men's dated chapters; lists closed by verdicts; reading that tells the reader the future; reading the *sending* as evidence; and paying only in years while the book uses him as the warning. See map §5 "Branch, precisely," and `01`–`04`.

**9. What must James do that Branch doesn't?**
Ask by name for what he wasn't sent, sort what he was sent by who sent it, and spend a conclusion on a person. Branch reaches his verdict and keeps reading (`04`). James is the narrator, so he can't be kept short and offstage the way Branch is. See map §5.

**10. Is James already Branch on the page?**
In temperament, yes. v7 The money: "What I do is read everything in the order it comes, at the same speed." v8.2 Enjoy it while it lasts has him read all four hundred pages. The difference must come from acts, not from a change in temperament.

**11. Where does Branch stall on purpose, and why does it work?**
2 July (`02`): about 900 words of the Warren Report and later deaths, right after Everett learns Oswald is real. It works because it is short, because it is placed after a turn, and because it tells us that one of the shooters, whom we have only just met, will be shot. The stall is the point, and the next chapter moves.

**12. What does the Curator do that matters for SDF?**
He chooses what Branch gets (autopsy photos, goat skulls), then holds things back ("some limit inherent in the yielding..."), then sends novels and a Lincoln–Kennedy study. Branch suspects the room was built to hold him (`03`, `04`). That is SDF's thermostat aimed at one reader, and it is why the v8.2 opening's tray, already set, matters (map 5.1).

**13. How is that different from Orwell's "why could I find it?"**
Orwell's is one piece of bait (the book). Libra's is a supply regime: an order, a withholding, a flood. ADDENDUM 05 places Orwell's at The money. Map 5.2 sorts all of James's material by how it arrived, in Frank, loose.

**14. Does Libra support mechanism A, the gate?**
Yes, from new angles. Seen from outside, Oswald has every paper in Mexico City and a clerk still won't say yes (`03`). Mackey's oak-tree gate is ignored when Oswald fires early (`04`). Ruby's gate ("if I'm late, it wasn't meant") hands the choice to chance (`04`). For SDF, a gate James can't fail is no gate.

**15. Mechanism B, the canary?**
Run by the conspiracy. Mackey leaks Miami on purpose ("Let everyone know," `03`), and the Milteer leak cancels Miami (`04`). Small versions: Oswald feeds technical terms and watches reactions (`01`), and signs up for an exam to see whether Naval Intelligence notices (`02`). Ruby's box-number test is the cost of being wrong played as farce (`04`).

**16. Mechanism C, the front? ADDENDUM 05 says it never becomes an act.**
Libra contradicts that. Everett builds a two-layer trail (`01`), and Oswald himself hides the rifle "to give them something to uncover, a layer to strip away" (`04`). Map 5.4 turns this into James's act in The sheets: a true, dull ticket for the count to read.

**17. Mechanism D, lock and key?**
Supported, with a limit: a key gets talk, not a choice. Ferrie uses the Walker secret and Oswald "says no" (`03`). Karlinsky's key to Ruby is his need to be somebody, plus an unlocked door (`04`). SDF's keys are already on the page (Groningen, Els, Noor's shelfmark), so nothing to build.

**18. What mechanisms are genuinely new here?**
The slip at the middle level; the originator cut out; the archive as containment; the decoy task; the found man. See map §4, last paragraph.

**19. How does "the slip at the middle level" map onto SDF?**
SDF's handlers are Libra's middle. If the method's word is *visit* and one handler made it *kill*, the deaths are his failure, not the method. Map 5.3 puts that on a sheet in What was on the phone and The night before, paid off in A market, Sunday. It fits "killing is rare and a failure."

**20. Doesn't 5.3 contradict Monday's "they have killed before, and it was cheap"?**
Not necessarily. The organization still owns the killing; the sheet shows *how* it happened in this quarter. PLACEHOLDER: whether Zwolle (A pension visit, Zwolle) and Deventer, March fall under Kalder's code. Leiden, nine years ago, with Kalder asking "Who else?", supports it.

**21. Who in SDF is Everett, the originator cut out?**
Possibly Bram: sincere, believes the story, never had the experience he counts (v8.2 Part 0). Libra suggests he might learn from paper what his count became. No proposal is made, because James never meets Bram on the page. PLACEHOLDER if you want it for The leak.

**22. Is any SDF helper's task a decoy, like the Oswald doubles?**
Pieter's "security test" (the honeypot tier in What was on the phone) is the closest: a task that keeps a helper busy and reassured. Libra's version (`04`) suggests the tier list could include one task that exists only to occupy. Already partly on the page through the tiers.

**23. What is "the found man," and does SDF have one?**
Everett looks to script a gunman and finds one already scripting himself (`01`, `02`). In SDF the brewery man ("I'm *in*," The sheets) and James's own twelve-year forum persona are the nearest. Gibson 5.5 covers the persona, so no new proposal.

**24. How does Libra handle documents as objects?**
The Navy medical form is set as a typed form (`01`), Mackey's inventory is a list (`02`), Oswald's Historic Diary is paper he smuggles home (`02`), and the Milteer tape is a transcript (`04`). Each reaches the reader as a thing. That fits SDF's "reveals are objects."

**25. "A fact is innocent until someone wants it." Where, and how to use it?**
"In Fort Worth" (`02`), on Collings's price for a job. It is the rule for James's received documents: each one becomes intelligence only because someone wanted him to have it. It sits under map 5.2.

**26. How does DeLillo keep it funny?**
Comedy is built into the plotting: Oswald gets the Walker date wrong, Dupard is dusted in flour, Banister has fifteen Kennedy doubles, Ruby asks who Earl Warren is (`02`, `03`, `04`). The jokes are also the timing: Ruby is late for reasons that make it work (`04`). That fits SDF's "funny to the end."

**27. What does the 22 November chapter teach about form?**
The shooting is built from a dozen partial views, and nobody holds the whole event (`04`). The form is compartmented. For SDF, The night before, with twelve doors and twelve partial views, could borrow that shape. It is not proposed, because the chapter is drafted (v8).

**28. What is the pattern of "why he cannot stop"?**
Every row's reason is a compartment. Each man stops seeing at the edge of his own task, so none can call it off (map §2). SDF's "nobody who matters knows anything" is the same fact, used by James as a weakness (Compartments).

**29. Could James's own sheets leak the way Everett's plan did?**
Yes, and Libra warns that the designer of a compartmented operation can't control each piece. In The sheets each recipient knows only his own page, so one may act beyond "Nothing. That's the point." The v8.2 leak is the brewery man's refusal, and Orwell 6 makes it James's choice. Keep it to one leak.

**30. What does Libra contradict in ADDENDUM 05?**
The C verdict (see Q16). It also qualifies D: keys open talk, not choice (Q17). It confirms A and the "cost after the gate" (Ferrie's exit plan comes with a killer at the theater, `04`).

**31. Which real-world items must SDF not copy?**
Real people as complicit (Ferrie, Banister, Ruby, de Mohrenschildt, Shaw); invented men listed beside the real dead in Branch's lists; fakes pinned on real CIA units; Mackey's break-in. See every note's Flags and the map's standing-rule note.

**32. Does Libra put invented edits on real books?**
No. Oswald's library *Das Kapital* with "weird notes" is texture only (`01`). Nothing to flag for SDF's book rule.

**33. Is there an equivalent of SDF's count, the nightly list of who asked?**
The hunter's trace: Parmenter's memo records his own interest (`01`), and Oswald writes down the FBI agent's plate "to build up the record" (`02`). Asking leaves paper on both sides. SDF already has this in The payload.

**34. What did the reading notes flag about the source text?**
Note `04`'s chunk ends with a stray fragment (pp. 344–354, Ruby and Tony; "We create our own Oswald") that belongs earlier and also appears in `03`. The duplicated last quarter was cut before splitting (PIPELINE stage 1). Notes `01`, `02` and `04` disagree on whether Carmine Latta is an invented or stand-in name.

**35. If Santi takes only one thing from Libra, what should it be?**
Map 5.1 or 5.2. Both turn James from Branch into a man who asks and sorts, at almost no cost in words. 5.3 is the strongest for Part Three, but it is also the riskiest for tone.
