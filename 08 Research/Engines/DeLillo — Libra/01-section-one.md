## Why it grips
- **The reader knows the ending; the plotters don't.** All the tension sits between Win's "We miss him" (17 April) and the November date every reader brings along. Each safeguard Win designs reads as a crack. By 20 May the brief is "Hit, don't kill" a Secret Service man, and Mackey answers, "This isn't a controlled experiment."
- **Two lines that do not know they are converging.** The Oswald chapters are titled by place and the plot chapters by date. The plotters hunt for a man while we watch him, years earlier, making himself into one: Hidell, the derringer, the U-2 talk. The section ends where they meet. Ferrie hears the young man's voice through a wall and cannot see his face.
- **The plot is small.** Five men, a paint scraper from the hardware store, a briefing in a parked car with the windows up against a pickup's dust. Nobody big needs to be behind it, and that makes it frightening.
- **The scene that does the most work** is the de Mohrenschildt lunch (26 April): wine talk and an ambassador saying hello, then one remark ("I think I know who tried to kill him") turns the fiction into a person. Parmenter laughs at the "daisy chain of rumor" and then goes back and writes a memo. The scene is light first, then ends in an act on paper.
- **Funny under dread:** "Texas Woman's University. Savor the name."; the college's old initials spelled CIA; Ferrie's pasted eyebrows.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The committee lecture (17 April) sets the rule: men leave the room and others enter, and the President is "the summit of unknowing". Every later scene repeats it on a smaller scale. Win does not know Mackey's real name. Parmenter hides his interest from de Mohrenschildt. Banister is asked to hold rifles without being told why ("You do know. Take my word"). Mackey tells Raymo and Frank there will be a shooting but not the target, the city or the miss. Oswald hides his lost clearance from Braunfels while she hides her purpose from him.

The result: **the one man who knows the whole plan cannot control the shooters.** Win designs a miss, and the men holding the rifles have not been told it is one. The compartments that protect the plan are what will let it get away from him. The section plants this and never states it. The plan also pulls in a network that already existed: Ferrie met the teenage Oswald back in "In New Orleans" over a broken .22.

## How the lore gets in
- **The committee structure (17 April), about 600 words of Win's thoughts** between two lines of office dialogue. Its comedy keeps it moving (poison cigars, the coughing monkey). No stall.
- **Win's pitch (17 April), about 800 words, a monologue in a parked car.** A pickup's dust interrupts it, it ends on a turn ("We miss him"), and the next scene is a phone call. The lore is the plot itself, so it cannot stall.
- **Mackey's Bay of Pigs (26 April), about 1,200 words of broken stills** while he waits in a hot room. It gives his motive: "a clear and simple reading".
- **Parmenter's Guatemala and front companies (20 May), about 1,000 words** set inside a marriage argument. Beryl's needling carries it, and it reveals his motive: Cuban oil.
- **Branch (17 April, end of 26 April), 700 to 900 words each.** These are the only real stalls, and they are short and placed at chapter edges.
- **A document:** the Navy medical record of the gunshot (In Atsugi), set as a typed form. It is the first page of Oswald's paper trail, and it reaches the reader as an object.

## Mechanism notes
- **Branch is SDF's Part Two problem, made into a character.** He receives documents: the Curator sends "precisely the right document". He is never blocked and pays only in years ("becoming bodiless"). He has written "precious little" because "It is impossible to stop assembling data." DeLillo keeps him in short slices and runs the clock in other people's scenes. For SDF: if James mostly receives, the momentum must come from someone acting on a deadline in the next scene, or James must stop receiving. Branch is James if he reads everything and never has to decide.
- **C (front), designed by the antagonist.** Win's trail has two layers on purpose. The first points to Cuban intelligence. The second, "even more unclear", points to the CIA's own plots against Castro, "the little surprise he was keeping for the end" (26 April). SDF's "findable" seat list could be built the same way, so that each layer James solves is the cover story the next one needs.
- **A (gate), held by the hunters.** Oswald's scorebook disagrees with itself ("either the number is wrong or the designation is wrong"). The plotters do not resolve this; they widen the search, and widening is how a plot leaks.
- **B (canary), at small scale.** Oswald feeds Konno and Braunfels technical terms, "studying her reaction to the terms". It is a canary done only by talking, which suits James, who reads pauses.
- **D (lock and key).** Each recruit opens to one thing: Raymo and Frank to Cuba plus $500 a month, Banister to his hatred of Kennedy, Oswald to being given a task.
- **Fifth: the found man.** Win wants to "script a person or persons out of ordinary pocket litter" and finds a man already scripting himself. The cost is that the found man brings his own plot. An SDF helper may already be telling himself a story about his one task, and that story is the door.
- **Fifth: the hunter's trace.** Parmenter's memo records his interest, much like SDF's nightly count of who asks. Asking leaves paper.

## Quotes
- "Each level of the committee was designed to protect a higher level." (17 April): compartments as a rule.
- "We do the whole thing with paper." (17 April): a plot built as a trail meant to be found.
- "Branch must study everything. He is in too deep to be selective." (26 April): the reader of everything, stuck.
- "The other thing he did not say was that they were supposed to miss." (20 May): the compartment that will break the plan.

## Flags
- Real people are made complicit: de Mohrenschildt as an Agency asset who thinks he pushed Oswald toward Walker; Banister with a CIA weapons cache; Ferrie carrying money from "Carmine Latta" (a stand-in name [from memory, not from the text]); Branch's list of real deaths; Kennedy wanting Castro dead. SDF must not do this.
- The useful structure: the planners (Everett, Parmenter, Mackey, Raymo, Vasquez, Branch) are invented and placed among real people. SDF's rule that people and companies are invented goes further and makes nothing real guilty.
- No invented edits on real books. Oswald's library *Das Kapital* has "weird notes in an obsessive hand", which is texture only.
