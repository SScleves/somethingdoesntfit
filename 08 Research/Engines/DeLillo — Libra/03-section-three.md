## Why it grips
- **The reader knows more than anyone in the scene.** Walker's monologue about the invisible Control Apparatus ("In Dallas") comes after the reader has watched the car turn onto Avondale, so the rant holds because a bullet is on its way. The miss arrives as a hole the size of a half-dollar and Walker picking metal out of his arm with tweezers.
- **Dark comedy built into the plotting.** Oswald gets the date wrong (the 17th, it is the 10th). Dupard comes dusted in bakery flour. Oswald forgets to visit Trotsky's house. The book stays funny while it walks toward a killing.
- **The hand that steers him stays out of sight.** "In New Orleans" is told from inside Oswald, so the plotters show up only as feelings: things moved in his spare room, people at 544 Camp looking at him differently. Then Ferrie says it in the Habana Bar ("You're the night-rider"). The scene that does the most work is the Trailways terminal: Lee asks what they want, Ferrie will not say, and Lee answers "You know. I don't." The man at the centre of the plot knows least about it.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The plot gets smaller and loses touch with the man who started it. Mackey stops contact with Everett and Parmenter, "leaving them to their hieroglyphics" (6 September). In "4 October" Everett does not know whether his plan is still alive. He learns the President is coming to Texas from three or four lines buried in a local paper, and he daydreams about a polygraph as his way out. [From memory, not from the text: his original design was a staged miss to be blamed on Castro.] The escalation is on the page. Mackey tells Frank that even if "all the gears don't mesh... Someone has to die" (6 September). The wall of shooters planned for Miami (25 September) shrinks to a small Dallas killing (4 October), and a plot that small needs Oswald more.

The conspiracy moves Oswald in two ways, and neither is force. It shuts doors somewhere else: Banister says Mackey "can fix it so the Cubans won't take him" (25 September), and in Mexico City Oswald blames bureaucracy. It also gives him things: ammunition he could not find, a place at the camp, an office at 544. Mackey bets on where Oswald will go instead of ordering it.

**Compartments everywhere.** Alpha 66's CIA mentors deal with only one or two men, under false names. Wayne picks up Leon's role only from "sideline dialogue". Mackey tells Raymo "You don't tell Frank or Wayne". Banister "doesn't know the exact nature of the role". Bateman insists no other agency share his informant, while Oswald works out of Banister's office. Tony Astorina's job for Carmine is to tie him into his body armour each morning. Each man knows one task, close to SDF's helper structure. DeLillo's twist: with the compartments sealed, nobody can stop the operation, its designer included.

**Branch drowning in paper (6 September).** The Curator sends autopsy photos, goat skulls and a test bullet. Branch cannot tell why these, why now, and decides the choice is itself a message, a mocking one. His notes stop leading anywhere, and the record gives Oswald's eye colour, height and handedness three ways each.

- **Walker's monologue** (about 1,200 words, inside his head): Birch-style lore. It does not stall, because the reader is waiting for the shot.
- **Frank's Cuba backstory** (6 September, about 900 words, memory during a drive): ends in his deduction that Kennedy is Alpha's target.

- **Banister and Ferrie's briefing** (25 September, about 900 words): the shooting gallery and "Oswalds all over Texas".

## Mechanism notes
- **A (gate), inverted.** In Mexico City Oswald brings every document and the gate still holds, because a person chooses ("She does not say si. She does not say no."). SDF could refuse James once with full paperwork in hand. That is a cost of being wrong about what papers can do, and it fits the rule that doors open only by choice.
- **B (canary), run by the conspiracy.** Mackey leaks Miami on purpose ("Let everyone know", 6 September) and welcomes the Chicago leak (4 October). Bateman will feed Banister selective information. SDF's organisation could bait James the same way.
- **C (front).** Miami fronts for Dallas. 544 Camp has two entrances: "One for who you are, one for who you say you are." The made-up Oswalds cover the real one.
- **D (lock and key) that fails.** Ferrie uses his key, the private knowledge of Walker, and Oswald still does not turn: "He says no" (25 September). A key gets someone talking. It does not make the choice for him.
- **Fifth: read why the documents were sent.** Branch treats the Curator's choice of material as the clue. This is the strongest fix for James's passive Part Two: every document that reaches him raises the questions of who sent it and why now, and he can get that wrong (PLACEHOLDER).
- **Sixth: the originator cut out.** Everett learns of his own plot from a newspaper: a model for an SDF character who designed part of the method and lost track of it.

## Quotes
- "Stamp some numbers and letters. This makes it true." ("In New Orleans"): self-made documents as identity.
- "The notes are becoming an end in themselves." (6 September, Branch): the man who reads everything goes under.
- "We create our own Oswald. A second, a third, a fourth." (25 September): a made-up trail that discredits instead of preventing.
- "Small, spur-of-the-moment, that's what we want. An everyday Texas homicide." (4 October): the plot shrinks and gets harder to stop.

## Flags
- Real people shown as complicit: David Ferrie, Guy Banister, Clay Shaw (with Ferrie and Oswald), Jack Ruby (seeking money from the invented Carmine Latta). The Giancana and Kennedy affair comes as gossip. The CIA is said to run Alpha 66, a real group. SDF must not do this.
- Branch's list of aliases puts real names (Ruby, de Mohrenschildt) beside invented ones (Everett, Mackey, Latta) as if they were the same kind of fact. It works, and it is exactly the mixing SDF forbids.
- No invented edits on real books in this section.
