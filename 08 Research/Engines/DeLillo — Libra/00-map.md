# Don DeLillo, *Libra* — the map

> **Coverage:** English, converted text with no edition data in the files (note `04` has running heads to p. 456). The conversion repeated the last quarter of the book; it was cut to one copy, so the whole novel to the Author's Note is covered by four section notes (`01`–`04`, about 153,000 words), read in full, 11 Nov 2026. Anything from outside the notes is marked *[from memory, not from the text]*.
> **Brief:** compartments; a plot that is smaller than it looks (Everett's staged miss, which gets away from him); and Nicholas Branch, the archivist drowning in paper, who is SDF's Part Two problem made into a character. Set against SDF's "few hundred professionals on millions of one-task helpers."
> **Standing-rule note:** real people are made complicit (Ferrie, Banister, Ruby, de Mohrenschildt, Clay Shaw, Roselli, the mob with "people... in the police"; `01`–`04`). Branch's lists put invented men (Vasquez, Latta) beside the real dead (`02`, `03`). Everett's fakes are pinned on real CIA units, and Alpha 66 is said to be CIA-run (`02`, `03`). Mackey breaks into Oswald's flat (`02`). Take the shapes, none of that.

---

## 1. What it is

Two lines that do not know they are converging. In chapters named by date, Win Everett, a CIA man pushed out after the Bay of Pigs, designs a fake attempt on Kennedy: shots that miss, and a paper trail to Cuban intelligence. In chapters named by place, Lee Oswald makes himself into exactly that man, years earlier: the Marines, Minsk, a rifle, an alias. The plotters find him. Their compartments let one middle man turn the miss into a kill, the designer loses touch, and chance does the rest. Between them, an archivist, Branch, reads the whole record decades later and cannot stop.

What makes it work: the reader brings the date, so every safeguard Everett builds reads as a crack. And no one inside the plot can see more than his own piece, so the reader is the only person who holds the whole thing.

## 3. How the lore gets in

- **Everett's pitch** (17 April, about 800 words, a monologue in a parked car; `01`). The lore *is* the plot. It ends on a turn ("We miss him"), and a phone call follows.
- **Backstory as motive, inside a scene**: Mackey's Bay of Pigs in broken stills while he waits; Parmenter's Guatemala inside a marriage argument (`01`).
- **Lore as the terms of a deal**: Bateman's Alpha 66 briefing is a job offer (`03`); Karlinsky's 1,800 words to Ruby end in instructions for the alley and the door, and the next scene is the act (`04`).
- **Lists**: Mackey's inventory of the flat, read through Everett's panic (`02`); Branch's lists of deaths, each closed by a verdict ("No arrests in the case"; `02`, `04`).
- **Where it stalls**: Branch on 2 July (`02`), about 900 words of the Warren Report and later deaths, placed right after Everett learns Oswald is real. The stall is deliberate, it is the subject, and it is short.
- **Where it doesn't**: Walker's 1,200-word John Birch-style monologue ("In Dallas", `03`) holds because the reader has already watched the car turn into his street.

## 4. The mechanism we came for: the compartment that breaks its own plan

**Setup** ("17 April", `01`): "Each level of the committee was designed to protect a higher level." Compartments are presented as protection.
**Rule**: one task, one reason per man. "He has his work, we have ours" (`02`); "You don't tell Frank or Wayne" (`03`).
**First use** ("20 May", `01`): Mackey briefs the shooters without target, city, or "that they were supposed to miss." The only safeguard sits in the only compartment with no rifle in it.
**Escalation** (`02`–`03`): Everett reaches Mackey only through Parmenter. Mackey, alone in a car, decides on a kill (15 July). Parmenter can't find him (12 August). Mackey cuts them off (6 September). Everett learns of the Texas trip from a newspaper (4 October). The plan gets *smaller* as it gets deadlier, from Miami's wall of shooters to "an everyday Texas homicide."
**Cost**: the designer is cut out of his own plot, and he knows plots "move toward death" (15 July).
**Payoff** (`04`): Oswald fires before the oak tree, against orders. The staged miss happens inside the hit. The police beat Wayne to the theater, so the silencing passes to a fresh compartment: Ruby, a debt and an unlocked door. Branch's verdict: "due mainly to chance."

For a writer: compartments make a plot strong against outsiders and blind from inside. SDF's Compartments chapter has the first half. Libra adds the second: a failure *inside* the organization that nobody above the middle can see.

**Against A–D.** **A** supported, and seen from outside: Oswald brings every paper to Mexico City and the clerk still won't say yes (`03`). Ruby's self-set gate ("if I'm late, it wasn't meant") is no gate (`04`). **B** run by the conspiracy (Mackey leaks Miami on purpose, `03`), and as farce: Ruby's box numbers "didn't match" and he shrugs (`04`). **C** contradicts ADDENDUM 05, which says C is only ever learned. Here it is *built*, by the antagonist (Everett's second layer, "the little surprise he was keeping for the end", `01`) and by the patsy, who hides the rifle "to give them something to uncover, a layer to strip away" (`04`). See 5.4. **D** supported, with a limit: a key opens talk, not choice (Oswald "says no", `03`).

**New mechanisms** (not in ADDENDUM 05): *the slip at the middle level* (lethal one level down, unseen above, `02`); *the originator cut out* (`03`); *the archive as containment*, where the Curator chooses, holds back and floods (`03`, `04`), extending Orwell's "why could I find it?" from one piece of bait to a whole supply; *the decoy task* (the Oswald doubles as busywork, `04`); *the found man*, already scripting himself (`01`).

## 5. Transfer to SDF

**Branch, precisely.** DeLillo keeps a pure receiver from stalling the book in six ways. (1) Short slices, 700 to 1,000 words, at chapter edges (`01`). (2) Branch is never the engine. The clock runs in the dated chapters, against a date the reader brings. (3) His material comes as lists closed by verdicts, so the form sets the pace (`02`, `04`). (4) His reading tells the reader the future ("No arrests in the case" for a man we have just met), so receiving builds suspense in the *other* line (`02`). (5) He reads the *sending*: why these objects, why now, and later what is held back (`03`, `04`). (6) He pays only in years, and the book uses him as the warning.

James can't have (1) or (2), because Part Two is his. SDF already has (2) by other means: the villain-alone chapters run the clock, and the opening gives the reader the date. It has (3) in v7 The other ten. It also has Branch's temperament: "What I do is read everything in the order it comes, at the same speed" (v7 The money). So James must do what Branch never does: **ask by name for what he wasn't sent; sort what he was sent by who sent it; spend a conclusion on a person.** The thin book already says *Ask*, and Part Two never has him do it.

Already on the page, so not proposed: one task per helper (v7 Compartments); the hunter's trace (The payload); bait set from their side ("Let her," A room with a long table); helpers' self-stories as doors (Aylin in v7 The sheets). The gate, the homework and the canary are in ADDENDUM 05.

**5.1 Ask anyway.** *The reading room (the opening); The reading room, continued; The sheets.*
- *Change:* the tray is set before he arrives, so the archive answers before he asks. He asks anyway.
- *James does:* at Aylin's desk he writes the thin book's reference on a request slip. "It's already up." He hands it over; she stamps it. Two lines.
- *Cost:* his hand now sits in the visitor book's "Shelfmark requested" column, with the same number as the professor's March line, marked *Deceased*. "Counted the night you asked" becomes an object he made, and the old man can read it back to him ("He asked too").
- *Already on the page?* No. v7 The sheets: "No shelfmark; I hadn't asked for one."
- *Already proposed?* No. The King map counts *Ask* as a theme, not an act.
- PLACEHOLDER: is the tray's ledger the item the reference names? Noor's "nobody has ever simply asked" (v8.2 The vet) conflicts with the professor asking in March.

**5.2 Pulled, not handed.** *Frank, loose.*
- *Change:* before Frank comes, James sorts everything he holds by how it reached him.
- *James does:* two piles on the table. Pulled: the thirty-one years of invoices he queried himself (v7 The money). Handed: Saskia's folder, the annex role, Julian K.'s eleven-minute reply after Kalder's tip, the schedule. Frank sees only the first pile, and James tells him why.
- *Cost:* the annex with Pieter's children, the piece most likely to move Frank, stays in the bag, so Frank's belief stops at the invoice. Saying "handed" aloud names Julian, whom Frank liked at the barbecue, as the hand behind two of the items.
- *Already on the page?* Partly. v7 Frank, loose picks "one thing he couldn't argue with" but has no rule for picking it.
- *Already proposed?* It adds to ADDENDUM 05's Frank gate and Orwell 2. New is the sort, which is the habit Branch never forms. It also explains "I believe the invoice": it is the one paper nobody gave him.

**5.3 One code, all the deaths.** *What was on the phone → The night before → A market, Sunday.*
- *Change:* on Kalder's phone, the handler codes set against this quarter's outcomes put every death under one code, his. The other codes' visits ended in sales, moves and quiet. This is Mackey: the method said "visit," a man in the middle made it "kill," and nobody above checked, because each ending was plausible.
- *James does:* he makes one extra sheet (address, date, outcome, code) and puts it only in Anneke's envelope.
- *Cost:* he hands the organization its cover story (one handler went too far), and he knows what they do with a man who embarrasses them. A market, Sunday becomes his act, and "I wanted it to be me" carries it.
- *Already on the page?* Partly. v7 What was on the phone has codes, statuses and one personal note against Kalder's. v8 A market, Sunday leaves how he was found unexplained.
- *Already proposed?* No. PLACEHOLDER: do Zwolle and Deventer fall under his code? It keeps the rules: killing is rare and a failure, and James kills no one.

**5.4 A layer to strip.** *The sheets → The leak → Leave it.*
- *Change:* while the envelopes go out by hand, James gives the count something to read. A car park says silence is worse, so he asks out loud again, about something true, dull and findable: Oswald's rifle, hidden "to do the expected thing."
- *James does:* he raises page 212 in stand-up, files a ticket, and asks Frank to co-sign. The organization does what it always does: the ticket is closed as a duplicate, and a forum thread laughs at "the blind spot."
- *Cost:* Frank, "never in it," goes on the nightly list for the first time. The decoy buys days, not safety, because the brewery man still leaks (PLACEHOLDER: how many days).
- *Already on the page?* Partly. v8 Leave it files a ticket against `rcl-agg`, and v8.2 one against page 212. That filing becomes their second.
- *Already proposed?* No. It answers ADDENDUM 05's question of whether C can be an act. Risk: James as manipulator (compare Orwell 6), though the ticket is true.

## 6. Do not take

- Real people as plotters, and invented men listed beside the real dead (`01`–`04`).
- Branch's ending: the reader who never stops, sealed in the room. James publishes, and the final conversation must not become Branch's room.
- Killing as the plot's success. Libra's plot works *because* someone dies. In SDF a death is one man's failure, and the organization prefers a sale, a move, quiet.
