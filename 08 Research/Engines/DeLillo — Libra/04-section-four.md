## Why it grips
- **Suspense moves from what happens to who knows what.** The reader already knows the outcome. The tension is in the gaps between compartments. Oswald thinks the theater is his pickup. Ferrie suspects they will both be killed in Yucatán. Wayne sits two rows behind Oswald with a silenced pistol. Mackey's private checklist ends with Ferrie spending a lonely night in Galveston, which is exactly what Ferrie does later. Each man plays his part inside a frame he cannot see, and the reader can see all of the frames.
- **The shooting is built from a dozen partial views** ("22 November"): Oswald, Raymo, Hargis, Nellie Connally, Agent Hill, the woman with the camera, Mackey across the plaza, and teletype fragments. No viewpoint holds the whole event. Oswald thinks he missed with his last shot, and Raymo thinks Oswald is "picking them off". The form itself is compartmented.
- **Small things keep interrupting the plot.** The washing machine Oswald promises Marina, Ruby wiring $25 to a stripper (receipt stamped 11:17) and opening Alpo for his dog before he walks into the garage, Wayne joining the cry of "Lights, lights" so the film starts again. These are the jokes and they are also the timing. Ruby is late enough that the timing works by accident.
- **It stays funny.** Ruby asks a DJ who Earl Warren is. Ruby boasts that he learned to vomit out of a moving car as a boy in Chicago. Mackey calls a fire truck coming down Main Street the dumbest thing he has seen in twenty years.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The plot turns out to be smaller than it looks, and the section shows how. Ferrie says outright that they did not arrange Oswald's job or the motorcade route; the coincidence, not the plot, puts Oswald in the window. Mackey thinks his own plan has one thing Win Everett's careful design lacked: luck. Mackey treats Oswald as "redundant, strictly backup", useful mainly for leaving a traceable trail. The staged miss Everett planned [from memory, not from the text: Everett wanted a spectacular miss that would be blamed on Castro] does happen, inside the hit: Oswald's shots wound and miss, and by his own account he killed no one. The plot then breaks down in steps. The police reach the theater before Wayne can shoot, and the job of silencing Oswald passes to a new compartment: Karlinsky recruits Ruby over loan forgiveness and a door left unlocked. Branch states the conclusion in the second "In Dallas": the whole thing worked through chance.

The compartments drive every scene, because every handoff is a person told only his own task and lied to about the rest. Branch's theory adds a twist: the Oswald doubles were busywork to keep Alpha 66 stuck in "countdown" while Mackey's own team was "sitting on go". In other words, the job a helper is given can be a decoy to keep him occupied.

## How the lore gets in
- **Milteer tape (first "In Dallas"), about 600 words: a transcript, then two numbered theories, then the doubles.** It is a document in Branch's hands, so it is an object. It ends on a riddle (the Mexico City photograph matches descriptions of Mackey) and cuts straight to Branch buried in paper. No stall, because each fact ends in a question.
- **Two lists of deaths (Branch passages), about 300 and 400 words.** Short dated entries, each with its verdict ("Ruled a heart attack"; "No arrests in the case"). The pace comes from the list form. They end on Branch suspecting that someone wants him to believe in coincidences.
- **Karlinsky–Ruby (second "In Dallas"), about 1,800 words of conversation.** The mob's logic arrives as an appeal to Ruby's Jewishness, his debts and his wish to be somebody, and it ends with operating instructions (the alley, the unlocked annex door, the fire stairs). The next scene is the act. No stall.
- **Marguerite's monologue ("25 November"), about 2,200 words, addressed to an unnamed judge.** It is cut in with the burial scene, so the account and the scene take turns. It is the theorist's voice: real anomalies (three yearbook photos of a boy who attended one month) beside nonsense (Marina is "trained").
- **Weird Beard on the radio, teletype, police radio:** atmosphere carried by broadcast fragments, under 400 words each.

## Mechanism notes
- **A (gate), ignored.** Mackey gives Oswald a strict gate: wait until the car clears the oak tree. Oswald fires early ("Leon fired too soon"). Ruby's gate is the reverse: he lets the clock decide (if he is too late, he was not meant to do it). For SDF: a gate a man sets himself can be fake, a way of handing the decision to chance. James's gate should be one he can fail.
- **B (canary), done cheaply and wrong.** Ruby tests a theory with physical evidence, comparing the box number on the ad with the one on the billboard: "The numbers didn't match." He shrugs it off. That is the cost of being wrong that SDF lacks, shown as farce. The Miami leak (Milteer) also worked as an accidental canary, and it cancelled the Miami operation.
- **C (front), stated by the patsy.** Oswald hides the rifle "just to do the expected thing", giving investigators a layer to strip away. Hidell is "a dozen layers", and Mackey's design means for Oswald's leftism to point to Castro. Each layer exposed is the cover story for the next.
- **D (lock and key), a door literally unlocked for one man.** Karlinsky's key is Ruby's need to make a name. Ruby walks in and thinks that if it is this easy, they must want him to do it. Marina is the counter-example: the door she keeps shut.
- **Fifth: the archive as containment (strongest for SDF).** The Curator floods Branch, holds some things back, then sends fiction and a 400-page study of Lincoln–Kennedy parallels. Branch suspects someone wants him to believe in coincidences, and decides the room was built to hold him. Proposal: SDF's diagnosis (James mostly *receives* documents) could become the plot. James, who reads everything, slowly sees that the documents arrive to keep him reading, the way SDF's organisation funds nonsense so every real question has a clown beside it. Receiving becomes the trap he has to notice. Not on the page in v8.2 as far as this reader knows (PLACEHOLDER).
- **The reader becomes the researcher.** Oswald in his cell plans to read every book about his own case, and Marguerite vows to time his movements. Three people end up reading the same case: the killer, his mother and the archivist.

## Quotes
- "We didn't arrange your job in that building or set up the motorcade route." (first "In Dallas"): the plot admits how small it is.
- "He wanted to give them something to uncover, a layer to strip away." ("22 November"): the front, planned by the patsy.
- "the conspiracy against the President was a rambling affair that succeeded in the short term due mainly to chance." (second "In Dallas"): Branch's verdict.
- "Branch wonders if there is some limit inherent in the yielding of information gathered in secret." (second "In Dallas"): the archive holds things back from its own reader.

## Flags
