## Why it grips
- **The planner learns his invention exists.** On 19 June Everett is at his worktable flattening a Diners Club card and looking forward to choosing his gunman's names. On 2 July Mackey's inventory arrives as a list: two draft cards, Hidell as chapter president, signatures in different hands, a Minox at the back of a closet. Everett feels displaced and panics, and the reader does too. There is no longer any gap between the man he made up and the real one.
- **Each conspirator has his own chapter, and none has the whole plot.** Banister drinking at the bar, Elko on a bench in Denver, Ferrie at Carmine's lodge, Raymo and Frank at an old men's softball game. Each scene is complete and often funny, and each holds one piece. The reader puts together what the men cannot. Elko guesses the job is serious from how little Raymo and Frank say.
- **Both sides are guessing.** Kirilenko (In Moscow) suspects the KGB knows less about the boy than the boy knows about them, and he has a recruitment quota to meet. The U-2 altitude dispute turns Oswald's possible mistakes into intelligence. The enemy service is as baffled as the reader.
- **It stays funny:** Banister's fifteen Kennedy doubles, Oswald writing to Connally because he thinks Connally runs the Navy, the State Department getting rid of Marguerite with a snow forecast, the Weird Beard on the radio.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The plot is small by design. Everett wants a miss: a Secret Service man winged so the attempt looks real, and a trail of papers to Cuban intelligence, half fabricated and half real. It gets away from him through the compartments he built himself. He reaches Mackey only through Parmenter, and Camp Street only through Mackey. Driving alone, Mackey decides to take it all the way. He picks each recruit for a single job (Elko kept short-range, nowhere near the President). He thinks about inventing a target for Banister so Banister never learns the real one. Banister has already stated the rule: "He has his work, we have ours." By 12 August Parmenter cannot find Mackey, and Everett lets himself be reassured. So nobody owns how this ends. Everett's own theory (15 July) is that plots move towards death, and the rules he built to keep his plot contained are what let it get there.

## How the lore gets in
- **Everett's planted documents (19 June), about 450 words, told as he walks to his car.** A fake DDP memo, a news item planted in an exile magazine, phone doodles on TSD stationery. It does not stall, because each item is something he is making.
- **Mackey's inventory of the flat (2 July), about 500 words, a list.** It comes through Everett's panic, so it lands as a shock.
- **Branch (2 July), about 900 words.** The Warren Report's contents, one FBI memo given verbatim, the photographs, then three later deaths (Banister, Vasquez, Roselli), each closed with "No arrests in the case." It sits straight after Everett learns Oswald is real. Records of what happened later arrive as flat entries in a file, and they tell us ahead of time that Frank, whom we have only just met, will be shot. It stalls on purpose: Branch believes every item belongs, the Agency paid to fireproof his room, and he bills them for less than he spent.
- **Kirilenko's thinking (In Moscow), about 1,200 words, interior.** It gives the KGB's procedure through one tired man's doubts, broken up by the polygraph and Powers scenes.
- **Marguerite's monologues addressed to "your honor", and Postcards 3 to 5.** She is the first to say conspiracy, and sounds unhinged saying it.

## Mechanism notes
- **C (front) is Everett's whole design.** The Cuban trail is a layer meant to be solved, and he leaves it imperfect on purpose so that it reads as real. That is SDF's deliberate typos and the seat list left "findable". What Libra adds is a way for the front to fail: the real man turns out stranger than the cover. SDF's cover stories could be outrun by a real helper who goes further than he was asked.
- **The plan slips at a middle level** (15 July, 12 August). It turns deadly one level down, with one man in a car going one step further, not at the top. That is a ready-made way for SDF's one-task helpers to fail, and it fits the rule that killing is a failure. PROPOSAL: in The other ten, one of the deaths turns out to be a helper who acted on his own, and the organisation is as alarmed about it as James is.
- **D (lock and key)** (In Fort Worth). Collings's price is one specific truth, how the KGB handled Oswald, and he pays with a job. Each SDF helper could likewise be opened by the one question only his own task can answer.
- **B (canary), turned round** (In Moscow). Oswald signs up for a Russian exam to see whether Naval Intelligence notices him, and scores "poor" all the way down.
- **Branch is the warning for James.** Branch has every document and no nose for which one matters, and the people paying for his room are the people he studies. PROPOSAL: in The reading room continued, someone points out that James's reading is supplied by the organisation, and the order of what he was handed shows what they wanted him to read first. That gives him a cost of being wrong.
- **Fifth: everyone makes paper.** Banister opens an empty file and says material will come to it. Oswald writes down the FBI agent's licence plate "to build up the record". Beryl writes to her friends in newspaper clippings. In this section the conspiracy runs on documents, SDF's own medium.

## Quotes
- "It was no longer possible to hide from the fact that Lee Oswald existed independent of the plot." (2 July): the turn, as a single realisation.
- "Once you start a file, Delphine, it's just a matter of time before the material comes pouring in." (19 June): the file pulls in material.
- "A fact is innocent until someone wants it. Then it becomes intelligence." (In Fort Worth): the rule for the documents James receives.
- "Plots carry their own logic. There is a tendency of plots to move toward death." (15 July): the planner is afraid of his own plot.

## Flags
- Real people are shown as complicit or connected: Banister, Ferrie, Ruby, de Mohrenschildt (passing Oswald's work to the CIA) and Roselli. The invented planners (Everett, Parmenter, Mackey), mob boss (Carmine Latta) and CIA man (Collings) are mixed in with them, and Branch's list puts the invented Vasquez beside the real dead. SDF must not do this.
- Everett's fakes are pinned on real CIA units and on real attempts on Castro.
- No invented edits on real books.
