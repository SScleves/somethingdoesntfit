## Why it grips
- **Several clocks run together and share one fear.** The SD are one phone call behind Baynes (ch. 11); Frink's money is leaking out; Joe's talk makes Juliana more uneasy each mile. Chapter 11 ends the Reiss scene on a joke: the second phone call is a teacher asking for scenic posters.
- **Dramatic irony does the work.** The reader knows Frank Frink and the Jew in the Kempeitai photo are one man (ch. 7). Childan signs a paper that leaves him pleased about "law and order", and Dick lets him think it all through.
- **Each important scene is a trade of face.** In the Kasoura dinner (ch. 7) and the office scene (ch. 11), each man offers something and the other decides what it means. Childan reads Paul's praise of the pin as a cold insult, and the reader cannot be sure whether Childan or Paul understood it right.
- **Violence is quick and comic first.** The Colt scene (ch. 12) turns from a siege of phone calls (including Tagomi's stage-rage at the SD) into a jaw shot away in two sentences, followed by Tagomi saying "Sickening".

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The conspiracy is in the foreground and it drives events. Dandelion (the plan to destroy Japan) is told to Tedeki in about 800 words (ch. 12). It gives the five-way scene its reason: the SD must catch Baynes before the message reaches Tokyo. Goebbels' call to Reiss (ch. 11) is the proof that the SD can use the Chancery's name, and the attempted snatch follows from it. The conspiracy also fills the background in smaller ways: Reiss's habit of covering himself with timestamped replies (ch. 8), and Kempeitai's paper that "ends your involvement" (ch. 7), which is how the state gets signed statements from ordinary people. It costs the people on the edge: Frink is arrested for something real (the Navy-officer pose) that the state then upgrades into something larger.

## How the lore gets in
- **The Grasshopper, read aloud by Reiss and then by Juliana (ch. 8, 10).** Excerpts of a page or two each, set in the middle of scenes where someone else interrupts: Pferdehuf cuts in again and again in ch. 8, Joe interrupts Juliana in ch. 10. The reading stalls on purpose and the interruption relieves it.
- **Joe's summary of the book's plot (ch. 10, about 700 words).** This is a debate inside a moving car, and Joe's reaction to the book (he is angry at the praise for the New Deal) shows his character more than it shows the book. The story keeps moving because a man is driving.
- **Dandelion (ch. 12, about 800 words).** A conversation between three men who each have something to hide. The lecture is given as a negotiation: Tedeki fills in the missing parts ("The date, sir") so the reader hears the plan as a test of the speaker.
- **Paul on wu (ch. 11, about 700 words).** A monologue, but the listener is wrecked by it, so the scene is about Childan's reaction.

## Mechanism notes
How each leak of another world is delivered in this section:
- **Leak 1: the novel (ch. 7, 8, 10).** In Childan's hands it is an object he picks up as small talk, and his reading changes nothing; he decides to buy it as "patriotic duty". In Reiss's hands it is contraband in a briefcase; he reads it in a locked office, resents its grandeur, and almost writes a murder order (ch. 8). In Juliana's hands it is a book in a car; she asks to hear the plot and Joe gives away the ending. Cost: Reiss's anger, and Childan's shame in front of the Kasouras. It **pushes** the plot twice (Reiss's note to Skorzeny, burned; Joe's plan to visit the author, ch. 10) and **colors** it elsewhere.
- **Leak 2: the pin and its "historicity" (ch. 9, 11).** The piece is made by two men who are not artists. Childan sees it as a hedge ("no historicity either real or imagined"). Paul says it has wu. Cost: Childan's obligation. It **pushes**: Childan must now deal with the importer, and Frink and Ed's venture changes.
- **Leak 3: the oracle (ch. 9, 12).** Tagomi gets "Shock" at 7 a.m. (ch. 10) and the telephone rings at once with Yatabe's arrival. Frink avoids asking (ch. 9) and later says he needs no oracle to know defeat (ch. 12). The oracle is a **coloring device** that nudges Tagomi to act, but it never tells him anything he did not already half know.
- **Leak 4: "an external frame of reference" (ch. 12, Tedeki).** Tedeki says the book will help Tagomi recover. That is the section's only direct claim that a leaked fragment of another world has a use: it gives a man something to stand on after an act with no meaning.
- **A gate / lock-and-key.** Dandelion needs Baynes to pass his information on to the Japanese, and the SD must stop him. Baynes uses a department-store clerk and a code phrase, a named clerk ("Larry") (ch. 10), and the Japanese agent answers within six minutes. This is a lock-and-key door that opens by recognition, not force.
- **Front.** The "Swedish industrialist" cover is a front, but Tedeki says it exists "to require the formalities in case of exposure". A cover story exists to make the other side prove its case in law.
- **No canary here.** Nothing is planted to see who believes it. Joe steering Juliana toward visiting the author (ch. 10) is the nearest thing, and it is his own aim, not a test.

## Quotes
- "Amazing, the power of fiction, even cheap popular fiction, to evoke." (ch. 8): Reiss's reaction to a leaked world, in the mouth of its enemy.
- "To have no historicity, and also no artistic, esthetic worth, and yet to partake of some ethereal value—that is a marvel." (ch. 11): the section's description of an object with value but no history.
- "That book will help him, for it provides an external frame of reference." (ch. 12): the book as a tool for a character in trouble.
- "Bit of knowledge like that goes a long way." (ch. 9): knowing a thing is fake changes a man's whole relation to his stock.

## Flags
- Slurs and antisemitism appear in the characters' heads and mouths (ch. 7, 8, 12). They are the characters' views and Dick treats them as such, but SDF must not copy the voice.
- Real people named in the fiction as complicit in alternate-history crimes (Goebbels, Heydrich, Bormann, Skorzeny, Ohlendorf). SDF must not do this with real people.
- The Grasshopper is an invented book with invented events about real history. SDF's rule bans an invented edit on a real book; this novel does something else (a whole invented book), so nothing here transfers directly.
- Outside knowledge was not used.
