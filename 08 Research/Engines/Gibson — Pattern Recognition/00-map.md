# Gibson, *Pattern Recognition* (2003) — map

> **Coverage:** English, read in full via three section notes (`01-chapters-1-to-11.md`, `02-chapters-12-to-26.md`, `03-chapters-27-to-43.md`), 11 Nov 2026. Plot facts and chapter numbers are the notes'.
> **Brief:** read for a protagonist with a nose, not a method; a forum as a real place; clues as physical objects. Question: how does the hidden thing make Cayce act, and what would James *do* if we borrowed it.
> **Standing-rule note:** real brands as triggers (Bibendum, Tommy Hilfiger), Kevin Bacon's dog in passing, real institutions as background (Echelon, an NSA pension, "ex-KGB"), 9/11 as the private wound. None is complicit, but SDF wants invented companies. Take the mechanics, not the brands.

---

## 1. What it is

Cayce Pollard, a logo consultant whose body reacts to brands, is hired in London by the ad man Bigend to find whoever makes anonymous film fragments that a web forum, F:F:F, obsesses over. Her flat is entered, a rival (Dorotea) attacks her through her phobia, and a forum friend's invented Japanese girl gets her a watermark number in Tokyo. The number leads to a map, to a Russian apparatus that has been watching the forum, to an ex-NSA man who trades an address for an antique calculator, and to the maker: Nora, whose footage is the shape of the shrapnel in her brain. Cayce is drugged and held, refuses to name her source, and is paid off with a file on her missing father.

What makes it work: every step is an object someone hands her, and every handover costs her something personal (shame, privacy, a lie told in person). Her gift is also the hole in her armor, so the same nose that finds things is what her enemies use to steer her.

The "cannot stop" column runs on two motors: competition early (CNN, a rival finding the maker), then, from ch. 24, the fact that stopping gives the enemy what it paid for. Nobody threatens her into going on; Bigend lets every no stand and changes what she knows instead (01).

## 3. How the lore gets in

- **The footage** stays an object: fragments watched (ch. 1–2), then data (a number, a T, a 75% shape match, ch. 33), then meaning, by watching one edit (ch. 37).
- **The forum**: factions in about 700 words, read behind a barricaded door (ch. 6); Parkaboy's emails, 300–900 words, each ending in a question or an instruction (ch. 8, 14, 16, 19).
- **Bigend's theories** (about 1,000 words, ch. 6–7) are always a pitch, so we watch him work on her.
- **The watermark**: short Q&As with people who argue back (Voytek, ch. 9; Boone over noodles, ch. 11; Ngemi on Echelon, to justify a price, ch. 30).
- **The Russians**: a hostile confession (about 900 words, ch. 24) and a 2,000-word explanation placed *after* the climax (ch. 41), where each item explains a weirdness already shown.

**Where it stalls:** Win's 9/11 disappearance (ch. 15), about 1,000 words of memory held up by one falling rose petal. Deliberate, and still the one place the plot stops: nothing in it changes what she does next. **Where it doesn't:** Dorotea's confession (ch. 24). Cayce interrupts, Dorotea needles, every exchange lands a fact, and we wait to see if Cayce walks out. The rule: a lore scene holds if someone in it wants something from the listener *now*.

## 4. The mechanism we came for

**Setup.** The nose is defined by its price before it is used: a yes/no instrument with no reasons ("either blue or it's not," ch. 2), then the Hilfiger collapse in Harvey Nichols (ch. 3). Gibson spends as long on the damage as on the gift.

**Rule.** The nose reads surfaces, not people working against her. It misses the scooter rider who is tailing her (ch. 16); Boone, who has a method, spots him. And it comes with a built-in check, her father's rule on paranoia (ch. 13, ch. 36). Labor is split: nose (Cayce), method (Boone), reach (the forum); she never cracks anything herself (02).

**First use, against her.** Dorotea's "wrong envelope" with Bibendum gets a verdict out of her through her trigger; then the doll hangs outside her rekeyed door (ch. 10). The locks hold and don't help.

**Escalation.** The forum goes from refuge to fishbowl (ch. 7, 9) to people ("everting," ch. 23). Every clue is a copy that can be lost or taken: napkin, palm, notepad, laptop (ch. 17–19).

**Cost.** Shame and privacy: catfishing Taki (ch. 17), the therapist's notes (ch. 24).

**Payoff.** The allergy becomes the alarm: the hallucinated Michelin Man with her father's voice, "She's drugged the water. Scream." (ch. 38). And the tripwire is revealed: one throwaway post guessing at a Russian patron put her on their list (ch. 41). She had reread it twice (ch. 33, 36) without knowing.

**Against A–D.**
- **A (gate): weak.** Only social tests (Taki checks "Keiko" with *Anne of Green Gables*, ch. 17). Nothing must be proved before she acts; Gibson does not fix SDF's missing gate.
- **B (canary): central, and it bites back.** Keiko is invented to see who bites (ch. 8); Judy, the real model, finds out and threatens a journalist (ch. 23). Reversed: Mama Anarchia gives herself away by knowing Tokyo, and a friend notices (ch. 27).
- **C (front): layered, then deflated.** Franco → Dorotea → a Russian "if indeed he was from Cyprus" → "them" (ch. 24), and the top is small and protective (ch. 41).
- **D (lock and key): with a price.** Baranov trades the address for a Curta bought on Bigend's money, and the tie comes due (ch. 29–31). Forum trivia works as a password (ch. 40).

**Fifth mechanisms the evidence supports.** (1) **The nose as attack surface**: whoever knows what the gifted person reacts to can feed her or freeze her (ch. 10, 27). (2) **Holding back as the active choice**: three allies in a Hummer, each withholding; her silence makes her part of the lie (ch. 20). For a James who mostly *receives*, deciding what to pass on is the action. (3) **The hero's own old post as the tripwire** (ch. 41). (4) **The reward as a trap**: they pay her to be quiet; she gives the money away and keeps the file (ch. 43).

## 6. Do not take

- **The violent third act.** Gun, drugging, kidnapping, a private prison (ch. 29, 38–40). SDF's organization discredits and kills rarely; the one drugging in the book is Ana's.
- **Outsourced intrusion.** Rice decrypts, Baranov sells sig-int, Boone trails and copies (ch. 17, 25, 29–31). If a helper hacks for James, James has hacked.
- **The deflated conspiracy.** Gibson's top turns out small and protective, an uncle shielding nieces (ch. 41). SDF's is large and real. Take "we are most often the merest of cogs" for the helpers, not for the old man.
