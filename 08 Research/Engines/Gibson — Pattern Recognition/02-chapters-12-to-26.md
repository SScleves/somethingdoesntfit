## Why it grips
- **Each object is handed on and copied, and every copy is a risk.** The number goes from Taki's Palm to the napkin (the ink is already spreading), to her palm, to a hotel notepad (ch. 17–19), then to Boone and Rice. The map goes from Mystic to Taki, to "Keiko" (really Parkaboy), to Cayce, to Boone. Holding a copy becomes the plot: the muggers go for her bag, and Boone's first question is "Is it in the laptop?" (ch. 17).
- **The nose gets checked, not trusted.** When Billy Prion turns up on her flight (ch. 13), she runs her father's rule on paranoia over the hunch, and a TV ad shows he had an ordinary reason to be there (ch. 14). Then her nose misses the real threat: she clocks the RAF roundel on a scooter rider's parka (ch. 16) without linking him to the visor in Kabukicho. Boone, who has a method, spotted the tail. Her gift reads surfaces, not people working against her.
- **The suspense is in what allies keep from each other.** Chapter 20 puts three people in a Hummer, each holding something back. Boone leaves out the head-butt and the map, and Cayce realises her silence makes her part of the lie. The scene that does the most work is the meeting room in ch. 24. The man with the taped nose has just run past her at the door (ch. 23), and the enemy sits at the table as a new hire. Bigend's "you are talking about loyalty, not honesty" turns the conspiracy into an employment deal.
- **Still funny** throughout: the "dickhead" phone call (ch. 23), Ngemi buying "Stephen King's Wang" (ch. 26).

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
Gibson runs the two hidden things on separate tracks.

**Who makes the footage** moves only through objects and the people who give them up: a number, a map pulled from a white flash, a watermarking firm. Cayce cracks nothing: Mystic decrypts, Taki hands over, Parkaboy forwards, Rice confirms segment 78 (ch. 25). What Cayce brings is access and judgement, and the access costs her shame.

**Who wants to know** moves through pressure on her body and her privacy: the alley (ch. 17), then the therapist's notes (ch. 24). Its stated aim is to drive her away, not to stop her. That is very close to SDF's organisation, which discredits rather than prevents. Gibson's turn: the opponent is *bought*, not beaten. Bigend hires Dorotea, so the front-line agent becomes a source, and the pressure moves up a level, to "an organization of some kind" behind the man from Cyprus (ch. 25), and the threat grows with it: Boone says they could turn Dorotea back with a threat to her life (ch. 25).

## How the lore gets in
- **Win's 9/11 disappearance (ch. 15), about 1,000 words, memory.** A deliberate stall between the jet-lag night and the Taki errand, held up by one small object: a dead rose petal falling in a shop window.
- **Parkaboy's emails (ch. 14, 16, 19), 300–600 words each, documents.** Each has a joke and ends in an instruction or a new object. No stall.
- **Cynthia's EVP email (ch. 21), about 400 words, a document.** It gives four numbered "files" of what her dead father supposedly says. Funny and painful, it repeats the plot's idea of a message hidden in noise, like the map pulled from the whiteout. A 700-word memory follows; Damien at the stairs ends it.
- **Dorotea's confession (ch. 24), about 900 words, a hostile conversation.** Cayce keeps interrupting, Dorotea needles her, and every exchange lands a fact. No stall: the reader waits to see whether Cayce walks out (she sits with her legs out from under the table).
- **Hobbs Baranov (ch. 26), about 600 words, dinner gossip.** The next helper is planted as a tipsy sibling squabble, before he is needed.

## Mechanism notes
- **A (gate), small and social.** Before he gives up the number, Taki tests whether "Keiko" is real by asking if she likes Anne of Green Gables (ch. 17). The invented persona nearly fails. Cayce then sets her own gate on Boone: she will keep working with him only if he keeps her informed (ch. 25). Both test belonging, not a theory.
- **B (canary), already central, and it bites back.** Keiko is an invented person planted to see who believes in her, and Taki believes. Chapter 23 sends the bill: Judy, the real model, finds out, takes Taki's side and threatens to go to a journalist. Boone runs a quieter one in the car (ch. 20–21): he leaves the map out to see if Bigend already knows. For SDF: let the canary bite back through a real person.
- **C (front), layered.** Franco works for Dorotea, Dorotea for a Russian "if indeed he was from Cyprus", and he for "them" (ch. 24). Each exposed layer opens onto another; Boone even suspects Blue Ant (ch. 18).
- **D (lock and key).** Literal first: Damien's new German keys, and whom to trust with them (ch. 12). Then the number unlocks the map, and the map points to the next lock, Sigil.
- **Fifth: the forum becomes a place with a past.** On the plane Cayce searches a CD of every F:F:F post and finds her own first one, from before she added the P (ch. 13). For James, whose posts get edited, this is the model: the forum archive is an object, and what the forum made up can turn up as a person.
- **Division of labour.** Nose (Cayce), method (Boone, ch. 25), reach (the forum): the heroine never hacks. SDF can copy this if James pays each helper in shame, trust or money.
