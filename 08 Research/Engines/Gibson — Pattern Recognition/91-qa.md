# Gibson, *Pattern Recognition* — questions an SDF agent will ask

> Answers point to the section notes in this folder: `01` = `01-chapters-1-to-11.md`, `02` = `02-chapters-12-to-26.md`, `03` = `03-chapters-27-to-43.md`. SDF chapters are named by their v8.2 title. Everything is a proposal; Santi's decisions are PLACEHOLDER. 11 Nov 2026.

## The nose

**1. What exactly is Cayce's "nose," and how is it different from James's?**
It is a yes/no reaction to brands with no reasons attached ("either blue or it's not," ch. 2), and it is close to an allergy (ch. 1). James's nose, in "The good life," is a hunter's instinct for things that don't fit, with a checking habit behind it. Cayce's is a bodily reaction that can be set off on purpose; James's isn't, yet. See `01`, "Why it grips."

**2. How does Gibson keep a gifted heroine from being always right?**
He gives the gift a blind spot and a check. It reads surfaces, not people working against her: she sees the RAF roundel on the scooter rider and doesn't connect him to the man who tails her (ch. 16). And her father's rule on paranoia is run over every hunch (ch. 13). See `02`, "Why it grips."

**3. Does the nose ever get checked and come back negative?**
Yes. When Billy Prion turns up on her flight, she runs her father's rule, and a TV ad shows he had an ordinary reason to be there (ch. 13–14). That is the shape 00 SYNTHESIS Rule 2 asks for, but it costs Cayce nothing. Proposal 5.2 in `00-map.md` adds a cost. See `02`.

**4. How do enemies use the nose against her?**
Dorotea shows her Bibendum in a client meeting and gets "It works" out of her to make it stop, then hangs a felt one on her door (ch. 10). Later Dorotea's forum persona, Mama Anarchia, is written for her by a hired writer (ch. 38). Whoever knows the trigger can steer her. See `01` "Mechanism notes" (fifth mechanism) and `03`.

**5. Can that "nose as attack surface" work for James without breaking SDF's rules?**
Yes, if the bait is information, not force. Kalder mentions an invented book whose printings differ, and James spends money and a lie to Ana chasing a clean negative (proposal 5.2, in "The corporate day" or "Enjoy it while it lasts"). No threat, no break-in. See `01` and `03`.

**6. Does the nose pay off at the end?**
Twice. The allergy becomes the alarm: a hallucinated Michelin Man with her father's warning, "She's drugged the water. Scream." (ch. 38). Then the allergy is gone (ch. 43), closed with her father's case. See `03`, "Mechanism notes."

**7. Who does the work the nose can't do?**
Boone has the method, the forum has the reach, and specialists do the decrypting (Rice, ch. 11 and 25; Mystic, per `02`). Cayce brings access and judgment and pays for it in shame. SDF can copy the division of labor only if no helper hacks for James. See `02`, "Mechanism notes."

## The forum

**8. How is F:F:F made to feel like a real place?**
It is her refuge and a home ("a friend's living room," ch. 7), it has factions with names (ch. 6), and its people have voices in long, funny emails (Parkaboy, ch. 8, 14, 16, 19). Then it "everts": the people turn up in the flesh (ch. 23). See `01` and `02`.

**9. Who watches the forum in the book?**
Bigend reads her posts and knows CayceP is Cayce (ch. 7). Blue Ant's Trans seeds the footage and debriefs agents (ch. 9). And a Russian apparatus pays graduate students to lurk; one broke the no-posting rule (ch. 41). See `01` and `03`.

**10. What is the "old post as tripwire"?**
Cayce's throwaway post guessing at a Russian mafia patron is what put her on the apparatus's list: from her ISP to her name, to her father's dossier, to the break-in (ch. 41). She rereads the post twice (ch. 33, 36) before she learns that. See `03`, "How the conspiracy moves the story here."

**11. Is that already in SDF?**
Partly. "The gorilla mask" has James's twelve-year-old persona edited (v8.2; v6 draft has the eleven readers). v8 "Leave it" says the invoice stepped up the week he typed a question into a forum, which conflicts with v8.2 and v7 "The money" (the ticket week). What Gibson adds is the old post as the first cause, found by rereading. See proposal 5.5 and `03`.

**12. Does Cayce search her own forum history?**
Yes. On the plane she searches a CD of every F:F:F post and finds her first one, from before she added the P to her handle (ch. 13). The archive is an object she holds. For James: a copy of his own thread before the edits, printed and put on the tablecloth in "Twenty minutes." See `02`, "Mechanism notes."

**13. Do forum facts work as passwords?**
Yes. Parkaboy proves who he is with forum trivia, "Who started the thread that gave Completism its first formal basis?" (ch. 40). SDF already plans a password for "The sheets" (the deleted phrasing). Gibson's version tests belonging to a place, not knowledge of a document. See `03`.

**14. How did Noor-style contact happen in Gibson?**
Not by a forum message. The closest is Ivy, a forum friend, noticing that Mama Anarchia knows Cayce is in Tokyo, which she can't know, and reporting it (ch. 27). That is already the shape of v6 "Leiden": Noor spots a style-guide phrase in the reply to James. Don't propose it again. See `03`.

**15. What is the forum's canary in Gibson, and does it cost anything?**
Parkaboy and Musashi invent "Keiko" to see who bites; Taki does and offers the number (ch. 8). The bill comes in ch. 23: Judy, the real model, finds out, takes Taki's side, and threatens a journalist. See `01` and `02`.

## Clues as objects

**16. How does Gibson turn an abstract mystery into an object?**
He narrows "who makes the footage" to "what is the number in #78" (ch. 11), then the number to twelve digits on a napkin (ch. 17), then a T-shaped map (ch. 19). Each step is a thing that can be carried, copied, or stolen. See `01` "How the conspiracy moves the story here" and `02`.

**17. Why does copying matter?**
Every copy is a risk and becomes plot: napkin to palm to hotel notepad to Boone (ch. 17–19); the muggers go for her bag, and Boone's first question is "Is it in the laptop?" (ch. 17). SDF's equivalent: James photographing the annex because he no longer trusts systems to keep things overnight (v7 "The money"). See `02`.

**18. How does the final reveal arrive?**
As a shape match with no meaning (75% of the T matches part of a Claymore mine, ch. 33), then a hint in Stella's story (ch. 35), then the meaning by watching Nora edit for three hours (ch. 37). Data first, feeling later. SDF's 02:14 does the same across "Leave it" and "The night he stops being afraid." See `03`.

**19. Does any big reveal come as a document?**
Yes: Win's last morning, three blue sheets she reads three times (ch. 42), confirmed by lawyers (ch. 43). It closes the private wound, not the plot. See `03`, "How the lore gets in."

## Lore and pacing

**20. Where does the book stall?**
Win's 9/11 disappearance (ch. 15), about 1,000 words of memory between two errands, held up by one rose petal. Nothing in it changes what Cayce does next. See `02`, "How the lore gets in."

**21. How does a 2,000-word explanation not stall at the end?**
It comes after the climax and works as a rereading: each item explains a weirdness already shown (the dust rectangle, the therapist's notes, the man from Cyprus). Wiktor pressing her for the source keeps it tense (ch. 41). Compare "The final conversation," which also comes after the win. See `03`.

**22. How are Bigend's theory speeches kept moving?**
Every lecture is part of a pitch, so the reader watches him work on her (ch. 6–7). The old man in "The reading room, continued" can work the same way: his method speech is a pitch for five seats. See `01`.

**23. What makes Dorotea's confession work as lore?**
It is hostile: Cayce interrupts, Dorotea needles, every exchange lands a fact, and the reader waits to see if Cayce walks out (ch. 24). A lore scene holds if someone in it wants something from the listener now. See `02`.

**24. How long are the forum documents?**
Parkaboy's letter about 900 words (ch. 8); later emails 300–600 (ch. 14, 16, 19); Cynthia's EVP email about 400 (ch. 21). Each has a joke and ends on a question, an instruction, or a new object. See `01` and `02`.

## The engine and the opponent

**25. Why can't Cayce stop?**
Early: competition (CNN airs #135, ch. 8) and a contract. From ch. 24: somebody paid Dorotea to make her leave London, so quitting gives them what they paid for. See `00-map.md` section 2 and `02`.

**26. Does anyone threaten her to keep her going?**
No. She refuses Bigend three times and he lets each no stand, changing what she knows instead (ch. 7, 10). Pressure comes from the opponent, aimed at making her leave. That fits SDF's rule that doors open only when a person chooses. See `01`, "Why it grips."

**27. How close is Gibson's opponent to SDF's organization?**
On aim, close: it wants her gone, not stopped ("To make you sufficiently uncomfortable that you would leave London," ch. 24). On scale and violence, not: it ends small and protective, and it uses a gun, drugs, and a private prison (ch. 29, 38–41). See `02` and `03`.

**28. Is there a front (mechanism C)?**
Yes, layered: Franco works for Dorotea, Dorotea for a Russian "if indeed he was from Cyprus," and he for "them" (ch. 24). Bigend then hires Dorotea, so the agent becomes a source (ch. 24). See `02`, "Mechanism notes."

**29. Is there a gate (mechanism A)?**
Weak. Taki tests "Keiko" with *Anne of Green Gables* (ch. 17); Cayce makes Boone keep her informed (ch. 25). Nothing must be proved before she acts (03). For SDF, proposal 5.3 uses the social test as Noor's gate in "Leiden." See `02` and `03`.

**30. Is there a lock and key (mechanism D)?**
Yes, with a price: Baranov won't take money and trades the address for a Curta bought on Bigend's money, which ties her to Bigend (ch. 29–31). See `03`. Don't copy the sig-int; copy the idea that the key is paid for with someone else's credit, which comes due.

**31. What does "holding back" look like as action?**
Chapter 20: three people in a Hummer, each keeping something back; Boone lies to Bigend and Cayce's silence makes her complicit. Later she lies to Boone and refuses to name her source (ch. 32–41). Proposal 5.4 gives James the same choice with Pieter in "The money." See `02` and `03`.

**32. How is the payoff used as a trap?**
The apparatus pays her: cash and a file on her father. She gives the money away and keeps the file (ch. 43). In SDF the deal in "Monday" and "The final conversation" is a payment for silence; James asks only for the book he may publish. See `03`.
