## Why it grips
- **The gift is the weak spot.** Cayce's "nose" is set up in ch. 2 as a yes/no instrument with no reasons attached ("She has no way of knowing how she knows"; the carpet is "either blue or it's not"). Gibson spends the same pages on its price: the Tommy Hilfiger collapse in Harvey Nichols (ch. 3), the duck mantra (ch. 4). So when Dorotea pulls Bibendum out of the envelope (ch. 10), the reader already knows exactly how much damage it does. The scene is built for that payoff: a fake "wrong envelope", Stonestreet unable to see the board, and Cayce saying "It works" to make it stop. Her enemy gets the verdict out of her through her nose.
- **Small physical anomalies do the threatening.** No intruder is seen. The menace is one browser-history line above the forum's, a roll of tape on its end, one redial (ch. 5). She tests each against memory (new keys, the only set), and the checking builds the dread.
- **Refusal as the engine.** She says no to Bigend three times (ch. 7, ch. 10). Each time he lets the no stand ("You need to sleep on it") and changes what she knows instead: CNN, Trans, Boone. She is never pushed; the world shifts until no stops being worth saying.
- **Funny next to the dread.** Teaching Bigend to wear his Stetson (ch. 7), "be French" (ch. 9), Kevin Bacon's dog (ch. 10), Parkaboy's voice (ch. 8).

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
Two hidden things, and the section stitches them together. The maker is a pure question that blocks nothing and asks nothing of Cayce; the forum is where she puts her worry (ch. 6, "Psychological prophylaxis"). Then come the people who want to know. Bigend turns the question into a job; Magda's bar story (ch. 9) makes it worse: Blue Ant is already seeding the footage and debriefing its agents "for responses". The secret worth fearing is now the hunters', and they are inside her refuge.

Dorotea is a separate threat with no proven motive (Bigend offers jealousy, ch. 7). Her attacks are deniable: a burn, a browser line, a doll. Her old trade, Bigend says, was "black PR". She wrecks Cayce's footing without stopping her, and Bigend uses the moment to bring in Boone, the man with the tools.

The driving question narrows into an object, from "who makes it" to "what is the number in #78". A number can be fetched, at the cost of a lie told in person.

## How the lore gets in
- **Forum factions (ch. 6), about 700 words, narration** (Progressives, Completists, Garage Kubrick). Read behind a table jammed against the door, so it runs over live pressure; it ends with her posting as referee and the phone ringing. No stall.
- **The Curta (ch. 4), about 200 words, an object plus a lecture.** Ngemi puts the "grenade" in her hand, then explains it. It also plants Hobbs (ex-NSA, ch. 9) long before he is needed.
- **Parkaboy's letter (ch. 8), about 900 words, a document.** The biggest piece of lore, in a funny, vain voice, ending on a question ("So what to do?") that turns lore into a decision.
- **Bigend's theory (ch. 6–7), about 1,000 words over two scenes, conversation.** Every lecture is part of a pitch, so we watch him work on her.
- **Watermarking and rendering farms (ch. 9 and ch. 11), each about 250–350 words, Q&A.** Voytek answers in broken English; Boone argues over noodles and Cayce wins with feeling ("Lonely?").

## Mechanism notes
- **B (canary), run twice, by both sides.** Parkaboy and Musashi invent Keiko, a person who does not exist, and watch who bites. Taki does, and offers the number (ch. 8). Blue Ant runs the corporate version: Trans plants a mention, then debriefs for responses (ch. 9). Maurice asks if the Vatican story is a hoax (ch. 9): the forum tests too. For SDF the useful part is the bill. The invented persona pays off only if a real person turns up and plays it (ch. 11, "And a lie"). If James plants something through his old forum persona, collecting on it should mean being that persona, in person, at a cost.
- **The watched forum.** Bigend knows CayceP is Cayce from her posts (ch. 7). He never breaks in; he reads, and seeds. That is close to SDF's edited posts, and it suggests the edits should come with a reader who quotes James back to himself.
- **D (lock and key).** Cayce secures the flat properly (new locks, hair and powder, ch. 6–7). The perimeter holds, and the enemy hangs the doll on the outside of the door (ch. 10). Locks do not stop someone who knows what frightens you. Taki's number is a key he will hand only to someone he wants to meet.
- **C (front).** Trans under Blue Ant; Dorotea's intelligence-run consultancy (ch. 7). Weak here.
- **A (gate).** Self-set and soft: "If it's real, and you can get it" (ch. 11). She must confirm the number exists before the partnership means anything.
- **Fifth: the nose as attack surface.** Whoever knows what the gifted person reacts to can steer or break her. Someone who knows what makes James pause could feed him exactly what he will chase, or what will freeze him: he pays for his talent with no hacking or force.
