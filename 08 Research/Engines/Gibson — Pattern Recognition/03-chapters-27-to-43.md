## Why it grips
- **The clue turns out to be the answer, in physical form.** The T-shaped "map" has been an abstract puzzle since Part One. In ch. 33 Parkaboy's shape-matching bot returns a fact with no meaning attached: 75% of the T matches part of a Claymore mine's arming mechanism. Gibson holds back the meaning for four chapters. In ch. 35 Stella mentions a bomb in a tree and "The last fragment" that can't be removed. Only in ch. 37, watching Nora work, does Cayce see it: the city in the footage is the shape of the wound. The puzzle everyone was decoding was shrapnel. It works because the object was planted as plain data long before it was given any feeling.
- **The reveal is a disappointment that then becomes something greater.** Every forum theory was too big; the truth is one wounded woman, her sister, and a rich uncle. Then the three silent hours of watching Nora edit (ch. 37) make it bigger again. Gibson shows one edit, not an explanation: a zoom on the corner of a mouth changes the frame.
- **The way in is a letter that was never meant to be sent** (ch. 32). Following her therapist's exercise, she writes to the maker as if to the dead, types the address, and "watches as it sends." No clever move gets her there. Her grief does, and the reply comes within half an hour.
- **Cost and comedy stay together.** After the gun, Ngemi says "People don't have guns." (ch. 29). When she asks Parkaboy his real name he gives a joke one first (ch. 34), and he arrives to rescue her with a card that reads MIDDLE-AGED WHITE GUY (ch. 40).

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The hidden order is a security apparatus split into two halves that don't trust each other: the ex-KGB "traditionalists" and Sergei's web people (ch. 41). It moves the plot twice. **First, it watched the forum, and that is how it found her.** Paid graduate students lurked on F:F:F, and one of them broke the rule against posting. Cayce's throwaway post guessing at a Russian mafia patron touched a nerve. From her ISP they got her name, from her name her father's dossier, and from there the break-in, the bugs and the copied therapist's notes. She rereads that post twice (ch. 33, 36) before she learns it was the tripwire. **Second, it puts Dorotea in the room**, and Dorotea's need to know the source of the address leads to the drugging and the prison. Then the conspiracy deflates. Its secret was protective (an uncle shielding his nieces), and its last act is to pay her, with cash and a file on her father.

## How the lore gets in
- **Ngemi on Baranov and Echelon (ch. 30), about 600 words, conversation on a train.** It explains why she has to buy the calculator rather than pay cash, so the lore justifies the price. The email arrives within the chapter.
- **Stella's story (ch. 35), about 1,500 words, conversation in a café.** It doesn't stall because the reader has wanted it all book, because Cayce keeps losing her nerve and grabbing at "next question", and because it ends with drivers in leather coats and a blue light.
- **Parkaboy's prison briefing (ch. 40), about 700 words, in helicopter headsets.** Told in flight; ends on landing.
- **The dinner explanation (ch. 41), about 2,000 words.** The longest lecture, placed after the climax. It works as a rereading: each item explains a weirdness already shown (the dust rectangle, the therapist's notes, the man from Cyprus). Wiktor pressing her for the source, and hinting her father is alive, keeps it tense.
- **Win's last morning (ch. 42), three blue sheets.** She reads it three times before it makes sense. The book's private wound is closed by a document, and in ch. 43 her mother's lawyers confirm "every last thing" in it.

## Mechanism notes
- **The forum as tripwire.** Her old post is what put her on their list (ch. 41), and she can search it up herself (ch. 33). For SDF: James's old forum persona could be how the organisation first noticed him, years before the book, so that his edited posts read as surveillance he walked into.
- **B (canary), in reverse.** Mama gives herself away by knowing one fact she shouldn't: Tokyo (ch. 27). Nobody planted it, but the logic is the canary's, and it takes a friend (Ivy) to notice and report. Mama is also a fake poster written for Dorotea by a hired "puppenkopf" (ch. 38). A ghost-written organisation persona on James's forum fits SDF's rules.
- **D (lock and key), with a price.** Baranov won't take money. He trades the address for the one object he hates paying for himself (ch. 29–31). The key is bought on someone else's credit, and the tie to the employer comes due later. Parkaboy proves who he is with forum trivia (ch. 40), so a forum fact works as a password.
- **A (gate), weak.** Nothing has to be proved before she may act. The cost of being wrong comes as Dorotea (ch. 38), and Cayce gets out because she refuses to give up her source.
- **Fifth: the reward as a trap.** The antagonist pays to make her quiet (the cash, the file). She gives the money away (ch. 43) and keeps the file.
- **The nose.** Her allergy becomes the alarm: the hallucinated Michelin Man comes with her father's warning, "She's drugged the water. Scream." (ch. 38). Afterwards the allergy is gone (ch. 43). Win's rule against seeing patterns everywhere (ch. 36) is the book's check on its own heroine.

## Quotes
- "when you suggested in a post that the maker might be a Russian mafia type" (ch. 41): the forum post that set everything off.
- "Who started the thread that gave Completism its first formal basis?" (ch. 40): forum trivia used as a password.
- "Only the wound, speaking wordlessly in the dark." (ch. 37): the reveal comes from watching, not from an explanation.
- "the actual conspiracy is not so often about us; we are most often the merest of cogs in larger plans." (ch. 41): the conspiracy is smaller than feared.
