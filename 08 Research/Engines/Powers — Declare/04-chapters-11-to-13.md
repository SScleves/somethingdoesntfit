## Why it grips
- **The two timelines are tied by an echo before any explanation.** In ch. 11 a toy telephone on the cliff speaks a lecture on djinn thought. In ch. 12 we hear the same words coming out of Hale's mouth at the same hour in a hotel room nearby, and he hears Philby's "Shut her up!" through the window. The reader sees the psychic link a chapter before Hale asks about it (ch. 13).
- **Each debrief is a negotiation, and the lore is what gets traded.** Elena (ch. 11) and Mammalian (ch. 12–13) both hold the thing the speaker needs: escape, or a place in the operation. Every piece of backstory is a payment, and the reader watches what is held back (Philby's birthday, Hale's amomon).
- **Reversals that land as objects.** The plastic toy phone; the gold skull no bigger than a marble on an empty saddle (ch. 12); the snap-link and the weather-balloon release pin coiled in the jeep (ch. 13); the amomon embroidered on a wife's jacket.
- **Funny at the worst moments.** Philby coughs gin out of his nose while the CIA quote Matthew at him. Burgess has his potato-face theory of Slavs at the Soviet wire. The Kurds threaten to surrender. Bin Jalawi wants to get dying over with so nobody has to cook dinner.
- **The scene that does the most work** is the CIA visit to the Carlton bar (ch. 11): comedy, then two hits (the fox, the saxophone case), then Philby's gun on Elena under the table, until one line from her (the real birthday) turns the threat into a deal.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
Powers' engine is that the documented Philby record becomes the cover story for the secret. Each known fact gets a second, hidden cause:
- Philby's head injury in Beirut. *[from memory, not from the text: the record says he fell and hit a radiator.]* Here he was shot, and he hit his head on the radiator a second time to hide the wound (ch. 11).
- The Volkov affair of 1945 (ch. 12) is told straight: the delay, the stretchers, the bandages. Powers adds one hidden item to Volkov's samples, the Ararat aerial photographs.
- Philby surviving the Spanish shell of 31 Dec 1937 that killed three companions (ch. 11): a real anomaly, explained by the supernatural.
- Philby's border survey in 1948 ("Operation Spyglass", ch. 13) is the cover for a balloon that lures djinn. Burgess visiting Turkey puts him at the wire. *[from memory: both events are on the record.]*

The conspiracy also makes paper that will come back later. Theodora's cover story for Hale includes a crime that "can’t be disproved" because the illegal "kept no records", and in 1948 he gave Hale the name of a real Soviet agent while telling him the agent was invented (ch. 13). That 1948 forgery drives 1963: it makes Hale a fugitive whom Mammalian trusts and then doubts, which threatens Hale's revenge. It also turns Elena against him. The CIA men show surveillance as record-matching: IATA tickets against bylines (ch. 11).

## How the lore gets in
- **Philby's confession to Elena (ch. 11), about 4,000 words, a conversation under threat.** The birds circle closer every page or so, so the danger grows while he talks.
- **The djinn lecture, about 150 words, delivered twice** (toy phone in ch. 11, Hale's mouth in ch. 12). The repeat turns the rule into a plot event.
- **Hale's debrief (ch. 12), about 1,500 words, to a wire recorder.** Mammalian's answer sets the cost straight away: the stone will be destroyed.
- **The Wabar flashback (ch. 12), about 9,000 words, dramatized.** The theory is tested in the field, its source a real book read against its author ("This is the work of God, not man" becomes "angels, not man"). Within a page the danger arrives.
- **Theodora's briefing (ch. 13), about 1,500 words, while walking.** Broken up by the Kurd anecdote.
- **The Khan's amomon lore (ch. 13), about 800 words, over dinner.** The proof is objects: the honey, Balaklava, the jacket, the stone talisman. The torches cut him off mid-gift.

## Mechanism notes
- **A (gate):** Elena names it outright: no exfiltration until she is convinced he will tell everything (ch. 11). The Wabar trip is a gate of proof. A hostile witness (the king) confirms the stone has killed. In 1963 the full account costs him the stone.
- **B (canary):** the SIS feeds "barium meal" information to Philby so that a leak can be traced to him (ch. 11). The CIA drop "Charles Garner" and the fox to watch how he reacts. The Philby record supplies B ready-made.
- **C (front):** this is the strongest showing in the section. Each documented fact covers the next fact down (radiator, Spyglass, the Armenians). For SDF, each anomaly James solves could come with a dull, true explanation that is also the organisation's cover.
- **D (lock and key):** Elena opens Philby with one private quotation from Maly's own lips (ch. 11). The Khan's wife's jacket opens Hale.
- **Fifth: telling has a cost.** Each disclosure in the present changes the plan. When James tells someone what he found, the telling should change what the organisation does next.

## Quotes
- "bash his head again, against the radiator, to conceal from his wife the fact that he had been shot" (ch. 11): a documented accident turned into the cover for a hidden act.
- "He was beginning to relax—this story was verifiably true" (ch. 11): a spy, and the author, relax on documented ground.
- "the illegal kept no records, so it can’t be disproved" (ch. 13): a fabricated record built to be unfalsifiable.
- "Don’t hint, say." (ch. 11): the interrogator forces the lore out as payment.

## Flags
- Real people made complicit in the supernatural plot: Kim Philby, Guy Burgess, Theodore Maly, St. John Philby, T. E. Lawrence (said to have destroyed translations), Konstantin Volkov, Nicholas Elliott, Peter Lunn, Flora Solomon. SDF must not do this.
- A real book used as an occult source: St. John Philby's *The Empty Quarter* is reread for the occult and not altered. Whether its Wabar dream passage is in the real book cannot be checked from the text; Lawrence's lost files are invented. Rereading a real book fits SDF's rules; adding to one does not.
- *[from memory, not from the text: the real large Wabar iron was found on site in 1965. Powers' removal of it in 1948 may strain his own rule of never contradicting the record.]*
