## Why it grips
- **The strands are out of order, and each points at the gap.** Chapter 7 jumps 22 years and is full of events the reader has not yet seen: the Berlin "Ark" wedding in 1945, five men lost on Ararat in 1948, a Wabar meteorite, Elena calling him "Cannibale", Cassagnac shot "yesterday". The present arrives as a list of consequences, and the past strand now has to supply their causes. Reading the Paris chapters, we already know Cassagnac will save their lives and that Hale will one day be made to look like his killer.
- **The hero refuses to name what is happening.** After the radio night (ch. 5) and the jump (ch. 6), Hale finds he cannot tell Elena, out of "embarrassment, or shame". Powers holds back the magic mainly through his main character's pride, not through plot machinery. The reader sees more than Hale will say, and each rationalisation ("Ball lightning, probably") gets weaker than the last.
- **The best-built scene is the steam window (ch. 7).** Hale's own past is struck down by an object: four messages breathed onto glass by an old man who probably cannot read them, each one worse than the one before (STAND + DECLARE, then WATCHED, then the Ararat line, then GO), and a squeegee wipes the document away. Hale argues his way out ("a gambit"), then remembers Theodora's rule given the day before, and the rule closes the argument. So the hero's own reasoning shuts the door on him.
- **Cost is constant.** Elena, a beating, his country; in 1963 he must betray a plan he still finds "so internally consistent, so convincing".

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
Powers' engine is that every documented fact stays true, and a second reason sits behind it. The Soviet networks really were rolled up through reused signals and pads; the Simex front really served the occupiers; Marty really shot people in Spain for "Trotskyite sympathies"; Hale really goes through Camp 020, where captured spies are played back. Powers never contradicts any of it. He adds a motive underneath: Centre lets networks burn as "a distillation"; Marty "was trying to weed out" agents who had joined "a transcendent order"; Stalin's purges were "an unsuccessful effort to eliminate the Rabkrin element". The record checks out, and the secret history gets the credit. [from memory, not from the text: Simex/Simexco, Raichman, Theo Maly, Camp 020 and Cowgill are all real Red Orchestra / MI5 history.]

The conspiracy also pushes the plot by acting on Hale's body and choices. Centre's summons ends the love story (ch. 6). The playback doctrine Cassagnac teaches in 1941 is the trick Theodora runs on Hale in 1963: he is sent in as a double whose reluctance is genuine, because "Theodora had arranged all this so that it would be" (ch. 7). Each layer Hale gets through turns out to be the cover for the next one. He thought he worked for the GRU, and it was Rabkrin; the Okhrana became the Cheka; the passport officer's Russian dolls go one doll deeper (ch. 6).

## How the lore gets in
- **Cassagnac in the catacomb (ch. 5), about 1,000 words, conversation.** The purges, the "guardian angel" and playback, given as advice to agents who are adrift. It ends in two concrete orders: a strange botany question, then "buy a belt". No stall, because the lecture produces an object.
- **Elena's Albacete story (ch. 5), about 400 words, a memory told walking in the rain.** It goes through the textual oddity: "Declare" is in the Protestant Job and not the Douay. The lore hangs on one word that differs between two versions of a book.
- **Philby's interrogation (ch. 6), about 1,200 words, interrogation.** Lore given as accusations (Drummond's Bank, the Epiphany birthday, Theodora's private spies). The stammer and the colonel's digs keep it moving.
- **Ishmael's Rabkrin history (ch. 7), about 1,800 words, a lecture inside a recruitment.** Okhrana, Agentura, Lenin's deal, the calendar change, Wilson's and Lenin's strokes. It does not stall: the lecture is also the interrogation ("when is your birthday?"); birds are woken to beat the microphones and a parrot keeps interrupting ("Hayhat!"); a lie is caught; it ends in an oath and Hale tearing up his tinfoil ankh. Each history paragraph sits between demands.

## Mechanism notes
- **A (gate), as an oath.** The Rabkrin will not take Hale until he says "I break it now" and destroys his protection (ch. 7). The gate costs him something real (the ankh) and is witnessed. For SDF, a gate can be a renunciation James has to make out loud, not just a proof.
- **B (canary), several times.** The fish signal at St.-Sulpice shows who is still running on Centre's orders (ch. 5). Ishmael catches a vain lie and trusts Hale more for it, and he reads Hale's indifference ("No!" to dying for the cause) as a point in his favour. Theodora's rule turns authentication upside down: an order is proved real by being hateful. A canary whose sign is the reaction, not the belief.
- **C (front), the strongest here.** Russian dolls (Johns, ch. 6); GRU turns out to be Rabkrin (ch. 7); the Okhrana lives inside the Cheka. Each solved layer is the cover story of the next.
- **D (lock and key).** The belt as recognition sign; Cassagnac's botany question; Johns' leading "declare?". Each door needs one specific word or object.
- **Fifth: playback as policy.** The organisation lets visible networks be rolled up to protect a hidden one (Simex). That is SDF's "discredit, don't prevent", with a cost the hidden side accepts on purpose. James could find that a leak he trusted was *allowed* to happen.
- **For SDF's text rule:** the "Declare"/Douay difference is a real variant between real editions, used as a sign. That is the legal version of "given"/"arose"; Powers never invents a variant.

## Flags
- Real people made complicit in the supernatural: André Marty, Yezhov, Dzerzhinsky, Lenin, Stalin and Kim Philby. Real deaths recast as murders: T. E. Lawrence "bludgeoned", Alan Turing "fed a poisoned apple" (ch. 7); Wilson's stroke made to have a hidden cause. SDF forbids this.
- A real company (Simex) cast as a hermetic Soviet front (ch. 6). SDF invents its companies.
- No invented edits on real books. The Job variant is real.
