## Why it grips
- **A past strand leaves a question that the present scene answers.** The poker hand in chapter 14 stops before anyone declares, because the shelter is thrown over. Two chapters later Philby's stammered "Declare?—low on the l-list of likelihoods" settles it in front of Mammalian (ch. 16).
- **The hero's mistake comes back as a fact.** Chapter 14's disaster turns out to be a technical error: Hale had the mould when he needed what comes out of it. The guilt the reader has been carrying becomes information, and the new plan is built on top of it.
- **A second viewpoint changes how the first one reads.** Chapter 15 shows the same night through Elena: "Nous cannibales", and the jeep that drove past her unlocked door. Hale's departure in chapter 14 hurts after the fact, and she gets a 1963 motive (she would prefer Philby dead) that Hale does not know about.
- **The lore gets in through slapstick and pain.** Farid interrupts Hartsik's briefing three times: he hits the double too hard, then hits Hale again, then brings in coffee. The longest lecture is also the funniest scene.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
Declare works by **making records for the enemy to read**. Hale's 1948 confession to the Rabkrin was bait ("all that math was bad"; it will "enhance the look of the old math"). A double takes the beating and a script makes him "Charles Garner". A clerk the Soviets pay is called in on his day off, so that Mammalian hears from an eyewitness that nothing was said. Hale has to match those records, down to the bruise and the coffee stain. The danger is wherever the record does not fit him. The double's line about a dog that wouldn't hunt was not "the sort of thing he would say", and that is the line Mammalian picks on.

The documented record is the engine. Powers takes each known Philby fact as fixed and invents only its cause. Then he uses that cause to force the next scene. The death of Jackie the fox explains Philby's reluctance, his request to *The Observer* for leave and his feelers to France. His defection becomes Declare's order ("cross at the Aras River"). The end of the USSR becomes the delayed effect of a pellet in his corpse. Burgess's father dying in bed gets a djinn at the window. The reader knows the ending and reads on for what forces it. [From memory, not from the text: Philby reached Moscow in January 1963 and died there in 1988; the USSR ended in 1991; the fox and Burgess's father are in the biographies.]

## How the lore gets in
- **Philby's envelope (ch. 14), about 500 words of dialogue.** The envelope is an object (bloodied, signed "Maly") and it is the stake in the card game. Philby explains the GRU purge, the telegraph rhythm and the cure for death while the cards are dealt, so the story does not stall.
- **Elena's backstory (ch. 15), about 9,000 words of flashback framed by a single morning at a window.** The Machikha Nash lore is one summary paragraph, delivered while she is drunk and being conditioned. Each part of the flashback ends in a violent act, and it returns to the present with Hale at a terrace table.
- **Hartsik in the jail (ch. 16), about 4,000 words.** The biggest dump in the section: death-shapes, Solomon's "sword", the half-brother, the fox, Burgess, the pellet plan. It is anchored to objects (lead balls rolled across the desk, a clockwise-rifled derringer) and interrupted by Farid. Philby is waiting in the hall when it ends.
- **Survey photos (ch. 16), about 300 words.** American wartime films, the 1959 Turkish Geodetic survey and NSA drone photos are given as sources: real archives lend the lore provenance. The "real Ark" is a formation about twenty miles from Ararat, exposed by the 1948 earthquake. [From memory: this is the real Durupınar formation.] Powers keeps a real anomaly real and supplies the explanation.

## Mechanism notes
- **A (gate):** In 1948 Hale acted before he understood the mould and the bullets, and men died. In 1963 he is given the answer, so the gate is passed by a briefing handed to him, which is SDF's trap. The real engine is the cost of being wrong, paid fifteen years later.
- **B (canary):** The strongest lesson here. Declare plants false maths and a staged arrest, then watches what Mammalian believes. One phrase that sounds wrong almost gives the plant away, which is the risk a canary carries. Proposal for SDF: James's invented detail reaches the organisation in a document he did not write, and they test him on the one phrase that is not in his voice.
- **C (front):** Each cover hides another. Hale's defection covers his Declare work, "Garner" covers Hale, and Philby's 1952 doubling covers his Soviet loyalty, which covers his Rabkrin role.
- **D (lock and key):** Philby turns on one specific truth, the old challenge "O Fish, are you constant to the old covenant?", and on his own fear: he would rather live as a prole than die as an aristocrat. The door opens because Philby chooses, though Declare's death threat stands behind that choice.

## Flags
- **Invented edit on a real book:** Hartsik says copyists changed a word in 1 Kings 3 to "sword" (ch. 16). That is an invented variant of a real text, which SDF forbids.
- **Real people named as complicit:** Philby, Burgess, St John Philby, Menzies, Cumming, Marty, Beria and Macmillan are written into the occult plot. The Ukrainian famine is recast as a sacrifice. SDF must not do this.
