## The method (secret history that never contradicts the record)

**1. What exactly is Powers's rule?**
He lists the odd details in the documented record and keeps every one of them, with every date. Then he asks what single unrecorded fact would explain them all. The Author's Note says so outright ("I could not change or disregard any of the recorded facts, nor rearrange any days of the calendar"), and compares the method to inferring a planet from the wobbles it causes in other orbits (`06`).

**2. How is that different from SDF's rule?**
SDF keeps real anomalies real and invents edits, people and companies, which is the same discipline. The difference is where the invented cause goes. Powers puts it *inside* a real event and a real person: Philby's head injury becomes a hidden gunshot (`04`), and Cowgill's real loss of his section gets a supernatural reason (`03`). SDF may only put an invented person *beside* a real event and leave the cause unsaid, as Urfa, 1994 does ("The book never says he found it").

**3. Does SDF already use the method anywhere?**
Yes, in the real interludes. Down House, 1860; Croton, 530 BC; Washington, 1953; Urfa, 1994; and Mexico City, 1994 keep the real record, and the invented plot runs next to them (Tresidder walking east in 1929; the invented Utrecht physicist). Powers adds one more lesson: put an invented scene on a real night so that the calendar does the hurting. Chapter 17 times the departure to the documented Beirut dinner, the night before the wedding anniversary (`06`). SDF can do this only with invented characters on real dates.

**4. Does Powers ever break his own rule?**
Two notes suspect it. Note `04` flags that the real Wabar iron was found on site in 1965, so having it removed in 1948 may strain the record *[from memory, not from the text]*. Note `05` flags Hartsik's claim that copyists changed a word in 1 Kings 3, which is an invented variant of a real text. It is a character's claim, though, not the narration's, so in SDF terms it is the old man's licence in The final conversation and nobody else's.

**5. Can SDF reread a real book the way Powers does?**
Yes, provided nothing is added to it. Powers quotes *The Empty Quarter*, *Seven Pillars* and *My Silent War* accurately and reads them as cover stories (`04`, `06`). Elena's point that "Declare" appears in the Protestant Job but not in the Douay is a real difference between real editions (`02`). That is the legal version of SDF's "given/arose", which SDF keeps for its invented 1926 book.

## The engine

**6. Why can't Hale stop?**
Every step closes the door behind him. "Raincoat" means he can't go home (ch. 1). The frame for murder means he can't ask anyone to confirm his orders (ch. 3). Refusing the djinn's orders "confirms" him, and there is no way back (ch. 8). An abort in 1963 means he will be killed (ch. 16). See the map, section 2, and `01`, `03`, `05`.

**7. When does the reason become something Hale did himself, the switch Addendum 05 asks for?**
In chapter 3, when he keeps the "Bueno Año" slips from Theodora (`01`). It is his first break with the service, and from then on a private reason (Elena) runs beside every order. SDF has the same shape in The math, where James lies to Ana about where he was, but there the private reason costs only her trust.

**8. Who is the antagonist that pushes back?**
The paperwork, more than the Soviets. Declare's forged records (ch. 3, 13) make Hale a fugitive whom the Soviets trust and then doubt, and they turn Elena against him (`04`). That is SDF's organisation exactly: it discredits rather than prevents. Powers simply aims it at the hero.

**9. What is the cost of being wrong, which SDF's Part Two lacks?**
The 1948 raid fails because Hale "mistook the mold for the bullets". Men die, and fifteen years later the error returns as a single fact that the new plan is built on (ch. 14, 16, `05`). This supports King map proposal 2 (The schedule: the date James trusted was moved by his own verb). It doesn't add a new one.

## The timelines

**10. How do the two strands force each other?**
The present quotes the past before the past shows it ("Kiss the bride quick, Andrew", ch. 8, then the wedding, ch. 10). Chapter 7 hands over a list of results (five dead men, Cassagnac shot "yesterday") that the past then has to explain. And a phrase left open in one strand is settled in the other: the covenant phrase (`03`), the poker hand (`05`). See `02` and `03`.

**11. Does that help SDF's frame (the opening reading room, then "Eight months earlier")?**
Yes. It argues for letting the opening say one line that Part Two later explains. The ledger at page 118 already works this way. Powers would add a spoken line as well. On PLACEHOLDER #7, that favors planting the old man's "some of us remember" once in the opening, as a joke.

**12. Should SDF's villain-alone chapters be cut against the James chapters more tightly?**
Possibly. Powers's past strand answers the question the present has just raised (`01`). SDF's Leiden, nine years ago answers who Kalder is, but it comes before The vet asks. Moving it later is a structure call, PLACEHOLDER. Nothing in Declare forces it.

## Lore

**13. What is the largest lore dump, and why does it not stall?**
Hartsik in the Beirut jail (ch. 16, about 4,000 words, `05`). It is tied to objects (lead balls rolled across the desk, a derringer), it is interrupted three times by Farid's slapstick, and Philby is waiting in the hall. For SDF's Leiden or The reading room, continued: keep one object moving and one person interrupting.

**14. Where does Declare stall?**
In the archive montage (ch. 9, `03`) and the Moscow travelogue (Epilogue, `06`). Powers keeps the montage short and funny, gives Hale a duty (sign off the files honestly), and ends on a chain of words instead of an explanation. He keeps the travelogue moving with a fixed noon deadline.

**15. What does the Number 10 briefing teach, given SDF bans crimes?**
Run lore beside a task in the hero's hands. In Declare the task is bugging a table (ch. 3, `01`). In SDF it would be a real job task: the reconciliation in The money (Orwell proposal 1), the drill in The drill.

**16. Does lore ever arrive as payment?**
Yes, all the time. In ch. 11 Philby pays Elena in disclosures for his escape. In ch. 12–13 Hale trades his history for a place in the operation, and the telling costs him the stone (`04`). SDF has this already: in v7 The reading room, continued, James realizes he has told the system what he is watching for.

## The mechanisms

**17. What is "records made for the enemy to read"?**
Declare writes papers so that the enemy will read them: Hale's Kuwait crimes and Registry file (ch. 3), a 1948 crime that "can't be disproved" (ch. 13), a double's beating and transcript (ch. 16). Hale then has to match those papers in person. The danger is wherever the record doesn't fit him, and here that is one phrase in the double's transcript (`05`). Map proposal 5.1 gives James this move.

**18. What is the forged heartbeat (map 5.1), in one paragraph?**
After Noor says a loud reader is manageable (v6 Leiden), James schedules harmless forum posts so that he will look loud. Bram (A room with a long table) reads the timer as a performance, and the source of the advice as Noor. In What was on the phone James sees his posts marked *performed, source Leiden*. It needs the twelve-years-of-posts canon (Addendum 05 d, PLACEHOLDER).

**19. Why not put the forged record after The night he stops being afraid instead?**
That works too. James could keep posting frightened questions to stay in the left column, and Kalder could read that the jokes about dying have stopped. But The long table, again already has "he said soul to the dog", so it would be a second tell in one scene. The Leiden placement gives the cost to Noor and to the passive stretch of Part Two the diagnosis names.

**20. How does Declare's front (C) become something the hero does?**
Hale wears fronts, "Charles Garner", the defector and the Burobin journalist, and has to live up to them physically (ch. 16, Epilogue, `05`, `06`). That contradicts Addendum 05's finding that in Tier 1 the hero only learns the front. Map 5.3 has James send the sheets under the continuity programme's own format, true facts in his real role.

**21. Isn't 5.3 impersonation?**
It is not, if he uses only his real role and invents no task. He is on the continuity programme (Leave it: "Continuity want you"), and a sheet really is a reconciliation of the recipient's records. If Santi reads it as misusing the bank's systems, drop it (PLACEHOLDER).

**22. What is "they write him onto the list" (map 5.2)?**
After James refuses the five seats, the bank's annex is revised to include him, which discredits him by improving his record (ch. 3: "you never left the force"). He prints it on every sheet with "added the week after I said no", initialled. The cost: the brewery man, and at least one other, use it against him.

**23. What does Declare add to the gate (A)?**
Three ways through it. You can pass it by refusing (ch. 8: "no SOE infiltrator would have perversely refused my orders"), by an embarrassing honest report to the person who hired you ("Magic, that is, sir", ch. 10), or with leverage (the solicitor, Epilogue). And there is an inverted gate: a hateful order proves itself genuine (ch. 7). See `02`, `03`, `06`.

**24. And to the canary (B)?**
It runs in both directions. Ishmael trusts Hale more after catching him in a vain lie (ch. 7). Philby mutters a phrase to see who flinches (ch. 9). Declare plants false maths and then watches what Mammalian believes (ch. 16). See `02`, `03`, `05`. For On land, it is a reminder that Kalder's "Oh, that one. Yes" may itself be a canary aimed at James. The v8.2 plan already allows that reading.

**25. Lock and key (D)?**
Present and ordinary: "Bless me", "Segne mich", "I did not kill Cassagnac", the covenant phrase (`01`, `03`, `06`). Elena had already decided before the key was used. That matches SDF's rule that a door opens only when a person chooses.

## SDF fit and limits

**26. Can SDF use Hale's solicitor?**
Not as leverage. A dead-man's switch is a threat, and SDF bans threats. Monday already gives James the same protection without one ("You made yourself too expensive. I don't think you planned it."). That is the better line for him (`06`; v8 Monday).

**27. Then what of "the hero writes the leak himself"?**
It is already SDF's ending. Leave it is the book he is allowed to publish, and The final conversation has "If anyone asks, it's a novel" and "Nobody reads the middle". Map 5.4 adds only Powers's Author's Note: a back page of real, checkable oddities, because nobody reads the middle.

**28. Is ending on a checkable fact useful to SDF?**
Yes, if the fact is real and its cause is left unstated. Declare's last beat is that Philby was buried, which any reader can check (Afterword, `06`). SDF's last pages are invented (page 212 gone, the invoice stepping down). A real anomaly could close the book's back matter (map 5.4), never the narration's claims.

**29. Which real people does Declare make complicit, so that nobody copies the pattern?**
Kim Philby, Guy Burgess, St John Philby, Macmillan, Dick White, Menzies, Cumming, Cowgill, Footman, Marty, Lenin, Stalin, Beria, Yezhov, Dzerzhinsky, Volkov, Elliott, Lunn, Flora Solomon. Lawrence, Turing and Kitchener are hinted to have been murdered, and Simex is recast as a hermetic front. See the Flags sections of `01`–`06`.

**30. Does the organisation in Declare kill like SDF's?**
It kills more and more casually ("we've killed bigger heroes, famous ones", `06`), and Theodora plans a quiet death and an "enduring disgrace" for Hale. SDF's organisation kills rarely, and when it does it has failed. Take only the disgrace, which is the discrediting half (map 5.2).

**31. Is Declare funny, as SDF must be?**
Yes, at the worst moments: Philby coughing gin out of his nose while the CIA quote Matthew at him (ch. 11), Farid hitting the wrong man (ch. 16), "two middle-aged Englishmen killed ten Spetsnaz" (ch. 18). See `04`–`06`. Its longest lecture is also its funniest scene, which is the model for SDF's Leiden and The reading room, continued.

**32. Is there a dog?**
A fox. Philby's fox Jackie, whose death explains his reluctance and his feelers to France, is a documented detail that Powers turns into the hidden reason (ch. 16, `05`; Author's Note, `06`). It is the nearest thing in Declare to Buddy: an animal whose loss moves the plot.

**33. What should an agent check before proposing anything from Declare?**
Three things. First, whether a real person would be given an invented act or motive: if so, drop it. Second, whether v8.2 or a v7/v8 draft already does it: The final conversation, Monday and the visitor book's *Pending* already cover a lot. Third, whether the King, Orwell, Eco, Gibson or Borges maps, or Addendum 05, have already proposed it.
