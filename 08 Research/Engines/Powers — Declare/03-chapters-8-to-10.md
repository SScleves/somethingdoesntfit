## Why it grips
- **The present strand quotes the past strand before showing it.** In ch. 8 Hale remembers "Kiss the bride quick, Andrew, before you die" and Cassagnac calling Elena his bride. The scene itself arrives two chapters later (ch. 10). Ishmael's challenge, "O Fish, are you constant to the old covenant?", is spoken in 1963 (ch. 8), muttered by Philby in 1945 (ch. 9), and shouted by Hale at the storm (ch. 10). The reader carries the phrase as an open question until the past cashes it in.
- **Ch. 8 ends on an image the next chapters have to explain.** Elena with Philby in Beirut is a cliffhanger made of history. Chapters 9–10 then show the night Hale lost her, so that by the time the novel returns to 1963 the image weighs what it should.
- **Lore and loyalty test arrive in the same moment.** At the sulphur pool the rules ("Never startle them, never reason with them"; "things of the water demand a life") come out while a .45 is pointed at Hale's face. The scene is a series of orders (kneel, kill your friend, kill me), each refused; the Catholic reflex he thinks he has lost decides it.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
Powers uses the documented record as a timetable that the secret story has to fit. The real facts set the dates and places, and the hidden story only explains *why*. Philby is in Berlin because of his documented good-will tour of liberated capitals (ch. 9). Hale's question, "why in God's name did the man have to be in Berlin on this particular day?", turns a known date into a plot point. Cowgill's real loss of his section to Philby gets a hidden reason: he was "cracking under the strain" over a "huge, hostile service" he kept tracing to Arabs (ch. 9). *[from memory, not from the text: Cowgill really was squeezed out by Philby's Section IX in late 1944.]* The Soviet stone in the shell-crater is called "a cornerstone for a wall they might build one day" (ch. 9). That ties a real future event, the Berlin Wall, to an invented cause, without changing what happened.

The conspiracy also gives orders: Ishmael's are the price of getting further in (ch. 8); Theodora's "total evasion procedures" is the one Hale breaks to reach Elena (ch. 9–10).

The ordinary explanation is always there beside the real one. Moscow's metallic smell is "cheap Soviet diesel oil", and a Berliner says so to Hale at the moment of the killing (ch. 9). By morning Hale doubts what he saw, and Theodora names that as the danger: the cover story works on the witness first (ch. 10).

## How the lore gets in
- **The sulphur-pool oracle (ch. 8), about 4,500 words, a scene.** Rules of the djinn come out only as action: the covenant formula, a life in exchange for testimony, "My name is Legion." No stall, because a gun and a deadline (riders approaching) run under all of it.
- **Archive montage (ch. 9), about 1,300 words, documents and interviews.** Three years compressed into absurd file titles, White Russian translators who cross themselves, and refugees' stories. A duty drives it: to sign files off "in good conscience" he has to read the primary sources. It ends with a chain of words, not an explanation: anchor, Ankara, Yakor, Lubyanka, ankh. This is the one real stall, and Powers keeps it short and funny.
- **Theodora's car briefing (ch. 9), about 450 words.** The solstice, the stone and the job, ending in an order. The next scene is the autobahn.
- **Elena's Lubyanka vow (ch. 10), about 250 words, a confession at the table.** It plants a clock: noon in St Basil's on her fortieth birthday, 22 April 1964, which falls just after the 1963 strand.

## Mechanism notes
- **A (gate), twice and turned upside down.** In 1963 the gate is passed by *refusing*: "no SOE infiltrator would have perversely refused my orders" (ch. 8). In 1945 the gate is an honest report. Hale has to say "Magic, that is, sir" before he may go further, and Berlin "was a test" (ch. 10). For SDF: James's gate could be confessing to the person who hired him the thing he would rather explain away. It costs him credibility, and passing it buys the next layer.
- **B (canary).** Philby murmurs "Constant to the old covenant" at the rain and watches Hale startle (ch. 9). He also drinks the Arab Bureau's gin-and-insecticide initiation, a sign only an insider would read. A phrase dropped to see who recognises it is exactly a canary, and it costs Philby nothing.
- **C (front).** Each layer comes with a respectable cover: fertiliser salesman, Section One historian, "Charles Garner" journalist, diesel oil, Cowgill's breakdown. *[from memory, not from the text: Philby really wrote for The Observer and The Economist from Beirut, so the Garner cover borrows a real career.]*
- **D (lock and key).** Old code phrases open people: Elena's "Bless me!" (ch. 9), the Rote Kapelle horn signal (ch. 10).
- **Fifth: the dull file as engine.** The hero is handed boring paperwork, and the duty to sign it off honestly makes him read the sources behind it. That duty, not curiosity, is what assembles the conspiracy (ch. 9). This is the closest thing in Declare to James's situation: receiving documents can count as action if signing them off carries a duty and that duty has a cost.
