## Why it grips
- **Dying is a chapter break.** When a viewpoint character dies, the next section starts in a changed timeline with a nosebleed and a clean memory of a different route. Barry's death in the corridor is written in full, then replaced by a run where he does not die: the reader gets the death and the retry.
- **A calm stretch the reader knows is borrowed.** Barry's second life is summarised in a few lines per year (graduation, divorce, his mother's death exactly as remembered). Then Ann Voss Peters lands on a car, and the pleasant years become what the cost is measured against.
- **Exposition at a table or in a car, never in gunfire.** Helena cooks Barry's favourite meal while telling him what has not happened yet.
- **Cuts on action.** Helena's 2018 chapter ends on her cutting her arm; the next opens on Barry watching.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
Hotel Memory turns Barry's grief into a plan, then a raid. DARPA replaces it as the thing Helena must stop, and holds her in an apartment with cameras, a contract and a pitch. Shaw's argument drives Book Four: each use saves lives, so refusing looks like harm. Then the design leaks and the choice disappears, because the thing is now everyone's.

## How the lore gets in
- **Slade's test (July 2009), about 900 words.** A conference room: Slade says the experiment already happened, and the reader feels the rule before it is explained.
- **Barry's bathroom (2018), about 1,200 words.** A family explains the rule to itself, so an emotional crisis carries the exposition.
- **Helena's kitchen (Book Three), about 2,500 words.** Future events told over a meal; eating gives the telling a pause.
- **Dead-memory Q&A in the car, about 400 words.** Barry's questions ("couldn't you have gone back?") do the lecturing.
- **DARPA ground rules (Day 17), a five-item list.** Each rule is broken in turn.

## Mechanism notes
How each leak is delivered, what the character does, what it costs:
1. **Slade's test.** To Helena and Reed, minutes later, as a nosebleed and a replay of the failed experiment. Helena acts: she secretly maps the memory of Jee-woon's first visit and dies on purpose to return to it. Cost: nine years in hiding. Reed's own leak is a memory of a beach at age two, and he wishes he had not been revived; he hangs himself.
2. **Barry's anniversary.** The first life arrives eleven years late. Julia knocks at three in the morning; Barry works out that Meghan has it too and races to her flat. Cost: in the new timeline Meghan cannot hold both lives and dies the same day.
3. **The Big Bend.** A building, delivered to a whole city with memories of having eaten in it. Barry acts by reading a news caption and going to the hotel. Cost: three more dead in Meghan's building and more suicides.
4. **Helena's future memory.** She remembers a goodbye with a Barry four months ahead and uses it as proof: his eclipse, his orchard, his father.
5. **The save point.** She cuts her arm to leave herself a vivid short memory. A leak turned into a tool; she replays the ambush with Barry's wound in mind.
6. **Slade's own mind.** He has lived too many lives to map a new memory and gets only flashes of lifetimes. The leak costs its user.
7. **School shooting (Day 8 to 11).** Delivered to a nation as coverage of events that never happened; Helena sees a "murdered" student recounting being gunned down, a little unhinged. It gives Shaw his best argument.
8. **Day 136.** The same device with the timing changed: every averted massacre since January arrives in one gulp.
9. **Tunnel, bridge, Grand Central, Trump Tower (Day 160).** Four timelines in minutes. Helena dies in a flood, wakes, watches the bridge burn, shifts again. She keeps the physical feeling of impacts that never happened.
10. **The girl in the Silverado.** The direction reverses: Helena's older mind takes over a sixteen-year-old's hands. Planted earlier by a photograph of her in the same truck in her mother's care-home room.

How a leak becomes a thing to act on:
- **A tell and a clock.** A nosebleed signals arrival; the date is fixed by when the other self died, so characters can predict it ("tonight is the night").
- **A second person holds it too.** Barry cannot sit on it, because Meghan has it.
- **Evidence, not feeling.** The dead memory holds a route, a gun position, a code.
- **A price paid by the one who acts.** Barry is shot twice; Helena dies again and again.

How it escalates without repeating: one man, a family, a city, a nation, the world on one morning, a stranger rewriting the same morning four times, then one person taken over from the inside.

For the four fixes:
- **A (gate).** Shaw's five rules and a vote; orders from above break it.
- **B (canary).** Helena asks that the killer be left under the window with his tools so the group becomes a rumour; a newspaper profiles it at three months.
- **C (front).** Shaw offers a false-front program for his superiors; the real one is later exposed.
- **D (lock and key).** Helena's private facts open Barry. The strongest scene in the section.
- **Fifth: a scheduled leak.** A known date on which the hidden memory arrives for everyone present. For SDF, past lives could surface on the anniversary of a reset.

## Quotes
- "Every time Reed dies in the tank, he orphans a string of memories that become dead in our minds after we shift." (Helena, July 2009): the rule, stated by the person exploiting it.
- "I think of it as a timeline anniversary." (Book Three): a name that makes the leak predictable.
- "Healthy minds are being made unwell; unwell minds are being driven over the edge." (Barry, 2018): the cost to people who did not choose it.
- "Cause and effect are still alive and well." (Helena, Book Three): why nothing is undone for free.

## Flags
- Real people and organisations are named: Greenwald and the Guardian, WikiLeaks, Trump Tower, Turing, Hitler, Franz Ferdinand, DARPA, RAND. SDF must not do this.
- Suicides, a child murder and mass shootings are shown. The book is not funny in this stretch.
