## Why it grips
- **Two clocks that meet.** Barry's chapters run forward through 2018; Helena's run forward through 2007–09. They touch in the chair. The reveal is staged in Helena's room: Slade at the door is played twice, first as the "dead" version, then with champagne, and the repeat is how reader and Helena learn what happened.
- **A hook that costs a life at once.** Barry's failure on the ledge sends him to Montauk, so his curiosity has grief behind it.
- **The best single scene is Barry's first memory (6 Nov).** The reader waits through the straps and the questions about Meghan, then the tank. Crouch builds the memory by sense, in order: smell of the house, taste of the beer, the screen, then touch, then her voice. The pain of dying fades as the memory sharpens. When his finger moves, the reader learns before Barry does that this is not a recording.
- **The comic edge is thin.** Gwen's bar banter is the only relief.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The hidden thing is two layers deep. On the surface it is a hotel that invites the dying and the desperate; underneath it is a man who changed history and has left the world's memory in a mess. In this section it works in three ways:

1. **It blocks nothing, it invites.** Joe gives Barry the address and the garage door opens by itself.
2. **It threatens once.** Vince tails and drugs Barry, then the hotel switches to rewarding.
3. **It makes the detective a client.** The cost of the secret is that Barry is given what he wanted most. By the end of the section he has more reason to protect the conspiracy than to expose it, and he has been told to tell no one.

## How the lore gets in
- **Ann's account (1, about 700 words), a spoken confession on a ledge.** It gives the emotional rule of FMS (grey, small details hurt most) and ends in her death, so no stall.
- **Gwen's CDC briefing (about 600 words), dialogue in a bar.** Barry reports the state of knowledge: 64 cases, 10 percent suicide, social spread, the Mandela Effect. Gwen's jokes and her doubt (maybe Ann was just unwell) dress it; it sits between a death and a blackout, so it does not stall.
- **The police report (about 300 words), a document.** It contradicts Ann and is the object that makes Barry act.
- **Joe's phone call (about 600 words), on a train.** One rule, an address, then a hang-up before he says who "they" are.
- **Slade's explanation (Book Two, about 2,000 words), a conversation.** The only true lecture: time as illusion, then the first timeline, Jon Jordan, Slade's own death. The fish and sensory-volume passage slows it, but it ends on a threat and a question, and the next chapter returns to Barry.

## Mechanism notes
**How each leak is delivered.**
- **Ann (Ch. 1).** A whole second life, grey and in small physical detail (Sam's birthmark, the cake fight, her husband's morning breath), arrives after a nosebleed and a stabbing headache. She acts: she tracks down Joe, tells him her plan, and goes to the ledge hoping he will come. It costs her the life she is in.
- **Barry (diner, 5 Nov).** A nosebleed and glint of pain; three days of memory replaced with a grey set where he sat watching the Knicks. He cannot dismiss it, because it matches what Ann said, and it pushes him to the hotel (he says he is more afraid than ever).
- **Helena (Book Two).** A grey, haunted memory of the previous half hour, in which a man died in the tank and she tried to throw a chair. It arrives at the exact moment the original event would have happened, which is how Slade proves it.

**What the leaks share.** Sensory, grey, small, with a nosebleed, at a fixed moment. Each is also proof: Slade uses Helena's as evidence, Barry uses his as a reason to move.

**How a leak becomes something a character must act on.** Three devices:
1. **Someone else has a stake in the contradiction.** Ann's claim that Franny jumped and the report that she lived are checkable by a detective in an afternoon. The leak becomes a puzzle with a train timetable.
2. **The leak is shared.** Gwen's social-spread detail means Barry cannot treat it as a private illness.
3. **The leak carries an instruction.** The man in the chair tells Barry to change what happened, and Vince adds rules.

**How the book explains its rules without stalling.** Each rule is delivered exactly when a character is under pressure. The CDC facts come over whisky; Joe's explanation is cut off by a hang-up; Slade's physics comes while Helena is angry and wants to know what he did to her. Nobody asks "how does it work" until the reader already has the evidence.

**Fixes.**
- **A (gate).** Absent. Barry proves nothing before acting; he is just curious.
- **B (canary).** Weak: Slade's planted fact ("my mother's name is Susan") proves a mechanism, not a believer.
- **C (front).** Present: hotel hides chair, chair hides the timeline change.
- **D (lock and key).** Present: Joe opens only because Barry already knows Ann's name and the Poe Building ledge. Barry's private fact (the last night with Meghan) is the key the hotel then uses on him.
- **Fifth: the gift as a lock.** Vince's rules make the rescued man a silent helper, close to SDF's helpers who know one task and the word "continuity".

## Quotes
- "FMS isn’t what people think it is." (Barry, Joe's call): one line that turns an epidemic into a choice, then the call is cut.
- "Would you like a do-over?" (Barry, Joe's call): the recruitment is a classified ad, small and banal.
- "Don’t try to game the larger system with your knowledge of what’s to come." (Barry, 2007): the rule is given as a courtesy, not a threat.
- "You’ll have two sets of memories—one live, one dead." (Helena, Book Two): the whole leak mechanism in eight words.
