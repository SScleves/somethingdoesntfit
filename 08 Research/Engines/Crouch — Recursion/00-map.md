# Blake Crouch, *Recursion* — the map

> **Coverage:** converted English text with no edition data in the file. Read in full through three section notes (`01-book-one.md`, `02-middle.md`, `03-books-three-to-five.md`, about 90,000 words), 15 Nov 2026. The text has no chapter numbers, so cites are by Book, date or loop, and note file. Tier 3, so shorter than the Tier 1 maps.
> **Brief:** the leak. You have proposed that fragments of past lives, or of the world before a reset, leak into this one. This is the novel that builds a whole plot on a leaked memory, so it is read for how a leak becomes something a character must act on, how one device grows for 300 pages, and where it goes wrong.
> **Standing-rule note:** real organizations and people are named (DARPA, RAND, WikiLeaks, the Guardian, Trump Tower; `02`). The heroes raid a building with guns and kidnap Slade (`02`, `03`). Suicides, a child murder and mass shootings are shown, and the middle is not funny (`02`). The cause of everything is a machine. Take the handling of the leak, none of that.

---

## 1. What it is

A New York detective, Barry, fails to stop a woman jumping; she says she remembers a whole life that never happened. Checking her story leads him to a hotel where a chair sends a dying person back into an old memory to live on from there. He takes the offer and saves his daughter. Every use leaves the abandoned timeline behind as "dead" memories, which arrive in everyone on a fixed date. Helena, who built the chair, and Barry try to destroy it; the state takes it; the design gets out; the world remembers and fires its missiles. Helena restarts the same twenty-nine years again and again until Barry goes back to the first timeline and stops the invention.

What makes it work: the leak has one tell, one rule and a date, so a character can predict it, share it and check it. And the same device is shown at a larger scale each time, so the reader never gets the same scene twice until the last third.

## 3. How the lore gets in

- **Ann on the ledge** (about 700 words, `01`): a confession that ends in her death. It gives the feel of a leak before any rule.
- **The CDC briefing in a bar** (about 600 words, `01`): numbers and the spread between people, carried by Gwen's jokes and her doubt, set between a death and a blackout.
- **The police report** (about 300 words, `01`): the shortest piece and the one that makes Barry move.
- **Joe's call** (about 600 words, `01`): one rule, an address, and a hang-up before "they" are named.
- **Barry's bathroom** (about 1,200 words, `02`): a family explains the rule to itself at three in the morning. The crisis carries it.
- **Helena's kitchen** (about 2,500 words, `02`): the future told over a meal she cooks from his own memories.
- **The five ground rules** (`02`): a list, each item broken in turn.
- **Where it runs:** Slade through the cell glass in loop six (about 900 words, `03`). Barry has to unlock the door to get the answer, and the clock is visible.
- **Where it stalls:** Slade's first long explanation (about 2,000 words, `01`) and the neutrino and wormhole pages (`03`). In both, nobody in the room can lose anything by what is said, and the physics in `03` pays off nothing, because the answer turns out to be a human act.

This confirms ADDENDUM 05's rule on lore: it holds when someone in the room can lose by it.

## 4. The leak

**The rule in one line.** When someone restarts time, the abandoned stretch comes back to everyone who lived it as a second, gray set of memories, on the date the restart happened (`01`, `02`).

**How a leak becomes something a character must act on.** Five devices, and the book uses all five in its first eighty pages.

1. **The tell.** A nosebleed and a stab of pain behind the eyes, every time (`01`, `03`). Because it is bodily and always the same, a character can recognize it in someone else across a room, and the reader learns to fear a tissue.
2. **The fixed date.** The leak arrives at the moment the other self died. Helena calls it a timeline anniversary (`02`). So people can say "tonight," wait up for it, and be wrong about how bad it will be.
3. **The second witness.** Barry cannot treat it as his own illness, because Meghan and Julia have the same memories on the same night (`02`). The CDC's detail that it spreads between people who know each other does the same job earlier (`01`).
4. **The checkable contradiction.** Ann says Franny jumped. The report says Joe saved her. That is a train ride and an interview (`01`). The leak is given to a man whose job is to settle exactly this kind of disagreement.
5. **Evidence inside the leak.** Dead memories hold a route, a gun position, a door code (`02`). Helena opens Barry with things only he knows: his eclipse, his orchard, his father (`02`). The leak is treated as a document that can be read for facts.

Add a sixth that is easy to miss: **the leak hurts in its small details.** A birthmark, a cake fight, morning breath (`01`). Crouch never gives a leaked life in summary.

**Escalation without repeating.** Scale changes every time: one woman, one detective, a family, a city (a building nobody built), a nation (news coverage of shootings that never happened), the world in one gulp, one morning rewritten four times, and last a reversal, where an older mind arrives in a younger body (`02`). Then the device turns into a tool: Helena cuts her arm to leave herself a vivid memory to return to (`02`). Then it costs its user: Slade has lived so many lives he gets only flashes (`02`).

**Rules under pressure.** Each rule arrives when someone needs it to decide something in the next minute. Nobody asks how it works until the reader has seen it work (`01`).

**Payoff.** The first timeline is the one place with no earlier leaks. Barry reaches it by refusing the perfect memory. The leaks stop, and he alone keeps them (`03`).

**What goes wrong.**
- **The physics lectures.** See section 3.
- **The villain hands over the fix.** Slade tells Barry what to do. The scene survives only because he withheld it for a stated reason and because Barry must free him to hear it (`03`).
- **The loops numb.** Two of the seven anniversaries are nearly the same nuclear strike (`03`). The notes say the characters name their own fatigue, which helps a little.
- **The cause is a chair.** Once the leak has a machine behind it, the last third is about the machine.

**Against A–D.** A: weak (Shaw's five rules, a synaptic number; `02`, `03`). B: one small use (the killer left under the window so a rumor starts; `02`). C: present but only learned (hotel, chair, timeline; `01`), which agrees with the Tier 1 verdict. D: strong, in a new form: a private memory is the key (`02`).

**Further mechanisms**, checked against ADDENDUM 05's list. Two are new. *The dated arrival:* a hidden thing that surfaces by itself on a known day. *The perishable finding:* what the hero learns is lost at the next restart and survives only if a person carries it (`03`). A third, the gift as a lock (the detective is given what he wants and told to keep quiet; `01`), is the addendum's co-optation with a better price.

## 5. Transfer to SDF

**Where you already are.** SDF has a leak, and it is of one kind: what a person carries across a death. The funeral gives the flash (cold mud, a cracked hand). The night he stops being afraid pays it: the hand seen all his life, lives cut off by somebody's administration. v6 The floor already lists *remembered → imagined* beside *transmitted → arisen*. The organization's own word for what it counts is "recall" (Utrecht, 1993; `rcl-agg` in Leave it; the invoice in The money). The final conversation says the remembering is the one thing they took out of every book and kept. So the obvious reading you describe is already latent: the edits remove the paper trace of anything crossing, and the count finds the people it crosses in.

What SDF lacks is Crouch's kit. James's flash has no tell, no second witness, nothing checkable and nothing usable in it, so for forty chapters he can only file it. The four proposals below add those, in order of scale: two people, two sentences, one table, one room.

**Where the leak idea breaks the plan or a rule. Say no to these.**
- **More than one kind of leak.** You listed other realities, past lives and earlier societies. Crouch has one kind and one tell. Take past lives only. The world before the reset reaches the book through people who carried it, which is who the givers were.
- **Leaked objects.** Crouch's building that appears (`02`) would wreck SDF. If things can change by themselves, a sentence that differs between two printings stops being evidence of an editor, and Leiden, 1931 becomes unnecessary. `01` flags that Crouch uses the Mandela Effect exactly this way. Nobody in SDF may offer a leak as the reason a book changed.
- **A new right column.** Bram defines it as people whose fear is leaving. Keep that. The leak is the sign a handler looks for, as in 5.1.
- **James as the chosen one.** The sentence must be common: the professor, Henk and the quiet ones all have it.
- **A public leak on a date.** Leave it says nothing loud happens. The dates stay private and small (02:14, the log).

**REVIEW 01, findings 1.1 to 1.3.**
- *1.1 (the edits mean three things):* yes, if you take one kind of leak. What was given was the method of carrying across, the givers were the people who carried it, and "the thing that passes" is the carrying. That is the review's own cheapest join, and 5.2 gives it a visible form before Part Three.
- *1.2 (the hidden thing is in every bookshop):* partly. A leak is the one thing a bookshop cannot sell, because it is yours, ordinary and unasked for. That supports the review's reading (they remove the ordinary versions and make the rest exotic), but Noor still has to say it.
- *1.3 (no motive):* mostly. A person who recalls is a witness to the world before the record was arranged, so the paper is corrected and the witness is found. That is self-interest in one sentence. It must arrive on paper before Part Three (the folder's *When a Reader Notices* is the place), or the old man becomes Slade. Kalder still needs his line.

## 6. Do not take

- A machine behind the leak, or any physics. SDF's leak has no cause the book can name.
- The villain as the source of the fix. The final conversation already risks this.
- Restarts. A book about resets must not reset on the page, must not make one free, and must not show the same catastrophe twice. SDF's reset stays offstage and in the past.
