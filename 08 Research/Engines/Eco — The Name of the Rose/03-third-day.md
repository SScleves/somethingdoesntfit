## Why it grips
- **The stall is named, then paid for.** The first hour's title ends "and that is all". Eco tells the reader nothing happens, and Berengar's absence then hangs over a whole day of lectures. The open question is answered on the last page, so the lore never quite reads as dead time.
- **A clock and a worse detective.** The abbot's letter (Vespers) makes the case urgent: solve it by tomorrow or the inquisitor takes over. Gui wants a confession, not the truth. Losing to a rival investigator means people burn.
- **Small payoffs inside long talk.** The heresy lecture (Nones) ends with William saying, "By the way", that he has cracked the cipher. The decoded line is itself a riddle. Each gain comes with a new lock.
- **Method and nose, side by side.** William works from the outside: windows counted, "first let us find the rule, then we will try to explain the exceptions", fifty-six rooms on a tablet. Adso works by drift: he goes in with no plan, gets lost, and stumbles on the night traffic the method could not have predicted. His throwaway "There are only the baths" finds the body. The nose supplies the stray fact and the method places it; SDF could split James's two sides between James and whoever reasons beside him.
- **One scene does the most work.** The kitchen at night (After Compline) gathers everything Adso heard that day and ends on an object, the heart. William's laugh ("what man could have such a big heart?") turns horror into a clue about the abbey's economy.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The hidden order in this section is the library's withholding, and on the Third Day it acts through **access, not violence**. The catalogue is open: Adso leafs through it "after obtaining Malachi’s permission". The shelves are not. The abbot denies the library has anything to do with the murders. The building is designed to lose people ("The maximum of confusion achieved with the maximum of order"). Adso's Terce meditation gives the archive its justification: knowledge wears out with use, so keeping it means keeping it from everyone. A foundation reading room that sits on a bank's recovery site could claim the same thing ("we keep it safe; safe means unread"), and Aylin could believe it sincerely.

It forces William round the gatekeeper (the outside map) and sets the clock, because the abbot hides the case to keep his power and so rations William. Berengar's body is hidden rather than displayed, and William's comment that the searchers "were looking for a corpse lying somewhere theatrically" says outright that this order hides some deaths and displays others.

A second order sits on top: the inquisition, which discredits rather than refutes. William explains (Nones) that the inquisitors "attribute to one the errors of the other", so every reform movement is made to look like the most violent one. That is SDF's clown beside every real question, practised as policy.

## How the lore gets in
- **Adso's scriptorium meditation (Terce), about 1,200 words, interior.** The story stops, but this is the archive's thesis, tied to watching hands at work.
- **Salvatore's life (Sext), about 3,500 words, reported speech.** Mostly texture. It ends on a hook: at Dolcino's name he crosses himself and leaves.
- **William's heresy lecture (Nones), about 4,000 words, dialogue at a workbench.** It stalls, broken up by lens-grinding and Adso pushing back, and ends with the cipher reveal.
- **The abbot's letter (Vespers), about 1,000 words.** An object that turns politics into a deadline. No stall.
- **The library geometry (Vespers), about 1,500 words, a walk with a tablet.** The lore is a task: Adso draws as he is told. It ends with a plan for tonight.
- **Ubertino on Dolcino, the written history, and Michael's burning (After Compline), about 8,000 words together.** A conversation, a document found on a desk, and a memory. The longest stall so far, held up by Ubertino's unsettling closeness and by Adso climbing the stairs as he remembers. It pays off when the same words for flame return in the sex scene: the lore causes the act.

## Mechanism notes
- **A (gate), inverted.** The abbot's gate is a permission: show the library connects or stay out. William goes in by stealth instead, which SDF forbids. Usable version: Aylin opens a shelf only when James can show the connection.
- **B (canary), seed.** Alinardo's apocalypse pattern is a story an old man believes, and William half-adopts it ("whether there were not some element of truth"). It finds the body. [from memory, not from the text: later the killer follows the pattern because William believes it, a canary that works backwards on the detective.] For SDF: the organisation may plant a pattern for James to "discover".
- **C (front), clear here.** The cellarer's night trade (sex for offal) and his possible Dolcinian past are a real secret that explains strange movement at night but not the murders. One solved layer is the cover story for the next. The abbot's fear of Gui is another: political scandal covers the book.
- **D (lock and key).** Salvatore hands Adso a lamp unasked ("Peut-être your magister wants to go in dark place"): a helper opens a door by choice, which fits SDF. William's leverage plan does not.
- **Fifth: the edited record.** Brother Michael protests that his written confession has been altered by the notary. William tells Adso that the story of Francis preaching to sweet birds is one "the order has revised today", when the original audience was carrion birds. Two institutional edits of the record in one day: SDF's "given" into "arose", already a background fact.

## Quotes
- "can maintain this learning unsullied only if it prevents its reaching anyone at all, even the monks themselves" (Third Day, Terce): the keeper's creed, preservation as withholding.
- "The maximum of confusion achieved with the maximum of order: it seems a sublime calculation." (Third Day, Vespers): the archive's defence is its design.
- "Michael protested that the notary had now added “many false consequences”" (Third Day, After Compline): an edited record as a weapon.
- "if there is need, the cellarer and Salvatore are ours" (Third Day, Night): the detective's leverage, which James may not use.

## Flags
- Real historical figures appear as persecutors (John XXII, Bernard Gui, Clement V). That is recorded history, not complicity in the fictional murders.
- William's stealth entry and his leverage plan break SDF's rules against breaking in and pressure.
- No invented edits on real books.
