## Why it grips
- **The clock is the liturgy.** Each chapter is an hour of the office, and the legation's deadline (Nones) sits on top of it.
- **Every witness steers.** Benno points to the library to keep William from the desk; the abbot points to heresy to keep him from the library; a thrown book pulls him away from the desk (Compline). William says each steer out loud, so the reader follows both what is said and why.
- **The best scene, built in three beats** (Compline): William finds a page; Adso's clumsiness with the lamp brings out hidden writing (an accident does the revealing, no genius needed); the intruder takes the *glasses*, not the page. He leaves the document in William's hand and takes away his ability to read it.
- **Fear, then a laugh.** Each horror in the library ends with William explaining it ("A mirror!", the wind slits), except the drugged vision, which is the one that hurts Adso.
- **Method admitted to fail.** Asked if his maze rule works, William says "Almost never" (Night); they find the stairs by luck.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The hidden order is the library's self-defence, and here it acts in layers, from soft to hard:
1. **The catalogue lies about status, not existence.** Entries are real, in marks "that only the librarian understands" (Prime); asking gets "lost", or Jorge's "ruined by mice" (Terce). The record is findable; the shelf is closed.
2. **The building discourages.** The east tower corner of the scriptorium is the coldest, so nobody sits near the only stair up (Terce, Adso's guess).
3. **Stagecraft that turns witnesses into madmen.** Herbs, mirrors, moaning wind, a lit thurible to suggest the dead walk. Aymaro says it outright: the powers of hell are used "to derange the minds of the curious" (Terce). Whoever gets in comes back with a ghost story, like Berengar. The archive discredits visitors rather than stopping them: SDF's organisation in miniature.
4. **The body points away.** William reads the dragged trail: the killer "does not want attention to be concentrated on the library" (Matins). The death is staged to point away.
5. **Power offers a front.** The abbot ties the murders to the Franciscans' politics and hands William a heretic cellarer. William: "So he offers me the suggestion of the cellarer, to distract my attention from the Aedificium" (Nones).

It makes William break the abbot's ban, and it makes him pay: the lenses, Adso's health, the abbot's trust.

## How the lore gets in
- **Benno's account of the laughter quarrel (Prime, about 1,200 words, conversation).** The lost *Poetics* book arrives as gossip and ends on "finis Africae" and a librarian's lie. No stall.
- **Aymaro on abbey politics (Terce, about 800 words, spite in the kitchen).** It slows a little, then ends on a warning ("be careful at night").
- **The Avignon negotiation (Nones, about 2,500 words, summary plus debate).** The longest still stretch, and it stalls for a page or two. It earns its place by ending in a deadline, and because the heresy argument is also the abbot steering.
- **The library's layout (Night, about 4,000 words, walked).** The lore comes as rooms crossed, with a shock about every page. No stall.
- **Cryptography (Compline, about 600 words, lecture).** The page is in his hand and the lenses are gone, so the lecture is the only way forward.

## Mechanism notes
- **A (gate), present as a deadline plus a ban.** William must rule out the abbot's alternative (the heretic cellarer) before the legation arrives; if he is wrong, the abbey goes under French control. For SDF: a gate gets weight from a third party's deadline that James does not control.
- **B (canary), a clean, legal version.** In Terce William says *loudly* that nobody may touch Venantius's desk. It "set the monks themselves to keep watch over Malachi", and by After Vespers Malachi, Benno and Berengar have each tried to steer him away, so they have shown themselves. No forbidden act. Cost: he has shown his hand, and the next document arrives already arranged.
- **C (front), three times.** Benno's library tip covers his desire for the desk; the abbot's heresy lead covers the library; the thrown book covers the theft. Each cover is true enough to follow.
- **D (lock and key).** Alinardo gives the key because William sits with him (price: chickpeas); Nicholas will make lenses because he wants to try something new. Each door opens by choice.
- **Fifth: disable the reader, not the text.** The thief leaves the page and takes the glasses. SDF's organisation could leave James the document and take his access, his credibility, or the one colleague who reads the format.
- **Method vs nose.** William's method is real (snow tracks, drowned faces), but his best moves are nose: the abbot's glare means "another door does exist" (First Day, Compline); Severinus pauses "too long" (Matins); the cipher starts from a guess. That is James's skill. But William also *presses*: he lets Berengar fear torture (Prime). James may not.

## Quotes
- "not only committed Malachi to keep watch over the monks, but also set the monks themselves to keep watch over Malachi" (Day 2, Terce): a canary made by one public sentence.
- "though it did indeed exist in the catalogue, it had been ruined by mice fifty years earlier" (Day 2, Terce): the catalogue lies about status, not existence.
- "the first rule in deciphering a message is to guess what it means." (Day 2, Compline): the method starts from the nose.
- "Knowledge is used to conceal, rather than to enlighten." (Day 2, Night): the archive's whole way of defending itself.

## Flags
- **Real people.** Pope John XXII is called a necromancer who feeds hosts to his morays (Alinardo, After Vespers), and real churchmen and princes (Michael of Cesena, Louis, Arnald Amalric and Béziers) are written into the plot. SDF must not do this.
- **Real book, invented copy.** The lost second book of the *Poetics* is a real gap; the plot hints a copy survives here. Not an edit to a real book, but close to SDF's line.
- **Pressure.** William gets Berengar's confession by letting him fear torture (Prime). This is forbidden for James.
