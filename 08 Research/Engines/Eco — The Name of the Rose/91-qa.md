## The archive and how it guards itself

**1. How does Eco's library actually keep people out?**
Mostly not with locks. There is no door between the scriptorium and the library, so the abbot's ban has to be stronger than any door (`01`). Then come layers: a public catalog that is useless without the room, a plan known only to librarian and assistant, rumors of ghosts for the simple, stagecraft (herbs, mirrors, moaning wind) for the bold (`01`, `02`), and finally a poisoned book (`05`, `06`). SDF's reading room, a public catalog plus a keeper, already has the first two.

**2. What is "the status lie," and does SDF have it?**
The catalog admits a book exists and lies about its state: "lost," or "ruined by mice fifty years earlier" (Day 2, Terce, `02`). The record is findable; the shelf is closed. SDF has the phrase ("withdrawn for conservation," List #17; "records moved for conservation," The schedule) but nobody reads the depot's own catalog as a record of when it was used. Map proposal 1 does.

**3. Who knows what in Eco's library?**
Custody is split. Malachi keeps the catalog and goes up into the library but does not know what he guards; Jorge, blind, knows what each title means (Day 6, Prime, `05`). The abbot's order after Malachi's death: monks "may consult the catalogue. Nothing else." So access is controlled at the catalog. Nicholas adds that librarians become abbots, which ties the secret to power (`05`).

**4. Is Aylin Malachi or Jorge?**
Malachi. v7 The sheets has her say she has kept the visitor book nine years and "never been told what the last column means." The old man is a partial Jorge: he knows the method but "doesn't read manuals" (v7 The reading room, continued). In SDF nobody holds the whole meaning, which is the Compartments point. Note `05` raised the question; the drafts had already answered it.

**5. Does the archive kill in person?**
Rarely, and that is the useful part. The poisoned book does the guarding when nobody is in the room, and it gets only those who want to read (`05`, `06`: "only to the extent that he wanted to read"). SDF's equivalent is the count: asking is what puts you on the list (The payload). Both make the victim's own curiosity the trigger.

**6. How does the archive treat people who get in?**
It makes them sound mad. Berengar comes back with a ghost story; Aymaro says the powers of hell are used "to derange the minds of the curious" (Day 2, Terce, `02`). This is SDF's discredit-not-prevent, at the scale of one building. The forum edit in The gorilla mask is the same move.

**7. What does the keeper believe he is doing?**
Preserving. Adso's Day 3 meditation states the creed: learning stays unsullied "only if it prevents its reaching anyone at all" (`03`). William later turns it over: the library "now lives to bury" its books (Day 5, Vespers, `05`). Aylin's "heritage… somebody has to" (v7 The sheets) is the sincere version; keep her believing it until the sheet.

**8. Why does hiding work at all if the open books lead to the hidden one?**
William's answer (Day 4, Terce, `04`): over centuries it is useless, over years or days it has some use. The archive delays the truth. That matches Monday's "you've bought time, a generation" and explains SDF's premise: if open books lead to the hidden thing, the open books have to be edited.

**9. Is the reading room on top of the recovery site like anything in Eco?**
The treasure crypt under the church is the nearest match (`05`): mostly fake relics, yet real wealth, so the institution's worth doesn't depend on its holdings being true. The library itself is a front for one room (the finis Africae). In SDF the structure is already there, the archive on top of the seats. The ADDENDUM's verdict on C stands: the building is something James learns, not an engine.

**10. How does Eco's archive end, and is that useful?**
By its own design: few entrances, untidy rooms, wind, a thrown lamp (Day 7, Night 2, `06`). Don't take the fire. Take the small echo already in v8 The night before: Aylin propping the outer door with a fire extinguisher, "the loudest thing she ever said."

## The engine

**11. Why can't William stop?**
Until Day 5, because of other people's clocks: the legation (Day 2), Gui's arrival (Day 3), the abbot's dawn expulsion (Day 6) (`02`, `03`, `06`). From Day 5, because of his own mistake (he set the book aside) and because the official answer is wrong and he knows it (`05`). See the map's engine table.

**12. Where is William's mistake, and what does it cost?**
Day 5, Sext (`05`). Filtering for Greek, he set aside a bound volume whose first pages were Arabic; it was the book. Severinus was already dead; Benno walks off with it, and the next man who holds it dies. He recovers by remembering Severinus's exact word, "strange." SDF lacks a mistake of James's that someone else pays for; the King and Foucault maps propose versions.

**13. What is the "false solution" and why is it strong?**
Bernard's trial gives the abbey a guilty man, a confession and an end (Day 5, Nones, `05`). The reader knows it is wrong because William says so, and Malachi's death that night breaks it in public. A closed case that reopens turns harder than an open case that stays open. In SDF the seat list in The money is the smaller true secret, and James's freezing theory is the case closed wrongly; the reopening is spread across Part Three.

**14. Does Eco use a rival investigator?**
Yes. Bernard questions only servants, uses fear, and condemns the girl the night he arrives (`04`). He is the clock: if William doesn't solve it, Bernard's story becomes the official one. SDF has no rival reader. The handlers (Kalder, Bram) judge the right column, but they are not racing James to an answer.

**15. How do the deaths keep the pace?**
Each death removes the person who knew most, so "who knows" is the threat: "After Berengar we are the ones who know most" (Day 4, Lauds, `04`). In SDF the professor, Noor and the Zwolle engineer play this role off-page. James is never told, in so many words, that he is now the one who knows most.

## The mechanisms

**16. What's the cleanest canary in the book, and can James use it?**
William announces loudly that nobody may touch Venantius's desk (Day 2, Terce, `02`), which sets the monks to watch Malachi, and by evening three have steered him away. One true public sentence, no invented item. James already does this in v7 The reading room, continued (he orders the sheets to see what moves Anneke). Don't add another.

**17. Does this book support the gate?**
Yes. William sets his own ("rather than appear foolish afterward, I renounce seeming clever now," Day 4, Vespers, `04`), and Bernard shows the cost of having none. All the clocks belong to third parties, as the ADDENDUM says they should. Note `04` suggests a PLACEHOLDER: James reads a pause as guilt and someone else pays, before he learns the gate.

**18. Is the front (C) any better here than in Tier 1?**
Yes, partly. Layers cover layers: the cellarer's night trade covers the murders (`03`), the heresy lead covers the library (`02`), Remigio's letters close the inquiry (`05`), and the forbidden text sits fourth in a binding of disreputable texts (`06`). It drives the plot when officials accept the smaller secret as the end. Map proposal 3 uses the binding.

**19. What is "the guard wears the hunter's theory"?**
Jorge heard that William believed the deaths followed the seven trumpets and fed the next sign to Malachi, so the deaths fit the pattern (Day 7, Night 1, `06`). William: "I conceived a false pattern… and the guilty man fell in with it." For SDF: the organization could let James's freezing theory stand as cover. The old man's "No dogs; I'm told it's a question of air" (v7 The reading room, continued) already half does it.

**20. What does "disable the reader, not the text" mean for SDF?**
The thief leaves William the page and takes his glasses (Day 2, Compline, `02`). For SDF, the organization leaves James the document and narrows his access, as Enjoy it while it lasts already does with page 212. Already on the page; not proposed.

**21. Co-optation: is it new for SDF?**
The move is: answer a questioner by giving him the job (Benno, Day 5, Vespers, `05`). SDF already does it to James ("Continuity want you," Leave it; the five seats). What is new is doing it to a helper who has already turned. Map proposal 2 does that at Pieter's door in The leak.

## The final conversation

**22. How does Jorge's talk compare with the old man's in The final conversation?**
Jorge is not beaten when he talks: he chose the room and the hour, talks to stall and tempt, and offers the book as "your prize," poisoned (`06`). SDF's old man is retired and talks because it no longer matters (v8 The final conversation). Note `06` says the beaten head still needs something on the table: an offer, a test, or a thing to destroy. Map proposal 4 gives him a status line on a card.

**23. Who explains in Eco's last scene, the hero or the villain?**
The hero. William reconstructs the deaths and Jorge confirms (Day 7, Night 1, `06`), then Jorge gives his reasons. In v8 The final conversation the old man does almost all of it. If Santi wants James to earn the talk, one option is for James to say his own reconstruction first and be corrected on freezing. PLACEHOLDER.

**24. What does Jorge fear, and does it match the old man?**
Not laughter or heresy, which "we know how to destroy" and whose "presence is precious to us," but one respectable text that turns what common people do into doctrine: "Provided the act is not transformed into plan" (`06`). That is the old man's "it comes out all the time… we're there first" (v8 The final conversation). It also explains why the sheets work where forums didn't: they are the bank's own paperwork.

**25. How did Jorge pick William out?**
By a question William never asked: "you never asked questions about the library" (`06`). SDF already has the reversed form: "stopped asking out loud, which is worse" (A car park). Not proposed.

**26. Does James laughing in the final conversation have a source here?**
It has a match. The forbidden book is about laughter, and William answers Jorge's speech with insults and friar jokes (`06`). v8 The final conversation already has James laugh at "Walt." Keep it; it's the right weapon against a man who needs to be believed.

**27. Should the old man destroy something at the end, like Jorge?**
Not a fire, and not violence. SDF's organization discredits rather than prevents. The scaled-down version is a status change, "withdrawn for conservation," written in his hand on his last day, which Aylin overrules when James asks (map proposal 4).

## Transfer specifics

**28. Which of the map's proposals is cheapest to write?**
Proposal 2 (Pieter's door). It adds one object to a scene that already exists in v7 The leak and one line to the twelfth envelope in The night before. Proposal 1 needs Thursday beats across Part Three that v8.2 does not list.

**30. Is Aylin's nine years matching Kalder's transfer nine years ago canon?**
The numbers are both in the drafts (v7 The sheets for Aylin; v8.2 Leiden, nine years ago for Kalder) but nothing connects them. Proposal 1 uses the match. PLACEHOLDER: intended or coincidence.

**31. Which proposal conflicts with other maps?**
Proposal 3 (the paperback) competes with Foucault 5.2 (a fake sheet on the reading-room table, cost with Anneke) and 5.4 (a negative in the grants, cost with Frank). Take one of the three. Proposal 3 is the only one where James is right and still pays.

**32. Does proposal 3 break the real-book rule?**
Not if both books are invented: the forty-one entries and the channelled paperback. Never put an original wording into a real crank book, and never claim a real publisher was funded.

**33. Which section-note proposals did the map drop, and why?**
Note `02`'s "ask Aylin publicly for a damaged record" in The reading room, continued: James has no visit before 2 December and the public canary is already in that draft. Note `04`'s "a catalog that skips the lower floor": canon puts James on that floor at The drill. Note `05`'s "Aylin offers James a role": already done by Leave it and the five seats.

**34. What should never be borrowed from this book?**
William's stealth, his leverage plan, the fear of torture he uses (`02`, `03`); real popes and inquisitors as plotters (`01`–`05`); and inventing the contents of a real lost book (`02`, `06`). See map section 6.

**35. Where is the best single scene to study for craft?**
Day 2, Compline (`02`): William finds a page, Adso's lamp reveals the hidden writing by accident, and the intruder takes the glasses, not the page. Three beats, and the document is in the hero's hand at the moment he loses the ability to read it. Day 5, Nones (`05`), the trial, is the best lesson in lore that doesn't stall.
