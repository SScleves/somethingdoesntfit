## Why it grips
- **A clock set by the wrong man.** The abbot's dismissal (Day 6, Nones) gives the last act its deadline. William's fury on the stairs ("that inflated wineskin") keeps it funny while the stakes go up.
- **The withheld key comes from the sidekick, twice.** Adso's dream points to the Coena Cypriani (After Terce). Adso's "tertius equi" gives away the mirror lock (After Compline). In both scenes the master's method only becomes useful once someone else blurts something out.
- **The confession scene is a seduction.** Adso says outright that the two men admire each other (Day 7, Night 1). William does most of the explaining. The villain's speech comes only after the hero has earned it, and it is offered as a prize, "Here is your prize", in the form of a poisoned book. So the explanation is a weapon too: will William lick his finger?
- **The win becomes a loss on the page.** The book is eaten in the dark, and the fire starts from a lamp knocked over in a scuffle.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The conspiracy here is one blind keeper and the people he controls through who holds each post: an abbot he got elected, two librarians he ran (Day 7, Night 1). Abo protects the abbey's honour and Jorge protects a book, and that clash is what kills the abbot. The abbot's reflex is SDF's organisation in one line: he does not deny the story, he tells William it will not be believed. Jorge's reasons go further than the abbot's. He does not fear laughter, heresy or rebels; he says "we know how to destroy them" and that "their presence is precious to us". He fears one respectable text that would turn what common people do into a learned doctrine ("Provided the act is not transformed into plan"). That is SDF's clown beside every real question, with the reason spelled out: the noise is useful; the danger is one document with authority.

The defence in this archive is reading. The poison only gets the people who want to read. Jorge picked out William by the question he never asked ("you never asked questions about the library"). SDF's nightly count of askers, turned around: Jorge counts the missing question.

## How the lore gets in
- **The catalogue (Day 6, Sext), about 900 words, an object read with a method.** Changes of handwriting and dated papal bulls set the order of the librarians. It ends in "Only some premises", and the linen-paper clue follows at once. No stall: a date points to a person.
- **The abbot's numerology and gems (Day 6, Nones), about 1,000 words, a speech as a power move.** He dazzles Adso with the ring and orders him to "Swear"; William breaks in.
- **William's reconstruction (Day 7, Night 1), about 2,000 words, the hero's own explanation.** The detective explains the deaths, the villain confirms; the open poisoned book keeps the pace.
- **Jorge's reasons (Day 7, Night 1), about 1,700 words, one monologue.** It is the only long stretch of pure argument. Carried by six days of waiting and the threat on the table; William answers with insults and friar jokes.
- **The fire (Day 7, Night 2), about 3,500 words, a scene.** Why the archive dies is physical: few entrances, untidy rooms, wind.
- **Last Page, about 2,000 words.** History in a few paragraphs, then Adso's fragments: a deliberate, elegiac slowdown.

## Mechanism notes
- **C (front), already in the catalogue.** The forbidden text is the fourth item in a bound volume, between an Arab book of jokes, an alchemist's "ravings" and a Coena gloss, and it is listed as a book about virgins and prostitutes (Day 6, Sext). The disreputable company is the hiding place: "No one would lend an ear" (Day 7, Night 1). For SDF: what Aylin's catalogue hides could be filed inside the nonsense the organisation pays for, the entry true but unappealing.
- **Fifth: the guardian uses the investigator's theory.** Jorge heard William believed in the seven trumpets and fed it to Malachi ("the power of a thousand scorpions"), so the deaths fit the pattern and the wrong theory became cover. The reverse of B. For SDF, a wrong theory of James's could come back to him as confirmation, and he pays for believing it: the missing cost of being wrong.
- **A (gate), as a clock.** The expulsion forces a decision by morning, but no gate checks whether William is right. Eco then shows what that costs: William reaches the right man by a false route and says so himself ("I discovered it by mistake", Day 7, Night 2).
- **D (lock and key).** The mirror opens with one exact truth ("quatuor"), but it is a mechanism; in SDF the lock must be a person.
- **The defeated head talks: a contrast with SDF.** Jorge is not defeated when he talks. He chose the room and hour and talks to stall and tempt, and "wins" only by destroying the book and himself. SDF's old head, talking in defeat, still needs something on the table: an offer, a test, a thing he can destroy.
- **Method against nose.** William's method works on objects (handwriting, dated bulls, paper). His nose (the right questions, listening for what people avoid) is what Jorge noticed. Breakthroughs come from another's chance remark. James, *a nose, not a method*, fits this ending better than William does. William's ladder you climb and throw away lets James be wrong and still arrive.

## Quotes
- "Speak of it, if you wish: no one will believe you." (Day 6, Nones): the guardian discredits instead of preventing.
- "the victim poisoned himself when he was alone, and only to the extent that he wanted to read" (Day 7, Night 1): the defence works through reading.
- "I conceived a false pattern to interpret the moves of the guilty man, and the guilty man fell in with it." (Day 7, Night 1): the hunter's theory turned into cover.
- "The library had been doomed by its own impenetrability, by the mystery that protected it, by its few entrances." (Day 7, Night 2): how the archive ends.

## Flags
- Real historical figures (Louis the Bavarian, John XXII, Bernard Gui) are background only; none is complicit in the murders.
- Eco makes up the surviving contents of a real lost book (the second book of Aristotle's Poetics) and has it destroyed. Not an invented edit, but close to SDF's rule: invent the hidden document outright, not as a real lost work.
