## Why it grips
- **The detective's mistake is the engine.** The book is lost because of William's method, not bad luck. He was filtering for Greek and threw out a bound volume of several texts whose first pages were Arabic. He corrects himself by recalling Severinus's exact word, "strange" (Day 5, Sext), so the method is rescued by remembering what someone actually said. That is close to James's nose. The error has a price: the book moves on, and in the next section the man holding it dies. SDF lacks exactly this, a hero whose mistake gets someone hurt.
- **A false answer arrives before the true one.** Bernard's trial (Day 5, Nones) gives the abbey a guilty man, a confession and an end. The reader knows it is wrong because William says so. Malachi's death at matins (Day 6) then breaks the official story in front of everyone. A closed case that reopens is a stronger turn than an open case that stays open.
- **Still funny:** "No, that's Arabic, idiot!"; the John the Baptist skull "at the age of twelve"; William's whisper that Jorge's Antichrist "seems his own portrait".

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The archive protects its book three ways, and this section shows all of them working.
1. **The book defends itself.** Readers die with black fingers. Severinus put on gloves; Malachi held the book and did not. Nobody has to be present for the guarding to work, and that is how the library can kill so rarely in person.
2. **It recruits the curious.** Malachi does not threaten Benno. He makes him assistant librarian, and Benno hands the book back. William: "Now he is the guardian of those secrets he wanted to know." The person who asked questions becomes a keeper. Malachi also sets two keepers watching each other, and William copies the move by sending Benno to watch Malachi.
3. **It divides knowledge.** Malachi keeps the catalogue but cannot read what it lists ("a guard dog ... with no knowledge of what he was guarding", Day 6, Prime). Jorge, who is blind, knows what each title means. The abbot orders that monks "may consult the catalogue. Nothing else." The catalogue is where access is controlled.

The secret is also tied to power: by tradition the librarian becomes abbot. Nicholas's gossip that they died "so that they would not become abbot" is a wrong explanation the reader cannot yet rule out.

Bernard models SDF's discrediting. Indifferent to who killed, he uses one confessed murderer to tie respected theologians to Dolcino's ravings (his "sixth sign" condemns "the authors of those books" heretics used). Every serious argument gets a madman beside it: SDF's clown beside every real question.

**For Aylin (proposal).** Which keeper is she, Malachi or Jorge? Eco's split suggests the reading room should have both: a visible keeper who runs the catalogue, and someone behind her who knows what the entries mean. James's nose would catch the keeper deferring. The treasure crypt under the church matches the reading room on the bank's recovery site: the relics are mostly fakes ("an entire forest") yet real wealth, so the foundation's worth need not depend on its holdings being true.

## How the lore gets in
- **William's political speech (Day 5, Terce), about 2,000 words.** Played as comedy and cut off by the archers' news.
- **Remigio's trial (Day 5, Nones), about 5,500 words, a scene.** The Dolcino history comes in as a forced confession, with a life at stake in every answer. No stall: an oath counts as proof of guilt, and so does refusing one.
- **Jorge's sermon (Day 5, Compline), about 3,000 words, a public speech.** It sets out the archive's ideology ("Preservation ... not search") and doubles as a warning to whoever is still reading. It stalls, but the reader knows it is a threat.
- **Nicholas in the crypt (Day 6, Prime), about 1,500 words, gossip during a tour.** The succession history comes while he polishes glass cases, and the objects carry the scene.
- **Adso's dream (Day 6, Terce), about 3,500 words.** A recap and the biggest stall, but it plants clues: "a book shaped like a scorpion", Jesus with black fingers handing out pages, and William's "You have already seen it!" [from memory, not from the text: the dream follows the *Coena Cypriani*, which later helps William].

## Mechanism notes
- **A (gate), shown by its absence.** William will not warn the abbot without "convincing evidence" (Day 6, Lauds). Bernard rules nothing out and burns the wrong man: what James becomes if he skips the gate.
- **C (front), strong.** Remigio's Dolcino letters, hidden by Malachi "in the most inaccessible of the secret rooms of the library", are a real secret found in the same vault, and finding them closes the inquiry. A true but smaller secret serves as the cover story. Compare SDF's "findable" seat list: finding it should feel like the end of the hunt.
- **D (lock and key).** Benno's key is the job. Nicholas talks because he has just been promoted and is proud. Remigio breaks when Bernard gives him a past he can own. Each door opens because the person chooses to open it.
- **B (canary), weak.** William plants a fear of the book in Benno and watches who acts on it.
- **Fifth: co-optation.** The organisation answers a questioner by giving him the job, not by killing him. **Proposal for *The reading room continued*:** Aylin offers James a small real role in the catalogue. Taking it gives him access but costs him the right to tell anyone, and refusing it marks him. Either way he pays.

## Quotes
- "This library was perhaps born to save the books it houses, but now it lives to bury them." (Day 5, Vespers): the archive's purpose turned upside down, in one line.
- "Now he is the guardian of those secrets he wanted to know" (Day 5, Vespers): the questioner co-opted.
- "Malachi kept the catalogue and went up into the library, but Jorge knew what each title meant." (Day 6, Prime): split custody.
- "We looked, but did not see. Or else saw, but did not recognize." (Day 5, Sext): the method's blind spot.
