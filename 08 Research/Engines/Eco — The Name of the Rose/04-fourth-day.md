## Why it grips
- **The door is found before it can be opened.** The night tour is about 3,500 words of shelf-reading, carried by its purpose. It ends when mirror room S proves to be the tower with no heptagon. William argues from space (without the room the others would be larger), so the hidden room arrives as a deduction. Then the mirror won't move. The reader knows where the secret is and cannot reach it.
- **Questioning a man with numbers.** William lets Remigio reel off measures of land, oil and wine, then asks again, "How much did you say a square trabucco was?" (Prime). Each time the dull question comes back, it carries the Inquisition's threat.
- **Two investigators side by side.** Bernard questions only servants and lay brothers and uses "the fear of others" (Nones). William admits at Vespers that he beat Brunellus by luck. Adso, tired of not knowing, thinks "Good thing the inquisitor has come." Bernard's certainty condemns the girl that night.
- **Funny throughout.** Salvatore's black-cat spell, two bishops brawling over Christ's purse.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The hidden order is the library and whoever controls who reads what.

1. **It explains why hiding works only for a while.** Adso asks why anyone hides books when the open ones lead to the hidden ones. William answers that hiding is useless over centuries and useful over days, and agrees the library exists to delay the truth (Terce). An archive that delays the truth instead of destroying it is SDF's foundation. It also explains SDF's premise: if open books lead to the hidden one, the open books have to be edited.
2. **It puts the keeper in league with the inquisitor.** Malachi holds Remigio's secret ("Does he know something about you?" "Yes."), cannot refuse William a reading, and at Fifth Day Prime hands Bernard papers in the fog. His power is knowing everyone's secrets and deciding who reads, which is what Aylin's visitor book could hold.
3. **It discredits rather than prevents.** Bernard doesn't stop the meeting. He turns an innocent bundle (cat, eggs, a cockerel that looks black at night) into witchcraft with a lecture on sources, and William expects him to hold it back to wreck the talks later. The debate also wrecks itself: Jerome repeats a rumour he admits he hasn't seen, and a brawl breaks out while Bernard smiles. This is SDF's clown beside the real question, shown working.

## How the lore gets in
- **Severinus on poisons (Lauds), about 900 words, talk over the body.** It ends in the black tongue and a missing jar. No stall.
- **Remigio's Dolcino story (Prime), about 700 words, a confession under pressure.** It ends in a fact: Venantius dead by the sink. No stall.
- **Venantius's notes (Terce), about 100 words, a document.** The lore comes in fragments. William's point that the nature of the stolen object shows the nature of the killer turns them into a motive.
- **Adso's love meditation (Terce), about 3,000 words.** A deliberate stall while William reads; the weakest stretch.
- **The John XXII table talk (Sext), about 4,000 words.** Adso admits it "does not add much". Its one plant is John's doctrine that the dead won't see God before the Judgment, which pays off at Night when William jokes that Saint Francis may be "waiting for judgment day". Its subject is what happens after death, as in SDF, but it comes as gossip.
- **The library walk (After Compline), about 3,500 words, a tour with a map.** The lore comes as walking and reading the letters over the doors, and it ends at the walled room. The best delivery in the section.
- **The poverty debate (Day 5, Prime), about 3,500 words.** Argument that turns into a brawl.

## Mechanism notes
- **A (gate).** William sets his own gate: "rather than appear foolish afterward, I renounce seeming clever now" (Vespers). Bernard shows what acting without one costs: he takes the obvious explanation, and the girl pays. PLACEHOLDER for SDF: James reads a pause as guilt and someone else pays, before he learns to rule out the bigger explanation. Part Two currently has no cost for being wrong.
- **D (lock and key), literally.** The finis Africae opens only to one correct reading of one text (*idolum* as the image in the mirror). William has half of it. In SDF the lock can be a person who opens it when James reads a document correctly.
- **C (front).** Venantius's notes first read as Dolcinian instructions, which is the cover story tied to Remigio. The figs, stone and cicadas don't fit that reading, which points to a different book underneath.
- **B (canary).** William doesn't use one. Bernard does the reverse: he dresses a harmless object up as evil and watches who flinches.
- **Fifth: finding the absence in a plan.** The hidden room is found from the building's symmetry. PROPOSAL for "The reading room" or "The sheets": Aylin's catalogue skips a run of shelfmarks, and the lower floor James knows from The drill appears nowhere in it. He reads the gap himself. The door opens only when Aylin decides to show him the visitor book. Not on the page yet.
- **Method against nose.** Here the reader of faces is the inquisitor, whose weapon is the fear he sees. James's nose is nearer Bernard's skill than William's method, so his gift can do harm.

## Quotes
- "Over the centuries it is no use at all. In a space of years or days it has some use." (Day 4, Terce): the archive delays the truth.
- "The room exists, but cannot be reached." (Day 4, After Compline): the hidden room deduced from the plan.
- "But instead of conceiving only one, I imagine many, so I become the slave of none." (Day 4, Vespers): William's method.
- "He will keep it in reserve: it will be of use later, to upset the progress of the discussions" (Day 4, Night): discredit, don't prevent.

## Flags
- Real historical people get invented speech and charges: John XXII (perjury, greed, the penance tariff Jerome repeats), Bernard Gui, Michael of Cesena, Bertrand del Poggetto. The deaths of Clement V and Philip the Fair are treated as suspicious (Sext). SDF must not name real people as complicit.
- The library shelves Virgil of Toulouse under Hibernia ("They corrected the errors of nature."). That is a misfiling, not an edit to a text, so there is no invented edit on a real book here.
