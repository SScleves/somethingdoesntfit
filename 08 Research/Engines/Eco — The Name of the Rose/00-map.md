# Umberto Eco, *The Name of the Rose* — the map

> **Coverage:** William Weaver's English translation (note `06` names the Warner Books 1984 printing; the others give no edition data). Read in full through six section notes (`01`–`06`, about 184,000 words), 11 Nov 2026; map written 12 Nov 2026. Anything from outside the notes is marked *[from memory, not from the text]*.
> **Brief:** an archive that kills to keep one book unread: how the library guards itself, who holds which part of the secret, and how a beaten guardian talks. Compared with SDF's foundation archive on top of the recovery site, its catalog and visitor book, Aylin, the one thing taken out of every book, and the final conversation.
> **Standing-rule note:** real popes and inquisitors are plotters (`01`–`05`). Eco invents the surviving text of a real lost work, Aristotle on comedy (`02`, `06`): not an edit to a real book, but next door to SDF's line. William enters by stealth (`02`–`04`), plans leverage (`03`), and lets Berengar fear torture (`02`). Take none of it.

## 1. What it is

In 1327 the Franciscan William of Baskerville and his novice Adso reach a rich abbey to prepare a meeting between pope and emperor. A monk has fallen from a wall, and over seven days more die, each tied to the library, which only the librarian and his assistant may enter. William works around the ban, maps the labyrinth from outside, gets in at night, and loses his glasses, a book, and the case to an inquisitor who burns the wrong people. On the last night he finds the hidden room and the blind monk Jorge, who has poisoned the one book he cannot bear to have read. Jorge eats it, and the library burns.

What makes it work: the archive's defense is a set of ordinary arrangements (a ban, a catalog that tells you nothing, two keepers who each know half), so every step William takes is a step around a person. And whenever William gets close, someone else pays first, so the clock is kept in bodies, not hours.

## 3. How the lore gets in

- **The abbot on the library** (Day 1, about 900 words, `01`): a speech in a negotiation that ends in "no."
- **The catalog** (Day 1; Day 6, about 900 words, `01`, `06`): an object on a chain. The second reading uses a method (hands, dates, paper) and ends in a name.
- **The labyrinth** (Day 2, Night, about 4,000 words; Day 4, about 3,500, `02`, `04`): walked, a shock a page; the second tour ends at a wall. The best delivery in the book.
- **Jorge's reasons** (Day 7, about 1,700 words, `06`): pure argument, held up by an open poisoned book on the table.

**Where it stalls:** the John XXII table talk (Day 4, Sext, `04`); Adso admits it "does not add much." **Where it doesn't:** Remigio's trial (Day 5, Nones, about 5,500 words, `05`), which is the same Dolcino history that stalled on Day 3, now with a life at stake in every answer. The same material moves when someone in the room can lose by it.

## 4. The mechanism we came for: an archive guarding one book

**Setup** (Day 1, Terce, `01`). No door divides scriptorium from library; the ban must be stronger than a lock ("The library defends itself"). The catalog is public and useless without the room.
**Rule** (`03`, `05`). The keeper's creed: learning stays unsullied "only if it prevents its reaching anyone at all." After Malachi, monks "may consult the catalogue. Nothing else." Access is controlled at the catalog.
**First use** (Day 2, `02`). Soft before hard. The catalog lies about status, not existence ("ruined by mice"). Herbs and mirrors send intruders back with ghost stories. A body is moved to point away.
**Escalation** (`04`, `05`). Split custody: Malachi keeps the catalog without knowing what he guards; Jorge knows. The keepers hold everyone's secrets. The questioner is recruited (Benno). The book kills only those who want to read it. A true smaller secret (Remigio's letters, from the same vault) closes the case officially.
**Cost.** The archive "now lives to bury" its books (Day 5, Vespers); Malachi dies by his own guard.
**Payoff** (Day 7, `06`). Jorge chose the room and hour. William explains; Jorge confirms, then offers the book as "your prize," poisoned. He picked William out by the question William never asked, and fed William's wrong pattern to Malachi so the deaths fit it. He fears not laughter or heretics ("their presence is precious to us") but one respectable text that turns the act into a plan. He destroys the book rather than lose it.

**Against A–D.**
- **A, the gate:** supported. William sets his own (Day 4, Vespers); Bernard, with none, burns the wrong people. Every clock is a third party's. New: **the rival investigator as the clock**: waiting costs because a worse reader's answer becomes the official one.
- **B, the canary:** supported, and it corrects the ADDENDUM's assumption that B needs an invented item. William announces *loudly* that nobody may touch Venantius's desk (Day 2, Terce) and by evening three monks have tried to steer him off it. One public sentence about a real object.
- **C, the front:** stronger than in any Tier 1 book. Each solved layer covers the next (`02`, `03`, `05`), and the catalog files the forbidden text fourth in a binding, between jokes and an alchemist's ravings (`06`). It partly answers the ADDENDUM's verdict that C is only learned. It drives the plot when the official side accepts the smaller secret as the end and a death reopens it.
- **D, lock and key:** steady; doors open by choice (`02`, `03`). The final lock is a mechanism; in SDF it must be a person.

**New mechanisms**, checked against the ADDENDUM: split custody (`05`); the status lie (`02`); co-optation of the questioner (`05`); disable the reader, not the text, the glasses taken and the page left (`02`); the guard wears the hunter's theory (`06`); the truth filed among the nonsense (`06`).

## 5. Transfer to SDF

Checked against v8.2, the v8 brief, the v7 Part Two and Three drafts, and v8 The night before and The final conversation. **Already on the page, so not proposed:** split custody (Aylin "never told what the last column means," v7 The sheets, is Malachi); a keeper who believes the creed ("heritage… somebody has to," same draft); "no one will believe you" ("what they are *in a room* is nothing," v7 The reading room, continued); the public sentence that makes a watcher move (James lays the sheets "to see which one made the woman by the window move," same draft); co-opting James (Leave it; the five seats); laughter against the beaten head ("Walt fucking Disney," v8 The final conversation). Most of the section notes' own proposals fail canon: James cannot ask Aylin for anything before 2 December (`02`), and the lower floor is not hidden from him (`04`).

**1. Thursdays at the card index.** *The reading room, continued → The sheets → The night before.*
- *Change:* the "Same time Thursday?" James says yes to (v7) gets a purpose: he reads the one thing any reader may consult, the card index, and dates it as William dates the librarians (Day 6, Sext, `06`).
- *James does:* across Part Three's Thursdays he reads drawers, not books. Five or six hands since 1931; "withdrawn for conservation" in later hands, clustered in review quarters; the first card in Aylin's hand dated nine years ago, the season of Kalder's library transfer (v7 gives her nine years at the desk; PLACEHOLDER whether the match is meant). Her own withdrawals, dated, go on her sheet.
- *Cost:* Aylin. v7 says hers was the thinnest sheet, one James "couldn't make… say anything that would hurt her." Now it does, in her hand, and she watched him read it. Showing him the visitor book becomes the harder choice.
- *Already on the page?* Partly: the Thursday and the card index (v7); "the cards squared to the edge of the desk" (v8 The night before). Nobody reads them.
- *Already proposed?* No. Borges 1 and Orwell 4 use "withdrawn for conservation" on outside copies, not the depot's catalog.

**2. The man at Pieter's door brings a better seat.** *The leak → The night before.*
- *Change:* the visit after the brewery man's report carries an offer: an amended allocation, a fourth place, for Pieter's mother. Benno's promotion (`05`), and an echo of Utrecht, 1993 ("not his mother; it's a budgeting thing").
- *James does:* tells Pieter on the phone to sign it, keep the copy, say nothing. The signed form, dated the night of the leak, goes into Anneke's envelope as the organization's own reply to the sheets.
- *Cost:* Pieter signs his mother onto a list he now understands, at his kitchen table, with Marieke watching, on James's advice. PLACEHOLDER: does Pieter forgive it.
- *Already on the page?* Partly. v7 The leak has the visit, the tea, "*likes lists*," a card. No offer, no paper.
- *Already proposed?* No. Pairs with Orwell 6. Risk: James as manipulator; Pieter must be free to refuse.

**3. What they kept, filed in the nonsense they fund.** *The band → The reading room, continued → The final conversation.*
- *Change:* one cold-year grantee, an invented press of channelled-wisdom paperbacks, prints in a book's middle the original wording of one of the forty-one entries, credited to a spirit guide. Filed like Jorge's book, among company nobody hears (`06`).
- *James does:* buys the back list because he reads middles, finds the sentence, and lays the paperback at the reading-room table as an eighth item. It is the only one the old man picks up; he reads the cover copy aloud, and Anneke smiles.
- *Cost:* being *right* in the wrong binding. He has handed Anneke a reason to put him with the clowns. In The sheets her "the rest of it" leaves the paperback out. PLACEHOLDER: kept there on purpose ("took out of every book and kept"), or a crank owned a first impression.
- *Already on the page?* Partly: "Nobody reads the middle." / "I read the middle" (v8 The final conversation), with no object behind it.
- *Already proposed?* No, but it competes with Foucault 5.2 (a fake among the sheets) and 5.4 (a negative in the grants). Take one. This is the one where he is right and still pays.

**4. The beaten head's last act is a status line, and James finally asks.** *The vet → The final conversation.*
- *Change:* James never asks for the shelfmark. The ledger is on the tray before he can, and v7 The sheets says of his line: "No shelfmark; I hadn't asked for one." Noor's "nobody has ever simply asked" and the thin book's *Ask* are never paid. Jorge destroys rather than lose; the SDF-sized version: on his last day with keys the old man marks that shelfmark's card "withdrawn for conservation," in his own hand.
- *James does:* when the old man has gone, asks Aylin for the item by number. She strikes the status through, initials it, and brings it to a table that was empty all scene.
- *Cost:* his name enters the "shelfmark requested" column beside the professor's March line, in a book that will outlast Anneke's eighteen months; Aylin has overruled the old man on a card anyone can read. PLACEHOLDER: what the item is and whether he opens it. Keep v8's last line ("Pending" struck, nothing beside it).
- *Already on the page?* Partly: Aylin books the room and writes in the book (v8); the column exists (v7 The sheets). The request does not.
- *Already proposed?* No. Borges 1 and Foucault 5.1 use the same column for other moments.

## 6. Do not take

- William's methods: stealth entries, leverage on the cellarer and Salvatore, the torture he lets Berengar fear (`02`, `03`).
- A real lost book with invented contents, and real churchmen as plotters (`01`–`06`). Invent the hidden document outright.
- The stalls Eco can afford: the Day 3 heresy block, the table talk, the dream (`03`–`05`). SDF's lore must reach someone who can lose by it within a page or two.
