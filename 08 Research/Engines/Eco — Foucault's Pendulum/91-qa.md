## The canary itself

**1. What is the canary in this book, in one sentence?**
An invented thing placed where a believer will find it, so that what he *does* with it shows who he is and what he serves. Eco runs it seven times, from Ardenti's published theory (ch. 20) to Belbo's fake map (ch. 108). See map section 4 and `05-chapters-64-to-83.md`, `06-chapters-84-to-109.md`.

**2. What is the tell, exactly? Belief?**
No. In the clearest case Agliè doesn't claim to know the fake sect; he asks who mentioned it, asks again, says it does not matter, and checks his watch (ch. 76). The tell is the second question and the quick exit. In the later case (ch. 108) the tell is action on a concrete hook: a date, a place, a map (`05`, `06`).

**3. Kalder in On land says he has read James's invented book. Is that Eco's tell?**
It is a weaker one. It is closer to Diotallevi feeding numerology to Ardenti (ch. 19): a believer, or a pretender, takes in any addition and thanks you for it (`02-chapters-14-to-27.md`). It proves vanity or bluff, not who Kalder reports to. Map proposal 5.1 adds the step that does prove it: the hook gets acted on and the action comes back through a third party.

**4. Is the On land canary already written?**
Only in the plan. v8.2 On land has the invented book and Kalder's "Oh, that one. Yes," and The dinner calls it back ("the book you said you'd read"). The only draft, `01 Manuscript/v6/18 — On land.md`, is the older version built on the Deventer slip, with no invented book. No v7 or v8 draft of On land exists.

**5. Does the pipeline's claim that On land "drops" the canary hold?**
Half. Nothing in Part Two follows it up, but in the plan The dinner cashes it in Part Three as James's stated reason for doubt. What's missing is anything between the pub and the dinner that the canary *does*. See map 5.1.

**6. What is the gate inside the canary?**
The psychic case (ch. 24–25): a private phrase surfaces in public and its carrier vanishes, then it turns out her magazines had already printed the phrase. Once that is known, nobody can say what the canary showed (`02`). The rule for SDF: a bait tells James something only if nobody else could have printed or heard it. In On land, Ana laughing at something else (v8.2) is what lets the gate pass.

**7. Why plant a fake inside a list rather than say it alone?**
Because then noticing the fake is itself the tell (ch. 75–76, `05`). Someone who refuses everything in the same tone has told you nothing. Someone who asks about the one item he didn't recognize has confirmed the rest. Map 5.2 puts this in The reading room, continued.

**8. What does the canary cost the person who plants it?**
First a lie about the source, which can't be taken back without exposing the planter (ch. 76). In the end it costs him his credibility: Belbo's denial reads as proof (ch. 110, 118, `07-chapters-110-to-120.md`). The notes also find strangers counted: two Libyans detained after Belbo's train joke (ch. 108, `06`).

**9. Who believes the invention first?**
The inventors. Casaubon at Tomar (ch. 67), Belbo forgetting it is fake (ch. 68–69), all three "addicted," "converted" (ch. 87). Casaubon reads shop signs as signs as early as ch. 63 (`04-chapters-46-to-63.md`, `05`, `06`). For SDF that suggests a cheap negative: James seeing his own invented title out in the world (map 5.4, The band).

**10. Does the canary ever work for the hero?**
Never. Every canary in the book ends up turned on the people who planted it. SDF wants it reversed: James's instrument, with a price. What carries over is how it is built (a unique item, an actionable hook, a readout through a third party, a cost), not where it ends (map section 4).

**11. Is there a canary aimed at the hero?**
Yes. De Angelis says "Tres" to see whether Casaubon reacts, and Casaubon lies (ch. 53, `04`). Map 5.5 gives James the same test, set by Pieter in The drill, so that the lie costs him in The sheets.

## The clowns

**12. How do the Diabolicals work as clowns beside the real question?**
They come in as manuscripts at a publisher's door, sorted by a typology (ch. 10, 12), and they stay funny while a pattern forms (Bramanti in Rio, then in Milan; Camestres funny for two pages, then threatening, ch. 31, 41, 44). At the rite the same clowns kill (ch. 113). See `01-chapters-1-to-13.md`, `03-chapters-28-to-45.md`, `07`.

**13. Who bites a canary first?**
Clowns. Project Hermes's fake inquiries draw Bramanti and Camestres before any hidden order shows (ch. 43–44, `03`). Map 5.3 lets the forum man from The gorilla mask bite James's forum bait first, so James can't tell decoy from organization.

**14. Does any clown hold a true thread?**
Salon. He believes his own tunnel plot, but his father served the tsarist secret police under a man whose name echoes "Rakosky," and Casaubon refuses the link (ch. 78–79, `05`). Also the hack manuscript on Saint-Germain that arrives in the slush pile and gives his real name as "Marquis of Aglie" (ch. 109, `06`). SDF's Beard Man already plays this part (the bucket theory).

**15. How does the hidden side use its clowns?**
As cover and as detectors. "What better hiding place for the true Templar than in the crowd of his caricatures?" (ch. 61, `04`); "The mystic is useful, because he is conspicuous" (ch. 33, `03`). That matches SDF's thermostat, and it warns that any believer James watches is being watched as well.

**16. Is there anything like SDF's farmed forums?**
Garamond/Manutius: a respectable imprint that steers cranks to the paying one, and Garamond's rule that crank books must repeat each other (ch. 38, 42, 45, `03`). The gorilla mask's edited forum account is already SDF's version.

## Lore

**17. How does Eco keep 12,000 words of Plan history moving?**
He mostly doesn't, and the notes say so. Ch. 84–96 stall on purpose to show the habit setting in, held up by punchlines, crank epigraphs and Diotallevi's yellow skin (`06`). Don't copy this. SDF has no hanging promised in the frame to carry it.

**18. What is the best-carried piece of lore?**
The Rosicrucian manifestoes as pillow talk (ch. 29–30, about 4,500 words): Amparo heckles, the skeptic does the best reasoning, and it ends in sex (`03`). The other is the list of sects (ch. 75), a dead catalogue that pays off in the next chapter because the trap is inside it (`05`).

**19. How does the heckler work?**
Each lump of lore answers an objection ("Make beer?", "But suppose it's overcast?", ch. 82), so the objection forces the next invention and the lore arrives as a solved problem (`05`). For SDF: Frank in Frank's kitchen and Frank, loose, and Ana in Twenty minutes, already do this job.

**20. How does Agliè deliver lore without it reading as a lecture?**
He performs it and then turns it: the pyramid numerology recited, then knocked down by the lottery kiosk outside (ch. 47–48). He slips into the first person as if he had been there (ch. 52, 76), and the talk ends on an act ("Which manuscript?") (`04`, `05`). The old man in The reading room, continued and The final conversation can borrow the turn.

**21. Where is the thesis stated, and does it hurt?**
Ch. 118, about 2,500 words of notes on a plane after the climax. It stalls on purpose, because the action is over (`07`). Ch. 7's essay on credulity states it early and is slow too (`01`).

**22. Is any lore delivered as an object?**
Ardenti's theory comes as a stack of objects: a newspaper, a ledger, a sheet that falls out of a book, a cipher wheel (ch. 17–20, `02`). Lia's laundry list is a document on the table (ch. 106, `06`). The last reveal is Belbo's juvenilia behind a key (ch. 119, `07`). All of these fit SDF's rule that reveals are objects.

## Structure and cost

**23. Why can't Casaubon stop? Is it the same reason throughout?**
No. Early on the reasons are borrowed: a missing friend, a dead colonel, a phrase from a psychic. From ch. 75 on, each reason is something the heroes made, a lie or a gap or a bait already taken (map section 2). SDF's Part Two needs the same switch.

**24. Does the engine ever idle?**
Yes. In Brazil (ch. 28–33) Casaubon simply stops, flying kites for a year, and only the frame keeps the pull (`03`). That is the honest equivalent of James receiving documents in The drill and The money.

**25. What is the most effective cost scene?**
Ch. 78–80. While Salon talks tunnels, Casaubon plots, and he gets home to a note on the kitchen table: Lia gave birth without him. The Plan's first cost is private and an hour long (`05`). It is the model for costing James time with Ana rather than putting him in danger.

**26. How does the order handle Belbo once he has baited them?**
It frames him. His own train jokes and clothes become witness evidence, a police sketch is made, a firecracker of a car bomb scares off the inspector, and Garamond proves to be inside (ch. 108–109, `06`). They never stop him talking. They make his story unbelievable, and that is SDF's "discredit, not prevent."

**27. Who is the reliable witness?**
Gudrun, "never part of our story" (ch. 118, `07`). For SDF: the widow in Zwolle in The other ten, already on the page in v7.

**28. Does Eco ever confirm the conspiracy is real?**
No. The Provins message is a laundry list (ch. 106) and the museum shows no trace, but two people are dead and Garamond is missing (ch. 118, `07`). The Plan is false and the believers are real. SDF is different, because its organization is real, so take the ambiguity only for individual results (the psychic, ch. 25).
