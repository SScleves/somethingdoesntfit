## Why it grips
- **The narrator is already in the trap.** Casaubon tells all this from the periscope in the museum on the Saturday night, so every scene carries a line looking back from the disaster: "the first drops of the poison" (ch. 21), "This was our first, remote contact with the Plan" (ch. 19). The reader knows the game will kill before it is invented, so every crank detail in ch. 17–20 could be a fuse.
- **The crank lecture is a comedy scene with three straight men.** Ardenti talks for four chapters. Casaubon corrects his facts, Belbo deadpans ("Your reasoning is flawless", "O basta la"), and Diotallevi joins in with numerology, which the colonel takes as proof. The method failing is the lesson (Belbo on the stars, ch. 20).
- **The disappearance stays open.** Only a drunk old porter saw the body (ch. 21), and the sober inspector half suspects he dreamed it. Every event gets a sensible reading (fraud, a drug debt) and a plotted one, never settled. In ch. 25 De Angelis's explanation "seemed the most economical", and the periscope narrator then gives the sinister one.
- **A character test under the lore.** Ch. 15–16 (the riot; Belbo's "Canal" file) set up his shame about running away; ch. 22 pays it off when he lies to the police and expects to be called a runaway again.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
The editors' Plan does not exist yet; this section plants it. Ardenti's theory moves the plot three ways.

1. **The theory pulls in a believer, and the believer pays.** Ardenti calls his book a fishing expedition: once two thousand readers know, killing him is pointless. Something bites within hours (ch. 20–21). This is mechanism B run by the wrong man. The cost falls on the one who believes, not on the doubters.
2. **The disappearance makes the bystanders complicit.** Belbo and Casaubon never believed the colonel, but they lie anyway, on Belbo's Piedmont rule that you never give away a name (ch. 21). The lie is the cost. A silly theory becomes real guilt between them.
3. **A detail leaks, and the leak can be traced.** The psychic (ch. 24) speaks the Provins phrases in a trance. Belbo moves toward her and she vanishes; De Angelis says either the police watch or Belbo's approach was seen. A private phrase surfaces in public, and whoever carried it is taken. That is a canary. Then ch. 25 takes it back: her magazines had already printed "the thirty-six invisibles" in a Rosicrucian article. Eco shows a canary result that cannot be read. You cannot tell whether the bird died because of you.

Ch. 25 states the book's rule (quoted below): the editors invented the Plan and reality ran ahead of it. Ch. 23 gives the inventor's motive to believe it: if the Plan is real, he is not to blame.

## How the lore gets in
- **The Templar trial (ch. 14), about 3,500 words, a lecture.** The narrator tells it over drinks at Pilade's, broken by Belbo's jokes and Diotallevi's cabala, and builds it as numbered "enigmas" (no resistance, matching confessions, the retracted retractions). Each fact becomes a question the colonel later answers wrongly. No stall: it is a performance with an audience, ending on doom.
- **Ardenti's theory (ch. 17–20), about 9,000 words, a conversation with documents.** It is long, but it is broken into objects: the 1894 newspaper, the Ingolf ledger, the sheet that falls out of a book, the cipher page, the Trithemius wheel, the decoding printed in capitals, a drawn rune. Each gets a listener's reaction, and the planted danger ("There are secrets that kill") pays off one chapter later.
- **Brazilian syncretism and Saint-Germain (ch. 23–27), about 8,000 words, travel scenes and a dealer's patter.** The slowest stretch, carried by the love story and Aglie's charm; ch. 26 stalls. It trains Casaubon in resemblance and brings Aglie in.

## Mechanism notes
- **B, canary.** Ardenti's book is a canary set by the believer, and it works: Rakosky comes, and Rakosky turns out not to exist (ch. 22). For SDF: the stranger who believes James's invented title and goes out with it is the one who gets counted, the cost Santi names. Eco puts it on a crank the reader half laughs at, which keeps it funny. The ch. 25 lesson is that any result can be explained away. So the canary only tells James something if he planted a detail nobody else could have printed. That is a gate (A) inside the canary.
- **Already on the page in SDF.** James's invented book that Kalder says he has read (On land) is Diotallevi's numerology in ch. 19: the believer takes in any addition and thanks you for it ("You’ve given me the courage to go on"). Eco plays it as comedy, not as a probe; SDF can have both.
- **The clowns.** Ardenti (a collaborator with the SS and a treasure fraudster, per De Angelis, ch. 22), the Picatrix séance with Radio Moscow on the speakers (ch. 24), Aglie playing Saint-Germain. Each crank stands beside something unexplained: the vanished body, the trance, what Aglie knows. That is SDF's *every real question has a clown beside it*, except that here nobody can tell whether anyone paid the clowns.
- **D, lock and key.** Ingolf's daughter gives the colonel the attic because he promises to honour her father (ch. 18), and he steals from her. The door opens by her choice, and what it costs the visitor is moral.
- **Copies.** In ch. 18 the colonel argues that Ingolf's copy is the original (quoted below). The crank's version of SDF's printings problem.

## Quotes
- "I’m casting the bait. We’ll see if anyone bites." (ch. 20): the believer as canary, stated by the believer.
- "You just have to decide which stars to omit" (ch. 20): every crank's method in one line, played as a joke.
- "Casaubon, when originals no longer exist, the last copy is the original." (ch. 18): how a copy nobody can check becomes scripture.
- "Since we invented “the Plan” ourselves, and only much later was it possible for reality not only to catch up with fiction" (ch. 25): the book's mechanism, said plainly while the evidence is still ambiguous.

## Flags
- Real people pulled into the occult plot by a crank: Otto Rahn, Hitler's "secret weapon", Saint Bernard as a Druid (ch. 20). A discredited speaker covers Eco; SDF should not allow even that.
- Real books appear only as crank evidence; none is said to be edited.
