## Why it grips
- **The reader learns it is false before the believers act.** Lia's laundry list (ch. 106) comes just before chapters 107–109. Straight after, the invented Plan produces a real bomb. The dread now comes from the Plan *not* being true. Her answer to the coded half is cut off at the part break.
- **Pace is set by the joke.** Chapters 84–96 hold about 12,000 words of pseudo-history, and Casaubon tells us how to hear them: a Keystone Kops film (ch. 85). Every chapter opens with a real crank's epigraph. Each lore run ends on a laugh ("Templars of the world, unite!", ch. 87).
- **A body under the game.** Diotallevi's yellow skin is mentioned in passing (ch. 87). His "gastritis" arrives during the Holocaust riff (ch. 100), then come the hospital (ch. 101) and the unnamed "bisyllable" (ch. 107). The illness keeps time for the Plan,.
- **The scene that does the most work is the train (ch. 108).** Each of Belbo's small rudenesses on the train comes back as evidence: the Burberry blazer, the maroon tie, a seat booked to Rome with a ticket only to Bologna, and his joke about "Arab metaphysics", told to scare off a chatty neighbour. By evening they are witness testimony on the news.
- **A comic defeat turns him.** A run-over dog and Lorenza leaving by train make one humiliating farce (ch. 107); that night he decides to put Aglie into the Plan.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
This section is where mechanism B fires, in this order:

1. **The inventors believe first** (ch. 87, the spy and the man who lives with a dog). By ch. 98 they keep their talk from Aglie with "the natural reserve of those who possess a secret". The first leak is inward.
2. **Leakage is ambient before it is deliberate.** Aglie spends "hours and hours on the Manutius address file" (ch. 98), Salon is seen coming out of Manutius (ch. 102), and the editors steal from the Diabolicals' manuscripts (ch. 85). Nobody tracks what moves through the house.
3. **The deliberate plant is a canary test** (ch. 108). Belbo passes the Plan off as Ardenti's ten authentic pages and adds three baits. The map is "in full view". The papers are destroyed, so the map now exists only in his head. He will travel "around the summer solstice". He holds back the final truth, and the holding back is what makes him believable (quote 2). Within a day Aglie hands him the suitcase.
4. **What the canary shows.** It shows that Aglie believes, that Rakosky, Ragotgky and Aglie are one name, that Garamond is in the same "spiritual knighthood" (ch. 109, overheard on his line), and that the Tres exists. He learns it all from what is done to him.
5. **The cost lands on him.** The organisation does not kill him and does not lock him out. It frames him: a police sketch, his own words, a threat to name seat 45. De Angelis's car bomb is "hardly more than a firecracker" (ch. 109), just enough to make him ask for a transfer. Strangers get counted too: "Two Libyan citizens had been detained in Bologna" (ch. 108).

## How the lore gets in
- **Ch. 84–96, the Plan's history (about 12,000 words), carried as a three-way game.** Short chapters, turn-taking, punchlines. It stalls on purpose, to show the habit taking hold.
- **"The Return of Saint-Germain" (ch. 97, about 4,000 words), Belbo's file.** All the lore is retold as pulp melodrama: a trapdoor, a knife, each group's envoy drowned in turn.
- **Hitler and the hollow earth (ch. 99–100, about 2,000 words), a rant.** Diotallevi's collapse cuts it off, so something happens in the room straight away.
- **Alamut (ch. 103–104, about 2,500 words), proofs at Pilade's bar,** broken up by Belbo's refills; it ends on Casaubon's verdict that Belbo is now "an adept".
- **Lia's laundry list (ch. 106, about 2,500 words), a lecture with the document on the table.** This is anti-lore, sourced from a tourist booklet.

## Mechanism notes
- **B (canary), in full and with its price (ch. 108–109).** Belbo's test is SDF's *On land* move (James invents a book; Kalder says he has read it). Eco does not drop it after the reaction: the reaction becomes the trap. The believer acts on the bait's actionable details (a date, a place, a map kept only in his head), not on its lore. For SDF, James's invented title or edit should carry one checkable hook (a printing, a shelfmark, a forum date), and whoever touches it should act on that hook. The brief's cost is in the text twice. Strangers are counted (the Libyans). The inventor becomes the keeper of a secret "that did not exist" (ch. 109).
- **Discredit, not prevent.** The Tres never stop Belbo from talking. They make what he says unbelievable: to the police his story would need a second, more outlandish story behind it (ch. 109). Garamond does the same thing gently ("Why, it sounds like a novel").
- **The clown's book holds the true clue (ch. 109).** A hack manuscript, *The True Story of the Comte de Saint-Germain*, arrives among the day's submissions and names "Marquis of Aglie" as Saint-Germain's real name. A reveal as an object, from the nonsense pile.
- **A (gate), as an absence.** Lia's test (is the source real?) is the gate the editors never passed, and Belbo plants without asking it.
- **Fifth: the frame made from the hero's habits.** Belbo's jokes become the witness description. James's dry asides could be used against him the same way.

## Quotes
- "there is no longer any difference between developing the habit of pretending to believe and developing the habit of believing" (ch. 87): the inventors are the first believers.
- "His reticence about the final truth guaranteed the truth of its premises." (ch. 108): how to plant a canary.
- "The drawing didn’t resemble Belbo, but Belbo resembled the drawing." (ch. 108): the frame in one line.
- "So Garamond, too, was part of the secret. What secret? The one that only he, Belbo, could reveal. The one that did not exist." (ch. 109): the canary's price.

## Flags
- Real people are named as complicit throughout the Plan (Rachkovsky, Nilus, Napoleon, Hess, Hitler). SDF must not do this.
- Ch. 100 turns the Holocaust into a search for the map inside the parody, and the characters themselves recoil. Do not borrow.
- Hidden codes are read into real books (Verne, ch. 84–85). These are readings, not invented edits, but SDF should keep any canary titles or edits off real books.
