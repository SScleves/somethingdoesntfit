## Why it grips
- **Everything is set-up, and the narrator tells you so.** Visits, parties and talk; little happens. The narrator, writing after the disaster, plants regret in advance (the Plan stirred on the castle night, 57; he should have listened to Lia, 63), so every comic crank is read knowing someone will take him seriously.
- **Lore comes in already doubted.** Agliè recites the pyramid numerology (47), then knocks it down with the lottery kiosk outside his window (48). The reader gets the lore and the joke together, and Agliè comes out stronger: he despises the cranks and still believes. Casaubon notices him reading the ceiling while pretending to remember (47), which plants doubt.
- **Each lore scene is broken by a personal sting.** The pyramid lecture ends with Lorenza at the door (48). The Gnostic Sophia myth arrives as Lorenza's drunk flirting, with Belbo jealous in front of his rival (50). The alchemical wedding is felt through what Casaubon thinks is Lorenza's hand, and she was never there (58–59).
- **Belbo's memories hold up the real question.** Uncle Carlo and Mongo saluting as veterans (49), the bullet in the corridor (55) and the bombardon (56) give Belbo a time when things were clear and a time he did not choose. Eco ties them to the conspiracy once, in the druids' trumpet (62), so the occult gets a private hook in him.
- **The rites are cut off just as they get real.** Agliè allows one peek and lowers the curtain (61). At the druid rite he says it is over just as the pigs appear and the knife comes out (62). Played as comedy, it still works as a hook.

## How the conspiracy moves the story here
There is no Plan yet. What this section builds is the network the Plan will later leak into. Agliè gets inside Garamond as a consultant (47), learns the Manutius trick (52), goes through the author file and proposes writing to every Manutius author of twenty years (54): a mailing list of people who want to believe. Salon and De Angelis show the firm is already watched because of Ardenti, the one real disappearance (51, 53). Agliè tells Casaubon that Salon is an informer, and that to an informer a secret is worth having whether or not it is true (57). That is the leak rule the plot will run on.

The first believer in this section is the narrator. In chapter 63 Casaubon catches himself reading shop signs and clouds for hidden stories, and Lia names the cost. The canary dies in the miner's own lungs first.

## How the lore gets in
- **Pierre's sorcery quarrel with Bramanti (46), about 1,500 words, overheard through a curtain.** Farce (a plaster Baphomet, a sorcery death that was cirrhosis); the eavesdropping keeps it moving.
- **Pyramid and kiosk numerology (47–48), about 2,500 words, a performance.** No stall: the scene is a hiring and ends in a rivalry.
- **Agarttha and Saint-Yves (52), about 700 words, office conversation.** Agliè slips into the first person, as if he had been there, so the lore carries the question of whether he is Saint-Germain. Ends on a punchline.
- **Synarchy (53), about 1,800 words, a café interview with a policeman.** Played as a card game (who learns more), with a bomb on a train as stakes; ends with the Tres, a named unknown. No stall.
- **The Chymical Wedding (58), about 1,500 words, a projected spectacle,** told through drink, incense and desire. The plot does not move, and the night is where the Plan is born (57).
- **Lia's lecture on the body (63), about 1,800 words.** She explains the symbols away through the body; the antidote, from the person with most to lose, ending with her pregnancy.

## Mechanism notes
- **B (canary), what is planted.** Three leak channels are built before anything is invented: the consultant who reads the files (Agliè, 52, 54), the informer who repeats whatever he hears (Salon, 51, 57, 60), and the policeman who asks about names (De Angelis, 53). De Angelis drops the Tres to see whether Casaubon reacts: a canary aimed at the hero, answered with a lie. For SDF: **On land** already has James test Kalder with an invented book; the reverse, James tested and lying back, would give him a cost.
- **The clown beside the question.** Agliè states SDF's organisation logic outright (61): the real Templars would hide in the crowd of their caricatures. Casaubon's joke to De Angelis (53) is the same idea turned round. A possible insider explains how cover works while seeming to side with the cranks.
- **C (front).** Synarchy (53) is a chain of fronts with nothing at the bottom: each public pact is denounced as cover for a secret pact, which turns out to be the same public pact. A model for making any SDF leak look like one more version.
- **D (lock and key).** Agliè decides how much the editors may see, one peek and then the curtain (61, 62). Doors open because a person chooses to open them, which matches SDF's rule.
- **Fifth: the counter-voice who pays.** Lia (63) is the one who sees it clearly, and she has the most at stake. The narrator's later regret at not listening to her turns her into the measure of what he loses.

## Quotes
- "it was definitely on that evening that the Plan first stirred in our minds" (ch. 57): the narrator dates the start before anything happens.
- "There exists a secret society with branches throughout the world, and its plot is to spread the rumor that a universal plot exists." (ch. 53): the joke that is SDF's premise.
- "What better hiding place for the true Templar than in the crowd of his caricatures?" (ch. 61): the clown as cover, said by a man who may be inside.
- "now I came to distrust the very masters who had taught me to doubt" (ch. 63): the first believer is the narrator.

## Flags
- Real people and groups appear as cranks or accused plotters: Crowley and the Golden Dawn (47), Saint-Yves d'Alveydre (51–52), and in De Angelis's synarchy history real people and groups (Navachine, La Cagoule, Du Mas and Canudo, Husson) (53). SDF must invent its cranks and accused.
- No invented edits on real books. Agliè claims to own a third copy of Khunrath's 1595 first edition (47). That is a claim about a copy, not an edit.
