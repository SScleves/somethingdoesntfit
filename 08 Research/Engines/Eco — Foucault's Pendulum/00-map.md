# Map — Umberto Eco, *Foucault's Pendulum*

> **Coverage:** English translation (William Weaver), read in full via seven section notes (`01`–`07` in this folder), 11 Nov 2026. Chapter cites come from those notes.
> **Brief:** mechanism B, the canary. How the invented Plan leaks, who believes it first, how heavy lore is carried without stalling, and how the Diabolicals work as the clowns beside the real question.
> **Standing-rule note:** Eco names real people, orders and bookshops as plotters (Bacon, Rachkovsky, Hitler, the Golden Dawn, the OTO, the Jesuits), reads codes into real books (Shakespeare, Verne), has a character insert a line into the sonnets (ch. 73), and turns the Holocaust into a map hunt (ch. 100). Our rules forbid all of it. Take the mechanics, never the roll-call.

---

## 1. What it is

Three Milan editors at a vanity press read occult manuscripts by cranks they call the Diabolicals. As a joke they invent "the Plan," a Templar conspiracy that explains everything, built with a computer, bar talk and stolen crank material. Agliè, a believer who consults for the press, takes it seriously. Belbo, the editor who wrote most of it, uses it to get revenge on Agliè and sends him after a map that does not exist. The believers come for him. The book opens with Casaubon hiding in a Paris museum on the night they hang Belbo from the Pendulum, and it ends with him waiting for them to come for him too.

What makes it work: the reader knows from chapter 3 that the joke kills someone, so every funny lecture after that reads as a fuse. And the conspiracy never arrives as information. The heroes make it, and every piece they make gets a reaction from a real person.

## 3. How the lore gets in

- **Templar history (ch. 13–14), about 8,500 words, two bar nights.** A lecture the listeners keep interrupting, the trial set out as numbered "enigmas" that Ardenti will answer wrongly when he walks in two chapters later. (`01`, `02`)
- **The Rosicrucian hoax (ch. 29–30), about 4,500 words.** Pillow talk, with Amparo heckling and doing the best reasoning. Never stalls. (`03`)
- **Agliè's talk (ch. 28 to 76), 700 to 2,500 words a time.** A performance with a turn: the pyramid book recited, then demolished with a lottery kiosk; a slip into the first person; the Masonry talk ends on "Which manuscript?" (`03`–`05`)
- **The Plan sessions (ch. 64–104).** Three voices, one a heckler ("Make beer?"). Each objection forces the next invention, so lore arrives as a solved problem. (`05`, `06`)
- **The rite (ch. 113), about 700 words of titles** read while Lorenza stands drugged: joke and countdown at once. (`07`)

**Where it stalls:** ch. 84–96, about 12,000 words of Plan history. Eco knows (the narrator calls it a Keystone Kops film, ch. 85), ends each run on a punchline, and lets Diotallevi's yellow skin keep time underneath. It is still the slowest stretch. **Where it doesn't:** the list of sects (ch. 75), 1,700 words of dead catalogue that pays off within a chapter because the trap is hidden in it. A list stalls only if nothing is hidden in it. (`05`, `06`)

## 4. The mechanism we came for: the canary

**Setup.** Before any bait, Eco builds the channels: a consultant in the author file (Agliè, ch. 54), an informer (Salon, ch. 57), a policeman who drops names (De Angelis, ch. 53). The antagonist states the rule from his side: believers are detectors (ch. 33). (`03`, `04`)

**Rule.** Put one invented thing where a believer will find it. The tell is what he does, not what he says.

**The progression:**
1. **Ardenti's bait (ch. 20–22).** A believer publishes so that whoever knows must show himself. Something bites within hours; the believer pays, and the doubters' lie to the police is their price. (`02`)
2. **The psychic (ch. 24–25).** A private phrase surfaces and its carrier vanishes. Then the result is taken back: her magazines had already printed it. **A gate inside the canary: the bait proves something only if nobody else could have printed it.** (`02`)
3. **Project Hermes (ch. 42–45).** Invented inquiries in the right channel. Clowns bite first; the first threat comes from a crank, not the order. (`03`)
4. **The Tres dropped on the hero (ch. 53).** De Angelis tests Casaubon with a name; Casaubon lies. The hero is the bird. (`04`)
5. **The Tres hidden in a list (ch. 75–76).** One fake sect in a real chronology, credited to someone else's manuscripts. Agliè does not claim to know it. He asks who mentioned it, asks again, says it does not matter, checks his watch. **The tell is the second question and the quick exit.** The lie about the source is now a debt. (`05`)
6. **Belbo's test of Agliè (ch. 108).** The Plan passed off as authentic, with actionable hooks: a map "in full view," papers destroyed so the map is only in his head, a trip "around the summer solstice." Within a day the believer acts on the hooks, and Belbo learns what the canary shows from what is done to him. (`06`)
7. **The denial that proves more (ch. 110, 118).** Belbo confesses there is no secret; refusal makes it bigger. (`07`)

**Escalation:** the setter moves closer (a crank, the boss, the police, the heroes) and the hook gets more specific (a theory, a phrase, a name, a date and a place). **Cost** climbs with it, from a lie to the police to two deaths. **Payoff:** "if you invent a plan and others carry it out, it’s as if the Plan exists" (ch. 118). **Who believes first:** the inventors (ch. 63, 67–69, 87).

**Against the four fixes.** **B** is supported and sharpened: Eco aims it at his heroes, SDF wants it as James's instrument, and what transfers is the anatomy (a unique item, an actionable hook, a readout through a third party, a price). **A** is added *inside* B: the psychic (ch. 25) and Lia's source test (ch. 106) are the gates the editors never pass. **C** is weak (Garamond/Manutius, ch. 38; synarchy as fronts with nothing at the bottom, ch. 53). **D** is small (the password, ch. 6; Agliè's one peek, ch. 61; the key, ch. 118). **A fifth: the frame.** The order never stops Belbo talking. It makes his own words and habits the evidence against him (the train, ch. 108; "Why, it sounds like a novel," ch. 109). That is SDF's "discredit, not prevent," working on one man in one day.

## 5. Transfer to SDF

Checked against the v8.2 plan, `01 Manuscript/v6/18 — On land.md` and the v7 Part Two drafts. **On land, as planned, has the invented-book test (Kalder: "Oh, that one. Yes."), and The dinner cashes it ("the book you said you'd read"). No draft contains it;** v6 On land is the older Deventer-slip version.

**5.1 Give the On land book a hook, and read it back through the Groningen dealer.**
- **Chapters:** On land; The schedule; The sheets.
- **Change:** the invented book carries one checkable detail, and James learns the bait was taken because someone acted on it.
- **James does:** he gives the title a printing year and a shelfmark in the depot's format (copied in The drill), says it only to Kalder, and dates it in his notebook. Weeks later the Groningen dealer, who said he would make no more calls, calls James instead: polite people asked whether anyone had come in for that shelfmark. Before believing it James runs the gate (ch. 25): who else heard the title? Not Ana; she was laughing at something else (already in v8.2). So it came from Kalder.
- **Cost:** the dealer has now been seen not to telephone. PLACEHOLDER: is Groningen one of the addresses in The other ten? Echo in The sheets: Aylin's visitor book has a "shelfmark requested" column (v7 draft), and the bait is in it.
- **Already on the page?** Partly (On land and The dinner, plan only). Eco adds the actionable hook (ch. 108), a readout through someone who chooses to talk, and the gate. Without them the test proves only that Kalder is vain.

**5.2 Bury one fake among the seven sheets.**
- **Chapters:** The reading room, continued; The sheets.
- **Change:** one of James's findings is invented: a grant to an invented documentary company in a year that fits the pattern.
- **James does:** he lays it fifth, credits it to "the published accounts," and watches. The old man waves off six. On the fake he asks one extra question: which year? That is Agliè's second question (ch. 76). By refusing everything except the one he did not recognize, he has confirmed the other six.
- **Cost:** Anneke saw him lie about a source. In The sheets she will not act until he walks her through the source of every line, and that delay lets the leak outrun the envelopes (The leak).
- **Already on the page?** No. v7 has seven true sheets and a flat refusal. It does already have the unreadable result ("Did it occur to you that nobody came because you'd gone?"), which is ch. 25.

**5.3 A second bait in the forum, and a stranger who gets counted.**
- **Chapters:** The gorilla mask; The vet; The sheets.
- **Change:** James answers the edit to his forum account with a *different* invented title, so a reaction tells him its channel.
- **James does:** he posts the title once, as a question. The first to bite is a clown: the forum man from the barbecue claims to have seen it (ch. 19, the believer who thanks you for the addition) and starts asking libraries. James posts that he made it up; the man believes it more (ch. 118).
- **Cost:** the stranger is counted for asking. He posts that he got a very polite correction in wording James knows from *When a Reader Notices*. James owes him a sheet (The sheets, the quiet ones by forum).
- **Already on the page?** No. The gorilla mask (v6) has the edited account and eleven readers; nobody plants anything. Gate: this title must differ from 5.1's, or neither can be read.

**5.4 One negative, and it costs him with Frank.**
- **Chapters:** The band; Frank, loose.
- **Change:** James sees one of his own invented titles in the grant list and builds on it for a weekend. It is a real, unrelated film with a near title.
- **James does:** he takes it to Frank as proof. Frank finds the real film in four minutes.
- **Cost:** in Frank, loose, "I believe the invoice" now carries a line Frank has earned: you saw your own book in the accounts last month. It lands harder because Frank has watched him be wrong (the inventor believes first, ch. 87; the investigator infected, ch. 115–116).
- **Already on the page?** No. In The money, The band and Frank, loose (v7) James is right every time; the SYNTHESIS asked for one costly negative per act.

**5.5 James as the bird: Pieter drops a word, James lies.**
- **Chapters:** The drill; The sheets.
- **Change:** De Angelis's test (ch. 53), run on James by a helper who thinks it is part of the security test.
- **James does:** at desk forty Pieter asks, lightly, whether he has come across the word "recall." James says no. It is on the invoice he has read.
- **Cost:** in The sheets Pieter says: you told me at the drill you'd never heard it. James has to own the lie before Pieter reads on.
- **Already on the page?** No. In v7 Pieter hovers and "the sentence isn't in the building."

**5.6 The uncontaminated witness** (Gudrun, ch. 118): the widow in Zwolle. **Already on the page: yes** (The other ten, v7). Eco adds only: in The sheets, show her the photograph without a question.

**Rejected:** a bait passed off as a genuine document (Belbo's ch. 108 move would make James a fraud toward the people he must later turn); any bait set in a real book; any readout that needs someone's account or mail.

## 6. Do not take

- The roll-call: real people, orders and bookshops as plotters; the Holocaust as a riff (ch. 73, 100, 113, 115).
- The 12,000-word stall (ch. 84–96). Eco can afford it because the frame has promised a hanging. SDF's Part Two has no such promise to carry it.
- The passive ending: a hero who watches the killing and waits. James acts, and wins small.
