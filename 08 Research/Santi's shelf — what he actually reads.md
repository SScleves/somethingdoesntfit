## What the shelf says about the novel

1. **He is a structure reader before he is a sentence reader.** DK maps, Big Ideas, Hall's *Secret Teachings*, Horowitz's *Seeker's Guide*: he wants the whole field in one diagram. The Chapter Map and the Competence Ledger in this vault are not AI habits; they are his. A reader like him will forgive plain sentences and will not forgive a structure he cannot draw.
2. **His fiction is procedural and audible.** Reacher and the Expanse are competence-and-pace machines told in short scenes with one problem each; Weir and Watts are the two ends of the same axis he named in the brief. Nothing on the shelf is literary-interior for its own sake except *In Cold Blood*, which is a checker's book: a true story reconstructed from documents and interviews, flat, with the horror in the flatness. That is the register the Faucet's "essay" mode and the K-past chapters want.
3. **Comics taught him the reveal.** Brubaker's *Criminal* and *Kill or Be Killed* are first-person crime with an unreliable narrator who is honest about everything except the one thing (Dylan's "demon"). *The Nice House on the Lake* is a group held in a beautiful house by a friend who turns out not to be human and who loves them — the handler who is kind, which is Kalder exactly. *Transmetropolitan* is the gorilla mask from the journalist's side. *Spy x Family* is a handler with a fake family who comes to need it. He has read the Kalder problem solved four ways in pictures. The vault's "reveals are objects, not speeches" rule is a comics rule.
4. **The conspiracy shelf is wider than the pipeline, and he has already sorted it.** Webb, Marrs, Farrell, Cooper, McGowan, Iron Mountain, Deaths at Davos sit next to the DK books and Pasulka. He did not put Farrell or Cooper through the verification pipeline. That is the ledger's lanes working before the ledger existed: read everything, verify the ones that can be verified, keep the rest as specimens. The novel's Frank is the pipeline; the novel's Told are the shelf.
5. **He steers himself and knows it.** Three ADHD books, Deep Work, Peak Mind, Waking Up, Tolle twice. The crossing in the novel is sober, domestic and practised because that is what he practises. The INNER track should read like a man doing a twelve-minutes-a-day course on a kitchen chair with a dog watching, which is already how the vault describes it.
6. **The warm things are specific.** Dog food recipes, a golf book written by a teaching pro, a book on long-marriage sex, a newlywed cookbook, a couples list. The HEART thread's rule in the Guardrails (Ana and the dogs "warm, specific, recurring") is the shelf's rule. Any scene with Ana that is about the plot and not about a kitchen is off-shelf.

## Where to draw from, concretely

- **For the K track and the reveal that hurts:** *The Nice House on the Lake* (the kind captor) and *Kill or Be Killed* (the narrator who has a reason he cannot say). Both are comics and cannot be read here as text; Santi knows them and can be asked which panel he thinks of.
- **For the Faucet's documentary mode and the K-past chapters:** *In Cold Blood*. Flat reconstruction, dated, with the violence arriving as a sentence.
- **For pace heard aloud:** Reacher's rule of one problem per scene and the Expanse's alternating POV with a cliff at every chapter end. He has nine audiobooks of it. The present/past alternation of the four tracks should be built so that a listener always knows which room they are in within one sentence.
- **For the crossing and the "receipt":** Egan's *Permutation City* (comp pending) and Watts (comp pending). Both are consciousness books by writers who refuse comfort.
- **For Frank:** Solaris. A station of scientists who cannot agree on what they are looking at and keep looking. Frank's "based on what?" is Snaut's.
- **Not to draw from:** the summaries shelf (Great Reset, Fourth Turning, Fauci), which is the Told reading the Told.
