# Narrator (name TBD)
First person, past tense. One head, no villain POV. Expat in a Dutch city ([[Home City]]); nationality never stated. Married to [[Ana]]; two dogs ([[Buddy]], [[Mora]]).

## The defect
Can't stop asking *how do we know.* Ana calls it a quality. Made his career, causes everything ([[The Defect]]).

## Job
"I work in IT." Software that watches other software; the problems are never his — outsider who must understand components he didn't build, from scratch, alone. Coroner: "why are you dying?" Thinks in HIS terms, also at work — never the reverse. Professional vocabulary allowed only when actually working (systems, or the mystery).

## Social operating mode (canon, ch. 1)
- Deflection engine: hands strangers their favourite subject in 30 seconds; signature move *"tell me something about yourself — not work, not family"* — stolen from a wannabe-gigolo colleague, rebuilt for a three-minute exit.
- [[The Faucet]]: did-you-knows as the safe deflection tool; a thirty-year rule — proven ones only, never the ones he's embarrassed to know in detail.
- "Charming" per everyone; privately unsure the full version exists ("good at the demo"). Feels-vs-models = his private ambiguity; never labeled.
- Ana's names: **Mulder** (believing), **Scully** (measuring). Manifesto joke; "more wine?"

## Wounds
- [[The Hospital Corridor]] — father died when he was sixteen; grief with no container. Opens ch. 25 only.
- The record-wound (ch. 5): at ten, read something, the source later changed, everyone said he misremembered. Thirty years of being the error instead of the record. → [[The Record]].
- Shrinks his whole life; they keep graduating him ("fired for being fine"). Fact surfaces ch. 5; full story ch. 25 beside the father; return-visit gun in Act 2.

## What he builds
[[The Record]] (proof-keeping) · [[The Switch]] (the one thing he never puts on the record).

## Arc
Funeral hypocrisy (the 85% yes) → the sentence → the list → the grammar → the timestamps (suppression has a heartbeat) → the crossing → the offer (one, not a hundred) → holding the door → the wake.

## NAME — decided 9 Sep 2026 by Santi

**James Klippe.**

Rules for using it, so it doesn't get wasted:

- He never uses it about himself. First-person narrators don't. It arrives from other mouths and other paper.
- **First appearance: ch. 5**, in the antiquarian dealer's reply — *Dear Mr Klippe* — which is exactly right for this book: the reader learns his name off a document, the way he learns everything.
- Ana almost never says it. She has Mulder and Scully. When she does use it — ch. 22, and once in ch. 26 — it should land like a slap, because it means she has stopped being playful.
- Frank uses "James" constantly and cheerfully. Frank is the only person in the book who says it casually.
- **The Keeper, ch. 20, says both names.** *James Klippe.* Full, unhurried, and correct — including the spelling that only exists on one form he ever filled in (ch. 17 pings). That's the moment the name stops being his.
- The roster in ch. 29–30: he looks for his own name on it. He is not on it. Neither are the guards.
