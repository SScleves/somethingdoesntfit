# H. M. Tresidder (placeholder name)
Reader in comparative mythology at a small English university whose department no longer exists. Author of *Waters Before Memory* (1926). Walked out of Urfa on foot in 1929, heading east; never found. Second impression (the edited one) printed 1931 — **in [[Leiden]]** for continental distribution (why the book is common in Dutch markets; why the plates are in the narrator's backyard).
- Entry #11 in [[The List]]. Martyr type: the Vanished.
- He knew what he'd written; the changer wasn't sharper — he had *context* (knew what a sentence like that does in a syllabus).
- Lineage: Tresidder → a student who pursued it and was ridiculed/recanted → that student's grandson = [[The Professor]]. Surfaces ch. 13–14 through the daughter.
- Plato's Timaeus (the priest to Solon) = the idea's public-domain ancestor; "nobody edits Plato — Plato is filed under story. Tresidder wrote it as method." *The myths were allowed to say it. The footnotes weren't.*
