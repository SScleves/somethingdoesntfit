# The Faucet (did-you-knows)

## Pre-existing habit (retro'd into ch. 1–3)
A social tool: the safe did-you-know steers a table away from anything that matters. Thirty-year rule: **proven ones only; never the ones he's embarrassed to know in detail.** ch. 2: the misfire (dogs' two states) — Ana: "That's sad… you're such a dick." ch. 3: collecting did-you-knows he can't say; the folder gets heavier.

## Two registers
- **Public** — true, checkable, fringe enough to make him weird. Hero's steam engine shelved as a toy; Antikythera; Göbekli Tepe older than Stonehenge by 6,000 yrs; Cappadocia's 20,000-person cities; Piri Reis; songlines mapping drowned coasts; the Vienna spear to Nuremberg 1938; Paperclip's 1,600. Never Atlantis, never floods-as-memory, never what he's chasing.
- **Reader-facing** — italic chapter closers, ominous, never spoken. *Did you know most of those scientists never went to prison? … Only the letterhead changed.* Unproven material lives here only, framed as what he read.

## Rules
- Fact = one sentence; reaction longer than the fact.
- Delivered as if helpful in context — the mismatch is the joke ("For the *dishwasher*").
- Never explains ("and that proves—" kills it).
- Rotate audiences: Ana (charm → weariness → fear), Frank, the four retirees (his only fan club), Beard Man (out-nerds him), once the Keeper visitor (finishes the sentence for him).
- He pursues almost none of them. ~3 across the book are rungs.
- Budget: 1/chapter Act 1 · 2/chapter Act 2A · **zero after ch. 20** — the silence is what Ana notices.

## Function
Exposition smuggled inside characterization; the persona cracking in public (he fits his own tinfoil hat — ch. 21 quotes him); the marriage's measurable arc.
