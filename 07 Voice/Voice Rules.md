# Voice Rules (locked through ch. 3; calibrated ch. 4)
1. First person past; one head; no Keeper POV.
2. "I work in IT." He thinks in HIS terms, also at work. Professional vocabulary only when actually working (systems or the mystery). Metaphors from him: bread, buckets, depositions, wet cement, the wall of comfort, the lab coat, the doorman.
3. No Latin-flavor details unless Santi writes them. Setting: Netherlands, expat unstated.
4. Swearing = punctuation ("Fuck. I don't hate my job at all. But fuck."). Sparing.
5. Humor self-deprecating; the register recipe: **serious observation → concrete detail → joke that's actually a confession.**
6. Darkness budget: one dark joke per few pages (manifesto, Blade). Dark = implication he declines to examine.
7. Subtext stays subtext: never label his wiring; "good at the demo" is the ceiling until ch. 25.
8. King rhythm (short punches, chapter-end hooks, ordinary life warm before it breaks); Weir clarity (figuring-out as fun; Rocky = the dogs); Holly-Gibney hedging ("I want to be accurate"; "I don't want to oversell it"). Watts fragments as seasoning, ~30% texture.
9. **Dialogue over monologue** when a fact can enter through a conversation: Ana and the dogs are the licensed listeners. Dialogue formatted as dialogue.
10. **Not Langdon:** insights *happen to him* (the dogs answer a question he wasn't asking); every inference smaller than the last draft's; hedged.
11. Reticent narrator ([[The Switch]]): omission marked once, residue after, never a wink. "I want that on the record" / the one folder he won't show.
12. [[The Faucet]] rules.
13. One beginning (ch. 1); milestones named as what they are.
