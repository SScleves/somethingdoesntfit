# A room with a long table
### March

He was there because somebody had to bring the folders up from the car and because the room was on the third floor and there was no lift.

The building was a bank once and is not a bank now. Long table, twelve chairs, a window at one end with a view of a wall, and a sideboard with a coffee machine on it that somebody had brought from home and that was better than anything the building provided.

Nine people. He knew two of them by sight and one by name.

He put the folders down where he was told and then stood at the sideboard end with his back near the door, which is where you stand, and nobody asked him to sit and nobody asked him to leave, and after about four minutes nobody in the room remembered he was in it.

---

They did the routine business first and it took fifty minutes, and it was about a building.

Not the depot. Another building, somewhere with a colder climate, and the discussion was about a chiller specification and a variation to a contract and whether a supplier's lead time was credible. A woman in her sixties at the far end was extremely hard on the lead time. Somebody made a joke about it that landed. Coffee was fetched twice.

If you had walked in at that point you would have thought you were at a housing association.

---

The second item was called the review and it took the rest of the morning.

He got about half of it, and half is what there is.

There was a set of numbers that everybody in the room could see and he could not, on a screen at the far end, and the discussion was entirely about **bands** — where a band currently sat, whether a band had drifted, and whether the correct response to a drift was to widen the band or to bring something forward.

The man doing most of the talking was in his fifties and had the manner of somebody presenting rather than deciding. He said, at one point, that four of the indicators had been outside their bands for nine consecutive periods, and that two of those four were the ones that had been outside in 2019 as well, and that the difference this time was the rate.

Somebody asked what the rate was.

He gave a figure. The room went quiet for a second in the way a room does when a number is worse than the last time it was said out loud.

The woman in her sixties said: "Then we widen. That is what we have always done and it has always been correct and I would like it minuted that I said so."

The man presenting said that widening had been modelled and that widening no longer bought what it used to buy, and used a phrase about the second derivative, and somebody else said something about *attention*, and there was then about twenty minutes that he did not follow at all.

What he did follow was the end of it.

Somebody said: "So we're proposing March."

And a man who had not spoken for an hour said, without any emphasis at all: "March of *this* year."

And that was the moment the room changed, and he noticed it because he had been watching the room and not the screen. Three people looked at each other. One person wrote something down fast. The woman in her sixties put both hands flat on the table and did not say anything, which from her was louder than the last hour.

"That's eleven months," she said eventually.

"Yes."

"Have the operating sites been told?"

"The operating sites will be told in the ordinary way at the ordinary time."

"That is not an answer to my question."

"No," the man agreed. "It isn't."

---

There was a break at that point and people went out for the toilets and the corridor, and he stayed where he was, and the woman in her sixties came to the sideboard for coffee and stood next to him for a moment while the machine did what it does.

She did not look at him and she was not talking to him.

"Nineteen thirty-one," she said. "Forty-nine. Seventy-one. Ninety-four."

The machine finished.

"Every single one of those was a widening," she said. "Every one. Somebody sat in a room like this one and made the band bigger and went home, and the entire thing survived, and the survival is *why we are here.* And now we've got a generation who have never had to do it slowly and they think the instrument is the plan."

She took her cup.

"You've been in the field a long time," she said, and he understood that she now was talking to him. "How long?"

"Twenty-two years."

"Then you know what happens when somebody brings a date forward." She drank some of it. "Everything anyone is halfway through becomes a problem."

---

After the break there was a third item and it was seven minutes long and it was a list of what the room called *open positions*, which he understood to mean people.

Names went past. Most of them meant nothing. Two got a sentence each. One of them was a woman in Leiden, and what was said about her was that she had stopped, which was noted with approval and no further comment.

Then there was a name he knew because it had been in an envelope, and the man presenting said that the position was static and was being handled and would not be affected by the review point.

The woman in her sixties said, "Static how?"

"He has an arrangement of his own that makes it not worth disturbing."

"And you're comfortable with that."

"I'm comfortable with the arithmetic of it."

She made a small noise. "That's a different thing and you know it."

Nobody minuted that either.

---

It finished at twenty past one. Somebody had ordered sandwiches that arrived at half past one and nobody stayed for them.

He carried the folders back down to the car, which is why he had been there, and he put them in the boot in the order he had been given and closed it.

Then he sat in the driver's seat for a minute or so with the door open and one foot on the tarmac and looked at a wall.

He got out his phone and looked at the calendar on it and counted forward, and put it away.

There is nothing to report about what he thought, because there is no evidence that he thought anything. What he did was start the car and drive four hours, and stop once at a service station, and eat something in the car park with the engine off.

But he counted the months first. That much is on the record, because he did it twice.
