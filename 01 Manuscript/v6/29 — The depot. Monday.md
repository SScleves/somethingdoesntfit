# The depot. Monday.

The depot had a car park and a sign and a woman at a desk who'd been there since the sign, and under the car park, the older half, the part that had been there before anyone thought to put a building with a reason on top of it, and he'd been given a badge for both and had used only the top one, because the transfer was paper and paper lived upstairs.

The weeklies were in the transfer. That was the point of him being there, or it was the point of him being *away*, and he'd stopped distinguishing. Thirty-one years of a post box's output, boxed, labelled, moved from a house that never threw anything away to a depot that catalogued it beautifully, by people who loved records and had been told they were conservation. He checked them in. He did it properly, box by box, the way a man he'd played golf with would have done it, and the woman at the desk said he was the first handler in her time who'd read the labels.

The current week was in the last box. One sheet. Two columns. He'd seen it on a long table, turned to the light; now it was the master, the one the Monday reading would be taken from, and the reading would go to people above Bram, and the people above Bram would look at the right-hand column and at the date, and bring it forward, or not.

He sat with it in a room with a lamp.

*J-0417.* The right column, up every week since April. Beside it, in Bram's hand, from the long table: *hands — confirmed (H-114).* And a note, from Sunday, in the same hand, accurate, as promised: *Offer declined. Wife declined. Not afraid. Recommend: review as scheduled.*

He read it twice. Then he read his own line, at the bottom, nine years old, with its date.

He had a pen. He'd always had a pen; it was a thing people said about him. He did not use it on his own line. He thought about that for some time and found that he'd never once, in nine years, considered it, and that this was interesting, and that it was too late to be interesting.

He used it on the other one.

Not the number. Numbers get re-added. Not the name. Names get asked after. He used it on the one word that would be read, in the column where the reading was taken, and he did it the way a man in a good coat had taught a printer to do it in a city he'd never been to: one word, the spacing held, nothing else on the page touched. The pen was the same black. The hand was close enough to Bram's that nobody would check, because nobody ever checks the way a man on a kitchen floor checks, and the man on the kitchen floor was the one person on earth who'd never see this sheet.

*hands — confirmed* became *hands — reporting ceased.*

That was all. Reporting ceased was a category. It was the category Leiden had been in for nine years. It meant: a reader who has gone quiet and from whom no reading is taken. It meant the right-hand column stopped, here, at this line, and the Monday reading would show a number one lower than it had been, for the first time since April, and the people above Bram would look at a slope that had flattened and would do what they always did with a slope that flattened.

They would leave it.

He put the sheet back in the box. He wrote the four lines he was there to write, on the transfer form, in his own hand, accurate: *Transfer received. Weeklies complete to date. One subject reclassified on review (see sheet). H-114.* He read the last line twice before he signed it, because it was true, in the way that a second impression is a first edition, and because he had learned which lines got read.

He gave the box to the woman at the desk. She thanked him. He said it had been a pleasure, and meant it, and went out through the door with the sign on it to the car park, and stood by the car with his hand on the roof, and did not get in.

Eleven minutes. He'd never known why eleven. It was just the number it took.

Somewhere behind him, under the car park, was the older half, and he had a badge for it, and he had never used the badge, and he found that he was not going to. There was nothing down there he needed to see. He'd seen it in a kitchen in Leiden with a flask, and on a floor through a back window, and across a long table in a man's face when he'd said the first name. Everything else was records.

He got in. He drove. He did not drive to Zwolle, because the chair was in a box now, labelled, in conservation. He drove to a flat with a name on the bell that wasn't his and fed a cat that wasn't his and sat for a while in a room with nothing of his in it, and then he took the flask out of his coat pocket, which was full, because he'd filled it that morning without deciding to, and he put it on the table, and looked at it.

*The reading on Monday was down by one. The review stayed where it was, and then was quietly returned to the end of the year, and then to a date nobody at Bram's level was told. His own line was never checked. Nobody checks the way the one man checks, and the one man had stopped.*
