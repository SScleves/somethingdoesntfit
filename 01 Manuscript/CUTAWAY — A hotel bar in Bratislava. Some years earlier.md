# A hotel bar in Bratislava
### Some years earlier

The bar was in the basement and had been done out in the eighties and never since, and at nine on a Tuesday there were four people in it including the barman.

He had the seat by the pillar because it faced the stairs, and a beer he was not drinking, and a paperback he was not reading.

At the other end, under the mirror, two men had been talking for an hour and ten.

---

The older one did all the talking. Grey, heavy in the shoulders, an accent from somewhere further east, a man who had clearly been told a great many times in his life to keep his voice down and had never managed it. He had a folder on the table and he kept putting his hand flat on it.

The younger one was about thirty-five and had not touched his drink either.

He could hear about a third of it. Enough.

The younger man had written something. That was clear from the first ten minutes; there was a journal involved, and a submission, and a date, and the older man had opinions about the date. There had been a difficulty with a reviewer. The word *reviewer* came up a dozen times and was, on each occasion, the younger man's word and not the older one's.

At about the fifty-minute mark the older man said something with the word *supervisor* in it and the younger man stopped talking for a while.

Then the older man opened the folder and turned it round.

---

He watched that part properly, over the top of the paperback, because it was the only part worth watching.

The younger man read whatever it was for about two minutes without moving. Then he pulled it towards him and read it again with a finger on the page, and then he sat back.

He asked a question. The older man answered in about four words.

He asked another one, longer.

The older man laughed — genuinely, not unkindly, the laugh of a man who has been asked something charming — and said a sentence that carried across the whole bar because he had never in his life kept his voice down:

"Nobody is asking you to say anything untrue."

---

After that it took nineteen minutes.

There was a period where the younger man had his hand over his mouth and looked at the mirror. There was a period where he wrote something on a beer mat, which was almost certainly a number, and turned it round, and the older man looked at it and did the thing with his mouth and did not say no.

And then there was the part that always happens and that people who have not watched it do not believe, which is that they got on.

The last twenty minutes were warm. The younger man told a story with his hands and the older man enjoyed it. There was a second round, the first drinks having gone flat and untouched. At one point the older man took out a phone and showed him a photograph of something and the younger man leaned in to look and said something appreciative.

He put a name in his own phone at that point, and the time, and nothing else.

---

At ten they shook hands at the foot of the stairs and the younger man went up first.

The older man came back to the bar to settle, and stood two metres away waiting for the barman with his card in his hand, and said, into the middle distance, in English, not looking round:

"That was cheap."

He did not answer, because he had not been addressed, and because the older man was not the kind who required one.

"They're always cheap at that age. They think they're going to do it again later, properly, when they're established." The card went into the machine. "There isn't a later. Nobody has ever once come back and done it properly. Not one, in my whole career."

He took his receipt and folded it and put it in his breast pocket, and stood there for a second looking at the stairs the younger man had gone up.

"He'll be fine," he said. "He'll be a professor. He'll be extremely good at it and he'll be well liked and he will supervise thirty students and not one of them will ever hear about tonight, and in about fifteen years he'll be the one buying the drinks."

Then, in a completely different voice, the voice of a man remembering he is in a room with somebody:

"Do you want anything? I've got the card out."

"No. Thank you."

"Suit yourself."

---

He waited a quarter of an hour after the older man had gone. Then he paid for his own beer in cash and went up to the lobby and out into the square, which was cold and empty and had a tram going through it.

There was nothing for him to do. There had never been anything for him to do; he had been asked to be in the room, which he had been, and to note the time, which he had.

He sent four words and switched the phone off and went to find something to eat.
