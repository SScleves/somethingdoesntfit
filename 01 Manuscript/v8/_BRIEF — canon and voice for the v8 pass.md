## 1. Canon — fixed facts (resolve every contradiction to these)
**James Klippe.** Narrator, first person past. Monitoring/observability engineer at a big Dutch bank ("four hundred years old"), eleven years. **He never changes employer.** No recruiter email, no Meerwijk. Wednesday rounds alone with the dogs; Saturdays with Ana. His employee number: 40176502. The forum handle is never spelled out ("a name that was a joke only I understood").
**Ana.** His wife. Believes her grandmother is okay now; the book never proves her wrong. Calls him Mulder; tinfoil-hat sizes every December. Swears when she's right. Leaves for her mother's when her address is on the schedule. Ends the dinner game herself with her mother's sleeping pills.
**Buddy** (twelve, grey, old, under every table, can't do the stairs) and **Mora** (black Doberman, six, food, chaos, "Blade"). Buddy is poisoned in "The vet" and **lives**. He dies only in the Epilogue, four years on, 2:15 a.m., on his blankets; buried in the garden by the gate. New dog in the epilogue: **Ida**.
**Frank.** Colleague at the bank, young (twenty-nine), single, permanently vibrating about the future, skull emoji. Skeptic who teases, then loosens. Never in it, never the villain.
**Pieter.** James's manager at the bank. A helper: told the orphan job is a security test. His name and his children are on the seat annex. Says "leave it."
**Saskia.** Bank colleague (minor).
**Kalder.** The golfer. Introduced as **"Julian"** — that's what he tells James, and what Ana calls him. The narration calls him Julian until Noor names him "Kalder" (in "The vet"); after that, Kalder. Handsome, funny, very good golfer, great company, genuinely likes James. Sells the bank its continuity (vendor lanyard). A handler who crossed nine years ago and took his seat. A psychopath, loyal to himself. Poisons Buddy to get into the house. **Never changes, never repents, never warns James.** Used, burned, found at the market.
**Bram.** The one who talks in the handler scenes. Sincere, decent, believes the story, has never had the experience he counts.
**The old head.** Unnamed ("the old man"). The bad one. In the reading room in the opening; defeated and talking at the end.
**Anneke de Wit.** The bank's head of risk. Silent in the opening (chair by the window); reasonable at the end; takes the chair.
**Aylin Pekcan.** At the reading-room desk (Keeper of Collections). Proud of the archive. Shows James the visitor book.
**Noor Haverkamp.** The professor's last student, paper conservator in Leiden, about fifty now (about forty-one nine years ago). Quiet because the professor told her to be. Made quiet by Kalder alone with a clipboard ("Leiden, Friday").
**Professor Hendrik Maasen.** Dead before the book starts: Deventer, March, in a blue chair, found because the bin wasn't put out. He wrote to the foundation's post box asking someone to come and talk. He did ask, so he IS in the visitor book in March.
**Els Maasen.** His daughter, curtain shop. Refuses James, then calls. Already turned when she gets her sheet.
**Beard Man.** Market book dealer. They have **never exchanged names** — he never says "James." Ninety percent nonsense; one nonsense theory (the bucket) turns out right.
**Henk Boersma** (Zwolle). Retired engineer who started saying strange things; drowned in a canal after "the nice man, the Volvo." Widow **Marijke** survives; the flask was left on purpose.
**The Groningen dealer**, **the brewery man** (five seats, "I'm *in*"), **the site manager**: unnamed.
**Utrecht 1993:** an invented Dutch physicist who replicated the two-rooms experiment; his friend dies in a car on a canal road in 1995. NOT the real Mexico City scientist. Mexico City 1994 is the real Jacobo Grinberg disappearance, facts only.

**The machine.** Editing (every "given" becomes "arose," typos left in); Counting (the nightly job at 02:14, forty-one kilobytes, left column = people who asked/wrote/posted; handlers judge the right column = who stopped being afraid); Pressure (ordinary load); the thermostat (funds nonsense when too few ask). "Review" = records moved "for conservation" and people visited, one address at a time, each ending plausible. Helpers are told one small reason and the word "continuity," and promised they'll be "kept."
**The seats.** Recovery seats in the bank's continuity contract; annex lists names, "dependants per allocation," and *companion animals are not accommodated*. Kalder sells it.
**The building.** The foundation's archive (reading room, catalogue, Aylin) on top of the bank's recovery site (lower floor, seats). James drills there with a lanyard and a sandwich.
**The thin book.** Forty-one edits. Its last page gives **a library reference, a date (the second of December), and the word *Ask*.** Not coordinates. The professor's appointment, which James keeps. Urfa reaches the book only via the Urfa 1994 interlude and Noor, never by James typing coordinates. The thin book stays in James's kitchen and is on the tablecloth in "Twenty minutes"; it is not stolen.
**The invoice.** Monthly/quarterly line to the foundation's post box, "continuity reconciliation"; charged per flagged person; the sum has stepped up six times in thirty-one years (first set up 1994; it steps up the week James asked). In "Leave it" it steps down for the first time.
**The schedule.** Eleven addresses in the region; three resolved this quarter, each plausible; four quiet; Ana's mother's/their house is on it ("Companion").
**Deventer slip.** Kalder names "the professor's place near Deventer," a place James never said — at the pub ("On land"), where Kalder also uses the obscure word *decanting*. The invented-book trap (v8.2 "On land") is the pub: James mentions a book that doesn't exist and Kalder says he's read it.
**Order of the coded scenes:** On land (pub) → The gorilla mask (barbecue) → The dinner. Kalder keeps coming round until the vet; after the dinner he is out.
**The ending.** The sheets → the leak (review brought forward to Monday) → the night before (twelve envelopes to the board) → Monday (board vote at item four, eleven to one, Anneke takes the chair; James on the sixth green with Buddy in the cart) → the final conversation → the market (Kalder surrounded by his own) → Leave it (the date passes, nothing loud, the sum steps down; the job is gone in a month — Frank and James file the last ticket) → Epilogue ("he's in a better place; I'll see him soon" — and this time he means it).
**Real-world rule.** Real anomalies may be real; edits, people and companies are invented. Never attach an invented edit to a real book. No real company named as complicit. Real works may be named only as the old man's *claims* in the final conversation, never confirmed by the narration.

## 2. Voice — sound like Santi's chapters 1–3 (read them before writing; ch. 4 is AI-touched, don't copy its cadence)
**Do:**
1. **Dashes for asides, jokes landing mid-sentence.** Santi runs ~15 em-dashes per 1,000 words; v6/v7 ran ~1. Aim for 8–14. "Mora patrolling the treeline for duck poop, which to her is caviar — she's gross."
2. **Blunt, unpolished confession.** "I suck." "Okay. It's clearly a defect. But hey — it pays the bills." Leave in slightly rough, non-native-feeling phrasing; don't sand it.
3. **Swearing as punctuation**, about one every two pages, James and Ana both. "Fuck. I don't hate my job at all. But fuck."
4. **Fake precision as a joke.** "about 85 percent like a yes" (write it as words), "it took about eleven seconds."
5. **Dogs gross and warm at once,** under every table, interrupting.
6. **IT thinking in passing, never explained.** "love is a terrible auditor," "my brain stamps them PROCESSED." Rationed: max two per chapter.
7. **Recipe:** serious observation → concrete detail → joke that's actually a confession.
8. **Ana's put-downs carry the humor**; she's sharp, funny, and right a lot.
9. **Insights happen to him** — slow, lucky, on the floor with a dog. He's "not the guy who finds a word and knows the secret of the pyramids by breakfast."
10. **Dialogue over monologue.** Facts enter through conversations. Explanations get interrupted, misheard, joked at.
11. **Endings are hooks or plain beats** ("I bookmarked them."), not aphorisms.

**Kill on sight:**
- Narrating the narration: "I'm going to tell you / describe / I'd like to tell you / the honest version / this chapter / this book / this is where the book turns."
- "On the record" — **zero** new uses (Santi's own two in ch. 2–3 stay; Bram may echo it once in the whole book, no more).
- "I want to be precise / clear / accurate" — max once per chapter, and never as a chapter closer.
- Aphoristic one-line paragraph closers and "It wasn't X. It was Y." / "Not X. Y." contrasts — one per chapter at most.
- Explaining the joke after the joke.
- Recycled catchphrase karaoke: "filed it," "foreman," "paying for every step," "forty more years," "you've got the face" — each of Santi's lines may be echoed once in the whole book, by whoever owns that callback, not sprinkled.
- Template endings (driving home in the dark; the dog as the final image every time).
- James out-deducing the book (Sherlock reads of objects, heist competence). He gets there by asking dumb small questions, like at work.
- Lectures. Any speech over ~150 words gets broken by the other person, a dog, a phone, a joke.

**Third-person pieces** (K, K-past, J-past, real interludes): plain, short, Le Carré-quiet. No explanations. Real interludes: real people doing documented things only, no invented documents; ≤ 1,200 words.
