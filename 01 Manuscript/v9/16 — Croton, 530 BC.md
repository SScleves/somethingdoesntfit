# Croton, 530 BC

The date and the town are a guess. Pythagoras is thought to have moved to Croton, in the south of Italy, around 530 BC, and to have acquired a following there. The story itself gives no place and no year.

Xenophanes told it. He was a poet from Colophon, a contemporary of Pythagoras, and he was not a friend. He made fun of most things people believed, and he told this one the way you tell a story about a man you think is a showman: to make the room laugh.

Once, he says, Pythagoras was passing by when a puppy was being beaten. He took pity on it and said: *Stop. Don't beat it. It is the soul of a friend of mine. I knew it when I heard it cry out.*

That's the whole thing. Four lines of verse. The poem they came from is lost, and the four lines survive only because Diogenes Laertius copied them into his life of Pythagoras some seven hundred years later, as an example of what people said about the man.

Nobody knows if it happened. Nobody knows who the friend was. The puppy is not described. We aren't told whether the beating stopped.

What we know is that about two and a half thousand years ago somebody was already saying that a dog could carry a person he had loved, and that you could know it by the voice. And that the oldest mention of the idea we have was written by a man who thought it was ridiculous, and wrote it down so that everybody could see how ridiculous it was. The first time it appears, it is already a joke.
