# Kastanjelaan
### A Thursday in August

He parked on the far side, four houses down, at nine forty at night, and did not switch anything off except the lights.

It was warm. Somebody two gardens over was having people round; you could hear the tail end of it, a laugh going up and then down, a chair on a patio.

The house was the one with the shelf in the front window.

---

The brief was two lines and it was about occupancy.

*Confirm household composition. Do not approach.*

That is all it was. That is genuinely all it was, and anybody who has done the work will tell you that ninety per cent of it is exactly this: sitting in a car for an hour to establish something that a competent person could establish by knocking on the door and asking for directions, except that knocking on the door is the one thing you may never do.

There was a light on downstairs at the back. There had been a light on downstairs at the back for four hours.

No car in front. The car in front had gone on the Tuesday of the previous week with a woman and a bag in it and a black dog in the back, and it had not come back, and the brief was to confirm that.

He confirmed it.

That took about eleven minutes and he sat there for another fifty.

---

At ten past ten the man came out into the front garden with a bin.

He watched him do it. It took a while; the man stood there afterwards with his hands on the lid, looking at nothing, for the better part of a minute, in the way people do when they have come outside for a task and the task is finished and they are not ready to go back in.

Then the man went in and the door shut and the hall light went off and the light at the back stayed on.

---

At twenty past ten he got out and walked up the far pavement, once, at the pace of a man walking, and did not stop, and did not look at the house except in the way that anybody looks at a house.

The dog started when he was level with it.

Not the young one; the young one was three hundred kilometres away in a garden at a mother-in-law's. The old one. A deep, slow, entirely serious bark from somewhere at the back of the house — three of them, then a pause, then three more.

He kept walking.

It went on for about forty seconds after he had passed and then it stopped, and then, faintly, through a window somebody had left open because of the heat, there was a man's voice saying something to it in the tone you use, and then nothing.

---

He got back in the car and wrote two lines.

*Single occupancy confirmed. Wife absent 12 days. One dog on premises, older animal, alerts to street.*

He looked at that for a moment and then added four words to the end of it, because it was true and because leaving out true things is how people get hurt:

*Would be a problem.*

Then he put the notebook in the door pocket.

He did not do anything else. He did not get out again, he did not go round the back, he did not approach the property, and there was never any instruction that he should, and there is no evidence in anything that follows that he ever did.

He sat for about four more minutes.

The party two gardens over broke up at some point; you could hear the goodbyes going on for ages, the way they do.

Then he started the car and drove to a hotel by the motorway and slept, and in the morning he drove to another city and did something else entirely, and Kastanjelaan does not appear again in anything he was ever asked to do.

---

Nine days later, at ten past three in the morning, in the kitchen of that house, an old dog tried to stand up and his back legs did not come with him, and he went on trying, on his own, in the dark, for a length of time that nobody will ever know.
