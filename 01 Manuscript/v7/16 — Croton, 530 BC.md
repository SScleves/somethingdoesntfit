# Croton, 530 BC

Nobody wrote it down for a hundred years, so take it as you'd take anything a hundred years old by the time somebody wrote it down.

Xenophanes told it. He was not a friend. He thought Pythagoras was a fraud and a showman, and he told the story the way you tell a story about a man you think is a fraud: to make the room laugh.

Pythagoras was walking in Croton, in the south of Italy, where he had gone to be left alone and had instead acquired a following, and he passed a man beating a puppy. And he stopped, and said: *Stop. Don't hit it. It is the soul of a friend of mine. I knew him by the voice when it cried out.*

That's it. That's the whole thing. Four lines in Diogenes Laertius, quoting a poem that is otherwise lost, by a man who despised the person he was quoting.

Nobody knows if it happened. Nobody knows who the friend was. The puppy is not described. We don't know if the man stopped.

What we know is that two and a half thousand years ago, in a town that no longer exists, somebody said out loud, in front of witnesses, that a dog could carry a person he had loved, and that you could know it by the voice. And that the only reason the sentence survived is that another man thought it was ridiculous, and wrote it down so that everyone could see how ridiculous it was.

It has outlived everything else he wrote.
