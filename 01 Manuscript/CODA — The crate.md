Sunday. Cobblestones, and rain that can't commit.

The market is the same market it has always been. A man has a trestle table under a tarpaulin and two crates on the ground at his feet where the unloved books go, and he is talking. He has been talking since before anybody here was born and he will still be mid-sentence when they've all gone.

A stranger kneels down at the crates.

Not browsing — *kneeling*, one knee on the wet stone, going spine by spine with two fingers, the way you do when it isn't a hobby.

"Estate sale," the man with the beard says, to nobody, to the rain. "Some fellow up north. Whole house of books and the family kept the furniture."

The stranger doesn't answer. Doesn't hear it, probably.

Mythology, mostly. The serious kind, footnotes in three languages. Something on calendar systems. A survey of flood legends with a water stain along the bottom edge.

And at the bottom of the second crate, under all of it, flat, a thin one with no title on the spine at all.

The stranger takes it out and turns it over and opens it in the middle.

Handwriting. Ruled columns, done by eye, with something that wasn't a ruler. Two hands — an older ink and a newer one — and a third thing at the very bottom of the last page, in a different pen, added later, by a hand that shook.

The stranger reads it for about a minute and a half.

Then closes it, and puts it on the pile with the others.

"How much for these?"

The man with the beard barely looks. He counts the stack the way you'd count sheep going through a gate.

"Eleven." He shrugs, and reaches down, and puts one more on top of it. "Have twelve. Twelve sounds better."
