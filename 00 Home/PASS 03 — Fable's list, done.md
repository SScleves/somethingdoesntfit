## What was done

**1. Ana.** Beats added to ch. 8, 11, 16, 17. Her absence registered in 29 and 30. **Ch. 7 left alone** — it already had a real scene (the *he's crossed nothing out* moment); adding more would have been padding.

- **Ch. 8** — she listens to the whole Göbekli story over the dishes and says *"That's the most excited I've heard you in about a year, and it's about a hole in the ground in Turkey."* Her mode exactly: not *is it true*, but *where are you*.
- **Ch. 11** — three weeks of hunting for a counter-example. *"So you're trying to prove yourself wrong." "That's the job." "Mm. I like this one. Do more of this one."* And then, quietly: it took him a year to realise the version of him she liked was the version that could still be talked to.
- **Ch. 16** — she gets the credit for the whole method. She read the object in nine seconds fourteen months earlier and does not know to this day that she said anything.
- **Ch. 17** — **he doesn't tell her about the forty per cent.** A man is offered that much more money and it isn't a decision, it's a *story* you bring home — and he deletes it, because the last paragraph mentioned golf and there was no short way to explain that. Not a lie. A thing that needs too much context, so it becomes a thing you don't mention.
- **Ch. 29** — on a wall in Anatolia, thinking about the boiler. He's had it serviced twice since July and has both certificates in a drawer and it hasn't helped. *It only has to be cheap, and it has to be about your house.*
- **Ch. 30** — he rings her from the gate. *"The boiler's fine, by the way. I had him back out. He thinks we're mad."* Which is how he finds out she'd been carrying it for four months too, and hadn't said, for exactly the same reason he hadn't.

**2. Ch. 29 — Aylin stays.** The chiller argument now runs through the runbook. She calls down the aisle — *"He says the British units are better. They are not."* — six metres from § 7, which she has read and signed for and which is not a secret from her, because nobody reads section four. **And the revision-rate now comes from asking her, not from reading a spine:** Rev. 8 for six years, then 9, then 10, then another one coming. Four in five years, which she thinks is a nuisance about formatting — and which is the same accelerating curve as the forty edit-dates in ch. 19. That pays off a thread that previously went nowhere.

**3. Noor moved to ch. 14.** Also fixes a logic hole: her message is triggered by the polite correction, which happens in 14, not four months later. She now has a beat in 15 (the Ghent failure — *"that is a promotion"*), the raking-light lesson relocated to 16 where it becomes the trigger for the metadata idea, and a new exchange at the ch. 17 midpoint where she is the first person to tell him it has changed category and he doesn't stop. **Dialogue in the old dead zone: ch. 15 went 12 → 19, ch. 16 0 → 10, ch. 17 0 → 8.**

**4. Darwin moved** to after ch. 6.

**5. Ch. 5 trimmed** — the coyness cut from four paragraphs to one flat sentence: *That's all I'm going to say about it, and the reason isn't suspense.* Not halved: the Antwerp section pays off directly in ch. 28 (*collecting permission slips*) and cutting it would cost more than it saves.
