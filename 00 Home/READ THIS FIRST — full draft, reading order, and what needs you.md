## What I changed after your notes, and why

**1. Chapter-references — killed, all 35.** You were right, it's cheap meta, and worse: it made the narrator a man writing a novel, when the ch. 31 reveal is that this manuscript is a *dossier sitting in escrow.* Every one is gone. Details in `02 Structure/FIX 01`.

**2. "I want that on the record" — 25 down to 4.** You were right that there had to be a better mechanism. There is, and it was already in the book: **he shows the log entry.** Italic, dated, clinical, terse. *Told A. I am on land. Present tense. Review.* That's an object, not an assertion — which is the book's own genre rule — and it can't go stale the way a phrase does. The four survivors: two are yours in ch. 2–3, one is load-bearing in ch. 18, and **one is now Bram using your phrase back at James in ch. 30**, which turns the tic into a payoff.

**3. Dave replaces the anonymous fairway stranger** (ch. 18 v2). Golf stays, per you. He asks *lots* of questions — all about the dog — and the chill is how fast James likes him.

**4. He killed the real one and wore the name.** Ch. 27: James runs "Julian Kalder" and finds a real conservator — accreditation from 1994, three good papers on ink corrosion, a sister, **dead in January 2011**, accreditation renewed every year since with the fee paid. The photograph on the 2002 conference page is a different man. We end the chapter knowing *less* than we did at the start of it. The last cutaway retires the name and issues a new one; the book never learns who he is.

**5. Glimpses of the plan, obliquely.** Never explained, always fragments: §4 indicator bands, §7 population phase, a review point brought forward from March, *assets in transit should be settled before the reissue.* Ruys thinks it's a poor decision and says so, which tells you more than any monologue would.

**6. Your mother's two sentences are in the book** and they're the spine of the ending. §7 of the runbook in ch. 29 is *maintenance of ordinary pressure* — not deprivation, which is unstable and expensive; just enough load that nobody has a spare hour. James understands it standing at a shelf, and he understands it *through being twenty-three and too poor to be depressed*. It becomes his counter-argument in ch. 30, and it's what makes ch. 32 mean something: **the revolution isn't knowledge, it's the afternoon.**

## Decisions I took (overrule any of them)

- **Ch. 31 — he stays, and "stays" means he stays silent.** Not death, not disappearance. The Switch is armed by his silence, so the only way to keep it worth anything is to be a man who never says a word of it. **And this manuscript is the payload** — you're reading it because he stopped. The book doesn't say why he stopped, and offers three possibilities, one of which is that he chose to.
- **The hoard is a lid, not a vault.** A real, funded, publicly listed conservation depot with a car park and a sign, built in 2013 over a rock-cut complex that was already there — with an access road that's on the imagery from 1998, fifteen years before the building.
- **The Erskine inversion.** He walks in expecting a trove of stolen things and finds the best-run archive he's ever stood in, saving material that would otherwise have been pulped, run by people who are proud of it. *The crime is the list of who's allowed in.* Fourteen names in a visitor book in two months.
- **The offer is a division sum.** 340 provisioned, ~900 maintaining, four independent public sources, eight per cent agreement. He hands it to a conservator and gets on a plane. Not victory — **extension.**
- **Council of Rome 382 → James Bruce at Gondar 1770.** The Damasus attribution is contested and shouldn't be built on. Bruce is better and it's Tresidder's exact mirror: a man walks *out* of a landscape carrying the book about the ones who taught, and is laughed at for twenty years, and the book sits unread in the Bodleian for fifty.
- **Interlude IV takes no side on Nuremberg.** Says atmospheric optics is very probably what happened, and that this makes it more interesting, not less, because the point is people with no vocabulary reaching for the nearest object on the bench.
