## The verdict

**It is a book. Not "there's a book in here" — it is one.** I was braced for the seam between your four chapters and the twenty-nine after them and I could not find it. The voice holds. The structure holds. The ending lands twice — the spade in the garden, and *"Check."*

**I'd put it at 8.** Higher than PASS 01 said. Opus was right about the structural gaps and wrong about two things:

- **It does not go grey at ch. 20.** The stand, *you write configuration files*, Aylin on the temperature, the tea towel, *because fifty* — it stays funny in the way you wanted, dry and inside the pain.
- **It is not one temperature.** Kastanjelaan, the pub, the vet's floor, Bruce at Gondar, the barbecue — those are five different temperatures. The register varies more than that doc claimed.

**What is genuinely, unusually good:** the control case (6); the click at the barbecue (21) and the slip on the terrace (22), which are the two best thriller scenes I have read in a draft this year; Noor refusing to be a witness (23); the vigil (23); the Erskine inversion in the stacks (29) — *the crime is the list of who's allowed in*; your mother's two sentences carrying the ending (29, 30, 32); the five cutaways, which are the coldest, cleanest writing in it and explain nothing.

## What I'd change — in order. Give this list to Opus as-is.

**1. Ana into the ten chapters she's missing from.** 7, 8, 11, 16, 17 need one beat each — four lines is enough. 29 and 30 need her *absence registered*: he's two thousand kilometres away thinking about the boiler. This is the single biggest fix and it is ~1,200 words.

**2. Ch. 29 — Aylin stays in the room.** Right now she walks off and James reads a manual at a shelf for 1,500 words. The climax of the ladder is a document read aloud. She should be six metres away talking about the chiller *while* he reads §7, cheerful, and he should get at least one section out of her by asking, not by reading a spine.

**3. Noor to ch. 14.** She arrives in 19, which is too late for the Rocky. Move her first message to the end of 14 and give her two exchanges in 16. That alone dissolves the 15–16–17 dead zone (5,600 words, twelve lines of dialogue).

**4. Move Interlude I (Darwin) from after ch. 4 to after ch. 6.** Reading it in sequence: it stalls the reader at *"where else?"*, the one moment they most want James. After 6 it lands better — he has just proven his method, Darwin proves the phenomenon at scale.

**5. Ch. 5 — halve it.** Still the weakest chapter. The days structure is fine; the redaction paragraph reads coy. Cut *"I am not going to describe it"* to one flat sentence.

**6. "I want to be [honest / accurate / clear / exact / precise]" — 32 uses.** The second tic. Cut to eight. It's the same move as the Record phrase: keep it where the hedge is load-bearing, delete it where it's throat-clearing.

**7. Buddy under the table in 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 27, 30.** Free, true, and it's the difference between a man working and a man alone.

**8. Ana's line in 26**, naming the gap: *"You can tell me exactly why you ask strangers that question and you couldn't tell he was one."*
