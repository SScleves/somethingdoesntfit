# FIX 01 — two tics, and the better mechanism
### Santi's note, 10 Sep 2026: *"u mention chapters a lot, like breaking the 4th wall? and I thought we agreed to stop or think better when to say I want it added to the record — there must be a different and better mechanism."*

Both correct. Both mine. Here's the diagnosis and the fix.

---

## TIC 1 — chapter-references

**35 instances across ch. 5–31.** *"in chapter one," "this chapter," "twenty-six chapters," "the last chapters," "this book."*

**Why it's wrong, beyond being repetitive:** it makes the narrator a man writing a *novel*. He isn't. The ch. 31 reveal is that this manuscript is the payload sitting in escrow next to thirty-seven diffs and four divisions. A man assembling a dossier does not say "as I mentioned in chapter four." The tic actively fights the ending.

**The fix:**

| Kill | Replace with |
|---|---|
| "in chapter five" / "in chapter one" | the *event*: "the night I found out he'd vanished", "at the graveside" |
| "this chapter" | "here", "this part", or cut the clause |
| "twenty-six chapters", "twenty-seven chapters" | elapsed time: "four years", "three years" |
| "the last chapters" | "the end of this" |
| "this book" | mostly cut. Survives ~6 times, and only where he means *the document he is assembling* — never where he means "the story you're reading" |

**Rule going forward:** he may refer to **what he wrote down**, to **when a thing happened**, and to **the reader** ("I'd like you to check the division"). He may never refer to the structure of the text.

---

## TIC 2 — *"I want that on the record"*

**25 instances.** It was designed as the Switch's tell — the phrase that means *I am preserving this deliberately* — and the whole value of a tell is that it's rare. At 25 it's a verbal habit, which is the opposite of a tell, and it's an *assertion* where the book's own genre rule says reveals should be objects.

### The better mechanism: **show the entry.**

The Record is a file with fields. He has been keeping it for ten years. He has a **notes field** where he writes things nobody will ever read — that's already established (ch. 4's retro insert; ch. 14's *Flag for review*).

So when he wants to preserve something, he doesn't announce it. **He logs it, and we see the line.**

Format: italic, terse, dated, clinical, sometimes with a field name. Flat as a bus timetable. The gap between the flatness of the entry and the size of what it's about is the whole effect.

**Already in the draft and working (keep as the model):**

> *Told A. it's finished. Reason given (to self): protecting her. Actual reason: unknown. Flag for review.* — ch. 14
>
> *FALSE. Production error. Erratum slip located, Ghent, 14 Feb. Struck.* — ch. 15
>
> *You want this to be true. Weigh accordingly.* — ch. 5
>
> *The absence is the fingerprint. Weather doesn't skip the cheap seats.* — ch. 15

**Worked conversions:**

| Was | Becomes |
|---|---|
| ch. 9: *"I want it on the record that I knew what I was starting."* | *9 Sep. Cleared the third shelf. Room for 38 more. Noted, in case it matters later, that I knew what I was doing when I did it.* |
| ch. 10: *"I want it on the record because it's the first symptom and I missed it."* | (cut entirely — the beat is stronger without a flag on it) |
| ch. 17: *"I want it on the record that this is all I ever claimed."* | *Claim, stated in full: the same three signatures on the body that decides which pages survive and the body that decides which people do. Nothing further. Do not extend this.* |
| ch. 22: *"I want that in the book."* | cut; the flat sentence carries it |
| ch. 25: *"I want it on the record, because everything proceeds from it"* | cut the flag, keep the content |

### Where the phrase survives — **four times, total, in the whole novel:**

1. **Ch. 2 (Santi's, locked)** — *"I want it on the record that I had that thought. It matters for later."* The origin. Untouched.
2. **Ch. 3 (Santi's, locked)** — *"I want that on the record too."* Untouched.
3. **One in Act 2B**, at the point of maximum cost — proposed: ch. 22, the pub, after the slip.
4. **Ch. 31**, the last one, where the reader finally learns what "the record" has meant the entire time.

Two of the four are already Santi's. That's the correct ratio: the phrase is *his*, established in the locked chapters, and I should have been spending it, not spraying it.

---

## Instruction for whoever drafts next

- Chapter-refs: **zero.**
- "On the record": **zero new ones.** If James needs to preserve something, write the Record entry instead.
- Record entries: no more than **one per chapter**, and skip chapters entirely — they should feel occasional, not structural.
- The flat connective sentence is still the main tool. *I checked the page again. Nothing. I went to bed.*
