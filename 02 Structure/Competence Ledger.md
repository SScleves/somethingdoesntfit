# Competence Ledger (load-bearing — no unearned competence, no villain decay)

## He CAN (established on the page)
- Notice small things everyone waves through; ask the uninteresting question; read the middle when everyone skims.
- Keep records nobody else keeps ([[The Record]]); compare; photograph; file.
- Fake a first impression for three minutes; deflect ([[The Faucet]]); read a room upside-down.
- Sit with doubt without breaking — kill his own certainty, then re-check.
- Build a heartbeat monitor ([[The Switch]]) — it is literally his job.
- Talk to dogs. Learn from them ([[Chapter 04]] floor scene).

## He CANNOT (and must stay unable)
- Code, hack, break in. He is *not a developer* (ch. 1).
- Fight, run, shoot, drive dramatically. He plays golf badly.
- Persuade institutions or crowds. Every public word gets "based on what, what?" or the tinfoil hat.
- Trust his memory (the founding wound) — he needs a shelf to walk to.

## Rules
1. **No win without a receipt.** Every victory traces to the CAN column; the reader can name which.
2. **No loss without a limit.** Every defeat traces to the CANNOT column.
3. **[[The Keepers]] never get dumber.** Floor = the 1931 plate (reader-aware, invisible, allergic to fingerprints). No monologues, no convenient clues, no explaining henchman.
4. **His edge is a shape, not a power.** Their invisibility is tuned to how people check. He checks where they don't. They can't defend against him without abandoning the method — and the method matters more than any reader.

## Where the map is most at risk
- ch. 28–31 (hoard): no action set-piece. Entry is a *question* ("Ask"). Sacrifice is holding a door, not heroics.
- ch. 24–25 (crossing): earned by months of shown discipline; sober.
- ch. 18–21: he never out-spies professionals; he notices what they didn't bother hiding from non-lookers.
