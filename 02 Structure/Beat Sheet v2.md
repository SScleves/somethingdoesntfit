# SOMETHING DOESN'T FIT — Beat Sheet v2

> Supersedes v1. Changes from v1 are marked **[CHANGED]**.
> Four threads: **LADDER** (clue trail) · **INNER** (perception track) · **CLOCK** (imminent reset) · **HEART** (Ana, dogs, grief, the afterlife question).
> Voice: locked via Chapter 1 v2. First person past. Metaphors come from him, not the job. "I work in IT" — depth only when the story forces it.

**Premise:** A grieving man who watches systems for a living discovers that the "gods" of myth are a lineage of elites riding a civilizational reset cycle — and that the reset isn't weather. It's a kill switch, about to be thrown again, because the world is getting too comfortable to stay asleep.

**Thesis line (Beat 15, once):** *The revolution is that he finally has time to love a dog.*

**[CHANGED] The INNER track's method — the Three Keys rule:**
1. **Psychedelics open the window.** They raise questions, widen perception, show him the wall exists. They never get him across. Ever. That limit is stated in-world and held.
2. **Meditation is the discipline.** Slow, repeatable, escalating. Past-life work, out-of-body attempts, "the weird place."
3. **The crossing itself is sober.** The search plus the right state of consciousness does the trick — Beat 12 happens in meditation, not on a substance. No reader gets to say "he was high."

**[CHANGED] The flash logic:** the funeral flash (ch. 1) was not the first anomaly of his life — it was the first he couldn't file away. The reveal at Beat 12 region: meditation reopens the flashes and unlocks the memory that they've been happening his whole life, each filed under tired/stress/nothing. The one system he never instrumented was himself. Same reveal-shape as the ladder: the anomaly was always in the record; he was taught to misread it.

## ACT 1 — THE WIRE (ch. 1–8)

### Beat 1 — Opening Image: The Funeral (ch. 1) ✅ WRITTEN (v2 locked)
Ana's grandmother. The wall of comfort. The 85% yes. The arithmetic. The flash (filed under lack of sleep). Buddy at the bottom step. The three options for the machinery. The corridor at sixteen — planted, door closed. The search begins; no vow.

### Beat 2 — Setup: The Good Life (ch. 2–3) **[CHANGED]**
Life resumes after the funeral. Ana grieving in the house; him orbiting her, useless and loving. The dogs in daylight — Mora all appetite and no brakes, Buddy slower every month. Golf as the still point where the voice finally shuts up. A normal week of the job — shown once, in his terms, not the job's. And the hobby, wider than books: **he hunts knowledge** — strange websites, forums, curious people who talk too long, old books as one branch of it. Harmless. Half-embarrassing. Secretly the whole plot.
- **AI as pure texture:** a headline, a colleague's comment. News, not plot.
- **[CHANGED] No childhood-wound flashback here** — moved to Beat 4.
- Chapter 2 ends with him catching himself: 2 a.m., afterlife tabs open. The search running without permission.

### Beat 3 — Catalyst: The Two Copies (ch. 4) — LADDER RUNG 1
The hobby produces its first real artifact: two copies of the same edition, one paragraph different. Small, deniable, personal.

### Beat 4 — Debate: Am I the Error? (ch. 5–6) **[CHANGED]**
He instruments it: provenance, printings, archives. Rational explanations mostly hold. Mostly.
- **[CHANGED] The record-wound flashback lands here:** as a kid he read something, the source later said otherwise, everyone said he misremembered. Thirty years of being told he's the error instead of the record. It surfaces now because he's living it again.
- **[CHANGED] INNER opens with psychedelics, framed as window:** first deliberate experience — questions raised, perception widened, nothing crossed. He clocks the limit immediately: *it shows you there's a door; it doesn't open it.*
- Ana notices him drifting. First friction: the search vs. the life.

### Beat 5 — Break Into Two: The Goalpost-Move (ch. 7–8) — LADDER RUNG 2
Göbekli Tepe. The definition revised to protect the timeline — his wound at civilizational scale. Site deliberately buried: a cover-up with a date on it. Hobbyist becomes investigator.

## ACT 2A — THE PATTERN (ch. 9–16)

### Beat 6 — B Story: Ana (anchored ch. 9)
The marriage is the B story and the ending's detonator. Her argument is never *is it true* — it's *where are you*. Dogs age in real time.
- *Guardrail: the machinery will try to shove her offstage every chapter. Refuse.*

### Beat 7 — Fun & Games: The Pattern Goes Global (ch. 10–13) — LADDER RUNG 3
Flood myths, knowledge-bringers punished, old gods rewritten into demons. Full Weir-mode: solving is the fun. He finds **the essay** — *Something Doesn't Fit* — the book-as-prop enters.
- **[CHANGED] Research texture now includes the afterlife literature:** NDE reports, after-death communication, and the **group-soul doctrine** — animals return to a species collective, *but love individuates them out of it*. He reads it as lore, files it as unverifiable, and it quietly stays with him (payoff: Beat 15). The reported evidence detail he can't shake: nobody ever meets an archetypal Dog. Always the specific dog. Waiting.
- Texture anomalies: Sphinx, Piri Reis, Antikythera, Baalbek — tabs, never lectures.

### Beat 8 — The Author (ch. 14–15) — LADDER RUNG 4
The essay's author: discredited, vanished, or dead. Noticing has a cost. First indirect contact with the opposition. He starts hiding the search from Ana "to protect her."
- **[CHANGED] INNER — the pivot from window to discipline:** psychedelics have plateaued; they keep showing him the same wall. He starts **meditating** — clumsy at first, then structured: past-life protocols, out-of-body attempts. The flashes return: extremely fast, unknown faces, many. And the unlock begins — *this isn't the first time.* Fragments of a lifetime of filed-away anomalies start surfacing.

### Beat 9 — Midpoint: The Rope Has Two Ends (ch. 16) — LADDER RUNG 5 · CLOCK STARTS
Same network funding narrative-protective archaeology is digging continuity bunkers now. History is a forecast. He stops being harmless.

## ACT 2B — THE CLOCK (ch. 17–24)

### Beat 10 — Bad Guys Close In (ch. 17–20) — LADDER RUNG 6
Outside: surveillance, a job complication, the author's fate as preview. Inside: marriage cracking; Buddy failing.
The turn: the **golden-age pattern** — civilizations fall when they're about to succeed. Suffering is the leash; comfort is the crack; every time the crack widens, the world conveniently ends.
- Predict-vs-cause stays ambiguous. AI texture darkens: replacement accelerating, oversight gutted.

### Beat 11 — All Is Lost (ch. 21–22) **[CHANGED]**
**Buddy does not die.** The cost restructures:
- **The vet scare:** a bad night that ends okay — but makes the clock audible. Decline is scarier than death; a death is an event, decline is a countdown.
- **Ana's ultimatum** (or she leaves for her mother's): the search vs. the life at full price.
- **The author's trail ends** in a body or a broken person. The whiff of death is everywhere — without spending the dog.

### Beat 12 — Dark Night of the Soul: The Crossing (ch. 23–24) **[CHANGED]**
Rock bottom. He goes in **sober** — the deep meditation, the search plus the right state of consciousness. The wall gives.
- The past-life bleed opens fully: lives glimpsed, brutal, short, love cut off mid-sentence. The flashes were never metaphor. He has been here before.
- **The full unlock:** the funeral flash, the lifetime of micro-anomalies — all one signal, misfiled for thirty years. He was taught to misread his own record.
- **[CHANGED] The corridor door opens here:** the father, the grief he never knew how to feel at sixteen because he had no container for it. Now — inside the crossing — he finally has one, and twenty years of postponed grief arrives at once. The personal answer and the cosmic answer land in the same scene.
- Reincarnation as-built is the Keepers' machinery: scatter, forget, restart, serve. The separation engine. INNER and LADDER converge — one seam. The title is the reveal.

## ACT 3 — THE OFFER (ch. 25–30)

### Beat 13 — Break Into Three: Same Weapon, Opposite Intent (ch. 25)
Exposure is pointless (the gorilla mask works). The one thing the system can't absorb: **crack the hoard open.** Not a bomb — an offer. Reconciliation with Ana: she becomes the search's reason instead of its casualty. This was always about seeing her again.

### Beat 14 — Finale: The Hoard + The Kill Switch (ch. 26–29)
The run at the hoard. The reveal: **the reset is a kill switch, and it's scheduled** — this era is exactly what the pattern predicted they'd never allow. The AI steering snaps into focus: they weren't afraid of it; they were aiming it. The offer splits the Keepers (two tiers: those who know and cling, those who inherited and never questioned). Some refuse — the book doesn't cheat. The hoard opens **to the world in-story**; the "how" never reaches the reader.
**The cost:** he holds the door and doesn't walk through — dies making the crack, or stays so it stays open.

### Beat 15 — Final Image: The Warm Coda (ch. 30) **[CHANGED — now two movements]**
**Movement 1 — the flash-forward: Buddy's death, done right.** Years later. An old man and an ancient dog. When Buddy goes, he buries him the way nobody could bury Ana's grandmother in Chapter 1: no borrowed phrases, no wall of comfort — because he doesn't need one. He knows. The group-soul lore from Beat 7 pays off in one line: the collective is the default; love is the exception. Thirteen years of being loved made Buddy *someone*. Grief with a container — looking forward, not back. The full inversion of the opening scene.
**Movement 2 — the wake.** A child, some-when later, partial memory, happy to pursue. The world glimpsed, never explained: machines doing the ugly work, people visibly unhurried. No formula stated — the reader infers what the world needed from the whole book. A young dog in the new life — doing **one specific thing only Buddy did.** Unconfirmed. And an almost-recognition of Ana — possible, not promised. The separation engine is broken; the finding is allowed.
The thesis line, once, transposed and earned.
