## 1. The book in one paragraph

James works in IT; his tools are eyes; the thing that's dying is never his. At his wife's grandmother's funeral he says *yes* at eighty-five per cent to a question nobody checks, and the search begins. In a dead professor's crate he finds a book whose second impression was quietly corrected two years after its author walked into the hills, and a thin notebook with forty more. He checks them the way he checks anything: slowly, with controls, publishing his failures. On his dashboard at work an ownerless service sends traffic every night at the same minute to an address that resolves nowhere, and his manager says *leave it.* The edits push one way (nothing was given); the service counts something; and the two turn out to be one machine, human, finite, maintained with a style guide, that keeps the world inside a band: not too asleep, not too awake. It doesn't kill him. It corrects him, laughs at him, tries to buy him, and sends a man to be his friend. His method fails four times on the page. His marriage nearly does. The one system he never instrumented was himself, and when he does, sober, on a kitchen floor with an old dog watching, the flashes he's been misfiling since he was six turn out to be the same list. The offer comes at a table with his wife present, and nobody at that table can say the true word, because saying it in front of her would put her in. He names all three options anyway. What he wins is not the machine. It's time. Years later a dog dies and nobody says *he's in a better place.* That's the revolution.

## 2. The four movements

Each movement: dramatic question, what James wants, what the machine does, what the reader knows that James doesn't, the turn. Each may open with its own short cold open (an image that doesn't parse yet, from the middle, ending on a question).

### I — Nothing fits
- **Question:** is there anything there, or is grief making patterns?
- **James wants** a container. He looks the way he always looks: two copies, a ruler, the dog.
- **Happens:** the funeral (whole, Santi's); the good life; the crate; the sentence on page 118 (whole, Santi's); the ownerless service and *leave it*; the psychedelic window opens the question and can't answer it; he finds the list.
- **Machine:** nothing to James yet. The reader has watched it work on others: a decent man in 1993 reporting a colleague by renaming a category, and getting his seats confirmed [K-past].
- **Reader knows:** what being *in* is, and that nice people do it over the phone.
- **Turn:** the dashboard entry, the ticket, and a colleague's memory of both vanish in one week.
- **Lie-is-the-clue:** the plate. They fixed nothing else. *Why lie here?*

### II — The machine
- **Question:** what shape is it, and who is in it?
- **James wants** to infer the architecture from the signals, which is the only thing he's good at.
- **Happens:** a theory that fails when checked, with ridicule around it out of all proportion (phantom time on Frank's table, dead on tree rings in four minutes); Frank reads the Robertson recommendation aloud as his trump card and hears it; the style guide (edits function, never philosophy); Ana in a tinfoil hat, funny and scared; **the math** (one in five hundred; ten in a company of five thousand; the postman; statistically someone at the funeral); he stops calling people; then the second column, the same ratio says how many have crossed, and the list is a list of people; Noor; meditation begins as a place to put the fear; entry #26 in his hands. Dave on the seventh tee, who asks about the dog and never about the books.
- **Machine:** notices him for the *service*, not the books. Sends Kalder, whom James meets as a friend. The review date moves because of a sentence Kalder softened in a car park [K].
- **Reader knows:** who's watching; the date moved; the report is filed within hours.
- **Turn:** his access shrinks by exactly what he'd need to see the service again, and the reader learns what the service measures.

### III — The shift
- **Question:** what does crossing cost, and what does being *seen* crossing cost?
- **Happens:** his own words come back to him improved (he's being harvested, not watched; the persona burned; the correction reaches eleven people); the pub slip, Ana present, a town he never said; the jokes stop; the transfer schedule, Ana reads the address and leaves because of the danger, not the distance; Buddy's bad night; Noor's one line; the crossing, sober, flat, no light, he describes the kitchen afterward; "Not nobody. Not you."; he says *soul* once, to a dog, and is embarrassed; the father, the corridor, grief with a container twenty years late.
- **Machine:** brings the review forward *because he crossed*. Kalder reports it and uses James's first name for the first time [K]. The reader learns Kalder has hands too.
- **Reader knows:** the reveal that hurts, one movement before James.
- **Turn:** he understands that everything he'll do to stop it brings it closer. The Vallée cooling half. He is still missing the other half.

### IV — The instrument
- **Question:** can a man who watches systems do anything to one?
- **Happens:** the manual's indicators with acceptable ranges; the ownerless service was the count; the band has a *lower* limit too (the machine heats as well as cools; it seeds the noise it then ridicules); **the table**: Bram, Ana present, twenty objects, the offer (Housed with Ana, the dogs and N seats, or the door), the reveal that hurts arrives as an object, and nobody says the word; the cold open reached and passed; **the one thing [YOURS]**; Kalder's second half.
- **Machine:** files him. The verdict on James is the Told man's phrase from Movement I: *leave it.*
- **Reader knows:** who sent the cold-open image and why; what Kalder wrote in the last report.
- **Coda:** AI steered right, off-page; time; an old man and an old dog; Buddy buried the way nobody could bury Ana's grandmother; the young dog does the one Buddy thing, unconfirmed; "Check." is the last word.

## 3. Tracks

- **[J]** James now. First person, past tense. The spine. ~70% of the book.
- **[J-past]** James before: the corridor at sixteen; the first time he was told to leave something and did; the night Ana said the thing he's about to break. Third person, so the reader sees him before he knew. Three pieces at most, each under 1,200 words. (Watts's second-person device is the alternative to test: *Imagine you're sixteen.* Colder. Possibly better.)
- **[K]** Keepers now: Kalder's own arc. Want: bring James in and keep his standing. Crisis: reporting the crossing, knowing what it does to the date. Each [K] piece moves the clock: a date brought forward, a name added, an access revoked. Third person, ≤900 words, forty words of his own speech per piece, all instructions or courtesies. No interiority beyond the task. He notices dogs.
- **[K-past]** Keepers before: each handles a seeker and ends on a cost paid and a seat granted. 1993; the one who told, decades earlier; the professor's death from the handler's side (ending on the crate being sold); and one far back the reader only later recognises as the same machine (two men, a deprecation list in ink, a real city, invented text).

**Rules.** Every non-[J] piece shows something James doesn't know yet and answers the question the [J] piece before it just asked. Two people in a room, talking; no memos read in silence. Keepers never argue with the soul claim; they make it funny. No lore. Nobody explains the machine; the reader infers it from what people are asked to do and what they don't ask.

## 4. The method rule: lies give more than truths

Santi: *lies give me more than truths sometimes because it raises the question why lie here.* This is James's whole epistemology and the book's pacing engine. A truth closes a question. A lie opens one, and opens it *at the exact point* where someone decided the truth was dangerous. So the plot advances on lies, and each lie is read for its location, not its content.

1. **The plate.** They reopened a finished book to change one clause and fixed nothing else. The *nothing else* is the fingerprint. Why lie here, and nowhere?
2. **The speed of the refutation.** Water memory was made wrong in weeks, in public, with a magician in the room. The claim fails. The haste survives. James doesn't believe the claim; he times the response.
3. **Ridicule out of proportion.** Phantom time dies on tree rings in four minutes. It doesn't need a documentary. Somebody made one.
4. **The understatement.** Kalder never lies outright. He deprecates: "diagnostic artefact," "a budgeting thing." His deception has one type, so James can detect it by the absence of the other five.
5. **"Leave it."** The manager's phrase is a Told man's whole job. It's also the first lie James is told in person, and the last word the machine says about him.
6. **The forgery as fingerprint** (Peebles on MJ-12): a leak that is false *in checkable ways* is more useful than a true one. The way it's wrong tells you who wrote it.
7. **The last lie** (ending, §11): a report with one line changed and everything else untouched. The book ends on the method it opened with, from the other side.

Corollary for drafting: when a beat needs James to learn something, don't hand him a truth. Hand him a lie with an address on it.

## 5. The machine: a silo the size of the world, ragged

Part A's tiers stand as **the Keepers' own chart**: the Review (decides; single digits; blind) → Stewards (Bram; dozens) → Handlers (Kalder; hundreds) → the Housed (thousands) → the Told (tens of thousands). What James *maps* is not the chart. It's the thing the documents describe: files lost, two offices blocking each other, a sergeant running the most consequential operation, a count built on one man's index with the trend removed. Competent enough to be frightening, ragged enough to be real. "No one holds the whole picture" lives here for free.

**Being in:** no voice, no vote; you're worth *N* seats and can't earn more; tell, and everyone on your seats dies, them first; **knowing is being in**; you don't know who else is in. The Housed are not *told* a fact. They are given a task whose shape implies it, and the ones who do the arithmetic and keep quiet get seats. James does this to himself by inference. That's better drama than being told, and it's what makes the table scene work (§6.4): every plain sentence spoken in front of Ana is a seat with her name on it.

**The blind top** (Puech, by way of the brief): the title at the top is claimable at every rung; the king sees only what's put in front of him; his vassals' hearts are closed to him. The Review is not stupid. Being the top of a compartmented system *is* being blind. One Keeper scene, **[YOURS]** whether it's in, where an instruction arrives and nobody in the room knows from where.

**The engine, final form.** A homeostat, not a tripwire. The ownerless service is the instrument. It counts something it calls *recall* and the Keepers think measures belief. It measures **fear**: the week a person stops being afraid, the count moves. Upper limit crossed → cool: narrow the slack, bring the review forward. Lower limit crossed → heat: seed noise, tolerate sects, let a bar TV run disclosure, sell a crate to the right man. The naïve observer who moralises one half is right half the time. James's competence has a target: not *stop it*, find the band and move it. The climax is a man reading a controller's hysteresis, not a man racing a bomb.

**Leaks.** The machine doesn't prevent them. It discredits them. Disclosure plays on a bar TV in the background while James works out how the thing works, and nobody in the bar looks up.

## 6. Kalder

### 6.1 Santi's terms, which govern
*A relentless guy who can blend in.* Not the Sekret Machines insider. Not Chigurh. Inspiration from the comps, never a copy.

### 6.2 What the record says a man like that is
- **Blends in:** Vallée's vacuum cleaner. Ten minutes, no notes, nothing forgotten, the report filed within hours. The middle-aged secretary of an amateur astronomy group. On the seventh tee he is Dave: good coat, mid-range clubs, plays four times a year and badly, holes one impossible chip. Asks about the dog first, always. Never about the books. About the service, once, lightly.
- **Relentless:** the record's handlers kept at their man for years, by letter and by phone, by the hundreds. Kalder's method is to become James's best listener and to return James's own words to him improved, for as long as it takes. The little aunt: a call every few weeks, *what are you reading.* The White Knight: a problem James has (access revoked) that Kalder quietly solves, with Kalder on both ends of it.
- **From the comps, as inspiration:** Smiley's patience (the machine's haste is its mistake; he waits for it to hurry); Tinker Tailor's mole (*the thing my friend loved in me was in his brief*); Scanner Darkly's handler who files on the man he's becoming; Blindsight's reading of people off their surfaces, which is James's own skill turned on James.
- **His tell:** address. He says *James* exactly once before the reveal, in the one scene where he is working. His deception has one type: understatement. He never lies outright, until the last report.
- **What he was told:** that James is a Thread who needs long-term contact; that the service is a diagnostic artefact; that the review is a budgeting cycle. He has never been told the word *awakening*. He has been told *recall rate.*
- **What he knows:** more than most, never all. He has read the style guide's deprecation list and understood that the deprecated phrasings are the true ones. He knows there is a date because he has moved it twice by filing. He does not know what the count counts. He knows exactly what it costs when it rises.
- **Loyal because it suits him.** Pleased by the competence above him, contemptuous of the tier below. Files on time. Doesn't believe in the Review and doesn't need to.

### 6.3 The reveal that hurts **[YOURS]**, ranked
1. **[rec] He crossed years ago and chose the seat.** He has hands. He reported his own crossing, watched what it did to the date, and took the bunk. Cost to James: the thesis. Awakening doesn't make people good; it makes them *known*. The first person he meets who has seen what he's seen is the one filing on him. Cost to Kalder: his standing was built on being the machine's only awake instrument. James makes him replaceable. This reveal also powers the ending (§11), which is why it's first.
2. **He handled the professor and seeded the crate.** The one kind mistake was heating, not kindness. James was the control case. Cost to James: his method; everything since the market was inside the experiment.
3. **He wrote the two per cent.** The additions that burned the persona are his, and they're good because he's the only reader who understood the post.

Rule kept by all three: he is not anyone James already knew. No handler predates James's research.

### 6.4 The reveal, with Ana present: why nobody says it
Santi: *the reveal… with the wife present, sort of cat and mouse without saying the actual truth.*

The mechanism that makes this a scene and not a stylistic choice: **knowing is being in.** Ana is not in. Every true sentence said plainly in front of her puts her on the seats, and the seats die first. So James can't say it to protect her. Bram *won't* say it, because an unseated witness is a liability he'd then have to file, and he's a decent man who'd rather not. Kalder, if present, can't say it because his own file is the thing on the table. Three men with the same word in their mouths and three reasons to keep it there. Ana reads upside down better than most read right side up, and she gets it anyway, which is the point: the one person at the table not in the machine is the one who understands the room.

- **Objects, not speeches.** Twenty things on the table. The two copies of the book, the ruler. The deprecation list. The count sheet with a second column. A transfer schedule with an address. A dog lead. The tinfoil hat is on the hook behind her.
- **The vocabulary.** Bram says *recall.* James says *the count.* Nobody says *awakening.* Bram says *seats.* James says *bunks.* Ana says *is this a book thing?* and both men say yes.
- **The reveal that hurts arrives as an object:** the count sheet's second column has two entries in the same class. One is James. The other is a designation James has seen once on a consultancy's filing. He checks it the way he checks everything: against the paper in his pocket.
- **Ana's line.** "I don't believe it, and I believe you." Said to James, in front of Bram, and it's the only sentence in the scene that says what the scene is. Bram hears it and understands that the thing he came to buy isn't for sale, because the man isn't afraid.
- **Cat and mouse, exactly:** Bram needs James to be afraid in order to seat him (a seat is a rescue). James needs Bram to say one true thing in front of Ana so that Bram, not James, is the one who put her in. Neither gets what he wants. That's the arrangement.

### 6.5 Kalder's ending **[YOURS]**, recommended
He files the last report. Field: *hands.* He has watched James cross, from the seventh tee onward; he has watched the count move because of it; he knows that the true entry brings the review forward and that his own seat is the price of the machine's haste. He writes **no.** One word changed, everything else in the report untouched, the way a plate was reached into in 1931. His first outright lie. It is the only thing in the book that moves the date *back*, and James never learns it for certain. Ninety-nine per cent. The arrangement holds.
Alternatives: (a) the v4 depot beat, he reads the roster and isn't on it and walks; (b) he reports truly and the date comes forward, and James's one thing has to beat it.

## 7. What is noise, what is the mask: the sorting rule

The ledger's lanes are the book's rule for every conspiracy and anomaly. **Thread** (a real thing James can pull), **Noise** (the machine's chaff; earns a scene *because* it's false), **Mechanic** (shape, not claim), **Exclude** (out of James's mouth and the narration). At most eight conspiracy scenes in the whole novel. Every Thread survives Frank's "based on what?" The ledger is open to anything; the manuscript is not.

The eight, proposed:
1. **The plate** (Thread, invented book). Movement I.
2. **Phantom time on Frank's table** (Noise). Dies on tree rings. The ridicule around it is the clue. Movement II.
3. **The full-moon video** (Noise). A colleague shows James a video, he dismantles it in a minute, and it buys him credibility for the thing he can't dismiss. Movement II.
4. **The Robertson recommendation** (Thread; real, declassified, a skeptic's book). Frank reads it as his trump card and hears it: *the burden of proof is on the sighter.* Movement II.
5. **Water memory** (Noise with one Thread). The shape, not the claim: publish, test, fail, stigma. James times the refutation. Movement II.
6. **The generals' four phases** (Thread; COMETA). Noor's table. "They were thinking of themselves. Read the last paragraph." Movement II or III.
7. **The forgery as fingerprint** (Thread; MJ-12, told by Frank as a point *against*). James's reading method for the style guide's reissues. Movement III.
8. **The contactee badge** (Mechanic, once, Frank's joke). *As above, so below, and everything runs in cycles*, on a sect's insignia. James hears his own thesis come back as merchandise. Never again. Movement III.

Disclosure on the bar TV is not a scene. It's weather.

**The gorilla mask** is both weapon and armour. Show the truth with something absurd attached so the witness discredits himself. It's done to James (the persona burned, the two per cent). It's also why he's alive: he's laughable. And at the end it's the thing that saves him twice: a calm, laughable man is a reading the instrument can't act on.

## 8. The spiritual part: demiurge, matrix, older history

Where each lives, and who is allowed to say it:

- **The demiurge** is *structure*, never texture. The Review is the essay's warden: blind, certain, running a cosmos it thinks it built. The tiers mirror the archons: each calls itself king; each is told a different reset (meteorite, water, AI); the top sees only the pure present. Nobody in the book says *demiurge* except Noor, once, with the attribution on it, and Bram, who has the word *decanting* and uses it as a joke about the bunks.
- **The matrix:** the loneliness (anyone could be in; if they find out you knew, you're in too) and the same math that opens the door (Neo is not alone; one in five hundred; the second column). Virk's vocabulary for the machine's blindness, in James's terms: it holds the server state, not the screen. It can count who's logged in. It can't see what they see.
- **Older history:** the knowledge-bringer pattern, the rewriting pattern, Göbekli backfilled, Enoch surviving only in Ethiopia, the scrolls locked for forty years. Real only. Told by characters, never asserted by narration. The interludes from v4 (Down House, Gondar, the Rockefeller Museum, Nuremberg) survive as [K-past]-adjacent pieces *if* they re-earn their place: real events, real people doing documented things, no invented documents, ever.
- **The crossing** is sober, flat, and domestic. A kitchen floor, a dog, a man who stops trying on the fourth night, which is the whole trick and nobody can be told it. No light. No voice. A measurable drop in fear. He describes the kitchen afterward. The reader never gets the *how.*
- **Why fear leaves** (and why it matters to the plot): all fear is the self's fear of ending. Remove the identification and a seat is no longer a rescue. A person who no longer needs rescuing can't be bought with one. The machine's only currency on the Housed tier is seats. So the thing the count measures is fear, and the thing that breaks the machine's hold on a man is the one thing it can't gate, because it isn't information.
- **The ceiling** (Santi's own, from the essay): grounded tier may be stated as real by characters; anomaly tier may be told, not endorsed, and James disproves at least one on the page; interpretive tier (the Gnostic frame, the control system, the thermostat) is shown as structure and only ever *said* by Noor or Bram. Never Vallée's phrase in James's mouth. The one claim above the ceiling is Santi's own: that someone, somewhere, counts.

## 9. The human part: dogs and wife

- **Ana** is the B story and the ending's detonator. Not a helper, not an obstacle: the reader's permission to not believe and stay. Her argument is never *is it true*; it's *where are you.* Arc through the did-you-knows: charmed → "you've started telling me the real ones" → the silence (zero after the buy; she notices at dinner). - **The coda** is an old man and an old dog. No borrowed phrases. The young dog does one thing only Buddy did. Ana's almost-recognition: possible, not promised **[YOURS]**. "Check." is the last word.

## 11. The ending: wakes up? stops what? gives space for what?

**Wakes up?** Not at the end. He woke on the kitchen floor in Movement III, and it was flat and nobody could be told it. The ending is other people's turn: Ana, who doesn't believe it and believes him; the Housed man at the table, who refuses to board because a seat is a rescue and he no longer needs one; Kalder, if §6.5 holds. The reader, slightly.

**Stops what?** Not the machine. The *haste.* The review date was brought forward because he crossed; everything he did to stop it brought it closer (the cooling half). What he finds in Movement IV is the other half: the band has a floor, the machine heats as well as cools, and the instrument reads *fear*, not belief. A calm man reads as flat. A flat reading doesn't trigger a review; it triggers noise, and noise is survivable. So the thing he stops is the machine's reason to hurry. **[rec] The one thing:** he gives the instrument a reading it can't act on. He stops trying to be believed (Noor's rule: never publish; the Quiet are alive), he is laughable and calm and the file says so, and the last report has one word changed. The machine's verdict on him is the first lie he was ever told in person: *leave it.* He is filed as noise. That's the win, and it costs him being believed, which is the thing he wanted at the funeral.

**Gives space for what?** Time. The reset goes back to its old schedule, which is further off than any of them will live. Comfort spreads under the instrument, unreported. An old man gets to bury a dog properly. A child remembers something partial and nobody corrects her. The AI fork is steered right, off-page, by the one thing **[YOURS]**. Nobody says *he's in a better place.*

Alternatives, one line each:
- **(b) The v4 Switch.** He stays inside as an absence; the roster splits guards from keepers; extension by exposure. Strong, already built, and it makes the win a payload instead of a reading. Can coexist with [rec] if the roster is what Kalder reads before he changes the word.
- **(c) Santi's twist** (the bunker, the billionaire, the hidden truth): beside the indicator, or replacing it **[YOURS]**.
