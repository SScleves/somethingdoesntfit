## MOVEMENT I — NOTHING FITS

**Cold open** (J) ⚠ DISCUSS
We are dropped into the climax, months ahead of the story. Everything James has found is laid out on a kitchen table as objects, and a man from the organisation is making him an offer. *Santi: the objects stay; decide whether Ana is at the table or James is alone, and either way the stakes must be higher than "an offer."* Options at the bottom. What is fixed: this is not the first coded conversation. The first one is with Kalder, at the pub, where Kalder makes a slip so subtle that nobody in a thousand would catch it, James catches it, Kalder is 99% sure he did, and James plays along so perfectly that Kalder never gets the last 1%. This table is the second.
*Intent:* open with the stakes already high and the question "what is this room and how did he get here."

**The funeral** (J, Santi's)
Ana's grandmother is buried on a beautiful spring day. James, who works in IT monitoring systems and cannot stop asking "how do we know?", stands behind his grieving wife and hears everyone say "she's in a better place." When Ana needs him to confirm it, he says yes without believing it, and this small lie is the thing that starts the book. During the service he has a flash of a different place: cold mud, a hand cracked at the knuckles reaching for his. He files it as lack of sleep. On the drive home he realises nobody even pretends there is a heaven for his old dog, Buddy, and that the comforting words at the grave were never knowledge. The search begins.
*Intent:* the narrator's defect, the marriage, the dogs, the one unanswered question under the whole book.

**The good life** (J, Santi's, with one expansion)
Ordinary life after the funeral: Ana's grief that visits rather than moves in, the unfinished knitting, the two dogs. James's job: he watches other people's systems die and asks the dull questions. Frank's link about AI tools ("enjoy it while it lasts"); "based on what?" slips out. His hobby, expanded: not only old books. He checks everything, reddit, 4chan, mIRC because he's old school, discord, the deep web; one week it's aliens, the next mole people, the next why rich men in robes worship Baal. It's messy, he doesn't know what he's looking for, and it has always been there, a calling with no object. The Record (sentences that changed between printings) is the one branch he'd defend in court. Golf is his sanctuary. Fourteen tabs at 2 a.m. about dying; he bookmarks them.
*Intent:* warmth before it breaks; the tools he'll use later are introduced as habits; the hobby is a hunger, not a method.

**Reading with a pen** (J, Santi's, with one expansion)
James reads seriously about death for the first time: hospital accounts, near-death reports, and the explanations that work harder than the mysteries. Ana finds his notebook and assumes it's about her grandmother; he lets her. He types a forum question about NDEs and deletes it. New theme: the loneliness of a man who thinks things all day he can't say. Does everyone think about aliens and just never say it? Football is tiring: five teams can win, the rest is influencers passing a ball to the centre. Politics: more of the same, and he remembers a joke he can't attribute, about the puppet on the left and the puppet on the right and the one man holding both (paraphrased, made funnier, his own). Then he feels like Zoolander asking "who am I?" and laughs at himself. His conclusion, which is the book: people think the same things; they either forgot to ask because of life, or they still ask and are embarrassed. Somebody taught us to forget and not to ask. Buddy stops walking the front nine. At the market Beard Man has a dead professor's crates and **won't split them: it's the whole lot or nothing.** James takes the lot, two bags, forty-odd books, including a thin one with no title, and shelves them.
*Intent:* plant the crate, the thin book, the fear of being seen believing, and the loneliness that the whole book answers.

**The sentence** (J, Santi's, reworked ending)
Going through the lot, James finds a copy of a dry 1926 book on flood legends he already owns, and this one is a first impression. He's thrilled, then scandalised: the professor has annotated a first edition, bent pages, written *no* in the margins. Checking the professor's abuse against his own copy, he finds the sentence on page 118 is different, and then two more, further in: three edits in one book, all of the same kind (someone *gave* it → it *arose*; *remembered* → *imagined*; *taught* → *developed*). Someone reopened the plates, changed three clauses, and left the typos and a blemish so nobody would check. On the kitchen floor at 4 a.m., playing fetch, he understands what the edits do: the original has a hand in it, the new one appears by itself. The author vanished in Turkey in 1929; the edits are from 1931. He writes *doesn't fit* in the Record and asks: if it was worth doing here, where else?
*Intent:* the central image (the hand edited out), the method (check, don't believe), and a pattern inside one book before any list exists.

**Down House, Kent. January 1860** (L)
True story told as a scene: Darwin, sick at his desk, correcting the second edition of *On the Origin of Species*, adds "by the Creator" to the last sentence under public pressure, and writes to a friend years later that he regrets having "truckled to public opinion." The world's most famous book changed between printings, quietly, under pressure, by the author's own hand. Ends with where the letter is today.
*Intent:* the first lore breath; what James found is a known kind of thing, which makes it worse, not better.

**A round with a stranger** (J, new)
A Wednesday round alone with the dogs. Introspective: why golf and never team sports (cause and effect are honest, nobody spins the ball), what dogs are to him, how he thinks, the relief of four hours where the mind shuts up. On the fourth tee the starter pairs him with a man playing alone. They connect over dozens of small things: the man asks about Buddy, knows what a foreman is, laughs at himself, has one good did-you-know, notices Mora's ritual with the duck poop. It's a good morning. James drives home thinking maybe he doesn't have to be alone, that a stranger can be fun without becoming a friend. We will learn, much later, that this was Kalder taking a first measure.
*Intent:* plant Kalder as a pleasant blur, so that when he returns it isn't obvious he matters.

**Utrecht. A phone call. 1993** (K-past)
A physics department head, Koster, rings a pleasant man at a foundation to report his friend Verhoef's unpublished paper (two people in shielded rooms responding to each other). We learn in a line how Koster was approached: a lunch, a second lunch, a question about his children's schooling, and a sentence about colleagues who had declined the lunch and whose departments were now smaller. The foundation man calls the paper "recall," tells Koster to reclassify it as "instrumentation" so the right people never read it, and confirms his seats (wife, children; not his mother: "the allocation is the allocation"). Koster goes and is kind to his friend for two hours. Closing lines: the department was restructured the next year; Verhoef was retired early, kept his copy, and died in 1995 in a car on a canal road.
*Intent:* how the organisation works: decent people, a phone call, a budget. And what happens to the ones who refuse the lunch.

**Leave it** (J)
At work James reads the full list of scheduled jobs and finds one with no owner, no ticket, no history, `rcl-agg`, 02:14, nine seconds, forty kilobytes to an address that exists only for those nine seconds. He raises it in stand-up. Pieter is **surprised**. It takes him a visible moment; then he knows what to say: "diagnostic artefact from the migration, leave it." James did the migration; it wasn't there. What James reads is the moment before the sentence, because he's always read people's out-of-placeness, the way he avoids sad films and detects any feeling that's wrong in a room and steers away from it, which is also why he doesn't eat animals; said lightly, never explained. A letter arrives from an antiquarian dealer: "Dear Mr Klippe… be plain about one's reasons." He opens a low-priority ticket.
*Intent:* the second thread (the job); the first lie told to his face, and James's reading of the pause before it.

**The control case** (J)
He tries to kill his own finding. He buys another copy of the 1931 printing (same three edits: the whole run), which is expensive, and lies to Ana about what it cost, and feels bad about it. He checks nine other books from the lot against other copies, page by page, three weekends: nothing, the control. But something still itches: the professor's shelf has a pattern of *subject*, not of edits. Why is *VALIS* there, a man trying to make sense of something that happened to him? Why *Blindsight*, read half to death? These were a man's conclusions, not his taste. The professor had asked to see something and been given an appointment. **The date is still to come:** the second of December. He died in March. James has an appointment he didn't make.
*Intent:* James is not the first; the list exists; a date in the future with his name not yet on it.

## MOVEMENT II — THE MACHINE

**Deventer. A Thursday in March** (V / K-past)
The professor had written to the foundation asking someone to come about his notebook, breaking his own rule. Somebody came. Tea, the blue chair. The professor says it plainly: forty-one books, always the same edit, a hand taken out; he wanted, before he died, to have asked. The visitor says nothing, makes him comfortable, photographs every page while he sleeps, reports from the car: "I'd rather wait. A body's a fingerprint." Eleven days later the bin isn't put out. In June, the furniture; the books have already gone to the market in two crates, whole lot: the one kind mistake.
*Intent:* who answers *Ask*; the crate reaching James was an accident now being corrected.

**Gondar, Ethiopia. 1770** (L)
True story: the Scottish traveller James Bruce is given the Book of Enoch at the Ethiopian court, a text lost to Europe for a thousand years in which heavenly beings *teach* humans metalwork and astronomy and are punished for it. He carries it home and is mocked as a liar in London for the rest of his life. Ends with where his manuscripts are.
*Intent:* a real case of the list's pattern: knowledge given, and the messenger laughed at.

**Frank's kitchen** (J)
Frank invites James round for a friendly, teasing round of debunking. Flat earth (killed with a ship's mast and a plane ticket). A full-moon earthquake video (no baseline, cherry-picked dates: a sock drawer). Phantom time (killed on tree rings in four minutes), and James's real point: a theory that dies on tree rings doesn't need a documentary, and ridicule aimed at bad theories trains people to dismiss good ones. Frank's two-column list. His trump card: the real 1953 panel that recommended debunking through mass media; James reads what it implies, a standard of proof taught until nobody remembered it was taught. "The memo's the table." On the stairs Frank half-remembers the orphan job for one second. **Frank is in it.** We don't know yet. The evening was Frank checking whether James is a man who swallows everything. He isn't. That's the report Frank files.
*Intent:* the book's rule for conspiracies, shown; and a test James passes without knowing he's being tested.

**The math** (J)
James goes to Els at the curtain shop. She refuses: everyone who comes about her father is polite and she's done. On his way out he says one thing (placeholder: a line about her father checking the way people check, or about the bin) that stays with her. He has lied to Ana about where he was. A week later Els rings: "Why did you say that? My father said that." She gives him the folder from under the lining paper of her mother's dresser, *Preferred Formulations, fourth revision*: deprecated/preferred columns (*was given to → arose among; taught by → developed by; remembered → imagined*), the dates page, and *When a reader notices* ("contradiction confirms; agreement, then reclassification"). She says she never gave it to the police or to any of the people who came afterward; it felt personal, it made no sense, and it felt important. James does the arithmetic (one in five hundred, asked to do one small thing once) and withdraws from everyone. Ana catches the join at breakfast; "reading," he says; a day's silence.
*Intent:* the organisation's method in writing, earned from Els by one true sentence; the handler's name is not yet attached to anything.

**The corporate day** (J, replaces the recruiter and the seventh tee)

**Leiden** (J)
A stranger messages James: the polite correction on his post used a phrase her old supervisor showed her on a photocopy in 2009. Noor, the professor's last student, now a paper conservator. Her kitchen. The style guide is a template. Entry 26 is Verhoef's paper (*shared → coincident*, the only entry about the present). Why she is alive: the professor told her, while they worked, long ago: "I'm fairly sure I'm already being watched. If you believe this and want to keep working on it, fine. But until we're a hundred per cent: never publish, never ask, always hide. And if anything happens to me, know that I was right, and that they're onto me, and be careful." She kept the rule. He broke it, at the end, with one word. The 1999 French report's last chapter (half a page). On the step: "You're counting the wrong column. And paper doesn't pay for itself: find the money." The shelfmark in the thin book is the depot's format; the appointment is real and it's in December.
*Intent:* James is not alone; the rule; the pointer to the money and to the date.

**Fullerton, California. February 1974** (L, replaces the Hills)
True story: Philip K. Dick, after dental surgery, answers the door to a delivery girl wearing a fish pendant, and over the following weeks has the experiences he spent the rest of his life trying to explain: the pink light, the knowledge that his son had an undiagnosed hernia (which doctors then confirmed), the sense of a second mind, the eight thousand pages of notes he called the Exegesis, and the novel, *VALIS*, in which he split himself in two to describe it. Told straight, documented facts only, no verdict.
*Intent:* the book on the professor's shelf, explained by its author's own life; a real man trying to make sense of what happened to him and being filed under his reputation.

**The money** (J) ⚠ DISCUSS (this replaces "Meerwijk" if we cut the new job; see options)
Recommended version: James stays at his own company. Noor said "find the money," so he finds it where his monitoring role can see it: his own company's finance system, a monthly payment for years to a foundation at a post box, described as *continuity reconciliation (recall index)*, billed **per flagged subject** in the company's population. The sum has stepped up in clean multiples, the last step the week he opened his ticket. He is a line in a budget. Then the other half, in public: Dutch foundations must publish annual accounts, and this one does, dully: grants to heritage projects, and, in certain years, a documentary company, a publisher, a retreat centre, a festival, a library's "digitisation."
*Intent:* the money is found without changing jobs, and the heating half is on public paper.

**Enjoy it while it lasts** (J)
The AI monitoring tools Frank joked about arrive, installed by a consultancy. James reads all four hundred pages of defaults and finds on page 212 that the tool is told never to report jobs exactly like the one he found: "noise." *Rev. 4*, review date end of quarter. He asks for the payment's history and is refused; his own access is narrowed by exactly the one category he needs. At the next round Kalder says "ask the person who'd be embarrassed, the name on the invoice." He emails; "Julian K." fixes it in eleven minutes, signing off with the same phrase. James notices and files it.
*Intent:* the blind spot is built into the tools everyone buys; the handler helps him closer.

**A room with a long table** (K)
Bram, the one who talks, and the handler. The weekly sheet for James: a left column, *reporting*, produced by the job; a right column, *hands*, produced by no machine, filled by handlers who have themselves crossed, because only a person who has had the thing can see it in someone else. The index counts people whose fear of dying is leaving them. The frightened can be seated; the unafraid cannot be housed, bought or ridiculed; the review exists for populations where too many appear. "When he gets to the floor, I'd like to know the week." "The wife will find the schedule. Let her."
*Intent:* what the count counts, and that the machine's only instrument for the thing it fears is people like Kalder.

**Leiden, nine years ago** (V)

## MOVEMENT III — THE SHIFT

**The gorilla mask** (J)
Ana insists on a barbecue so she can meet "golf man." Frank tells the 1943 gorilla-mask story. **On land** (J) — the first coded conversation
After eighteen holes, the pub, Ana present. Kalder makes the slip: "that professor's place near Deventer," a town James has never said or written anywhere. One hard knock of the heart. James tests him with a wrong town; Kalder accepts the correction; nothing leaves his face. Kalder is 99% sure James caught it and can't get the last 1%, because nobody ever catches it. James plays it perfectly: defends himself, gives nothing, keeps Ana out of it ("it's a book thing"). Kalder makes a threat as a joke about how fast nice houses burn, "good street, dogs," and leaves: "Good game, Jim. Let's not play another." James is sad, not frightened. In the car the tinfoil joke doesn't land; the jokes stop.
*Intent:* the handler reveals himself by one word; James outplays him and loses the friend; the marriage loses its joke.

**The schedule** (J)

**A corridor. He is sixteen** (J-past)

**The floor** (J)

## MOVEMENT IV — THE INSTRUMENT

**A river town, before paper** (L, optional)

**Leiden. A printer's shop. 1931** (K-past)

**The appointment** (J, the depot)

A legitimate building with a car park and a sign. A tray: the foundation's own first revision from 1931, handwritten, in the professor's five-field format; entry one is page 118. ## ⚠ DISCUSS — the open points, with options

**1. The cold open: Ana there or not, and higher stakes.** (a) Ana present, and on the table beside the objects is the schedule with their address and *tomorrow's* date, and Buddy was on the vet's floor two nights ago; the offer has a clock. (b) James alone with Bram, Ana already gone with the dogs to a place James doesn't know, and the offer is the only way to find out where; the reader hears a dog bark once from upstairs and James doesn't react. (c) The cold open is the pub slip itself: the one word, the heart's one knock, and we cut before James answers. My pick: (a). It keeps the objects, keeps the second coded conversation, and gives it a clock.

**2. What the forty kilobytes are.** (a) A list of employee IDs: who in the company has, this week, *reported* (a ticket, a post from a work machine, a search). Dull, checkable, chilling when his own ID appears. (b) Not IDs: a daily digest in plain text, the company's own HR and helpdesk systems mined for sentences, and James reads his own stand-up question quoted back in it. (c) Forty kilobytes of nothing: an empty heartbeat that says "still watching," and the content arrives elsewhere; James realises the job isn't the sender, it's the *receipt*. My pick: (a), with one line of (b): the list carries, next to each ID, a two-word reason, and his says *asked (job)*.

**3. Pieter and Frank: forgot, or lie well?** Your note makes Frank *in it*. Then both lie, and the book owes the reader a later answer to *how ordinary people lie that well*. Proposal: each tier is told a different reason, and the reason is believable. Pieter was told it's a security audit and the job is a honeypot, so a man asking about it is a man to be watched, and forgetting is procedure. Frank was told it's a vendor pilot and that James is on a watch list for leaking, and the evening was him checking whether James is reckless. Neither knows what the job counts. Frank's status surfaces late (the third column at the end becomes the moment James understands), or at the barbecue when Frank and Kalder know each other's names and shouldn't. Pick the moment.

**4. The car park: reveal the handler early, or not.** (a) Keep it but strip the voice to nothing identifying, so the reader knows *a* handler exists and the date moved, and does not know it's the golfer. (b) Cut it; the reader learns the golfer is the handler at the long table, after the corporate day. (c) Keep it as is: the reader knows from here, Hitchcock, and every golf scene after it is a bomb under the table. My pick: (a) now, (c)'s effect delivered by "Leiden, nine years ago."

**5. Meerwijk: cut or justify.** Your own alternative is the strong one: they let him in because he has a talent, as a test. But it costs a new building, a new boss, and a reason to leave a job he likes, and the critics already said Movement II is where the book stops moving. My recommendation: cut the new job. Everything Meerwijk gave us is findable where he is: the payment through his own company's finance system (his monitoring role can see invoices), the grants from the foundation's public annual accounts, the field schedule from the same records, page 212 from the tools his own company buys. The "test" idea survives in a smaller form: Pieter moves him onto the company's "continuity" project, which is the machine letting him explore, and which is what the offer at the end is the conclusion of.

**6. Replacing the Hills.** PKD's 1974 experience is in the file. The gorilla mask could be its own interlude instead (Muroc, 1943, told straight) and Frank's barbecue story becomes a callback. Either, or both.

**After "Meerwijk", does the rest hold?** Yes, with four consequences, all applied above: (i) every piece that lived in Meerwijk's building now lives in his own company and the foundation's public accounts; (ii) Frank being in it changes the barbecue, the third column and the coda's page 212, and needs one reveal moment; (iii) Kalder as a good golfer met twice before the pub changes nothing after the pub; (iv) the appointment being in the future turns the depot piece from "he asks" into "he keeps a dead man's date," which is better.
