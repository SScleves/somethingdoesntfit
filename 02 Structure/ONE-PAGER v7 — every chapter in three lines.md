## MOVEMENT I — NOTHING FITS

**2 · The good life** (J, Santi's) — Ana, the two dogs, the house, the Record (retractions nobody admitted to). Frank's AI link with a skull; "Based on what?" slips out. Golf as sanctuary. Fourteen tabs at 2 a.m., all about dying; he bookmarks them.

**4 · The sentence** (J, Santi's) — Page 118 of a 1926 book says something different in the second impression, typos and blemish left in so nobody would check. A fetch game at 4 a.m.: *transmitted* has a hand in it, *arisen* doesn't. The author vanished in 1929; the edit is 1931. "If it was worth doing here, where else?"

**L1 · Down House, 1860** — A sick man at a desk adds *by the Creator* to the last sentence of the most famous book alive, and regrets it in a letter for the rest of his life. Books change between printings; the famous one did it first.

**5 · Utrecht. A phone call. 1993** (K-past) — A department head reports his friend's paper (two shielded rooms, something moved) to a pleasant man at a foundation. "Recall." Reclassify it as instrumentation. His seats confirmed; not his mother. "It's a budgeting thing, Wim."

**6 · Leave it** (J) — On his dashboard a job with no owner runs at 02:14, nine seconds, forty kilobytes, to an address that exists for nine seconds. His manager has the sentence ready: "diagnostic artefact from the migration. Leave it." James did the migration. A dealer's letter: *Dear Mr Klippe… be plain about one's reasons.* He opens a ticket.

**7 · The payload** (J) — He captures the forty kilobytes: a list of 112 staff IDs, no names. The night after he asks in standup, it's 113. The new one is his. Asking about the job put him on it.

**8 · The control case** (J) — He tries to kill the find: a third copy (it's the run), nine other books checked page by page (nothing). The thin book opened: two hands, forty-one edits, entry 11 is his sentence in a dead man's grandfather's ink. Last page: a shelfmark, a date three weeks after the professor's funeral, and *Ask*.

**9 · The week it vanished** (J) — Beard Man: the blue chair, the bin, "a man in a van, good coat, didn't haggle." The ticket closed as a duplicate of nothing; the job gone from every backup; Frank and Pieter don't remember. Four people hold the backup keys. One is Pieter. He says nothing.

**V1 · A pension visit. Zwolle** (V) — A retired man who'd "started saying strange things about his father." Kalder spends an afternoon with him, learns he loves the old car and the canal road, borrows it to move a box, returns it washed. Three days later the man drives into the canal.

**10 · Groningen** (J) — The dealer, in person: "Be plain. Who sent you?" He was visited in 2011 and told to telephone if anyone asked for that title. He has telephoned, about James, three weeks ago. "Everyone who reads that book ends up in my shop."

## MOVEMENT II — THE MACHINE

**14 · The seventh tee** (J) — A man on the sixth green waves him through. "How old?" to the cart, before the handshake. A chip-in from the fringe: "I'm never going to be able to explain that to anyone." Coffee in the shed; "I'm a flask man." James drives home in a good mood and doesn't ask why.

**15 · A car park. Thursday** (K) — Kalder reports: "he's stopped asking." The voice: "out loud. That's worse." The review moves from end of year to end of quarter because of a verb. "Near enough to know what he loves." He'd bought a flask so the sentence would have been true.

**16 · Leiden** (J) — A private message: *whoever corrected you used a phrase my supervisor showed me in 2009.* Noor, Maasen's last student, who repairs paper and has never published. **L3 · Portsmouth, New Hampshire, 1961** — Betty and Barney Hill drive home from Montreal with two hours missing. The dress, the dreams, the doctor, the star map, and the way the country told the story back to them until they were a joke. No verdict. The gorilla mask on two real people.

**17 · Meerwijk** (J) — A finance house that has never thrown anything away. A ledger line, thirty-one years: *continuity reconciliation (recall index)*, paid to a post box per flagged subject. The same job, same minute. He captures it: 2,300 of the house's own people. The sum has stepped up four times; the fifth was the week he asked.

**18 · Enjoy it while it lasts** (J) — Frank's AI tools arrive; page 212 of the defaults tells the watcher never to see jobs like that one. *Rev. 4*, review date end of quarter. **19 · A room with a long table** (K) — Bram and Kalder. The weekly: left column *reporting*, which the job counts; right column *hands*, which only a handler who has crossed can read. The index counts fear leaving. "When he gets to the floor, I'd like to know the week." "The wife's going to find the schedule. Let her."

**V3 · Leiden, nine years ago** (V) — Kalder with a clipboard in a restructured library. Noor makes him tea and says nothing for twenty minutes and it goes through him. He drives to Zwolle, files his own crossing, watches the date move, and asks for more names. The seat is a promotion he wanted.
