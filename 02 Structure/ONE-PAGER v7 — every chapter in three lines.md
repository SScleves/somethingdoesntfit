## MOVEMENT I — NOTHING FITS

**Cold open.** James alone, driving to keep an appointment a dead man made, the second of December. A building behind a car park with a sign. The woman at the desk says they were expecting him, not today, but expecting him. A tray already on the reading-room table, a handwritten ledger from 1931 already open. He sits. Cut to eight months earlier.
*Sets up:* a date to dread, and the question of who expected him and why he's alone.

**The funeral** (Santi's). Ana's grandmother is buried. Everyone says she's in a better place; James says yes without believing it. A flash: cold mud, a hand cracked at the knuckles. On the drive home: nobody even pretends there's a heaven for a dog. The search begins.

**The good life** (Santi's). Ana, the dogs, the house. James's job: he watches other people's systems die. His hobby: a nose for things that don't fit, always onto something, messy, a calling. Old books that change between printings are the branch he checks carefully. Golf. Fourteen tabs about dying. One new line: the systems he watches belong to a financial house that has kept records for four hundred years.

**Reading with a pen** (Santi's). He reads seriously about death. Ana finds his notebook. He deletes a forum question. The loneliness: he thinks all day the things nobody says and suspects everyone does. Beard Man sells a dead professor's crates whole or not at all; James takes the lot, including a thin book with no title.

**The sentence** (Santi's). A duplicate of a book he owns, a first impression, annotated by the professor. Three sentences in it are different from his copy, all the same kind of change: something that was *given* now *arose*. Typos left in so nobody would check. The author vanished in 1929; the edits are from 1931. "Where else?"

**Down House, 1860** (real). Darwin adds three words to his second edition under pressure and regrets it for life.

**A round with a stranger.** A Wednesday alone with the dogs, then paired with a man playing alone: handsome, easy, a great swing, and he likes James. He says he's in business continuity, "insurance for things that can't be insured." James drives home giddy. The cool guy likes me. We learn much later this was Kalder taking a first measure.

**Utrecht, 1993.** A department head reports his friend's paper to a foundation man, is told to file it under "instrumentation," and gets places of safety for his family in return. His friend dies two years later in a car on a canal road.

**Leave it.** James finds a job on the company's systems with no owner that runs every night at 02:14 and sends forty kilobytes to an address that exists for nine seconds. His manager Pieter is caught off guard for a moment, then says "diagnostic artefact, leave it." James did that migration; it wasn't there. A dealer's letter: "be plain about one's reasons." He opens a ticket. Three weeks later Pieter moves him to the continuity programme: "they want the person who reads things."

**The payload.** He captures the forty kilobytes: a list of employee IDs with a reason beside each. The night after he asks about the job in stand-up, his own ID is on it: *asked (job)*. PLACEHOLDER for the exact form.

**The control case.** He tries to kill his find: a third copy (same edits, so the whole print run), nine other books checked (nothing), the professor's shelf as a pattern of subject. Then the thin book: a notebook in two hands, forty-one edits in forty-one books, his sentence is number eleven. The last page: a library reference number, the second of December, and the word *Ask*. The professor died in March with that appointment in his diary.

**The week it vanished.** The ticket is closed as a duplicate of nothing; the job disappears from every backup; Frank honestly doesn't remember; Pieter dishonestly doesn't. Four people hold the backup keys; one is Pieter.

**A pension visit, Zwolle** (villain alone). A man visits a retired engineer who's been talking about his father, borrows his car, returns it washed. Three days later the canal.

**Groningen.** The dealer, in person, thought James was from the organisation. He was visited in 2011 and told to telephone if anyone asked for that title. He telephoned about James before James opened the ticket. James tells him what the call does; he says he won't make another.

## MOVEMENT II — THE MACHINE

**Deventer, March** (villain alone). The professor asked for a visit, breaking his own rule. The visitor photographs the notebook while he sleeps and waits. Eleven days later the bin isn't put out. The books have already gone to the market: the one mistake.

**Gondar, 1770** (real). Bruce brings the Book of Enoch out of Ethiopia and is called a liar for life.

**Frank's kitchen.** Friendly debunking: flat earth, moon earthquakes, phantom time. James: ridicule aimed at bad theories trains people to dismiss good ones. The real 1953 panel text; "the memo's the table." On the stairs Frank half-remembers the orphan job.

**The math.** Els refuses him; one thing he says stays with her; she calls him back. The style guide from her mother's dresser: deprecated/preferred, and "when a reader notices: agree, then reclassify." The arithmetic: one in five hundred. He withdraws; Ana catches the join. **The corporate day.** The company's golf day. The stranger from spring is there with a vendor's lanyard: he sells the house its recovery seats. He gets them paired and hits a 320-yard draw.

**A car park** (the organisation). A handler reports "stopped asking"; "out loud, that's worse"; the review date moves closer. Nothing identifies the golfer.

**Leiden.** Noor, the professor's student. The style guide is a template. Entry 26 is the 1993 paper. The professor's rule in his words: never publish, never ask, always hide. The French generals' last chapter. "You're counting the wrong column. Find the money." The reference number is the depot's; the appointment is real.

**Fullerton, 1974** (real). Philip K. Dick's experience, the one he wrote *VALIS* to explain.

**The drill.** First week on continuity: a business-continuity test at the recovery site. A desk with his name, number forty; Pieter is twelve. Upstairs, behind a glass door, a foundation's archive. Kalder in the car park, waving.

**The money.** The programme's invoices: a monthly line to the foundation, "recall index," billed per flagged person, stepping up; a seat contract through Kalder's consultancy; and the annex: the seat list. Pieter, the head of risk, a brewery executive. Not Frank, not him. "Companion animals are not accommodated."

**Enjoy it while it lasts.** The AI monitoring tools arrive; page 212 tells them never to see jobs like his. His access shrinks by exactly that shape. Kalder: "ask the person who'd be embarrassed." Julian K. fixes it in eleven minutes with the same phrase.

**A room with a long table** (the organisation). Bram explains to the handler what the count counts: the job lists who reports; handlers who've had the experience judge who has stopped being afraid. "The wife will find the schedule. Let her."

**Leiden, nine years ago** (villain alone). Kalder crosses in Noor's kitchen, files it, and takes his seat as a promotion.

## MOVEMENT III — THE SHIFT

**On land** (first coded scene, Ana present). The pub. Kalder says a town he can't know. James catches it instantly, shows nothing, answers with a wrong town. Kalder can't tell if he's been caught. No threats. The evening goes on; both men play along; Kalder tests him lightly; James passes without knowing if he's passing. He now knows what it costs if he's right. The friendship continues.

**The gorilla mask.** The barbecue. Frank's story of the test pilot in a gorilla mask. James's forum account has been edited; his correction reaches eleven people. Kalder is wonderful with everyone. "Keep him." Buddy won't go near him. That night James starts building something at 4 a.m. and doesn't describe it.

**The schedule.** The foundation's field schedule, with their address and the word "companion." Ana reads it. "It's the dog." She leaves the address, not him, and takes Mora.

**Jerusalem, 1991** (real). "Conservation" as a lock, broken by a copy.

**A street, night** (villain alone). Kalder at the back gate. The bowl. "The old dog is not a problem."

**The vet.** Buddy at two in the morning. "Not tonight." Noor rings: the man in the barbecue photo is Kalder. James finally connects the paper to a face. Noor refuses to be a witness and gives him the reference number instead. Then she goes quiet.

**Leiden, Friday** (villain alone). Kalder makes Noor quiet in one afternoon.

**A corridor, sixteen** (James's past). His father dies; "leave it for now"; "he's in a better place"; the coin in the machine.

**The night he stops being afraid.** Four nights on the kitchen floor. On the fourth, the fear he's carried since the corridor is gone. The kitchen is exactly the kitchen. "Buddy, I think I've got a soul." The forty-one edits are one edit. He rings Ana: "I'm okay."

**Nuremberg, 1561** (real). A printer cuts a woodcut of shapes he has no words for.

**The long table, again** (the organisation). The handler reports it. "James has hands." The review brought to two weeks. "The dog." "Yes." Bram will make the offer himself.

## MOVEMENT IV — THE INSTRUMENT

**Twenty minutes.** James puts everything on Ana's mother's tablecloth and explains it in order. She doesn't believe it and she believes him. They go home; if anyone comes, she'll open the door.

**The other ten.** The other addresses on the schedule: three resolved, four quiet. The widow in Zwolle: a man came about the pension and brought a flask.

**The band.** The foundation's public accounts: every grant to nonsense fell in a year when too few people were asking.

**Leiden, 1931** (the organisation, past). A printer resets three lines and leaves the typos.

**The appointment** (the cold open continued). The second of December, alone. The foundation's own 1931 ledger of edits. He reads the deleted words on their own and understands they're a method: the thing taken out of every book is how to do it. PLACEHOLDER for the content. Bram comes in and makes the offer plainly: five seats, no dogs. James says no. He passes the door to the lower floor and doesn't open it.

**The dinner** (second coded scene, Ana present). Ana opens the door to Kalder. James tells him, in his own job's words, that he caught the slip at the pub and has been addressing the doubt ever since. Kalder, to Ana: "I hope Buddy's okay now," which James never told him. James, to Ana: "Have you ever seen *Burn Notice*? I thought when a spy gets found out he just gets fired. No. His own side burns him." Kalder understands: if James goes silent, he's the one who's burned.

**The depot, Monday** (villain alone). Kalder changes one word beside James's name, to save himself.

**The turning.** James goes to the people the house's part of the machine can't run without and gives each one document and one question. Pieter and the seat list. The head of risk who signed the contract. The woman at the depot desk. The brewery man with five seats. Each stops doing their one small thing. The house stalls; re-accommodating it takes a generation.

**Leave it.** The date passes. The committee's answer is "leave it," and now it means leave James. The payment steps down. He files one dull ticket against page 212. He shows Frank the money and Frank doesn't laugh. A repaired page from Noor. A full flask on the bench; Buddy sits beside it; James drinks it and never forgives him.

**A market, Sunday** (villain alone). The next name.

**Coda.** Four years on. Buddy dies at home. They bury him in the garden, and this time James says "he's in a better place; I'll see him soon," and knows what he means by it. Page 212 is gone from the new tools. The new dog sits at the bottom of the stairs the way only Buddy did.
