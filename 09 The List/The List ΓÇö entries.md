# The List (the professor's Record) — working entries

> The thin untitled book. ~40 entries in the professor's hand. Format per entry: **source · page · printing A → printing B · before → after.** No thesis. No commentary — except occasional marginal notes (a word, a place, a year). Tresidder is #11. The last entry is unfinished.
>
> **Design rule:** every entry has up to three layers — (1) the edit, (2) the fact behind it (may be a real anomaly), (3) the person who noticed first and what it cost. Persons and edits are ALWAYS fictional. Anomalies may be real.
>
> **Method rule (the Keepers'):** correct → ridicule → buy → vanish. Killing is the failure mode. The list is a map of which method was used on whom.

---

## Martyr taxonomy (what the list teaches, in aggregate)
- **The Vanished** — walked into a landscape, never out. (Tresidder.)
- **The Recanter** — alive when the "second impression" was printed; signed his own correction. Broken, not dead.
- **The Ridiculed** — published once, laughed out of the field, died a schoolteacher. The mask.
- **The Bought** — noticed, then joined. Second-tier Keeper by birth of convenience. (Foreshadows the two tiers.)
- **The Quiet** — noticed and *stopped*. Never published. Lived. (The narrator's mirror and warning — and, late, a hint: the ones who went "within" instead of "through us" were never on the list at all.)
- **The Professor** — noticed everything, wrote the list, died before the argument. Method used: still unknown at ch. 13.

## Entries (sample of ~40)

**#3 — cheap entry (ch. 7 verification).** A 1970s popular paperback on ancient engineering. p. 44. 1st printing: *"techniques we have not yet reconstructed."* 4th printing: *"techniques that were doubtless simpler than they appear."* Two paperbacks, one afternoon, both on eBay for pocket change. *Layer 3:* none. This one exists to prove the pattern isn't Tresidder alone. Direction: uncertainty → dismissal.

**#7 — the Sphinx (texture entry).** A 1979 geology paper reprinted in a 1986 anthology. Water-erosion dating of an enclosure wall. Original: *"rainfall regimes not present in the region since c. 7000 BCE."* Anthology: *"weathering processes not fully characterized."* *Layer 3 — the Ridiculed:* fictional geologist **Maarten Kessel**, tenure denied 1981, taught secondary school until 2004, died in a town nobody visits. Marginal note in the professor's hand: *"grand-daughter — Leiden?"* (Ch. 15/23 candidate.)

**#11 — Tresidder.** *Waters Before Memory*, 1926. p. 118. 1st impr.: *transmitted to the early.* 2nd impr. (1931): *arisen among the first.* *Layer 3 — the Vanished:* last seen leaving Urfa on foot, 1929, heading east. Marginal note: *"see 17. see 38."*

**#14 — the calendar book (ch. 3 crate).** *Reckonings*, a survey of calendar systems, 1934. p. 201. A precession figure — the ~26,000-year cycle — attributed in the 1st edition to *"knowledge evidently older than the Greek astronomers who recorded it."* 2nd edition (1949): *"first correctly described by Hipparchus."* Direction: inheritance → invention. *Layer 3:* the author lived to 1962 and never mentioned the change. **The Quiet?** Or **the Bought?** Undetermined — the ambiguity is the point.

**#17 — Withdrawn for Conservation (ch. 12 set-piece).** *Highlands of Anatolia*, 1963. p. 212. 1st impr.: *"eleven thousand years."* 1971 reprint: *"a much earlier period."* Three known copies of the 1963 printing; the only public one in a national library, *withdrawn for conservation, 1994.* Marginal note: *"1994 — the hill."* *Layer 3 — the Recanter:* fictional surveyor **Ilse Brandt-Ostrander**, who signed the 1971 revision and lived until 2011. Her correspondence (ch. 16) contains one sentence: *"I was shown what would happen to the department."*

**#22 — the plasma petroglyphs.** A 1990s conference proceedings volume; a paper matching rock art on four continents to laboratory plasma discharge forms. Present in the proceedings' first print run; absent from the library-bound second run — pagination closed up as if it were never there. Direction: presence → absence. *Layer 3 — the Bought:* fictional physicist who presented it, now on the board of a foundation that funds "heritage stabilization." (Ch. 17 — the rope's other end.)

**#26 — the neurophysiology entry (INNER).** A journal article, 1993: correlated activity in isolated subjects. Withdrawn "at the author's request" 1995. The author could not have requested it. *Layer 3 — the Vanished (second instance).* Marginal note: *"same method as 11. sixty-four years apart."* (Reader shouldn't clock that this is an INNER-track entry until ch. 25, when the list re-reads itself.)

**#31 — the P-59.** Not a book. A 1943 flight log. The original entry describes an aircraft "without visible means of propulsion." The typed transcript, filed 1944, adds one word: *"reportedly."* And a second sheet: a memo instructing that further sightings be "handled with humor." The gorilla mask, in its own handwriting. *Layer 3 — the Ridiculed, plural:* three pilots. (Ch. 21.)

**#34 — the textbook.** A secondary-school history text, 1972 → 1988 editions. The words *"the first civilizations"* appear 0 times in 1972 and 14 times in 1988. Nothing false was added. A word was *installed.* Direction: description → doctrine. *Layer 3:* none — this is the aggregate made visible in a single title. (Ch. 15, "The Grammar.")

**#38 — the physics entry (INNER).** A 1981 popular book on quantum measurement. p. 160. 1st: *"the question of what constitutes an observer remains open."* Reprint: *"the question is now generally regarded as settled."* Marginal note: *"see 11."* (Same instruction-edit as Tresidder: not a fact, a *rule for looking.*) Ch. 25 re-read.

**#40 — the last entry. Unfinished.** No source. No page. A set of coordinates in the professor's hand. A date, circled — three weeks after his death. And one word, in different ink, added later, shakier: *"Ask."* (Ch. 16; resolves ch. 27.)

## What the narrator can and can't do with the list (Competence Ledger check)
- **Can:** verify entries by finding two copies and reading the middle; trace people through obituaries, catalogues, daughters; notice the grammar across entries; notice which entries have marginal notes and which don't.
- **Cannot:** access sealed archives by stealth, pressure institutions, or "hack" anything. Every closed door stays closed unless someone *chooses* to open it — a librarian who read the middle once too, a daughter who kept a box.
- **The list exposes him:** each dealer inquiry, each library request, each visit to a grand-daughter is a fingerprint. The radar pings by ch. 17 because he did the work, not because he was careless.
